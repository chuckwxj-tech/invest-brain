## 原文

The reason I first started following $NBIS was the shortage of AI compute capacity.

As GPUs and Neoclouds continue to validate demand through orders and earnings, the next bottleneck is starting to shift toward optical networking.
That is why I have recently been focusing on several technology paths: 800G, 1.6T, 3.2T, CPO, OCS, silicon photonics, and InP.

What I’m looking for is a company that can remain inside the core AI data center supply chain even as the technology architecture changes.

The company I have screened down to has several characteristics that I find particularly interesting.
First, it has what I have repeatedly emphasized: architectural optionality.

If 1.6T continues to ramp, it has high-speed optical modules and core optical components.

As the industry upgrades toward 3.2T, it is already advancing technologies related to 400G/lane.
If CPO adoption accelerates, it also has high-power lasers, ELS, and related optical capabilities.

In other words, whether the architecture moves from Pluggable → NPO → CPO, it has a chance to remain part of the supply chain regardless of how the architecture evolves.

Second, I place even more value on the core components it controls.
Optical module capacity can be expanded. What is much harder to replicate quickly is InP, lasers, manufacturing yield, and advanced production capabilities.

The larger AI clusters become, the higher the requirements for the performance and reliability of high-speed lasers.

Compared with companies that mainly assemble optical modules, I prefer businesses that control these critical upstream capabilities.

Third, major customers have already started using real capital to secure capacity.

One of the world’s leading AI chip companies has already invested $2 billion, while also establishing a multi-year strategic partnership and purchasing arrangement.

Fourth, and ultimately the factor that determines whether the stock can go further:
Profits are already starting to materialize.

In the latest quarter, revenue was approximately $1 billion, up more than 100% year over year. Non-GAAP gross margin exceeded 50%, while operating margin has already moved above 30%.

Of course, I have also been reminding everyone over the past few days:
The hotter optical networking becomes, the more important it is not to focus on the story alone.
Once valuations become elevated, even a strong earnings report can still send a stock lower.

From $NBIS to the optical networking companies I’m researching today, my approach has never changed:
Find the next bottleneck that AI infrastructure must solve before the market fully recognizes it.
GPUs solve computing.

Neoclouds solve compute capacity supply.
Optical networking solves whether hundreds of thousands of GPUs can actually be connected efficiently.

This company is now on my priority watchlist, but for now, I’m not going to reveal the name publicly.
I’m putting together a complete research package covering its technology roadmap, orders, valuation, and key entry points.

If you are also researching opportunities in the next phase of AI infrastructure, feel free to reach out to me privately.

I can share the ticker and my research materials with you.

I hope the research can help you avoid some of the mistakes and detours that come with studying this sector.
