# They also demonstrated a 1.6T module using four 400G differential EMLs as a stepping stone toward future 3.2T optics. That’s why I increasingly think looking at $LITE simply as ...

## 用户备注

## 原文

They also demonstrated a 1.6T module using four 400G differential EMLs as a stepping stone toward future 3.2T optics.

That’s why I increasingly think looking at $LITE simply as a transceiver company misses the bigger picture.

Whether AI networking evolves through traditional pluggables, silicon photonics, near-packaged optics, CPO, external laser sources or OCS, Lumentum is trying to own some of the hardest photonic building blocks underneath those architectures.

Tomorrow I mainly want updates on three things.

First, has that roughly 30% gap between customer demand and Lumentum’s ability to supply narrowed at all? Given everything we’re hearing elsewhere in the optical supply chain, I wouldn’t be surprised if it hasn’t.

Second, how quickly is 1.6T ramping and what does management now expect from 200G EMLs? Last quarter 200G EML revenue more than doubled sequentially. Another major step-up would be a strong signal that the 1.6T cycle is moving from qualification into real volume.

And third, I want to hear about OCS and CPO.

The existing commitments here are already enormous, but revenue contribution was still relatively modest last quarter. If those businesses begin layering on top of an EML/transceiver business that is already supply constrained, the earnings power starts looking very different.

The fascinating part about $LITE right now is that the debate has changed.

A year ago the question was whether AI optics demand would actually materialize.

Today the CEO is effectively saying: we know the demand exists, we can see it years out, and the problem is figuring out how to manufacture enough product to satisfy it.

For a hardware supplier, that’s a very different problem to have.
