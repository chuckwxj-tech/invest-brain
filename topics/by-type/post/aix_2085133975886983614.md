TEST AND MEASUREMENT

VIAVI’S STRENGTH IS A POSITIVE READ-THROUGH FOR HIGH-SPEED TEST DEMAND, BUT IT ALSO SIGNALS COMPETITIVE SHARE PRESSURE FOR OTHER VENDORS (READ-THROUGH 9)

Affected companies: Keysight Technologies Inc. (KEYS: US), Teradyne Inc. (TER: US), Rohde & Schwarz, Anritsu Corp. (6754: Japan), and Advantest Corp. (6857: Japan)

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive for the high-speed test-equipment end market, but mixed by company; medium magnitude.

Supporting commentary and data: NSE excluding Spirent increased approximately 14.6% sequentially, while the data-center business excluding Spirent more than doubled year over year. Viavi cited demand across semiconductor laboratories, optical-module production, cable manufacturing, CPO, Ultra Ethernet, field deployment, and fiber monitoring. Management also indicated gross margins ranging from the low 60% area in some field instruments to the high 70% area in certain lab products.

Transmission mechanism: The underlying market for high-speed test and measurement is expanding because 800G, 1.6T, CPO, Ultra Ethernet, and increasingly complex optical systems require more validation, characterization, and production test. Keysight, Anritsu, Rohde & Schwarz, Teradyne, and Advantest can benefit from adjacent electrical, protocol, semiconductor, and system-level test demand.

The competitive implication is less uniformly positive. Viavi is extending from optical laboratory test into production, Ethernet traffic generation, semiconductor optical-plane test, and integrated network validation. The Spirent acquisition broadens its product portfolio and increases cross-selling potential. Keysight and Anritsu could face greater competition in Ethernet, transport, and high-speed communications testing. Teradyne and Advantest may encounter Viavi at the boundary between conventional semiconductor test and optical-plane validation as CPO integrates photonics directly into semiconductor packages.

Viavi’s statement that Spirent integration and restructuring were completed by the end of June indicates that the combined company can now compete with a more unified cost structure, sales organization, and roadmap. The Ultra Ethernet launch provides early evidence that the acquisition is generating product-level synergy rather than only cost savings.

The broader market read-through is positive because rising complexity expands total test content. The stock-specific read-through depends on competitive positioning. Vendors with differentiated electrical, protocol, optical, and production-test capabilities should benefit, while narrower platforms may face share pressure.

TELECOM SERVICE PROVIDERS AND WIRELESS INFRASTRUCTURE

WIRELESS TEST DEMAND REMAINS STABLE BUT WEAK, REINFORCING A DELAYED RADIO-ACCESS-NETWORK RECOVERY (READ-THROUGH 10)

Affected companies: Ericsson (ERIC: Sweden), Nokia Oyj (NOK: Finland), Samsung Electronics Co. Ltd. (005930: South Korea), Qorvo Inc. (QRVO: US), Skyworks Solutions Inc. (SWKS: US), and Keysight Technologies Inc. (KEYS: US)

Directional impact and magnitude: Negative, medium magnitude; negative near-term read-through with potential longer-term optionality.

Supporting commentary and data: Viavi stated that wireless products continued to experience “the same dynamic” with stable customer demand. Management remained optimistic about the longer-term outlook but identified no near-term recovery catalyst. Service-provider growth was characterized as approximately 1% to 2%, materially below data-center growth.

Transmission mechanism: Wireless test demand is linked to operator capital spending, new radio deployments, spectrum rollouts, network optimization, and equipment-vendor development activity. Stable rather than accelerating demand indicates that operators remain cautious and that the RAN spending environment has not entered a broad recovery.

Ericsson and Nokia face continued limited revenue acceleration from conventional 5G RAN deployments. Samsung’s network-equipment business faces a similarly subdued environment. Qorvo and Skyworks face an indirect negative read-through where weak infrastructure deployments and limited network densification reduce base-station radio-frequency demand. Keysight’s wireless test exposure may remain constrained even while its data-center and high-speed communications opportunities improve.

The signal is notable because Viavi’s overall NSE business is exceptionally strong. Wireless weakness is therefore not being obscured by a general communications downturn; it is an end-market-specific divergence. Data center and aerospace and defense are more than offsetting service-provider softness, but there is no evidence in the call that conventional wireless spending has turned.

The longer-duration optionality remains intact through 6G development, AI-RAN, Open RAN, network automation, and eventual operator replacement cycles. Those themes should not be treated as material near-term earnings drivers based on this call.

FIBER DEPLOYMENT AND CABLE NETWORK MODERNIZATION REMAIN HEALTHY BUT NOT HYPERGROWTH MARKETS (READ-THROUGH 11)

Affected companies: Ciena Corp. (CIEN: US), Corning Inc. (GLW: US), CommScope Holding Co. Inc. (COMM: US), Cisco Systems Inc. (CSCO: US), Harmonic Inc. (HLIT: US), and Vecima Networks Inc. (VCM: Canada)

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, low-to-medium magnitude; supportive near-term demand but not a major structural acceleration signal.

Supporting commentary and data: Viavi reported stronger seasonal service-provider demand, including increased demand for fiber-monitoring solutions supporting fiber buildouts and cable instruments supporting distributed-access-architecture migration. The service-provider category nevertheless remains the slower-growing portion of NSE.

Transmission mechanism: Fiber deployment creates demand for optical transport systems, fiber cable, connectors, field instruments, monitoring, and maintenance. Distributed access architecture requires cable operators to deploy remote PHY or remote MAC-PHY nodes, upgrade headends, and install new test capabilities.

Ciena benefits from optical transport and network modernization. Corning benefits from fiber and connectivity demand. CommScope benefits from outside-plant and cable-network infrastructure, subject to company-specific balance-sheet and portfolio considerations. Harmonic and Vecima benefit from distributed-access-architecture deployments. Cisco benefits from routing, optical, and cable-access equipment.

The positive conclusion is that fiber and cable modernization remain active and are contributing to field-instrument demand. The magnitude is more modest than the data-center read-through because Viavi described the broader service-provider business as growing only approximately 1% to 2%. The call does not support an aggressive carrier-capital-expenditure recovery thesis.

AEROSPACE, DEFENSE, AND POSITIONING

POSITIONING, NAVIGATION, AND TIMING DEMAND IS EMERGING AS A MULTI-YEAR DEFENSE ELECTRONICS GROWTH VECTOR (READ-THROUGH 12)

Affected companies: RTX Corp. (RTX: US), Northrop Grumman Corp. (NOC: US), L3Harris Technologies Inc. (LHX: US), Honeywell International Inc. (HON: US), Hexagon AB (HEXA-B: Sweden), and Teledyne Technologies Inc. (TDY: US)

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, medium magnitude; positive longer-duration fundamental shift with continuing near-term demand support.

Supporting commentary and data: Viavi reported another quarter of strong year-over-year aerospace and defense growth, driven by positioning, navigation, and timing products. Management described PNT as a “multi-year growth driver.” Aerospace and defense now represents approximately 17% of NSE revenue.

Transmission mechanism: Modern defense systems require resilient navigation and timing in environments where satellite signals are jammed, spoofed, degraded, or unavailable. Demand is increasing for inertial navigation, assured PNT, timing synchronization, test systems, and integrated sensing across missiles, aircraft, unmanned systems, naval platforms, and ground vehicles.

RTX, Northrop Grumman, and L3Harris benefit through platform content, avionics, electronic warfare, navigation, and mission systems. Honeywell benefits through inertial navigation and aerospace systems. Hexagon benefits through precision positioning and geospatial technologies. Teledyne benefits through sensing, instrumentation, and defense electronics.

The cross-portfolio significance is that Viavi’s diversification into aerospace and defense is not merely smoothing company-specific seasonality; it reflects broader demand for resilient navigation and timing technologies. This demand is likely to be supported by geopolitical tension, modernization programs, electronic-warfare requirements, and increased autonomy.

The principal caveat is that Viavi’s reported growth includes the Inertial Labs acquisition, making exact organic growth difficult to isolate. Nevertheless, continued strength after the acquisition and management’s multi-year characterization support a constructive sector read-through.

SUPPLY CHAIN, PRICING, AND COMPONENT COSTS

TEST-EQUIPMENT VENDORS RETAIN PRICING POWER DESPITE SEMICONDUCTOR INPUT-COST PRESSURE (READ-THROUGH 13)

Affected companies: Viavi Solutions Inc. (VIAV: US), Keysight Technologies Inc. (KEYS: US), Teradyne Inc. (TER: US), Anritsu Corp. (6754: Japan), and electronic-component distributors

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive for differentiated test-equipment suppliers; low-to-medium magnitude near term.

Supporting commentary and data: Viavi acknowledged semiconductor cost headwinds but stated that “so far, we’ve been just passing all those increases to our customers as part of the price adjustments.” NSE gross margin remained 64.1%, while consolidated operating margin expanded materially.

Transmission mechanism: High-speed optical and networking test systems are mission-critical, technically differentiated, and small relative to the total cost of a customer’s development program or production line. Customers are therefore less price-sensitive than buyers of commoditized hardware. Vendors can pass through component inflation without materially impairing demand, protecting gross profit.

Keysight, Teradyne, Anritsu, and other differentiated test vendors may have similar pricing resilience where equipment is required for product qualification, compliance, or manufacturing yields. The read-through is less favorable for undifferentiated instrument suppliers, where customers have more substitution options.

The qualification is that price increases can still reduce unit demand if customer capital budgets are fixed. Viavi explicitly acknowledged that higher semiconductor pricing could slow some growth. The net implication is positive for margins but not entirely neutral for volumes.

HYPERSCALER AND SEMICONDUCTOR CAPITAL SPENDING

NETWORKING SUPPLIERS MAY GROW FASTER THAN TOTAL HYPERSCALER CAPEX AS OPTICAL CONTENT AND TEST INTENSITY RISE (READ-THROUGH 14)

Affected companies: Arista Networks Inc. (ANET: US), Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: US), Marvell Technology Inc. (MRVL: US), Coherent Corp. (COHR: US), Lumentum Holdings Inc. (LITE: US), Credo Technology Group Holding Ltd. (CRDO: Cayman Islands), and Amphenol Corp. (APH: US)

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, high magnitude; longer-duration fundamental support.

Supporting commentary and data: Viavi stated that some of its relevant product lines should perform better than aggregate capital-expenditure growth because the company is entering applications where it was previously absent, particularly at 1.6T and in semiconductor optical-plane test. Management emphasized that capex initially includes construction and civil work, while equipment spending translates later into Viavi demand.

Transmission mechanism: AI data-center capital expenditure is shifting toward increasingly network-intensive architectures. Each incremental accelerator requires switching capacity, optical links, cables, connectors, retimers, and validation. As cluster size increases, network complexity and optical content can rise faster than compute-unit count. Suppliers can also gain incremental content through new architectures such as CPO, OCS, and higher-speed Ethernet.

Arista, Broadcom, and Marvell benefit from greater switch and connectivity content. Coherent and Lumentum benefit from higher optical content. Credo benefits from signal integrity and connectivity requirements. Amphenol benefits from cables and connectors.

The timing implication is important. Networking and optical revenue may lag the earliest phase of hyperscaler capex because initial spending includes land, buildings, power, and construction. As facilities move toward equipment installation and operation, the mix shifts toward networking and optics. This can extend supplier growth even if headline capex growth later moderates.

The risk is that not all hyperscaler capex converts proportionally into addressable networking revenue. A significant portion remains dedicated to compute silicon, power, cooling, and construction. Stock-specific upside therefore depends on content growth, market share, and deployment timing rather than headline capex alone.
