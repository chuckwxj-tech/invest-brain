⚙️AMD's Helios entered full production | the MI455X clears two advanced-packaging steps, and only one has a visible supply-demand picture $AMD $TSM

Seventy-two Instinct MI455X GPUs and eighteen 6th Gen EPYC "Venice" CPUs in a single rack, with 31 terabytes of HBM4 in total. That was the Helios configuration AMD laid out at Advancing AI on July 23, and Lisa Su spent real keynote time reading the numbers out. Helios is in full production, with partner shipments starting at the end of the third quarter and OpenAI expecting to bring it online from the fourth quarter.

Whether advanced packaging is loosening has been argued for months in CoWoS wafer counts. But the MI455X does not pass through only one advanced-packaging step.

The MI455X is less a chip than a stack. Compute dies built on 2nm are bonded on top of 3nm fabric and cache dies using SoIC, a 3D scheme that joins copper to copper directly instead of through bumps. That stack is then placed on a CoWoS-L substrate and wired alongside HBM4. The order matters: nothing reaches CoWoS-L until it has cleared SoIC.

With two steps in series, shipment pace settles at whichever is narrower. So which one is narrower?

Only one of them can be measured from outside. TrendForce sees TSMC's CoWoS capacity reaching 120,000 to 140,000 wafers a month by the end of 2026, with the supply-demand gap narrowing from roughly 20% to about 10%. The Commercial Times puts SoIC capacity at 15,000 to 20,000 wafers a month by the end of this year, doubling only by the end of next year. Setting those wafer counts side by side proves nothing on its own: the two steps carry different things, different areas, different yields. CoWoS comes with published expansion plans and a supply-demand outlook. SoIC comes with a capacity estimate and nothing about whether it covers demand.

There is no direct evidence yet that the earlier step is the narrow one. What is reported is how hard the later step is being pulled. Per the Commercial Times, part of the front-end chip-on-wafer work feeding CoWoS-L is expected to flow to ASE, and silicon interposers from UMC and Vanguard International Semiconductor are under consideration depending on demand. The 120,000 to 140,000 figure is a year-end outlook for CoWoS overall; the ASE transfer is a separate question about who handles the front-end step feeding CoWoS-L. Different scope, different timeframe. That an expansion plan and an outsourcing study surface in the same window still reads as strength of demand.

On the memory side, the same copper-to-copper bonding runs on the opposite clock. TrendForce reported on July 22 that Samsung is preparing a mass-production line with roughly 50 die-to-wafer hybrid bonders, but full-scale operation is seen in 2029 to 2030, timed to NVIDIA's Feynman generation. Bonding that memory could defer, logic cannot. Once you decide to stack compute dies on another die, the MI455X has no path around SoIC.

SoIC itself is not new for AMD. It has been in use since the MI300 generation. What is new is volume: racks of 72, deployed at gigawatt scale.

Measuring advanced-packaging tightness by CoWoS wafer counts alone is a habit worth moving one step upstream. What to track: whether SoIC allocation and supply-demand ever surface as published numbers, and whether that end-of-third-quarter shipment date moves.
