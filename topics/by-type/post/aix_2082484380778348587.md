$TER KEY READ-THROUGHS FROM TERADYNE Q2 2026 EARNINGS CALL

Teradyne’s Q2 2026 call provides unusually strong evidence that the AI infrastructure investment cycle is broadening beyond accelerators into memory, networking, data storage, power, board-level test, electronics manufacturing and industrial automation. The most important cross-market signal is that semiconductor test demand is no longer merely following wafer-fabrication investment; advanced packaging, higher device complexity and the rising economic cost of downstream yield loss are increasing test intensity per wafer, per die and per package. More than 60% of Teradyne’s revenue was AI-driven, total revenue exceeded $1.3 billion, all 3 operating groups grew both sequentially and year-over-year, memory book-to-bill exceeded 2, IST revenue increased approximately 2.5x sequentially, and electronics manufacturing and semiconductor robotics revenue increased 50% sequentially. The call is therefore positive for semiconductor test, memory equipment, high-speed networking, nearline storage, AI server manufacturing, power semiconductors and industrial automation. The principal negative read-throughs are that compute-related tester shipments will moderate during 2H26 before the next expected capacity surge in 1H27, Teradyne’s compute share gains will emerge gradually rather than through an immediate step change, memory and new-product mix will pressure equipment margins, co-packaged optics adoption remains highly uncertain, and mobile remains structurally weak relative to AI infrastructure.

SEMICONDUCTOR TEST AND CAPITAL EQUIPMENT

STRUCTURAL TEST INTENSITY IS RISING FASTER THAN SEMICONDUCTOR UNIT VOLUME ALONE WOULD IMPLY (READ-THROUGH 1)

Affected companies: Teradyne Inc. (TER: US); Advantest Corp. (6857: Japan); Cohu Inc. (COHU: US); FormFactor Inc. (FORM: US); KLA Corp. (KLAC: US); Onto Innovation Inc. (ONTO: US); Camtek Ltd. (CAMT: Israel); Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. (2330: Taiwan); ASE Technology Holding Co. (3711: Taiwan); Amkor Technology Inc. (AMKR: US).

Directional impact and magnitude: High positive for Teradyne and Advantest; medium-to-high positive for test handlers, probe cards, inspection and advanced-packaging metrology; positive but more capital-intensive for foundries and outsourced semiconductor assembly and test providers.

Supporting call evidence: Management stated that test equipment represented approximately 4% of semiconductor capital spending in 2023, approximately 7% in 2025 and approximately 8% during the first 5 months of 2026. Management expects the ratio eventually to settle within approximately 7%-9%, compared with much lower historical levels. Teradyne also expects wafer-fabrication-equipment capital spending to approach $250 billion by the end of the decade, supporting 5%-10% annual growth in 300-millimeter wafer production and 15%-20% annual growth in total transistor production. Management concluded: “We see a path for overall ATE TAM to reach or exceed $20 billion.”

Transmission mechanism: The conventional relationship between wafer volume and tester demand is being supplemented by a second, more powerful driver: the rising economic cost of allowing a defective die to proceed into an advanced package. A package containing a large accelerator, multiple HBM stacks, chiplets, substrates, optical components and advanced interconnect can carry several times the value of a conventional monolithic semiconductor. A single latent defect can impair the yield of the entire package. This raises the acceptable-quality threshold at every upstream test insertion and increases the economic return from additional wafer sort, final test, burn-in, optical validation and package-level inspection.

Teradyne and Advantest are the most direct beneficiaries because additional test seconds, insertions and device complexity expand automated-test-equipment demand even when wafer growth alone would not justify equivalent equipment additions. FormFactor benefits through additional probe-card complexity and replacement requirements at wafer sort. Cohu benefits through handlers, interface products and increasingly complex thermal and mechanical test requirements. KLA, Onto Innovation and Camtek benefit because multi-die packages require tighter inspection, metrology and process-control thresholds before expensive downstream assembly.

TSMC, ASE and Amkor should benefit from greater advanced-packaging volumes and higher revenue content per completed device, but the call also indicates that the capital and operational requirements of advanced packaging are increasing. More stringent known-good-die requirements, additional inspection steps and lower tolerance for quality escapes will require higher equipment spending and greater process-control intensity. This is favorable for revenue and customer barriers to entry, but potentially dilutive to incremental margins during initial technology ramps.

Near-term trading catalyst: The rise in test as a percentage of semiconductor capital spending supports continued upward revisions to 2026 and 2027 ATE-market expectations. It also reduces the probability that unusually strong current tester demand is purely a temporary accelerator-related order spike.

Longer-duration fundamental shift: Semiconductor test appears to be moving from a structurally slower-growing subset of semiconductor equipment to a category capable of growing at least in line with, and periodically faster than, front-end equipment. This supports higher normalized growth assumptions for Teradyne, Advantest, FormFactor and Cohu. The principal offset is that tester productivity, multisite efficiency, built-in self-test and shorter test times can partially counteract transistor growth, making the relationship structurally positive but not mechanically linear.

TERADYNE’S DUAL-VENDOR COMPUTE QUALIFICATIONS CREATE A GRADUAL SHARE HEADWIND FOR ADVANTEST, NOT AN IMMEDIATE DISPLACEMENT EVENT (READ-THROUGH 2)

Affected companies: Teradyne Inc. (TER: US); Advantest Corp. (6857: Japan); FormFactor Inc. (FORM: US); Cohu Inc. (COHU: US).

Directional impact and magnitude: High positive for Teradyne over the medium term; medium negative for Advantest’s relative SoC share; modest positive for interface, probe and handling suppliers that benefit from incremental platform qualifications.

Supporting call evidence: Teradyne delivered its 1st order for a merchant GPU customer during Q2 and completed correlation at a 2nd hyperscaler. Management stated that the dual-vendor qualification process normally progresses through opportunity, solution development, correlation and production ramp, requiring approximately 9-12 months from initiation to ramp. Teradyne currently has “one compute customer in the mature dual vendor phase, one in fast follower, and one in qualification prior to ramp.” During the fast-follower phase, management expects account share to increase incrementally from approximately 0% toward approximately 30%.

Transmission mechanism: The largest compute customers are increasingly qualifying 2 tester platforms to reduce supply-chain concentration, protect access to test capacity and improve negotiating leverage. The strategic significance is not simply that Teradyne can win an isolated socket. Once Teradyne establishes correlation and production capability on an initial device, software, engineering and service knowledge can be reused across subsequent devices within the same customer. This creates a pathway from a small second-source position toward a structurally larger share of the account.

The direct competitive risk falls on Advantest, which has a strong installed position in high-end compute testing. Teradyne’s success does not imply that Advantest’s absolute revenue must decline. The compute test market is expanding sufficiently quickly that Advantest can continue growing while losing incremental share. The more relevant risk is relative earnings growth and valuation: Teradyne can outgrow the market if additional hyperscaler and merchant GPU sockets move into production, while Advantest’s growth becomes more dependent on overall TAM expansion.

The qualification process also creates secondary demand for probe cards, handlers, device-interface boards and thermal-management systems. A new tester platform typically requires customer-specific interface hardware, program conversion and production qualification. FormFactor and Cohu can therefore benefit from greater platform diversity even when the test-equipment share merely shifts between Teradyne and Advantest.

Near-term trading catalyst: Completion of correlation at the 2nd hyperscaler is a meaningful de-risking event, but it should not be modeled as a large immediate revenue contribution. Correlation is a technical milestone rather than a production allocation. The call supports higher confidence in 2027 share gains, not a major 2H26 compute-revenue increase.

Longer-duration fundamental shift: Dual sourcing can gradually reduce the winner-take-most characteristics of high-end compute test. Teradyne’s share trajectory should improve socket by socket, but the process may take several years to reach mature dual-vendor status. This is positive for Teradyne and a persistent relative-share risk for Advantest, but not a near-term collapse in Advantest’s compute franchise.

The merchant GPU customer was not identified. No customer-specific conclusion should therefore be drawn regarding whether the initial program belongs to NVIDIA or AMD.

THE 2H26 COMPUTE TEST SLOWDOWN IS AN EQUIPMENT-TIMING AIR POCKET, NOT EVIDENCE OF AN AI SILICON DEMAND BREAK (READ-THROUGH 3)

Affected companies: Teradyne Inc. (TER: US); Advantest Corp. (6857: Japan); Cohu Inc. (COHU: US); FormFactor Inc. (FORM: US); NVIDIA Corp. (NVDA: US); Advanced Micro Devices Inc. (AMD: US); Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: US); Marvell Technology Inc. (MRVL: US); Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. (2330: Taiwan).

Directional impact and magnitude: Medium negative for 2H26 compute-related ATE shipment comparisons; neutral-to-positive for underlying AI silicon production; high positive for 1H27 tester-order reacceleration if customer capacity plans remain intact.

Supporting call evidence: Compute represented approximately 70% of Teradyne’s SoC revenue and increased nearly 600% year-over-year. Management stated that several networking and hyperscaler programs built test capacity during 1H26 and that this installed capacity is now being utilized. Management expects compute-related order timing to pressure 2H26 revenue, but stated: “The next surge for these customers is expected to be in the first half of 2027.”

Transmission mechanism: Tester orders are lumpy because customers purchase capacity ahead of semiconductor production ramps. Once a sufficient installed tester base has been deployed, equipment orders can pause even while semiconductor output continues increasing. A decline in Teradyne’s compute-equipment shipments during 2H26 therefore does not necessarily indicate falling accelerator, custom-ASIC or networking-silicon demand. It can instead reflect the transition from equipment installation to production utilization.

This distinction is important for interpreting cross-portfolio signals. Teradyne’s 2H compute softness should not be mechanically extrapolated into lower NVIDIA GPU shipments, weaker hyperscaler ASIC demand or reduced TSMC advanced-node utilization. The call indicates that recently installed tester capacity is being consumed by production and that customers are already considering the next capacity addition cycle for 1H27.

The near-term negative read-through is concentrated in the tester and interface supply chain. Teradyne, Advantest, Cohu and FormFactor may face difficult sequential comparisons as customers absorb installed capacity. The longer-term read-through is favorable because customers normally would not plan another major capacity surge in 1H27 unless they expected additional device generations, wafer starts or production volumes.

Near-term trading catalyst: Compute-related tester revenue could remain below the exceptional 1H26 level through Q3 and potentially Q4. Equipment names should therefore be traded on total-company breadth, memory strength and qualification progress rather than on an expectation of continued sequential compute acceleration.

Longer-duration fundamental shift: The expected 1H27 resurgence supports a continuing multi-generation AI compute cycle. The key risk is timing rather than end-market direction. Delayed advanced packaging, HBM, substrate or wafer capacity could push equipment orders between quarters without destroying the underlying demand.

MEMORY SEMICONDUCTORS AND EQUIPMENT

HBM STRENGTH HAS BROADENED INTO CONVENTIONAL DRAM, EXTENDING THE MEMORY CAPITAL-EXPENDITURE CYCLE (READ-THROUGH 4)

Affected companies: Micron Technology Inc. (MU: US); SK hynix Inc. (000660: South Korea); Samsung Electronics Co. (005930: South Korea); Applied Materials Inc. (AMAT: US); Lam Research Corp. (LRCX: US); ASML Holding N.V. (ASML: Netherlands); KLA Corp. (KLAC: US); Teradyne Inc. (TER: US); Advantest Corp. (6857: Japan).

Directional impact and magnitude: High positive for DRAM and HBM manufacturers; medium-to-high positive for memory wafer-fabrication and test-equipment suppliers; negative for consolidated tester gross margin because memory carries less favorable economics than peak compute configurations.

Supporting call evidence: Memory revenue reached a record $212 million, marking the 3rd consecutive quarter above $200 million. Teradyne expects the 2026 memory-test TAM to be more than 40% larger than in 2025, with material growth from 1H26 to 2H26. The memory book-to-bill ratio exceeded 2. Management stated that HBM remained strong, but that the surprise was “just how strong the DDR business is.” The strength was connected to renewed CPU-oriented applications, agentic AI and LPDDR usage in certain custom SoCs.

Transmission mechanism: The AI memory cycle is broadening beyond HBM attached directly to accelerators. Agentic inference, expanded CPU fleets, larger context windows, data preprocessing, retrieval systems and custom accelerators increase demand for conventional server DRAM and specialized low-power memory configurations. The result is a larger and more diversified memory-capacity requirement than an HBM-only framework would imply.

Micron, SK hynix and Samsung benefit from stronger utilization, improved pricing leverage and a more durable capital-spending requirement across both HBM and conventional DRAM. The breadth matters because an HBM-only cycle could be constrained by a limited number of accelerator platforms and advanced-packaging bottlenecks. Conventional DRAM growth increases the number of demand vectors and reduces dependence on any single architecture.

Applied Materials, Lam Research, ASML and KLA benefit as memory manufacturers add wafers, convert capacity to more advanced nodes and invest in HBM-related processing. Teradyne and Advantest benefit when new wafers and new memory generations require additional test capacity. Technology transitions, including future HBM and DDR generations, can increase test demand even when bit growth is partly offset by tester productivity.

A memory book-to-bill ratio above 2 indicates that customers are placing orders substantially ahead of current revenue recognition. This supports 2H26 and early 2027 equipment visibility, but it also requires careful interpretation. Elevated bookings can reflect capacity reservation and extended planning horizons rather than immediate shipment demand. The call nevertheless indicates that memory manufacturers are planning test capacity further into the future, reducing the risk of an abrupt 2027 memory-equipment decline.

Near-term trading catalyst: Memory-equipment and memory-producer estimates should receive support from stronger-than-expected conventional DRAM demand and a 2H-weighted test-equipment ramp. Micron, SK hynix and Samsung have the cleanest fundamental read-through.

Longer-duration fundamental shift: AI is increasing memory demand across a broader hierarchy, including HBM, DDR and LPDDR. This suggests that memory content per AI system can grow even when accelerator unit growth moderates. The negative offset is that aggressive capacity additions can eventually pressure pricing if supply growth exceeds actual bit demand.

NAND HAS MOVED FROM AN EXPECTED RECOVERY TO ACTUAL CUSTOMER CAPACITY PULL (READ-THROUGH 5)

Affected companies: Micron Technology Inc. (MU: US); Samsung Electronics Co. (005930: South Korea); SK hynix Inc. (000660: South Korea); Kioxia Holdings Corp. (285A: Japan); Lam Research Corp. (LRCX: US); Applied Materials Inc. (AMAT: US); KLA Corp. (KLAC: US); Teradyne Inc. (TER: US); Advantest Corp. (6857: Japan).

Directional impact and magnitude: Medium-to-high positive for NAND manufacturers and NAND-oriented equipment suppliers; high positive relative to the previously depressed NAND test base.

Supporting call evidence: Management distinguished the current environment from the beginning of 2026. At that time, Teradyne expected NAND demand eventually to improve but had not received corresponding capacity requests from memory customers. During Q2, management observed “the beginning of that inflection” and began receiving actual pull from customers for additional NAND capacity. Teradyne also identified a “resurgence in NAND final test.”

Transmission mechanism: Test-equipment orders are a tangible indication that NAND producers expect additional wafer output or final-device throughput. Customers generally do not add final-test capacity unless existing tester utilization, expected production volumes or upcoming technology transitions require it. The movement from conceptual demand recovery to actual tester orders therefore provides stronger evidence of an improving NAND capital cycle than commentary based only on end-market forecasts.

Data-center storage is an important driver. AI clusters require storage for training datasets, checkpoints, model versions, inference logs, retrieval databases and conventional cloud workloads. Not all of this demand is served by NAND, but the aggregate increase in storage capacity supports enterprise SSD consumption alongside nearline HDD growth.

Lam Research has particularly high sensitivity to NAND layer-count and capacity investment because more complex 3D NAND architectures require substantial deposition and etch intensity. Applied Materials and KLA also benefit from additional memory-fab activity and process-control requirements. Teradyne and Advantest benefit when final-test capacity is added, especially after several years of subdued NAND investment.

Near-term trading catalyst: Actual customer pull for NAND testers supports a more constructive 2H26 equipment outlook and increases confidence that NAND capital expenditures are moving off a depressed base. This can support memory-equipment earnings revisions before the full recovery becomes visible in wafer-equipment revenue.

Longer-duration fundamental shift: A sustained AI storage cycle could create a more balanced relationship between HDD and NAND rather than a simple substitution dynamic. HDD remains the lowest-cost solution for bulk storage, while NAND benefits from latency-sensitive and performance-oriented workloads. Both technologies can grow simultaneously as total data creation expands.

The principal negative risk is that stronger capital expenditure eventually creates additional supply and pressures NAND pricing. The current call supports an early-cycle recovery, not a conclusion that NAND has become structurally supply-constrained.

COMPUTE AND SERVER PROCESSORS

AGENTIC AI IS ADDING CPU AND DDR DEMAND, WITH ARM SHARE GAINS THE MOST IMPORTANT NON-GPU READ-THROUGH (READ-THROUGH 6)

Affected companies: Arm Holdings plc (ARM: UK); Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: US); Marvell Technology Inc. (MRVL: US); Micron Technology Inc. (MU: US); SK hynix Inc. (000660: South Korea); Samsung Electronics Co. (005930: South Korea); Intel Corp. (INTC: US); Advanced Micro Devices Inc. (AMD: US).

Directional impact and magnitude: Medium-to-high positive for Arm and custom-silicon suppliers; medium positive for conventional DRAM vendors; medium negative for Intel’s relative server-CPU share; mixed for AMD because total server-CPU demand can grow even as ARM gains architecture share.

Supporting call evidence: Management stated that CPU demand was strengthening and that stronger DDR demand was partly connected to “the resurgence in CPU-oriented applications, so agentic AI.” Teradyne also stated that it benefits more as the server-CPU mix shifts toward ARM and that a greater ARM share would create a larger compute-share opportunity. Management agreed that accelerator test intensity can be approximately 4x CPU test intensity, but emphasized that CPU volume remains economically important.

Transmission mechanism: AI infrastructure requires more than accelerators. Agentic systems use CPUs for orchestration, scheduling, retrieval, data movement, security, preprocessing and general-purpose workloads surrounding accelerator clusters. Inference deployments can also be more CPU-intensive than a narrow training-centric framework suggests. Rising CPU deployments increase server DDR demand and create additional opportunities for custom ARM-based processors.

Arm benefits because hyperscalers and cloud platforms can use ARM architectures to optimize power efficiency, workload-specific performance and total system cost. Broadcom and Marvell benefit from custom-silicon design activity associated with hyperscaler processors and supporting infrastructure. Memory producers benefit because CPU growth requires conventional server DRAM even when HBM growth remains concentrated around accelerators.

Intel faces the clearest relative-share risk. Teradyne’s statement does not establish an immediate decline in x86 unit demand, but it supports the view that incremental server-CPU growth is increasingly available to ARM-based architectures. AMD is less negatively exposed than Intel because it can continue gaining x86 share and benefit from overall server growth, but ARM penetration still limits the addressable x86 opportunity.

The approximately 4:1 accelerator-to-CPU test-intensity ratio means that 1 accelerator socket remains much more valuable to ATE suppliers than 1 CPU socket. CPU demand nevertheless matters because unit volumes can be significantly larger and because CPUs pull through conventional DRAM, networking and motherboard content.

Near-term trading catalyst: Stronger DDR orders and server-CPU activity support memory and custom-silicon demand during 2H26, partly offsetting the pause in new accelerator tester installations.

Longer-duration fundamental shift: AI infrastructure spending is becoming less narrowly tied to GPU units. A broader compute architecture that includes custom accelerators, merchant GPUs, ARM CPUs, x86 CPUs, networking silicon and multiple memory tiers creates a more diversified semiconductor demand cycle. This is positive for the ecosystem but increases competitive risk for suppliers tied to a single architecture.
