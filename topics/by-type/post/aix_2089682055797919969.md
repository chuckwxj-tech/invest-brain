BREAKING $AMD | Wedbush on $GOOGL Deal🚨

@Wedbush comment on potential @AMD @Google CPU/TPU partnership. 

“We would find it noteworthy if Google collaborated with AMD vs. Broadcom, Marvell, or Intel (with the latter seemingly the most often mentioned choice for such aspirational transitional projects of late),” Wedbush Securities analyst Matt Bryson wrote in a note to clients. “But we also would point out that even this speculation alone highlights the increased importance of 1) ASIC design capabilities and 2) IP blocks, suggesting that in this new hardware constrained world that for now the competition to build silicon is not a zero sum game.”

Little background on AMD & Google long term partnership where U shouldnt be surprised at this kind of collab, as AMD has the best CPU IP/Core. 

1. November 2016: AMD announces that its Radeon-based FirePro S9300 x2 server GPUs (dual Fiji GPUs with HBM) will become available on Google Cloud Platform starting in 2017. These accelerate Google Compute Engine and Google Cloud Machine Learning for parallel workloads such as machine learning, simulations, rendering, and scientific computing. This marks an early major public collaboration in cloud GPU acceleration (historically Nvidia-dominated territory).

2. 2017 onward: Deployment of the above AMD FirePro/Radeon GPUs begins on Google Cloud. AMD also advances its open-source ROCm (Radeon Open Compute) platform around this time, which supports broader GPU compute efforts including with hyperscalers like Google.

3. 2019 (building on multi-year collaboration): Google selects custom high-performance AMD Radeon datacenter GPUs (based on Vega/GCN 1.5 architecture, ~56 compute units, HBM2, ~10.7 TFLOPS, with ECC and virtualization features) for its Vulkan/Linux-based Google Stadia cloud gaming platform. AMD also provides software development tools and open-source Vulkan drivers. 

4. August 2019 / February 2020: Google begins using 2nd-generation AMD EPYC processors in its internal data centers. Google Cloud launches beta of general-purpose N2D virtual machines (VMs) powered by 2nd Gen AMD EPYC CPUs, offering improved performance and cost efficiency for balanced compute/memory workloads (later expanded).

5. 2020–2021: Continued expansion of AMD EPYC-powered instances on Google Cloud (N2D family and others). Collaboration deepens on performance, price-performance, and features like confidential computing (using AMD SEV technology).

6. June 2021: Google Cloud and AMD announce Tau VMs (first instance: T2D), powered exclusively by 3rd Gen AMD EPYC (“Milan”) processors. Optimized for scale-out workloads; claims of significantly better absolute performance and price-performance versus alternatives. Available later in 2021.

7. September 2021- February 2022: Google Cloud expands with refreshed N2D VMs using 3rd Gen EPYC (better price-performance) and launches C2D compute-optimized VMs also on 3rd Gen EPYC (suited for HPC, memory-bound, and high-performance workloads; general availability in early 2022). AMD EPYC now powers multiple Google Cloud VM families (general-purpose, scale-out, compute-optimized).

8. Ongoing through mid-2020s: Deep collaboration continues. Google Cloud offers a broad portfolio of AMD EPYC-powered VMs (including confidential computing options). AMD and Google emphasize joint optimization for performance, cost, sustainability, and workloads ranging from web/apps to HPC/AI-adjacent compute. Customers and case studies highlight cost/performance gains.

9. 2025: Google Cloud launches additional instances powered by 5th Gen AMD EPYC processors, including C4D (general-purpose/high-performance) and H4D (HPC-optimized), plus others such as N4D. These deliver further gains in performance, price-performance, core density, memory bandwidth, and features. Collaboration enables relatively rapid adoption cycles.

10. August 2026 (rumored/reported) indicates Google is working with AMD on aspects of its 10th-generation TPU (Tensor Processing Unit) project. Potential focus on hybrid designs incorporating AMD CPU IP, advanced packaging ( SoIC expertise), or related technology for CPU heavy workloads such as reinforcement learning/agentic AI marking a possible first major AMD role in a custom Google AI ASIC (Google’s primary TPU partner has long been Broadcom).
