## 原文

$LITE reports tomorrow, and going back through the last earnings call, I think the setup has actually become more interesting since then.

The biggest takeaway from the May quarter was not simply that revenue grew 90% YoY to a record $808M. It was how many different parts of Lumentum’s AI optics business were accelerating at the same time.

200G EML revenue more than doubled sequentially. Lumentum set records for both 100G and 200G EML shipments. Narrow-linewidth laser revenue for DCI grew more than 120% YoY. Cloud transceiver shipments increased more than 40% sequentially. And 1.6T transceivers were still only beginning their ramp.

Meanwhile, two of the opportunities investors have been waiting for — OCS and CPO — were still relatively early.

Management said its ultra-high-power laser ramp for CPO remained on schedule to generate meaningful revenue exiting calendar 2026, while also fulfilling the multi-hundred-million-dollar purchase order scheduled for the first half of 2027.

OCS was also progressing under the previously announced multi-year, multi-billion-dollar purchase agreement.

So despite $LITE already growing revenue 90%, some of the products that could become its largest AI growth drivers were barely contributing.

But I think what has happened since that earnings call is even more important.

CEO Michael Hurlston has continued talking about demand in language you rarely hear from a hardware supplier.

Earlier in the year Lumentum was saying it was sold out through 2027. Hurlston later said the company was falling further behind demand and could effectively fill 2028 capacity as well.

More recently, he said Lumentum is trying to build capacity as quickly as possible against demand visibility stretching roughly five years.

And then his comments around indium phosphide really stood out.

Lumentum operates five InP fabs, yet Hurlston said the company is still shipping more than 30% below what customers are asking for.

Talking about Lumentum and Coherent together, he went as far as saying:

“Between the two of us, I don’t think we can service the demand.”

Think about how unusual that statement is.

The AI optical bottleneck is increasingly moving upstream.

It isn’t simply about who can assemble another 800G or 1.6T transceiver. The scarce assets are becoming the lasers, InP manufacturing capacity and the underlying InP substrates needed to manufacture those lasers.

And Lumentum’s actions since earnings suggest management believes this shortage is structural rather than something that disappears over the next couple quarters.

On July 29, AXT announced a long-term InP substrate supply agreement with Lumentum running through the end of 2031.

Lumentum is committing to minimum annual purchases while AXT reserves capacity for them and supports additional capacity requirements.

That is important because Lumentum is simultaneously spending hundreds of millions of dollars expanding its own U.S. InP device manufacturing capacity in Greensboro.

The Greensboro fab is expected to significantly expand production of CW and ultra-high-power lasers, with NVIDIA specifically named as a customer.

So Lumentum is securing both sides of the equation: more internal laser manufacturing capacity and long-duration access to the upstream InP substrates required to feed those fabs.

That makes a lot more sense when your CEO is publicly saying customers want roughly 30% more product than you can currently supply.

The product roadmap is also getting much broader than just traditional optical transceivers.

Lumentum now has 200G EMLs, ultra-high-power CW lasers, external laser source modules, 1.6T OSFP transceivers, optical circuit switches and eventually CPO.

At OFC they demonstrated NVIDIA 1.6T modules using two different architectures powered by Lumentum technology — one using Lumentum’s ultra-high-power CW laser with NVIDIA silicon photonics and another using Lumentum’s 200G EMLs.
