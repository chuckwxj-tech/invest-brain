## 原文

I've written about the AI memory bottleneck before $SKHY $MU $SSNLF.

This post goes further, looking at how HBM and the related DRAM/NAND capacity behind it are becoming direct constraints on inference scale and ultimately AI revenue.

That supply constraint creates opportunities not only for memory manufacturers, but for an entire downstream ecosystem of equipment, packaging, controllers, interconnects and storage companies.

And this doesn't look like a short-term problem. The HBM market surged from roughly $4B in 2023 to $34.6B in 2025 and is forecast to reach approximately $54.6B in 2026, with a path toward $100B by 2027. Meaningful incremental supply doesn't really begin arriving until after 2028. 

Another issue: producing HBM requires substantially more DRAM wafer capacity than conventional DDR5, meaning the AI boom doesn't just tighten HBM -it can tighten the broader DRAM market with it.

Meanwhile, NAND and enterprise SSD demand is increasingly being pulled higher by AI infrastructure as datasets, embeddings, KV caches, checkpoints and colder storage tiers expand.

There are ways to mitigate the bottleneck:

→ KV-cache quantization and compression
→ Pruning, eviction and sliding-window techniques
→ PagedAttention
→ HBM → DRAM → SSD tiering and offload
→ $NVDA Dynamo and similar memory-management architectures
→ CXL memory pooling/disaggregation
→ Unified CPU-GPU memory
→ Denser HBM4/HBM4E stacks
→ I/O-aware algorithms such as Flash Attention and Ring Attention

These technologies reduce the amount of premium memory required per workload while making inference cheaper and more capable - which may actually enable even more memory usage.

The result, as I see it, is a larger and increasingly important memory hierarchy:

HBM → speed
DRAM/CXL → capacity
NAND → economical scale
Software → efficiency

Longer context windows and agentic workloads could compound one of the most durable bottlenecks in the AI stack through 2030.

Companies with direct pricing power, capacity discipline or enabling technology stand to capture outsized revenue - and especially profit/growth while the imbalance persists. $SKHY $MU  and  $SSNLF. 

*****NFA. This is only my opinion, and I'm not a qualified financial advisor. Some valuations already embed extremely aggressive expectations. We've repeatedly seen stocks sell off sharply when results were great but guidance merely failed to exceed the market's most optimistic assumptions.
