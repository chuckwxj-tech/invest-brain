## 原文

Key takeaways from the $COHR (Coherent) Q4 2026 earnings call.

1. The market's specific CPO fear got a direct on-record denial. Rosenblatt's Mike Genovese referenced chatter, some of it from Substack, claiming Coherent's CPO laser was having trouble. Anderson pushed back hard: production wafers are already running for December quarter shipments, and he said the customer is telling them to "please ship more as fast as possible." CPO, co-packaged optics, is the technique of mounting the optical engine right next to the switch chip instead of on a pluggable module, and it's one of the most contested debates in the whole optical space right now. This wasn't a hedge, it was a flat denial with a shipping timeline attached.

2. Backlog visibility now extends into 2028, and that's the real monetization proof. Anderson said order coverage through calendar 2027 is "exceptional" and customer orders now reach into calendar 2028, with LTAs (long-term supply agreements) running to the end of the decade. BNP's Karl Ackerman pressed for specifics, and Anderson confirmed the LTAs typically carry rising volume commitments each year plus locked-in pricing and take-or-pay minimums, meaning the customer pays even if they don't take full volume. That's a stronger revenue-durability signal than a simple bookings beat.

3. The growth engine is capacity-constrained, not demand-constrained, and the constraint is easing fast. Indium phosphide, the semiconductor material used in the lasers that go into transceivers, is the bottleneck. Coherent grew InP laser production 80% year over year in the June quarter and expects data center revenue growth to exceed 80% this quarter as a direct result. Six-inch InP output capacity is set to double this quarter, a full quarter ahead of the original plan, with yields beating the older three-inch lines across every device type they make. Assembly and test capacity, by contrast, isn't a constraint at all right now.

4. Guidance philosophy: management raised the multi-year target, and an analyst flagged it's well above Street numbers. Raymond James' Simon Leopold noted the newly reiterated target, a quarter above $3 billion in revenue by the end of fiscal 2027, is "well above current consensus." Management didn't walk that back, and Q1 guidance itself already beat what Wall Street was modeling on both revenue and EPS.

5. Competitive positioning: China-related import restriction speculation gets reframed as a tailwind. Simon Leopold asked about press reports on potential import restrictions on optical transceivers. Anderson called the reports speculative but noted Coherent is the largest U.S. supplier of transceivers with more than 20 U.S. production facilities, meaning any restriction would likely help them, not hurt them. That's the classic management move of turning a risk question into a moat answer.

6. Three new product lines, OCS, multi-rail, and Thermadite cooling, are all still pre-revenue or just starting. Optical circuit switching's addressable market was expanded from $2 billion to more than $4 billion, multi-rail systems, a new networking layer connecting workloads across multiple data centers, target a $2 billion market by 2030 with revenue starting in the first half of calendar 2027, and Thermadite thermal materials for XPU cooling don't start ramping until the second half of calendar 2027. All three are real optionality, none of them are in the current run rate.

Bottom line: Management's narrative is that fiscal 2027 accelerates from here on real backlog (bookings into 2028), a capacity constraint that's actively being resolved (six-inch InP ahead of schedule), and multiple new revenue layers that haven't even started yet (CPO, OCS, multi-rail, thermal). The evidence mostly backs that up.
