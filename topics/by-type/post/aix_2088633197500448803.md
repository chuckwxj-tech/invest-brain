## 原文

Intel memory-architecture notes circulating again: Z-Angle Memory (ZAM) and Cross-Batch Memory (XBM) as studied paths around HBM’s structural and packaging limits.

ZAM (with SoftBank’s SAIMEMORY in public discussion) leans on thinner tiers and denser vertical bonding for bandwidth density and power in a given footprint, with commercialization talk often pointed toward the late decade.
XBM, from patent coverage, aims to drop the expensive wide-interface silicon interposer in favor of high-speed serial links (UCIe-class) and backend-transistor DRAM stacks — matching footprint goals while changing cost and repair assumptions. Commercial windows in reporting sit after 2030 more often than “next year.”

Read as parallel R&D, not a 2027 supply shock.
HBM quals, offtake, and packaging still clear today’s AI racks. Alternative stacks matter if and when they survive customer reliability, yield, and ecosystem tooling — the same gate that slipped glass substrates.

Industrial hierarchy unchanged: contracted HBM bits first; paper architectures and patents second.
