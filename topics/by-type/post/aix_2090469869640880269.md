## 原文

Most people will read the Google news as “Marvell won a TPU socket.” I think that is too narrow.

The 8-K names five areas: AI inference accelerators, storage controllers, network-interface controllers, memory-interface controllers and near-memory compute. Google is testing whether Marvell can become a second custom-silicon platform beside $AVGO.

A warning on the $120B number: it is not committed spending or backlog. Almost all of Google’s warrant vests in 240 steps, one for each $500M of qualifying Marvell revenue through FY2033. Every step would take $120B.

The product story is everything around the accelerator.

HBM handles the hottest data beside the XPU. CXL, near-memory compute, SSD controllers and optical fabrics deal with capacity, warm KV cache and data movement beyond it. Marvell can monetize the memory wall without manufacturing DRAM.

NVIDIA has opened another route. Its $2B investment brings Marvell into NVLink Fusion for custom XPUs and compatible scale-up networking. Google gives Marvell access to the TPU stack; NVIDIA gives it access to the NVLink stack.

I think scale-up remains under-modeled. More GPUs, TPUs and custom XPUs mean more interfaces that need SerDes, switches, controllers and optics. Marvell can miss the main compute die and still win meaningful content around it.

Now comes the hard part: volume. Google’s purchases are discretionary. Full warrant exercise could dilute holders by roughly 6.7%. Several scale-up products are still samples or roadmaps, while NVIDIA keeps the richest software and compute economics.

My view: the TPU program gets Marvell into the building. The larger opportunity is becoming the connectivity layer shared by competing AI ecosystems.
