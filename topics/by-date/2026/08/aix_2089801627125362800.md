THE CHEAPEST WAY TO ADD A SCREEN TO ANY ELECTRONICS PROJECT COSTS $5. HERE'S EVERYTHING NOBODY TELLS YOU BEFORE YOU BUY ONE

$5 that's the price of the most satisfying upgrade you can make to any microcontroller project

the 0.96 inch OLED. tiny. bright. works with almost anything. two wires for data. two for power. that's the entire connection

the protocol is called I2C and it's the reason this display is so beginner friendly

most displays require 6, 8, sometimes 12 pins to communicate. I2C needs two. clock and data. your microcontroller talks to the display like it's sending a text message. one line. one conversation. clean

the specific model in this build has something unusual

dual color. the top portion of the screen is yellow. the bottom is blue. not switchable. not programmable. physically baked into the panel at the factory

which means if you want yellow text it goes at the top. blue content goes at the bottom. once you understand the constraint it becomes a design feature. status bar in yellow. data readout in blue. costs nothing. looks intentional

and here's the part every tutorial skips

they are fragile in a way that will surprise you

not fragile like "handle with care". fragile like "one slightly awkward push onto a breadboard and you're ordering a replacement". the glass substrate is thinner than it looks. the ribbon connecting the driver chip is microscopic. the vacuum seal that makes the display work does not survive mechanical stress

keep a spare

the display costs $5. the shipping takes a week. the project sitting on your desk waiting for a replacement costs more than the component ever did

I2C. two wires. $5. one spare in the drawer

the smallest screen you'll ever use will make your project look ten times more finished
