6-tech as the most noteworthy for the next 10 years

KAIST Professor Kim Jung-ho on the TTimes listed these six technologies as the most noteworthy for the next 10 years. 

I listed up the leading/strongest companies for each (as of mid-2026), with stock tickers where publicly traded:

1. HBM, HBF, HBS Memory Revolution
- HBM (High Bandwidth Memory): SK hynix ($SKHY / KRX:000660) — clear market and technology leader, primary supplier to NVIDIA; Samsung Electronics ($005930.KS); Micron ($MU).
- HBF (High Bandwidth Flash – NAND-based high-bandwidth memory): SK hynix ($SKHY) + Sandisk ($SNDK); Samsung Electronics ($005930.KS). Joint standardization and specs advanced via OCP; samples targeted around 2027. Professor Kim has projected the HBF market could surpass HBM around 2038.
- HBS (High Bandwidth SRAM): Primarily Professor Kim’s proposed concept of High Bandwidth SRAM (wafer-scale stacked SRAM). Alternatively refers to integrated mobile/edge DRAM + NAND solutions. Samsung Electronics ($005930.KS) and SK hynix ($SKHY) are the most capable implementers. Still largely pre-commercial.

Remarks: Korea is well-positioned to lead the multi-tier memory hierarchy (speed = HBM, capacity = HBF, ultra-speed = HBS).

2. 3D Packaging
- TSMC ($TSM) — dominant with CoWoS, SoIC, and advanced 2.5D/3D solutions for AI/HBM integration
- Intel ($INTC) — Foveros, EMIB
- Samsung Electronics ($005930.KS)
- ASE Technology ($ASX)
- Amkor Technology ($AMKR)

Remarks: TSMC currently holds the strongest position in high-value AI accelerator + HBM packaging.

3. Chiplet Technology (connecting multiple processors)
- AMD ($AMD) — pioneer of commercial chiplet architectures (EPYC/Ryzen)
- Intel ($INTC)
- TSMC ($TSM) — packaging and interposer enabler
- NVIDIA ($NVDA)
- Broadcom ($AVGO)

Remarks: The UCIe standard is accelerating heterogeneous integration. Design leaders + advanced packaging form the key combination.

4. Glass Substrate
- Intel ($INTC) — strong patent portfolio and commercialization roadmap (broader adoption expected 2026–2030)
- Absolics (subsidiary of SKC, KRX:011790)
- Samsung Electro-Mechanics (KRX:009150)
- LG Innotek (KRX:011070)
- Shinko Electric and material suppliers such as Corning, AGC, Schott  

Remarks: Glass substrates address warpage and density limits of organic substrates for large AI packages. Competition is intense among U.S., Korean, and Japanese players.

5. Silicon Photonics
- Intel ($INTC)
- Cisco ($CSCO)
- Broadcom ($AVGO)
- GlobalFoundries and TSMC ($TSM) — foundry platforms
- Marvell ($MRVL)
- Ayar Labs (private)
- NVIDIA ($NVDA) — heavy investment in CPO and partnerships
- Lumentum ($LITE)
- Coherent ($COHR)

Remarks: Critical for overcoming copper interconnect limits in AI data centers via co-packaged optics (CPO).

6. Quantum Computing, Neuromorphic Computing, Optical Computing
- Quantum Computing: IBM ($IBM), Alphabet/Google ($GOOGL), IonQ ($IONQ), Quantinuum, PsiQuantum (private), Rigetti ($RGTI), D-Wave ($QBTS). Different modalities (superconducting, trapped-ion, photonic, etc.). IBM and Google lead in platforms and scale; IonQ and Quantinuum excel in fidelity.
- Neuromorphic: Intel ($INTC) — Loihi series; IBM ($IBM); BrainChip.
- Optical Computing: Lightmatter (private), Lightelligence (private), Celestial AI (related to Marvell $MRVL), Luminous Computing, and similar firms pursuing optical matrix operations and interconnects for energy-efficient AI inference.

Remarks: These technologies are highly interconnected (e.g., 3D packaging + chiplets + glass substrates + silicon photonics support advanced HBM/HBF systems, with longer-term evolution toward quantum, optical, and neuromorphic approaches). 

📒Professor Kim particularly emphasizes a memory-centric AI era, in which the memory hierarchy will increasingly determine system performance more than the GPU itself, and highlights the strategic importance of Korean companies maintaining leadership in HBM and HBF.
