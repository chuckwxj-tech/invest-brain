What is happening in semiconductors world 👇

Samsung and SK Hynix are pushing beyond HBM into the next layer of memory innovation.

At FMS 2026, Samsung unveiled its LPDDR5X-PIM, the industry’s first processing-in-memory product based on low-power DRAM. PIM places small compute units inside the memory itself so data can be processed without constant back-and-forth to the processor. Samsung is now moving from research presentations into active customer engagement, with a deeper technical reveal planned at Hot Chips next month.

SK Hynix has also been showing multiple PIM prototypes, including AiMX, CuD and CMM-Ax. 

Micron confirmed it is researching its own PIM architecture.

CXL is the other major battleground. Samsung is preparing CXL 3.2 memory for production this year. 

SK Hynix recently showed comparable samples. Micron highlighted CXL at FMS, claiming 5–10x performance gains when applied to AI systems. 

As AI data volumes grow, CXL is gaining attention as a way to expand memory capacity beyond what HBM alone can deliver.

Other players are exploring alternatives or complementary approaches. Kioxia introduced its GP1 series SSD with XL Flash technology that can assist GPU compute without going through HBM. 

Intel has filed patents for cross-batch memory (XBM) aimed at simplifying connections and reducing reliance on silicon interposers. 

Qualcomm plans to launch an AI chip with high-bandwidth compute technology as early as the end of this year. 

China’s CXMT may begin LPDDR6 production in the second half. TSMC is preparing its Coupe silicon photonics platform for commercialization later this year.

The race is no longer just about stacking more HBM. It is about rethinking how memory and compute interact at the system level.
