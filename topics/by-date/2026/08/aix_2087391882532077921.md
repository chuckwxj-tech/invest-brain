AI inference is turning persistent storage into a growing bottleneck.
$SNDK
At the Future of Memory and Storage 2026 conference, analysts highlighted the rapid rise in demand for key-value cache capacity. 

Sandisk estimates that persistent KV cache (direct-attached SSDs plus network-attached compute and storage SSDs) could reach roughly 1 zettabyte by 2030. 

That scale would represent more than 70% of estimated 2027 industry NAND bit supply, even after allowing for assumptions on session retention, miss ratios and token volumes.

Much of the industry’s new cleanroom capacity is being prioritized first for high-bandwidth memory and then for conventional DRAM ahead of NAND. 

As a result, it is unclear where the additional NAND bits required for this scale of KV cache will come from. The imbalance points to tighter future supply-demand conditions and potential pricing support for NAND.

Other themes at the conference included growing interest in CXL-enabled pooled DRAM to raise memory utilization at hyperscalers, and early theoretical performance results for High Bandwidth Flash from Sandisk. 
$MRVL
Marvell also discussed CXL solutions and optical approaches to disaggregated memory for larger KV caches.

The discussion underscores how AI inference workloads are expanding the role of high-capacity, high-endurance NAND and SSDs within the overall memory hierarchy.

How significant do you expect persistent KV cache demand to become for overall NAND bit growth by the end of the decade?
