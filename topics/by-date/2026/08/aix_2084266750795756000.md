# The rumor is that Rubin Ultra has been downgraded from 384GB of 12-Hi HBM4E to 192GB of 8-Hi HBM4, with GPU power falling from 2,300W to 1,800W. My view: the cost-down direction...

## 原文

The rumor is that Rubin Ultra has been downgraded from 384GB of 12-Hi HBM4E to 192GB of 8-Hi HBM4, with GPU power falling from 2,300W to 1,800W.

My view: the cost-down direction is credible, but the exact configuration is not yet confirmed.

Several separate developments may have been combined into one headline:

1. SemiAnalysis previously reported that the four-die Rubin Ultra package had been cancelled, leaving a two-die design.

2. Korean industry sources told Damnang that a major customer had asked memory suppliers to evaluate 8-Hi HBM4 as a way to reduce cost per GB and total HBM procurement spending.

3. SemiAnalysis models a 1,800W Max-Q configuration for Vera Rubin NVL72, but that is Vera Rubin, not Rubin Ultra. It also discusses both 1,800W and 2,300W operating points, so the power difference may partly reflect SKU selection rather than a wholesale redesign.

There is also an arithmetic problem with the simplified rumor: moving from 12-Hi to 8-Hi reduces stack height by one-third, not one-half. To get from 384GB to 192GB, the package would also need fewer stacks, lower-capacity dies, fewer GPU dies, or another architectural change.

If the 192GB configuration is confirmed, however, it would be a real negative for HBM content per GPU.

A 50% reduction in HBM capacity requires GPU shipments to double just to keep total HBM bit demand unchanged. Lower power alone cannot offset that: moving from 2,300W to 1,800W allows roughly 28% more GPUs under a fixed power budget, leaving aggregate HBM capacity about 36% lower.

The more likely interpretation is therefore not "AI demand is collapsing," but "the AI infrastructure profit pool is shifting."

Less value goes to HBM capacity and the HBM4E premium. More value may move toward NVSwitch, scale-up networking, optical connectivity, high-layer-count PCBs, M9-class materials, connectors and power delivery.

For memory suppliers, this would pressure HBM ASP and product mix, especially for SK hynix and Micron. But it would not automatically end the memory upcycle. HBM still consumes substantially more wafer capacity per GB than commodity DRAM, inventories remain low, long-term agreements limit pricing downside, and inference growth continues to increase system-level demand for HBM, SOCAMM, MRDIMM, CXL memory and SSDs.

My confidence levels:

- Customers pushing for 8-Hi HBM4 and lower costs: medium-high.
- Rubin Ultra standardizing on exactly 192GB of HBM4: medium-low.
- This causing an industry-wide collapse in memory demand: low.
- Value shifting from HBM toward networking and advanced PCB content: medium-high, but dependent on the final Kyber/Oberon rack mix.

The key evidence to watch is not another supply-chain headline. It is NVIDIA's final package and HBM specifications, 2027 HBM4 pricing and LTA terms, and orders at high-layer-count PCB and CCL suppliers that reveal whether Rubin Ultra will ship primarily in Kyber or Oberon racks.
