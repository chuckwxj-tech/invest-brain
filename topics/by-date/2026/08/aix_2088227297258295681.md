## 原文

Nvidia is reportedly pushing its Feynman platform harder while Vera Rubin is still ramping into mass production.

Reports on Thursday describe resources shifting toward the generation after next, targeted for late 2028. Treat the specifics as industry-source reporting rather than anything Nvidia has confirmed.

Feynman is expected to use 3D chiplets on TSMC's SoIC stacking, high bandwidth memory, and co-packaged optics
The process is expected to be A16, TSMC's upgraded 2nm-class node
NVLink bandwidth is talked about beyond 1,000 terabytes per second
$TSM reported July sales up 45% year on year, so the demand side is not the question

The constraint here is not lithography. It is advanced packaging. SoIC, interposers and co-packaged optics all run through assembly and test capacity that is far smaller than wafer capacity and takes years to expand. A GPU whose die is ready but whose package is not is a GPU you cannot ship.

That is the number I would want before believing any 2028 date. Not transistor density. Wafer-per-month of packaging capacity, and how much of it is already spoken for.

The pattern to watch is whether TSMC guides packaging capex up again. If Nvidia is genuinely pulling Feynman forward, someone has to pour that building first, and buildings take longer than tape outs.
