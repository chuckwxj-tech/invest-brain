Samsung Electro-Mechanics Didn't Form a Glass Substrate Company — It Formed a Glass Substrate Materials Company

Samsung Electro-Mechanics (SEMCO) used its Q2 2026 earnings call to highlight its AI infrastructure bet: revenue of KRW 3.46 trillion (+24% YoY), operating profit of KRW 440.4 billion (+107% YoY, beating consensus by 8.4%), over 10 long-term supply agreements (LTSAs) with hyperscalers and chipmakers, and a newly formed glass-related joint venture.

The catch: the new JV doesn't make glass substrates.

On July 2, 2026, SEMCO signed a definitive agreement with Dongwoo Fine-Chem — a wholly owned subsidiary of Sumitomo Chemical — to form GlaSSEM, a joint venture producing "Glass Core," the raw glass material at the center of a finished glass substrate, not the finished substrate itself. SEMCO holds 66%, Dongwoo Fine-Chem 34%. Total investment is KRW 480 billion (~$310M), based at Dongwoo's Pyeongtaek facility, targeting full operation in H2 2027. This is upstream material self-sufficiency, distinct from SEMCO's own finished-substrate pilot line at Sejong, which is separately targeting prototype supply this year.

Meanwhile, on the finished-product side, SKC's Absolics remains ahead. Absolics has completed equipment installation at what's billed as the world's first dedicated glass substrate mass-production facility in Covington, Georgia, and is shipping mass-production-grade samples to AMD and AWS as it enters the qualification stage, targeting full mass production by the end of 2026.

Comparing SKC's end-2026 finished-product target against GlaSSEM's H2 2027 material-JV target conflates two different stages of the value chain. The accurate picture: SKC leads on finished substrates; SEMCO is running two parallel tracks — its own Sejong pilot line for finished substrates, and GlaSSEM for upstream material independence — to close the gap.

The glass substrate race has narrowed to a domestic Korean rivalry: SK Group's Absolics versus Samsung Group's SEMCO. SKC's edge is time; SEMCO's is group-level capital and a materials partnership with Sumitomo Chemical.

Bottom line: "Business formalization" overstates what happened. This is a materials-supply-chain move, not a finished-product launch — and on finished glass substrates, SKC still holds the lead.
