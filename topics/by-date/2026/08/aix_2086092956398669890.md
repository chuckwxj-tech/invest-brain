## 原文

AI is a race against the lack of compute supply. And the only bottleneck existing to fill the demand behind it are fabs.

But before any of that matters, you need the chips. And chips don’t exist without fabs.

The compute layer is the ignition point.

No advanced logic at scale means no AI accelerators at the pace demand wants. The only companies that can actually make those leading-edge chips at volume are the foundries, e.g.,  $TSM, $INTC Intel, Samsung.

Intel is America’s only real shot at sovereign advanced foundry capacity. 18A is real, customers are testing (including all of your favorite hyperscalers, like $GOOGL, $MSFT, $AMZN, $TSLA), and the government stake / national security angle makes this existential. If Intel executes, it de-risks the entire US AI stack, geopolitically and monopolization-wise. If it doesn’t, we stay dangerously dependent.

Flow of the real bottleneck chain:

1. Design/IP ($NVDA Nvidia, Intel, $AMD, custom ASICs, $ARM/x86)

2. Leading-edge Fabs THE FIRST HARD BOTTLENECK (TSMC dominant, Intel critical for US, Samsung)

3. Advanced Packaging (CoWoS, EMIB, Foveros, Co-EMIB SoIC)

4. HBM Memory ($MU, $SNDK, $DRAM - SK Hynix / Kioxia / Samsung)

5. Energy and Data Centers (the downstream problem everyone sees) - in my own growth portfolio, $QS is about to be a solution no one has seen coming.

Fabs are the gatekeeper. Everything else is downstream.

The market is pricing the downstream (power, data centers). If you're smart then your money is front-running the upstream foundry/capacity trade, especially US onshoring via Intel.

Fabs first. Everything else follows.
