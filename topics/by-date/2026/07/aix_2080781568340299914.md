Just in: Top 20 U.S. stocks by trading volume: San Disk fell more than 10%, and the target price of San Disk was lowered from $3,250 to $3,050 by analysts.

Micron Technology, which ranked No. 1 in U.S. stock market turnover on Friday, closed down 6.90%, with a turnover of US$37.329 billion. Micron Technology ended lower on Friday as investors took profits after a gain this week while increasing competition from Chinese memory chip makers weighed on the stock.

Micron had surged earlier this week after Tesla CEO Elon Musk said during the company's second-quarter earnings call that the automaker had secured a large supply of Micron's memory chips on favorable terms. However, the gains were reversed as traders reassessed the outlook for the memory chip market.

Chinese memory manufacturers continue to expand market share. These developments have raised questions about the competitive landscape of the future server memory market. At the same time, some bearish analyst comments also pointed out that as AI computing costs evolve, DRAM pricing may soften.

Micron shares have pulled back after recent gains, but Wall Street analysts remain generally optimistic about the company's long-term prospects. Second-place NVIDIA closed down 0.92%, with a transaction volume of US$23.734 billion.

In response to the explosive growth in demand for artificial intelligence infrastructure and to strengthen the domestic semiconductor supply chain in the United States, chip giant Nvidia and packaging and testing giant Amkor Technology have reached a multi-year strategic cooperation agreement worth US$1.5 billion.

Under the agreement, Nvidia will provide Amkor with an upfront payment to support the expansion of its advanced packaging and test capabilities in the United States, particularly in Arizona.

The two parties will jointly develop advanced packaging technologies for next-generation AI and accelerated computing platforms, focusing on key areas such as high-density interconnection and heterogeneous integration, aiming to integrate different types of chips into a single package to improve the performance and energy efficiency of AI processors.

Currently, advanced packaging has become a key bottleneck for AI chip performance and delivery. In the context of TSMC's CoWoS and other production capacity being in short supply, this cooperation is another strategic move by Nvidia to ensure its AI processor shipments after locking in the supply of key materials such as memory and glass substrates.

SanDisk, the third place, closed down 10.79%, with a transaction volume of US$20.437 billion. Mehdi Hosseini, an analyst at the investment bank Susquehanna, lowered the target price of SanDisk from US$3,250 to US$3,050, but still maintained a "buy" rating. The new target price means there is still about 80% room for upside from the current stock price.

Husseini said the target price reduction was mainly due to the company revising the revenue and earnings per share forecast errors in its previous financial model.

In addition, there are still uncertainties in the storage architecture of AI inference applications - if AI companies adopt KV Cache offloading solutions, the demand for enterprise-level SSDs will be greatly increased. On the contrary, if they continue to rely on DRAM/HBM, the demand for SanDisk's flagship products will be lower than expected.

Despite the price target cut, Wall Street's overall rating on SanDisk is still a "Strong Buy," with an average target price of about $2,368, suggesting there is still room for more than 40% upside in the next 12 months. Of the 24 analysts tracked by FactSet, 21 have buy or strong buy ratings. No.

Prominent investor Michael Burry said he is ramping up bearish bets on several technology stocks, adding short positions in Nvidia and the VanEck Semiconductor ETF, while insisting that he has not closed his profitable short bet on Tesla. Burry is world-famous for accurately predicting and shorting the 2008 U.S. subprime mortgage crisis.

He is also the prototype of the protagonist in the Oscar-winning film "The Big Short." "I'm not closing my short Tesla position. Intel announced its second-quarter financial report on Thursday, with total revenue of US$16.1 billion and adjusted earnings per share of 42 cents, both exceeding market expectations.

It was the largest single-quarter revenue increase in the past 15 years, and its third-quarter performance guidance was also higher than agency estimates. The AI computing power infrastructure boom boosted server processor sales. Data center business revenue surged 59% to $6.3 billion, and PC business revenue rose 13% year-on-year to $8.9 billion.

The company has currently signed 10 long-term supply agreements, and the production capacity supply is tight. OEM business revenue increased by 31% year-on-year to US$5.8 billion, and gross profit margin rebounded to 42% from 2.5% in the same period last year.

Despite its impressive performance, Intel's stock price fell on Friday, falling 28% in July and still rising more than 170% during the year. Apple, ranked sixth, closed up 3.53%, with a turnover of US$15.664 billion.

The latest report from market research organization Counterpoint Research shows that the folding screen smartphone market is expected to usher in a major turning point in 2026. Apple will launch its first folding iPhone in 2026.

The organization predicts that Apple is expected to quickly capture 25% of the market share in its first year of entering the market, becoming a key force in reshaping the competitive landscape. In addition, there are market reports that Apple is developing the next generation MacBook Neo, which will be equipped with the A19 Pro chip.

In terms of performance, the A19 Pro is about 10-15% faster than the A18 Pro in single-core and multi-core CPU tests. According to reports, the improvement in GPU performance is even more significant, and benchmark tests show that the performance of A19 Pro has been improved by up to 40%.
