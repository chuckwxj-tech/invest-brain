## 原文

Storage: AI Inference Triggers Demand Explosion and Paradigm Upgrading, Cycle Visibility Greatly Extended

This report was published by China Tai Securities analysts Wang Fang, Yang Xu, and Kang Lixia on May 16, 2026. Its core thesis is that the memory industry is evolving from a traditional cyclical oscillation driven by consumer electronics into a structural growth cycle dominated by surging AI inference workloads. Memory is advancing toward a collaborative architecture featuring "high bandwidth + large capacity + hierarchical management," with long-term agreements (LTAs) between original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) and North American cloud service providers (CSPs) extended to 3–5 years while locking in high price floors. Consequently, cycle visibility has been significantly prolonged.

The report's core data has been cross-verified across multiple authoritative sources:

Starting in Q3 2025, the memory chip industry entered an "unprecedented super cycle." SK Hynix explicitly stated that supplier inventory has reached its lowest level and the shortage could persist until 2028.
CFM Flash Market data shows that the global memory market size reached a record high of $58.459 billion in Q3 2025, with DRAM at $40.037 billion (up 24.7% quarter-over-quarter) and NAND at $18.422 billion (up 16.8% quarter-over-quarter).
TrendForce data indicates that Q3 2025 DRAM prices surged 171.8% year-over-year, with the contract price of 32GB DDR5 jumping from $149 in September to $239 in November (a 60.4% increase).

China Tai Securities' proprietary assessment—that "server demand share increased from the past 30%–40% to 50%–60% by May 2026"—is highly consistent with Open Source Securities' finding that "AI's demand share for memory had already reached 40% in 2025 and will increase further," as well as Phison CEO K.H. Pan's remark that "the memory boom driven by AI inference is quite rare and perhaps a once-in-a-lifetime occurrence."

Even more critical hard backing is that in October 2025, OpenAI launched the "Stargate" project, officially signing memory chip supply agreements with Samsung and SK Hynix to lock in up to 900,000 DRAM wafers per month, equivalent to 40% of global DRAM production. This provides epic buyer-side commitment and material proof for the report's core argument that "AI inference brings a comprehensive explosion in demand."

Technical Paradigm Upgrading

The judgment regarding technological paradigm upgrades is supported by solid underlying mechanisms and industrial deployment evidence. The three technical directions proposed in the report—"Prefill and Decode separation, compute-in-memory/computing replacement, and CXL memory pooling"—have all transitioned from academic papers to production deployment:

AWS and Cerebras' decoupled inference practice demonstrates that Prefill is compute-bound (limited by chip computing power) while Decode is memory-bound (limited by memory read speed). Handling both phases on a single hardware unit creates severe interference, and the DistServe paper quantifies that a single large prefill can inflate decode latency by 2 to 30 times.

By 2025, almost all mainstream inference frameworks—including vLLM, SGLang, NVIDIA Dynamo, and TensorRT-LLM—support Prefill-Decode (PD) separation, with NVIDIA Dynamo achieving a 30x request throughput increase on Blackwell hardware compared to non-separated services.
TF Securities' report on computing replacement shows that CachedAttention technology caches KV Cache into external storage media such as HBM, DRAM, and SSD, shortening Time-to-First-Token (TTFT) by 87%, increasing Prefill stage throughput by 7.8 times, and reducing end-to-end inference costs by 70%.
QLC + PCIe/NVMe + CXL are constructing the next-generation AI SSD foundation, upgrading SSDs from mere storage media to "long-term memory" carriers for AI inference.
CITIC Securities' GTC preview further confirmed that NVIDIA's 2025 launch of Rubin CPX aims to reduce Prefill costs (switching to GDDR video memory to replace expensive HBM), its acquisition of Groq brings LPU for Decode efficiency gains, and Huawei's Ascend 950 chip also includes PR and DT versions. PD-separation dedicated hardware is expected to become the global mainstream.
The report's judgment that "the relationship between memory and computing is moving from traditional decoupling to collaborative optimization" is completely aligned with the industry-wide technological evolution trajectory.

Long-Term Agreements (LTAs) and Counter-Variables

The judgment that Long-Term Agreements (LTAs) are reshaping industry rules has been verified by multiple sources, but the optimistic assumption of "eliminating periodicity" harbors severe counter-variables. An investigative report by the 21st Century Business Herald on May 27, 2026, revealed:

UBS analyst Timothy Arcuri's May 26 research report argues that LTAs are fundamentally eliminating the cyclicality of the memory industry. Hyperscalers like Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and Meta are proactively signing 3- to 5-year fixed-price long-term contracts with memory manufacturers backed by advance payments, representing binding procurement commitments (locking volume, locking price, and even locking wafer capacity).

UBS models calculate that even if DRAM spot prices plummet by 50% in fiscal year 2029, Micron's full-year EPS can still be maintained above $100. LTAs can narrow the price volatility of DDR from peak to trough by about 50%. By 2027, 20% to 30% of total industry DDR bit shipments could be locked in by fixed-price LTAs, and 60% to 70% of top hyperscalers' DDR5 procurement may already be under fixed contracts.

J.P. Morgan's mid-May report carried the direct title "LTAs Are Eliminating the Memory Industry's Cyclicality," while Citi's logic is that HBM production crowding out general DRAM wafer capacity has led to a long-term shortage of general-purpose memory. Micron's stock price surge has ushered in a "Davis double-play of profit and valuation system switching."
The original report's mention that "some buyers agreed to pay massive advance payments exceeding $10 billion" is supported by public reports from Korean media such as EBN. Samsung is in deep negotiations with Microsoft and Google for LTA prepayments exceeding $10 billion, which stipulate that if the buyer fails to complete the committed purchase volume within 3–5 years, the shortfall will be directly deducted from the prepayment. Micron disclosed its first 5-year strategic customer agreement in its Q2 FY2026 financial report and raised its FY2026 CapEx to $25 billion, with another $10 billion added for FY2027.
However, the 21st Century Business Herald simultaneously offered a cold counter-examination: Micron currently boasts a market capitalization of $1 trillion and a PE ratio of around 18x. The denominator is a super-cycle peak profit characterized by "DDR4 contract prices jumping 10x in 15 months, HBM sold out year-round, and gross margins leaping from 36% to 75%." Multiplying cyclical peak earnings by a seemingly reasonable multiple to arrive at a "not expensive" valuation is precisely the classic valuation trap when cyclical stocks peak.
More crucially, "the premise for LTAs to truly eliminate cyclicality is that the demand side does not collapse." The physical world contains nothing that grows at over 40% forever; it does not require an AI bubble burst—a simple deceleration from 45% growth down to 20% could flip the supply-demand balance of memory chips within 18 months. "The exact moment when an LTA is truly needed is precisely when it is most likely to fail."

Analytical Perspectives

1. Conservative Perspective (Data Verification & Caveats)
Spot Price Correction: The report's mention of "spot prices experiencing a narrow 10%–20% callback" lacks unified authoritative data sources and can only be indirectly supported by qualitative descriptions from industry media regarding channel inventory price softening. It should be treated as qualitative trader volatility rather than exact figures.
Prepayment Scale: Reports of "over $10 billion in massive advance payments" stem primarily from industry media reports and rumors (such as Korean media EBN) without full access to original contract texts. References must be labeled as "public reporting caliber."
2. Neutral Perspective (Consensus & Industry Validation)
The report's structural assertions align tightly with macroeconomic and industry data (SK Hynix 2028 shortage horizon, CFM record revenue, OpenAI 40% capacity lock, DDR5 price spikes).
Technical shifts toward PD separation, CXL memory pooling, and hardware co-design (Rubin CPX, Groq LPU, Ascend 950) are fully validated by recent engineering deployments.
2026 Three-Layer Market Differentiation: The market is not a uniform bull run:
Layer 1 (AI Storage): HBM, server DDR5, enterprise SSDs face severe shortages, price surges, and long-term capacity lock-ins.
Layer 2 (Mobile/Embedded): Forced to accept price hikes (Counterpoint notes Q1 2026 DRAM up 50% QoQ, NAND up 90% QoQ).
Layer 3 (PC Retail/Spot): Experiences reverse fluctuations with channel inventory dumping. Using a blanket term "memory" overlooks these distinct structural tiers.
3. Aggressive Perspective (Macroeconomic & Structural Risks)
The "LTA Perpetual Flywheel" Illusion: LTAs depend on downstream AI application profitability. If memory price increases contribute over 0.8% to US CPI, stoking persistent inflation and keeping interest rates high, a spike in long-term yields could trigger credit crunches in project financing for non-cloud operators. Prepayments could transform from profit reservoirs into catalysts for cascading credit defaults.
The Valuation Trap: Relying on peak-cycle margins (e.g., Micron's gross margins jumping from 36% to 75%) to justify 18x PE multiples mirrors classic cycle-peak blind spots.
Strategic Implications & Decision Framework
Strategic Redefinition: Semiconductor memory is no longer a traditional cyclical commodity; it has become the "primary physical bottleneck" equivalent to electricity and power in the Fourth Industrial Revolution.
Key Metric Pivot: Decision-makers must shift their core asset evaluation metrics from "GPU benchmark scores and single-card FLOPS (FLOPs)" to multi-server memory pooling efficiency (CXL penetration rate), end-to-end throughput latency (TPS) of Prefill-Decode separation, and arrival sign-off rates for large-capacity hierarchical caches (HBF).
Execution Guardrails: Organizations must internalize "computing-in-memory / full-link light-asset transition" as an organizational gene. Only by establishing verifiable hard-tech baselines across CXL penetration, TPS latency, and HBF fulfillment can companies navigate the dual traps of valuation corrections and LTA fragility during the 2026–2028 window.
