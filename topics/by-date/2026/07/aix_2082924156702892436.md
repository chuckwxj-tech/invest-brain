TAIWAN CLEANROOM AND AUTOMATION SPENDING WILL REMAIN ELEVATED, WHILE U.S. MANUFACTURING LOCALIZATION LAGS (READ-THROUGH 8)

Affected companies: United Integrated Services Co., Ltd. (2404: Taiwan), Marketech International Corp. (6196: Taiwan), Acter Group Corporation Limited (5536: Taiwan), L&K Engineering Co., Ltd. (6139: Taiwan) and Amkor Technology, Inc. (AMKR: United States).

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, high magnitude for Taiwan semiconductor-facility, cleanroom and automation suppliers; positive, moderate magnitude for Amkor’s relative U.S. localization positioning; negative, moderate magnitude for the speed of geographic diversification across the AI packaging supply chain.

ASE plans to spend approximately US$4.0bn on factories and facilities during 2026 and is simultaneously managing 13 greenfield and 8 brownfield projects. Management stated that these facilities should provide capacity through 2028 and potentially part of 2029. The company emphasized the need for construction partners that can deliver quickly, meet technical specifications and support multiple simultaneous projects.

The transmission mechanism for Taiwan engineering and facility suppliers is a multi-year increase in cleanroom construction, mechanical and electrical systems, utility infrastructure, factory automation, process integration and brownfield renovation. The spending is unusually visible because the constraint is not customer demand but the ability to complete facilities and bring equipment into service. Project delays directly limit ASE’s revenue conversion, increasing the strategic value of contractors that can meet schedule and quality requirements.

ASE also reiterated a Taiwan-first manufacturing strategy. The company operates in Fremont and San Jose and is expanding toward a 3rd and 4th U.S. facility, but those operations are primarily intended for chip-design support, test development, technology development and architectural collaboration. Manufacturing lines will initially be developed and optimized in Taiwan, with production transferred to the U.S. or other locations only after scale, automation, efficiency and know-how are established.

This creates a favorable geographic read-through for Taiwan facility and automation suppliers because the majority of near-term manufacturing investment remains concentrated within the domestic semiconductor cluster. No specific construction awards were disclosed, so the named companies represent exposed public beneficiaries rather than confirmed project recipients.

The same strategy creates a relative advantage for Amkor in U.S. localization. Customers requiring domestically located advanced-packaging manufacturing may place greater strategic value on OSAT capacity that is committed to the U.S. earlier in the deployment cycle. ASE remains highly competitive in technology, cost and scale, but its U.S. expansion does not yet represent near-term duplication of Taiwan manufacturing capacity.

The negative cross-portfolio implication is that supply-chain geographic concentration remains largely unresolved. ASE’s incremental AI packaging capacity will remain concentrated in Taiwan during the most important phase of the 2026-2027 ramp. This preserves cluster efficiency but leaves fabless customers, foundries and system companies exposed to common geographic, utility, logistics and geopolitical risks.

MEMORY ASP INFLATION IS REAL AND VISIBLE IN CUSTOMER BILLS OF MATERIALS (READ-THROUGH 9)

Affected companies: Micron Technology, Inc. (MU: United States), SK hynix Inc. (000660: South Korea) and Samsung Electronics Co., Ltd. (005930: South Korea).

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, moderate-to-high magnitude for near-term memory pricing and revenue; positive, moderate magnitude for earnings if higher prices are not offset by weaker unit demand or adverse mix.

ASE expects EMS revenue to increase approximately 40% q/q in Q3, but management explicitly stated that this is “largely because of the component price hikes, particularly in the memory sector.” Q4 EMS revenue is expected to remain at a similar elevated level, suggesting that the price effect is not expected to reverse immediately.

This is a direct confirmation from a large electronics manufacturer that memory cost inflation is sufficiently material to distort reported revenue. It supports the conclusion that higher memory pricing is being transmitted through customer bills of materials rather than existing only in spot-market indicators or supplier commentary.

The read-through is positive for Micron, SK hynix and Samsung because higher memory ASPs should increase revenue and operating leverage, particularly when industry supply remains disciplined. The call does not distinguish among DRAM, NAND or HBM, and the affected EMS products may contain multiple memory types. The signal should therefore be interpreted as confirmation of broad memory-component inflation rather than as precise evidence regarding a single product category.

The near-term catalyst is likely to be stronger reported memory revenue and improved gross margins, assuming contract prices follow the component inflation ASE is experiencing. The longer-duration effect is less certain because sustained price increases can raise the cost of servers, PCs, communications equipment and consumer devices, potentially affecting unit demand or causing customers to redesign memory configurations.

The call is more informative about price than volume. ASE stated that its EMS growth would have reflected typical seasonality absent the component-price increase. The 40% revenue increase should therefore not be used as evidence of a corresponding increase in memory unit consumption.

EMS AND ODM TOP-LINE GROWTH WILL OVERSTATE ECONOMIC GROWTH (READ-THROUGH 10)

Affected companies: Hon Hai Precision Industry Co., Ltd. (2317: Taiwan), Quanta Computer Inc. (2382: Taiwan), Wistron Corporation (3231: Taiwan), Jabil Inc. (JBL: United States), Flex Ltd. (FLEX: Singapore) and Celestica Inc. (CLS: Canada).

Directional impact and magnitude: Negative, low-to-moderate magnitude for reported margin optics, working-capital intensity and the valuation of revenue growth; broadly neutral for absolute operating profit where component costs are contractually passed through.

ASE’s Q3 EMS revenue is expected to rise approximately 40% q/q, yet management stated that normalized growth would resemble ordinary seasonality after excluding memory-component price increases. Reported EMS operating margin is expected to be 3.2%-3.4%, while management indicated that the margin would be approximately 3.7%-3.8% on a revenue base excluding the component-price inflation effect.

The transmission mechanism is mechanical. Higher component prices increase reported revenue and cost of goods sold with limited incremental gross profit. The revenue denominator expands, reported margin declines, and additional working capital may be required to fund more expensive inventories and receivables. Revenue growth can therefore accelerate sharply without an equivalent increase in economic value added.

The implication for EMS and ODM companies is not necessarily an absolute earnings downgrade. Many contracts provide pass-through treatment, and the assembler can preserve gross profit dollars. The negative read-through relates to revenue quality, margin optics and cash conversion. Companies with memory-heavy bills of materials may report stronger sales growth that reflects inflation rather than higher unit volumes, market share or manufacturing content.

Near-term trading analysis should place less weight on revenue beats and greater weight on gross profit dollars, operating profit, inventory, receivables, customer advances and operating cash flow. Reported margin compression caused by pass-through inflation should also be distinguished from genuine deterioration in pricing, productivity or product mix.

The warning is particularly relevant to AI-server and accelerator manufacturing, where high-value components can produce substantial revenue with relatively low incremental manufacturing margin. ASE’s own EMS computing growth is partly driven by AI accelerator products, but the Q3 revenue surge is principally a memory-price accounting effect rather than evidence of a 40% sequential increase in underlying AI system demand.

AI DEMAND IS BROADENING INTO POWER, INDUSTRIAL, CONNECTIVITY AND STORAGE SILICON (READ-THROUGH 11)

Affected companies: Monolithic Power Systems, Inc. (MPWR: United States), Infineon Technologies AG (IFX: Germany), onsemi (ON: United States), NXP Semiconductors N.V. (NXPI: Netherlands), STMicroelectronics N.V. (STM: Switzerland), Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: United States) and Marvell Technology, Inc. (MRVL: United States).

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, moderate-to-high magnitude for companies with direct AI infrastructure, power-delivery, high-speed connectivity and high-reliability exposure; positive, lower magnitude for broad industrial and automotive portfolios with less direct AI content.

ASE reported strong demand in industrial, power, connectivity and storage devices and stated that this demand is associated with AI infrastructure, electric vehicles and other high-impact, high-risk applications. Management believes ASE will outperform the broader general-semiconductor market because of its exposure to AI infrastructure and its automated manufacturing capabilities.

The exact revised general-segment growth target is inconsistent within the transcript, appearing once as 30% and later as approximately 20%, compared with prior guidance of 13%. The investment conclusion does not depend on choosing between 20% and 30%. Both represent a material upward revision and indicate that the AI hardware cycle is producing second-order semiconductor demand beyond accelerators and HBM.

The transmission mechanism is broad semiconductor-content expansion. AI servers require increasingly complex voltage regulation, power stages, power-management ICs, retimers, switches, optical and electrical connectivity, storage controllers, protection devices and thermal-management electronics. Data-center power density increases the value of efficient conversion and delivery. Larger clusters increase the number and speed of interconnects. Expanding training and inference workloads increase storage and data-movement requirements.

Monolithic Power Systems is directly exposed to high-performance power management. Broadcom and Marvell are exposed to connectivity, switching, custom silicon and storage-related functions. Infineon, onsemi, NXP and STMicroelectronics can benefit through power, industrial, automotive, connectivity and high-reliability devices, although the call should not be interpreted as evidence that every portion of their portfolios is accelerating.

The near-term catalyst is continued tightness in non-LEAP packaging and wire-bond capacity, which indicates that demand is already reaching the back end of the supply chain. The longer-duration shift is the movement from AI data centers into agentic systems, industrial automation, robotics and physical AI. Management emphasized that future hardware will incorporate more sensors, analog and mixed-signal components alongside digital compute.

A material caveat is required. ASE’s outperformance may reflect customer mix, market share and automated manufacturing capabilities as well as underlying end-market growth. The call supports positive demand for these semiconductor categories but does not establish a uniform cyclical recovery across all industrial, automotive or consumer products.

LARGER PACKAGES AND FULL-PROCESS OUTSOURCING SUPPORT HIGH-END SUBSTRATE AND MATERIAL CONTENT (READ-THROUGH 12)

Affected companies: Ibiden Co., Ltd. (4062: Japan), Unimicron Technology Corporation (3037: Taiwan), Kinsus Interconnect Technology Corp. (3189: Taiwan), Nan Ya Printed Circuit Board Corporation (8046: Taiwan) and Ajinomoto Co., Inc. (2802: Japan).

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, moderate magnitude for advanced substrates and packaging materials; positive, potentially high magnitude for suppliers qualified on the largest and most complex packages, subject to customer allocation and yield.

ASE stated that AI computational intensity is driving larger reticle-equivalent package sizes, greater logic-to-memory bandwidth and increasingly complex chiplet integration. The company expects more complicated CoWoS-type structures and panel solutions to accommodate larger packages. It is also expanding full-process capacity and entering additional process steps and new package types.

The transmission mechanism is higher substrate area, more routing layers, tighter line and space requirements, more demanding warpage control, higher-performance dielectric materials and greater yield sensitivity. Larger packages and more chiplets increase both the physical amount of substrate and the value of substrate performance. ABF and related high-end material consumption should increase as package complexity rises.

Full-process outsourcing also changes procurement dynamics. As ASE performs a larger portion of the packaging flow, more substrate and material purchasing decisions may be managed or influenced at the OSAT level rather than solely by the foundry or fabless customer. This can expand the importance of suppliers qualified directly within ASE’s advanced-packaging ecosystem.

The read-through is strongest for substrate and material companies with proven capability in large-body, high-layer-count, low-warpage and high-density applications. It is weaker for commodity substrate suppliers because package growth is concentrated in the highest-complexity products.

The principal risk is architectural substitution. CoWoS, EMIB, organic substrates, silicon bridges and panel-level approaches do not use identical material stacks. A larger total market does not guarantee equal growth for every supplier. ASE did not identify substrate vendors or disclose package-specific sourcing, so the category-level conclusion is more reliable than individual-company revenue attribution.

CPO AND PANEL-LEVEL PACKAGING ARE STRUCTURAL, BUT NEAR-TERM MONETIZATION—ESPECIALLY GLASS—REMAINS PREMATURE (READ-THROUGH 13)

Affected companies: Coherent Corp. (COHR: United States), Lumentum Holdings Inc. (LITE: United States), Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: United States), Marvell Technology, Inc. (MRVL: United States), SKC Ltd. (011790: South Korea), Corning Incorporated (GLW: United States) and AGC Inc. (5201: Japan).

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, moderate-to-high magnitude for optical-component and photonics-integration demand over the longer term; neutral-to-positive, low magnitude for near-term earnings; negative, low-to-moderate magnitude for aggressive near-term glass-substrate revenue expectations.

ASE stated that the integration of optical and electrical signals is “definitive,” with initial CPO launches potentially beginning toward the end of 2026 at low volume. Management expects important data on bandwidth, thermal performance, system performance, cost and yield after those initial deployments. More detailed disclosure regarding ASE’s CPO services, revenue and 2027 outlook was deferred for approximately 2 quarters.

The structural transmission mechanism is positive for optical suppliers. Moving optical connectivity closer to the compute package increases the need for lasers, photonic devices, optical engines, high-speed DSPs, precision packaging, thermal management and optical-electrical test. Coherent and Lumentum are exposed through optical and photonic components, while Broadcom and Marvell are exposed through high-speed connectivity silicon, DSPs, switching and CPO architectures.

Near-term earnings expectations should remain restrained. ASE characterized initial launch volumes as small and declined to quantify revenue. Industry adoption still depends on yield, reliability, thermal performance, system economics and the ability to manufacture optical and electrical components together at scale. The call validates technological direction, not an immediate volume inflection.

Panel-level packaging is more immediate. ASE’s fully automated 310 x 310 line is expected to begin production in Q1 2027. Management described it as complementary to foundry solutions and applicable to a similar customer set, package-size range, pitch and line-width requirements. The line supports additional packaging-equipment, inspection and materials demand and provides another route to larger package formats.

The glass-substrate read-through is less constructive in the near term. ASE stated that it does not expect glass substrates to enter production within the next 12 months. R&D is continuing with substrate suppliers, foundries and customers, but feasibility and economics remain under evaluation. This tempers aggressive near-term revenue expectations for companies positioned around glass-core substrate commercialization, including SKC, Corning and AGC.

The negative read-through should not be extrapolated into a rejection of glass substrates as a technology. It indicates that ASE’s commercial adoption is not imminent and that panel-level production can begin using other material systems. Glass-related investment cases should therefore be underwritten on customer-specific qualification milestones rather than broad thematic expectations.
