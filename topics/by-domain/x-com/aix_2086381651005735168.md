## 原文

Everyone prices "one fine-tune per customer" as a GPU problem. A new paper from @UCSanDiego, @SamsungDSGlobal, and @YukeWang1 (@RiceUniversity) shows the workload disagrees.

The inversion: 1,000 LoRA adapters on a 13B model at 4K context need over 1.3 TB. Adapter and KV-cache ops take 97.8% of the memory but only 3% of the compute. A GPU supplies the opposite ratio. Buying capacity in GPUs buys idle silicon.

PLoRA moves adapters and KV cache into pooled CXL (Compute Express Link) memory. Small near-data processing (NDP) cores, 2 TFLOPS each, sit beside the DRAM and return only reduced results. The exchange rate favors the cores: attention shrinks its input by at least 4096x, so shipping results costs thousands of times less than shipping operands. A cost model picks among four execution strategies per adapter, per batch.

On one H100 serving 1,000 adapters: 6.6x lower decode latency (avg) than S-LoRA, 2.23x the throughput of 4-GPU S-LoRA, 6.4x the throughput per dollar, under 3.4% added device area.

The buried lede: throughput saturates by 32-64 GB/s of link bandwidth. Jumping to NVLink 6's 1,800 GB/s adds at most +0.4%. Compute beside the data retires the link as a bottleneck.

The technology behind the technology: ship compute to the bytes, not bytes to the compute.
