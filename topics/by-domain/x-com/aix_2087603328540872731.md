## 原文

Intel $INTC may be quietly preparing for a major return to memory — and this could become a much bigger story.

CEO Lip-Bu Tan revealed that Intel is studying new memory architectures and has brought in Shil Lee, a former SK Hynix executive, to help with the project. He suggested Intel is exploring ways to combine CPU and memory through advanced stacking and new architectures, but said the company isn’t ready to disclose the details yet.

What makes this interesting is the timing. AI is creating a massive bottleneck around memory, bandwidth, power and data movement, not just compute. Tan believes memory has historically been treated too much like a commodity, but new technologies could turn it into a much more strategic part of the AI stack.

Intel is also pushing deeper into vertical integration — combining CPU/GPU design, advanced packaging and manufacturing. The company is working on technologies including EMIB, glass substrates, advanced cooling and potentially synthetic diamond materials.

Tan’s broader strategy is basically: don’t miss the next wave. Intel missed mobile, cloud and the first major AI wave. He wants Intel positioned across the next generation of agentic AI, edge AI, physical AI, data centers, optical interconnects, advanced packaging and memory.

His investment history is also interesting. Before becoming Intel CEO, Tan invested early in companies such as $CRDO Credo and SambaNova, as well as photonics companies including Celestial AI and Dust Photonics. His focus has consistently been on solving the next infrastructure bottleneck.

And now memory could be the next one.

The timing is especially interesting because $MU Micron, $SNDK SanDisk and $SKHY SK Hynix are already benefiting from extremely tight AI-driven memory demand. HBM and enterprise storage demand are growing rapidly, while new capacity takes years to come online.

So if Intel actually develops a competitive new memory architecture or CPU-memory integration technology, this wouldn’t just be Intel entering another commodity market. It could potentially become another piece of the AI infrastructure stack.

My takeaway: The biggest part of this story isn’t Intel suddenly becoming a traditional memory company. It’s the possibility that Intel is trying to reinvent the relationship between compute + memory + packaging + manufacturing.

That is much more interesting. 👀

AI isn’t only creating demand for more GPUs — it’s creating a huge need for more memory, bandwidth, optical connectivity and advanced packaging. Intel appears to be positioning itself across several of those bottlenecks at once.
