Breaking the Memory Wall: The Impact of Near-Data Processing, GPU, CPU, Storage, and High-Bandwidth Flash on AI Strategy and Policy 

Rationale for Specialization in AI Software by China and South Korea and Outsourcing of Hardware

Why China and South Korea should not manufacture rather Import AI Infrastructure from America 

The AI industry is at a critical point. As hardware logic becomes commoditized, inference economics are shaped more by memflation and the Memory Wall.

Building advanced fabrication and memory infrastructure is a zero-sum challenge. Technologies like High-Bandwidth Flash (HBF) use stacked 3D NAND to achieve ten times the capacity and bandwidth similar to High Bandwidth Memory (HBM). They require significant ongoing capital investment and face major packaging yield challenges.

Instead of investing heavily in duplicating semiconductor fab capacity, regional technology leaders such as China and South Korea could reduce their exposure to fab bottlenecks and supply chain shortages by importing standardized silicon components from U.S. suppliers. These could include GPUs, CPUs, HBM, storage, photonic devices, HBF, and CXL interconnects.

Domestic investment could then be directed toward areas where differentiation increasingly occurs at the system and software levels: abstraction frameworks, dynamic prompt management, key-value caching, and application development. HBF research is a useful example. Flash latency and page-based access cannot be addressed simply by producing better chips, more HBM, or additional storage capacity. They also require changes in how software and hardware are designed to work together.

Being a master at AI software has a different scaling profile from physical infrastructure and can be deployed across markets without reproducing fab capacity in every region. A greater reliance on established U.S. hardware suppliers could therefore leave more domestic capital available for algorithm development, on time release of the AI software, agentic user-interface platform, systems engineering, and TCO optimization.
