$COHR KEY READ-THROUGHS FROM COHERENT CORP. Q4 FY2026 EARNINGS CALL

Coherent’s Q4 FY2026 earnings call provides one of the strongest supplier-level validations to date that AI infrastructure investment is broadening from accelerator procurement into a sustained, multi-layer optical networking buildout. The most important signal is not simply the 66% year-over-year growth in data center revenue or 56% growth in communications revenue; it is the combination of record bookings, FY2027 being described as essentially fully booked, purchase orders extending into calendar 2028, long-term agreements extending through the end of the decade, and management’s expectation that quarterly revenue will exceed $3B by the end of FY2027. The call supports positive read-throughs for AI switching silicon, high-speed optical DSPs, Ethernet networking, data center interconnect, compound-semiconductor manufacturing equipment, and advanced cooling. The principal negative read-throughs are concentrated in merchant laser suppliers threatened by vertical integration, pluggable-transceiver vendors exposed to a longer-term migration toward CPO and NPO, Chinese optical vendors facing supply-chain de-risking, incumbent optical networking OEMs facing a vertically integrated new systems competitor, and optical manufacturers whose earnings growth requires materially greater capital intensity than the asset-light semiconductor companies serving the same AI cycle. Where Coherent did not identify a specific supplier, customer, or competitor, the company implications below represent industry-level inferences rather than confirmed contractual relationships.

AI ACCELERATORS, SWITCHING SILICON, AND ETHERNET NETWORKING

MULTI-YEAR AI NETWORKING DEMAND IS MORE CONTRACTED AND DURABLE THAN THE MARKET’S NEAR-TERM CAPEX DEBATE IMPLIES (READ-THROUGH 1)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: US), Marvell Technology, Inc. (MRVL: US), Arista Networks, Inc. (ANET: US), NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA: US).

DIRECTION AND MAGNITUDE: Positive, high magnitude for Broadcom, Marvell, and Arista; positive, moderate magnitude for NVIDIA because optical networking is critical to system scaling but smaller than NVIDIA’s accelerator revenue base.

TIME HORIZON: Immediate estimate-revision catalyst through FY2027 and a multi-year fundamental positive extending into calendar 2028 and potentially through the end of the decade.

CALL SUPPORT: Coherent reported data center revenue growth of 66% year over year and communications revenue growth of 56%. Management stated that “fiscal ’27 is basically completely booked out,” that purchase orders extend into calendar 2028, and that long-term agreements extend through the end of the decade. Those agreements generally contain increasing annual supply commitments, predetermined pricing, and minimum demand guarantees that management characterized as take-or-pay provisions. Management also stated, “We continue to see no signs of attenuation in customer demand,” and expects quarterly revenue to exceed $3B by the end of FY2027.

ANALYSIS: Coherent’s order visibility is a high-quality derivative indicator of underlying AI accelerator and networking deployment plans. Customers generally do not commit to multi-year volumes of high-speed transceivers, lasers, optical circuit switches, coherent modules, and fiber assemblies without corresponding visibility into accelerator clusters, switching platforms, and data center construction schedules. The existence of product-specific purchase orders into calendar 2028 is more meaningful than a generalized total-addressable-market forecast because it indicates that customers are already reserving physical capacity against defined platforms and deployment windows.

The strongest transmission mechanism is through networking content per AI accelerator. Larger clusters require more switch ports, more optical links, more network tiers, greater east-west bandwidth, and increasingly more inter-data-center connectivity. Coherent’s evidence that demand is simultaneously accelerating in data center transceivers, optical circuit switching, ZR and ZR+ products, pump lasers, coherent subsystems, and multi-rail systems suggests that the AI networking opportunity is broadening rather than remaining concentrated in a single front-panel transceiver category.

Broadcom benefits through Ethernet switch silicon, networking ASICs, DSPs, custom accelerators, and integrated optical platforms. Marvell benefits through high-speed optical DSPs, coherent DSPs, custom silicon, and data-center interconnect exposure. Arista benefits through continued demand for high-radix Ethernet switching and routing as accelerator clusters expand. NVIDIA benefits because increased optical availability reduces the risk that network bandwidth, power efficiency, or link density constrains the deployment of its accelerated-computing systems.

The near-term trading catalyst is Coherent’s expectation that current-quarter data center growth will exceed 80% year over year, supported by an approximately 80% increase in indium-phosphide laser production in the June quarter. Confirmation of this growth would reinforce upward estimate revisions across AI networking suppliers. The longer-duration implication is that networking expenditure should continue growing faster than accelerator unit growth as cluster scale, topology complexity, and optical intensity increase.

The principal caveat is the possibility that customers are reserving more capacity than their ultimate consumption requirements because optical supply is constrained. However, the combination of specific purchase orders, predetermined pricing, increasing annual volumes, and minimum purchase commitments reduces the probability that the entire order expansion is attributable to duplicative bookings.

THE 800G-TO-1.6T TRANSITION IS ADDITIVE RATHER THAN IMMEDIATELY CANNIBALISTIC, CREATING A DUAL PRODUCT CYCLE (READ-THROUGH 2)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: Marvell Technology, Inc. (MRVL: US), Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: US), Arista Networks, Inc. (ANET: US), Fabrinet (FN: US), Lumentum Holdings Inc. (LITE: US).

DIRECTION AND MAGNITUDE: Positive, high magnitude for Marvell and Broadcom; positive, moderate-to-high magnitude for Fabrinet and Arista; positive, moderate magnitude for Lumentum on end-market demand, partially offset by Coherent’s internal laser expansion.

TIME HORIZON: Near-term trading catalyst across calendar 2026 and 2027, with a longer-duration positive implication for the pace of high-speed networking upgrades.

CALL SUPPORT: Coherent expects 800G revenue to continue growing year over year during calendar 2026 while 1.6T ramps rapidly through the remainder of calendar 2026 and into calendar 2027. Management said the 1.6T ramp is “even faster than what we thought, say, three months ago.” June-quarter indium-phosphide laser production increased approximately 80% year over year, and those lasers are being incorporated into current-quarter 800G and 1.6T transceiver shipments.

ANALYSIS: The key read-through is that 1.6T is not simply replacing 800G on a 1-for-1 basis. Both generations are growing concurrently because customers are deploying different switch speeds, cluster sizes, network tiers, fiber reaches, and qualification schedules. The coexistence of both product cycles materially expands the near-term market because 800G remains a growth product while 1.6T adds a new layer of demand.

For Marvell and Broadcom, the transmission mechanism is higher demand for optical DSPs, serializer-deserializer technology, physical-layer silicon, retimers, drivers, and switch ASICs supporting 200G-per-lane architectures. A faster 1.6T ramp also accelerates the deployment of higher-capacity switch platforms and increases the value of low-power signal-processing solutions. Marvell has particularly high earnings sensitivity because optical and coherent DSP revenue can scale rapidly when module volumes inflect.

Arista benefits because the move to 1.6T optics generally accompanies adoption of higher-bandwidth switching platforms and denser AI network architectures. Fabrinet benefits because concurrent 800G and 1.6T growth increases demand for complex optical module manufacturing, precision assembly, testing, and high-volume production services. Lumentum benefits from stronger demand for lasers and optical components across the industry, although the benefit is diluted by vertically integrated customers such as Coherent increasing their own component production.

The near-term catalyst is evidence from optical DSP vendors, contract manufacturers, and networking OEMs that 1.6T shipments are accelerating without a corresponding decline in 800G. The longer-duration read-through is that product transitions in AI networking may remain overlapping rather than sequential. That structure supports a larger aggregate market and reduces the risk that the next speed transition merely shifts revenue between generations.

The most important negative risk is that early 1.6T volumes can carry lower yields, launch costs, and expedited logistics. Strong unit demand does not guarantee immediate margin accretion for every supplier. Companies with mature 200G-per-lane silicon and qualified module platforms should therefore outperform suppliers whose participation remains concentrated in older speeds.

INTEGRATED OPTICS, CPO, AND NPO

THE CALL DIRECTLY REBUTS CPO DELAY CONCERNS AND DE-RISKS NVIDIA’S OPTICAL NETWORKING ROADMAP (READ-THROUGH 3)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA: US), Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: US), Marvell Technology, Inc. (MRVL: US), Coherent Corp. (COHR: US).

DIRECTION AND MAGNITUDE: Positive, high magnitude for Coherent; positive, moderate magnitude for NVIDIA, Broadcom, and Marvell.

TIME HORIZON: Significant near-term trading catalyst around Coherent’s September PhotonLink launch and December-quarter shipments; major long-duration fundamental shift beginning in scale-out networking and later extending to scale-up architectures.

CALL SUPPORT: Management stated, “We’ve seen absolutely no push-out of CPO demand. In fact, it’s been the opposite.” Customer demand has increased and, in some cases, been pulled forward. Coherent has already started production wafers for December-quarter shipments of ultra-high-power continuous-wave lasers. Management said the relevant customer continues to ask Coherent to “please ship more as fast as possible.” Coherent also explicitly stated that its Texas facility is ramping ultra-high-power continuous-wave lasers for CPO solutions, including products covered by its NVIDIA partnership.

ANALYSIS: The read-through materially reduces the probability that CPO is experiencing a broad customer-driven schedule delay. Coherent has moved beyond sampling and engineering engagement into production wafer starts, which indicates that at least part of the CPO roadmap has entered a manufacturing execution phase. Production wafers do not eliminate qualification, yield, reliability, packaging, or customer-acceptance risk, but they represent a materially stronger signal than a design announcement or laboratory demonstration.

NVIDIA is the most important direct read-through because Coherent specifically referenced a partnership covering CPO-related continuous-wave lasers. Reliable availability of high-power laser sources is necessary to support co-packaged optical links at scale. Successful execution would help NVIDIA address bandwidth density, electrical reach, front-panel congestion, and power-efficiency constraints in future scale-out networking platforms. The call did not explicitly identify NVIDIA as the customer behind the “please ship more” statement, so that remark should not be treated as a confirmed NVIDIA quotation. The direct partnership disclosure nevertheless makes NVIDIA a clear strategic beneficiary.

Broadcom benefits because the call validates the broader CPO architecture and customer willingness to move optics closer to switching silicon. Broadcom is positioned across high-speed switching, digital signal processing, custom silicon, and co-packaged optical solutions. Marvell benefits through optical DSPs, interconnect silicon, and custom platform opportunities as customers experiment with multiple CPO and NPO implementations.

The near-term catalyst is Coherent’s PhotonLink launch on 09/21/26 and the commencement of fiscal Q2 revenue. Shipment volume, qualification status, yield, and repeat orders will matter more than nominal first revenue. The longer-duration catalyst is the conversion of electrical links to optical links inside increasingly dense AI systems.

A critical timing distinction remains. The CPO revenue beginning in Coherent’s December quarter is associated primarily with scale-out networking. Management continues to expect scale-up CPO revenue only in H2 calendar 2027. The call therefore supports a positive near-term CPO conclusion without implying that broad optical scale-up deployments are arriving immediately.

NPO REDUCES THE BINARY RISK AROUND CPO TIMING BUT CREATES A LONGER-TERM CONTENT MIGRATION AWAY FROM PLUGGABLE MODULES (READ-THROUGH 4)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: Fabrinet (FN: US), Innolight Technology Co., Ltd. (300308: China), Eoptolink Technology Inc., Ltd. (300502: China), Applied Optoelectronics, Inc. (AAOI: US), Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: US), Marvell Technology, Inc. (MRVL: US).

DIRECTION AND MAGNITUDE: Near-term positive, high magnitude for pluggable-module manufacturers because 800G and 1.6T remain strong; longer-duration negative, moderate-to-high magnitude for companies whose manufacturing content remains concentrated in front-panel pluggables. Positive, moderate-to-high magnitude for integrated-optics platform and silicon suppliers.

TIME HORIZON: Near-term positive through calendar 2027; potentially disruptive architecture shift over the following several years.

CALL SUPPORT: Management said that almost every large strategic customer is engaged in a CPO project, an NPO project, or both. Coherent views CPO and NPO as different physical attachment points with “comparable levels of content” for the company. Some customers are expected to use NPO as an intermediate step before migrating to CPO, while others may move directly to CPO. Management also expects 200G VCSEL technology to participate in selected NPO and integrated-optics applications.

ANALYSIS: NPO makes the optical integration transition less dependent on immediate broad adoption of CPO. Customers that are not prepared to place optics directly adjacent to the switch or accelerator package can move optical engines onto the motherboard, preserving many of the bandwidth and power advantages while reducing packaging, serviceability, and thermal complexity. This creates a practical adoption bridge and raises confidence that optical content will move closer to compute even if CPO schedules vary by customer.

The near-term read-through for Fabrinet, Innolight, Eoptolink, and Applied Optoelectronics remains positive because conventional 800G and 1.6T pluggables are still growing. Coherent explicitly expects 800G to grow year over year while 1.6T ramps rapidly. There is no evidence in the call of an imminent collapse in front-panel module demand.

The longer-duration read-through is less favorable. As optics move from front panels to motherboards or packages, value migrates away from the traditional pluggable enclosure toward optical engines, external laser modules, fiber-attach assemblies, polarization-maintaining fiber, isolators, connectors, packaging, and co-design with switching or accelerator silicon. Module manufacturers that do not secure integrated-optics assembly capabilities, advanced packaging relationships, or proprietary component content risk losing revenue per link.

Fabrinet could adapt by becoming a manufacturing partner for optical engines, CPO assemblies, and complex fiber-attach solutions. Its long-term outcome is therefore not predetermined. The risk is that the required manufacturing process, customer relationships, and margin structure differ materially from traditional pluggable transceiver production. Chinese transceiver manufacturers face the additional challenge of potential U.S. sourcing restrictions and trusted-supply requirements.

Broadcom and Marvell are positive beneficiaries because NPO and CPO both increase the importance of integrating optical and electrical architectures. The architecture shift should favor companies capable of supplying switching silicon, DSPs, drivers, custom ASICs, and photonic integration as a coordinated platform rather than as isolated components.

SIX-INCH INDIUM PHOSPHIDE IS A SUPPLY-SIDE INFLECTION FOR EQUIPMENT AND SUBSTRATES BUT A SHARE-LOSS RISK FOR MERCHANT LASER SUPPLIERS (READ-THROUGH 5)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: AIXTRON SE (AIXA: Germany), Veeco Instruments Inc. (VECO: US), AXT, Inc. (AXTI: US), Lumentum Holdings Inc. (LITE: US).

DIRECTION AND MAGNITUDE: Positive, potentially high magnitude for niche compound-semiconductor equipment and substrate suppliers; negative, moderate magnitude for merchant laser suppliers exposed to customers that increase internal production. The net read-through for Lumentum is mixed because strong industry demand partially offsets the vertical-integration risk.

TIME HORIZON: Near-term equipment and substrate demand through calendar 2027; structural cost and competitive implications extending well beyond 2027.

CALL SUPPORT: Coherent remains on track to double internal indium-phosphide output year over year by the end of the current quarter, 1 quarter ahead of plan, and expects to more than double output again by the end of calendar 2027. Additional capacity beyond 2027 is being planned. Texas and Sweden are ramping 6-inch production, and Zurich is expected to begin production in H1 calendar 2027. Management stated that 6-inch yields exceed 3-inch yields across EMLs, continuous-wave lasers, and photodiodes. The CFO stated that the 6-inch platform provides approximately 4x the output at approximately 50% of the cost of 3-inch production.

ANALYSIS: Coherent is establishing 6-inch indium phosphide as a scale-manufacturing platform rather than a limited technology demonstration. The combination of higher output per wafer, lower unit cost, better yields, and multiple production sites materially changes the cost curve for high-speed laser and detector components. The capacity expansion supports 800G, 1.6T, CPO, NPO, and other integrated-optics products simultaneously.

AIXTRON and Veeco are potential beneficiaries because expanded indium-phosphide wafer production requires additional compound-semiconductor epitaxy and processing capacity. AXT is a potential beneficiary from higher demand for larger-diameter indium-phosphide substrates. Coherent stated that it has secured substrates and other critical inputs for the announced expansion, which confirms that material procurement is already an important part of the ramp. The call did not identify any equipment or substrate supplier, so these are industry-level demand read-throughs rather than confirmed Coherent contract awards.

The negative transmission mechanism for Lumentum and other merchant laser suppliers is substitution. Coherent currently uses a combination of internally produced and externally sourced lasers, but management expects a greater proportion of transceiver demand to be supplied internally over time. Internal transceiver demand is consuming all available Coherent laser output, and management does not expect to sell material laser volume externally in the near term. Every internally sourced EML or continuous-wave laser potentially removes an external purchase while allowing Coherent to retain component margin inside the finished transceiver.

The 6-inch cost advantage also creates a strategic pricing weapon. Coherent can use lower internal component costs to defend transceiver share, absorb temporary module pricing pressure, or invest more aggressively in customer-specific platforms. Less-integrated transceiver companies may face structurally weaker gross margins if they must continue purchasing critical lasers from third parties during periods of scarcity.

The near-term read-through for merchant laser demand is not uniformly negative because the overall market is expanding rapidly and Coherent remains supply constrained. The structural concern emerges as Coherent and other vertically integrated companies add enough internal capacity to reduce external procurement and potentially change industry pricing behavior.

OPTICAL TRANSPORT, DCI, AND SYSTEMS

SCALE-ACROSS AND DATA CENTER INTERCONNECT ARE BECOMING A SECOND AI NETWORKING GROWTH LEG (READ-THROUGH 6)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: Ciena Corporation (CIEN: US), Marvell Technology, Inc. (MRVL: US), Lumentum Holdings Inc. (LITE: US), Arista Networks, Inc. (ANET: US).

DIRECTION AND MAGNITUDE: Positive, high magnitude for Ciena and Marvell; positive, moderate magnitude for Lumentum and Arista.

TIME HORIZON: Near-term positive through FY2027, with a multi-year fundamental benefit as AI workloads and clusters become distributed across multiple data centers.

CALL SUPPORT: Coherent’s communications revenue increased 56% year over year in Q4. Management cited particular strength in data center interconnect, scale-across applications, ZR and ZR+ transceivers, pump lasers, amplifiers, line cards, and complex optical subsystems. Coherent expects another quarter of strong sequential communications growth. Multi-rail samples have been delivered to multiple customers, initial revenue is expected in H1 calendar 2027, and the company estimates a calendar 2030 market opportunity above $2B.

ANALYSIS: The AI networking investment cycle is expanding beyond connectivity inside a single data center. As training, inference, data preparation, storage, and model-serving workloads are distributed across campuses and geographic regions, network capacity between facilities becomes an increasingly important constraint. This creates a second growth vector in addition to intra-data-center Ethernet.

Ciena benefits through coherent optical transport systems, pluggable coherent optics, routing, and data-center interconnect platforms. The transmission mechanism is higher bandwidth demand across metro and regional links, more wavelengths, faster coherent interfaces, and increased deployment of open line systems. Marvell benefits through coherent DSPs, optical PAM technology, custom silicon, and data-center interconnect products. Lumentum benefits from pump lasers, modulators, optical components, and coherent transport content. Arista benefits as customers deploy routing and switching infrastructure to interconnect multiple AI clusters and data center locations.

Multi-rail is potentially important because it moves Coherent beyond individual components into a system-level scale-across architecture. Initial customer samples and expected H1 calendar 2027 revenue indicate that the product is approaching commercialization. If successful, multi-rail could broaden the market for coherent optics and increase demand for the surrounding routing, switching, amplification, and fiber infrastructure.

The near-term catalyst is continued sequential communications growth and confirmation of multi-rail revenue. The longer-duration shift is the evolution of AI infrastructure from isolated large clusters toward interconnected regional or global compute fabrics. This architecture increases the amount of coherent optical content required per unit of compute.

Ciena’s read-through is not unambiguously positive over the long term because Coherent is also moving into subsystems and complete systems. Near-term end-market growth should dominate, but the competitive overlap should be monitored as Coherent expands multi-rail and OCS.
