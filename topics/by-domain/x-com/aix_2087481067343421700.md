Samsung Electro-Mechanics' glass substrate customer evaluations are in trouble…investment plans delayed again

Samsung Electro-Mechanics' investment plans for semiconductor glass substrates are being delayed. Bottlenecks have arisen in customer prototype reliability assessments.

As a result, the construction timeline for the production line of its core glass joint venture, GlaSSEM, has also been postponed, and the timing of related equipment orders has become unclear.

According to multiple industry insiders on the 12th, GlaSSEM, the core glass joint venture between Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Dongwoo Fine Chemicals, has not yet provided its partners with a timeline for equipment orders. One industry insider stated, "Until the end of July, the atmosphere was still very strong that orders were about to be placed, but now it's difficult to predict when it will resume," adding, "The timeline has been delayed multiple times, and even internally, it's difficult to roughly estimate when orders will be placed."

Samsung Electro-Mechanics had already communicated equipment orders for the GlaSSEM production line to its partners since November of last year, but the timeline has been delayed at least three times: first in December of last year, then in March, and then in June of this year. After June, any further guidance on the timeline completely disappeared.

Industry insiders pointed out that this is because they have failed to obtain reliability assessment certifications for glass substrate prototypes from global communication chip customers. An insider stated, "I understand the investment plan stalled because Samsung Electro-Mechanics failed the reliability assessment," adding, "Further delays to the existing timeline are inevitable as the prototypes need to be remade and resubmitted." The person added, "Because new prototypes cannot be produced within one or two weeks, there is uncertainty as to whether the production line investment can be completed this year."

Samsung Electro-Mechanics is currently manufacturing glass substrate prototypes at its Sejong Campus. To transition from pilot production to initial mass production, it is preparing to build a production line at GlaSSEM, a joint venture with Toyu Fine Chemicals, a subsidiary of Sumitomo Chemicals. The selection of key process equipment suppliers, including deposition, etching, laser drilling, and inspection, has been largely completed.

Last month, equipment manufacturers had high expectations for orders to begin in August. Samsung Electro-Mechanics originally planned to start with the deposition equipment, which has the longest lead time, transporting the main process equipment to Toyu Fine Chemicals' Pyeongtaek Campus and installing it by April next year, completing the initial production line in the second quarter. It is understood that it also considered placing orders for long-lead-time equipment under Samsung Electro-Mechanics' name first, and then transferring the contracts to GlaSSEM.

Delays to Samsung Electro-Mechanics' glass substrate mass production timeline are also unavoidable. Even if orders are placed this year, considering equipment manufacturing time, the production line won't be ready until the second half of next year at the earliest. Mass production start-up is believed to have been postponed to 2028 or later.

When GlaSSEM was established at the beginning of last month, Samsung Electro-Mechanics set a target of starting operations in the second half of 2027. Less than a month later, at its second-quarter earnings conference, it revised its operational start-up date to 2028.
