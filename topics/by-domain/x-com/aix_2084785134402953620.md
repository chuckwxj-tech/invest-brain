$ALAB KEY READ-THROUGHS FROM ASTERA LABS Q2 2026 EARNINGS CALL

Astera Labs’ Q2 2026 earnings call provides strong evidence that AI infrastructure spending is broadening beyond accelerator silicon into high-radix scale-up switching, signal conditioning, active electrical connectivity, memory expansion, fabric software and optical interconnect. Revenue reached $392.4 million, increasing 27% sequentially and 104% year-over-year, while PCIe 6 products increased from approximately 33% of revenue in Q1 to more than 50% in Q2. The Q3 revenue outlook of $540 million to $560 million implies approximately 40% sequential growth at the midpoint, principally driven by Scorpio X-Series moving into high-volume production and becoming Astera’s largest product family 1 quarter earlier than previously expected. The strongest positive cross-market implication is that merchant AI fabric content per accelerator is rising far faster than accelerator unit volumes, materially expanding the semiconductor and interconnect wallet surrounding each XPU. The strongest negative implication is that Astera’s revenue inflection should not be interpreted as a comparable acceleration in industrywide GPU units because a substantial portion of the growth reflects a step-change in content per system and a concentrated lead-customer production ramp. The draft transcript contains several obvious transcription errors; unambiguous references to Astera’s Aries, Taurus and Leo product families have been normalized, while the anomalous reference to a 7%-8% long-term gross-margin target has been excluded. No unnamed hyperscaler, Tier-1 AI platform provider, neocloud customer or component supplier has been identified beyond what management explicitly disclosed.

AI ACCELERATORS AND SCALE-UP FABRICS

OPEN MERCHANT SCALE-UP FABRICS ARE BECOMING A CREDIBLE ALTERNATIVE TO PROPRIETARY ACCELERATOR FABRICS (READ-THROUGH 1)

AFFECTED COMPANIES AND IMPACT: Advanced Micro Devices (AMD: US) — positive, high magnitude over the longer term. Broadcom (AVGO: US) — positive, medium magnitude for custom accelerator programs, partially offset by competitive risk in merchant switching. Marvell Technology (MRVL: US) — positive, medium magnitude for the custom accelerator ecosystem, but negative, medium magnitude for connectivity share. NVIDIA (NVDA: US) — negative, medium magnitude to the long-term durability of proprietary scale-up fabric lock-in, with limited near-term financial impact.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Scorpio X-Series entered volume production in Q2 and is expected to become Astera’s largest product family in Q3, 1 quarter ahead of prior expectations. Management disclosed more than 10 Scorpio X customer engagements, with multiple customers already receiving pre-production units and additional customers expected to ramp toward the end of 2026 or early 2027. Astera plans to deliver UALink-enabled Scorpio switches in 2027 and described UALink as benefiting from an increasingly active ecosystem of IP providers, tools and vendors. Management also stated that Scorpio’s hardware-accelerated Hypercast and in-network compute capabilities can provide “almost 2x improvement” in collective operations and that XPUs are beginning to communicate directly with the COSMOS software platform to orchestrate workloads.

TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: The principal constraint on non-NVIDIA accelerator systems has not been accelerator silicon alone; it has also been the absence of a mature, low-latency, high-bandwidth scale-up fabric and tightly integrated orchestration software comparable to NVIDIA’s NVLink, NVSwitch and software stack. A merchant switch capable of supporting high-radix topologies, hardware multicast, in-network compute, advanced reliability features and direct XPU orchestration lowers the system-level barrier for AMD accelerators and hyperscaler-designed XPUs. The availability of a broader merchant ecosystem also reduces the architectural and execution risk associated with deploying custom accelerators at scale.

The read-through is therefore structurally positive for AMD and for hyperscaler custom accelerator programs supported by Broadcom and Marvell. It increases the probability that non-NVIDIA accelerators can operate in larger, more efficient scale-up domains rather than being confined to smaller systems or less efficient Ethernet-based scale-out architectures. For NVIDIA, the implication is not an immediate loss of accelerator demand, but a gradual weakening of the proprietary fabric advantage that reinforces ecosystem lock-in and supports premium system pricing.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: UALink-related accelerator announcements, switch design wins and additional Scorpio production customers could support AMD, Broadcom and Marvell sentiment before material UALink revenue begins. No significant negative NVIDIA revenue read-through is supported for 2026 because UALink systems are targeted for 2027 and Astera is also participating in NVLink Fusion-related custom designs.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: A functioning merchant scale-up ecosystem would structurally broaden the addressable market for alternative accelerators and reduce the degree to which accelerator competition is determined by proprietary interconnect ownership. This is a 2027-and-beyond competitive shift rather than a near-term displacement event.

SOFTWARE AND IN-NETWORK COMPUTE ARE BECOMING REQUIRED DIFFERENTIATORS IN AI FABRIC SILICON (READ-THROUGH 2)

AFFECTED COMPANIES AND IMPACT: Broadcom (AVGO: US) — positive, medium magnitude given its ability to integrate switching silicon, custom accelerators and software. Marvell Technology (MRVL: US) — positive, medium magnitude from growing demand for intelligent networking and custom connectivity, offset by greater competition from Astera. Credo Technology Group (CRDO: US) — negative, medium magnitude over the longer term as value migrates from passive and signal-conditioning components toward software-controlled switches. NVIDIA (NVDA: US) — mixed; positive validation of its integrated fabric strategy, but negative because merchant suppliers are reproducing some historically proprietary capabilities.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Management stated that Scorpio’s differentiation is increasingly derived from Hypercast, in-network compute and COSMOS rather than lane count alone. COSMOS was previously used primarily for telemetry, diagnostics, optimization and firmware management. Management stated that it is now “becoming part of the orchestration layer” and that “the XPUs are talking directly” to COSMOS to run workloads. Management further argued that this creates “a lot more stickiness” because customers adopting the current generation are more likely to remain on Astera’s platform in subsequent generations.

TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: The strategic value of AI interconnect is moving beyond physical link transport. Once the switch participates directly in collective operations, traffic shaping, workload orchestration, GPU utilization and latency optimization, the fabric becomes part of the compute architecture rather than a replaceable peripheral. This increases switching costs, extends design-win duration and permits switch vendors to capture greater economics than suppliers of passive cables, standalone retimers or less differentiated connectivity components.

Broadcom and Marvell benefit from the broader shift because both can compete at multiple layers of the networking and custom-silicon stack. Credo benefits from higher link counts and electrical complexity, but its relative position is less favorable if the highest-value functions migrate into intelligent switches and software orchestration rather than remaining in AECs and signal-conditioning devices. NVIDIA’s existing strategy is validated because fabric software and accelerator integration are clearly important. The negative implication is that merchant vendors are beginning to offer increasingly sophisticated alternatives, reducing the uniqueness of NVIDIA’s integrated approach over time.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Additional disclosures around collective-operation performance, workload-level benchmarks and customer adoption could materially affect relative valuations across AI networking companies. The claimed 2x improvement is currently a management performance claim rather than a broadly quantified revenue metric.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: AI networking silicon with integrated software should command greater value and customer retention than hardware-only connectivity. The market is likely to stratify between vendors capable of participating in workload orchestration and vendors confined to physical-layer transport.

ASTERA’S Q3 REVENUE STEP-UP IS A CONNECTIVITY-CONTENT INFLECTION, NOT A CLEAN XPU UNIT-DEMAND SIGNAL (READ-THROUGH 3)

AFFECTED COMPANIES AND IMPACT: Credo Technology Group (CRDO: US) — positive, medium-to-high magnitude for connectivity content growth. Broadcom (AVGO: US) — positive, medium magnitude for networking and custom accelerator content. Marvell Technology (MRVL: US) — positive, medium magnitude for connectivity, custom silicon and optical content. NVIDIA (NVDA: US) and Advanced Micro Devices (AMD: US) — only modestly positive, low magnitude as a near-term accelerator-unit read-through.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Astera guided Q3 revenue to $540 million to $560 million, implying approximately 40% sequential growth at the midpoint. Management attributed the increase principally to Scorpio X-Series transitioning to volume production, continued PCIe 6 retimer strength and early Taurus 800G contributions. Management contrasted the original Astera content opportunity of approximately $50 to $100 per accelerator with a current portfolio opportunity of “multiple thousands of dollars per XPU.” Scorpio X-Series alone was described as offering more than $1,000 of content per XPU in high-radix configurations.

TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: Astera can materially outgrow accelerator shipments because the amount of Astera content installed around each accelerator is increasing. A system that previously required a limited number of retimers can now include high-radix switches, additional signal-conditioning devices, active electrical cables, memory controllers, software-enabled fabric functions and eventually optical engines. The transition from a lower-value retimer socket to a switch carrying more than $1,000 of content per XPU can create a substantial supplier revenue increase without a corresponding increase in accelerator units.

The appropriate broader read-through is therefore more positive for connectivity and networking silicon than for GPU volumes. Astera’s Q3 guidance validates a rising connectivity bill of materials and accelerating fabric complexity. It does not support a proportional upward revision to NVIDIA, AMD or custom accelerator unit estimates. Using Astera’s 40% sequential growth rate as a direct proxy for AI accelerator shipments would materially overstate the underlying unit signal.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: The earnings call supports upward estimate revisions for AI connectivity peers where exposure is tied to switch content, retimer attach, AECs and high-speed interfaces. It offers only a limited incremental signal for near-term GPU unit growth.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: The proportion of AI system value captured by interconnect, switching, memory architecture and fabric software is increasing. Connectivity suppliers can therefore grow faster than aggregate AI server or accelerator units for several generations.

HIGH-SPEED ELECTRICAL CONNECTIVITY AND RETIMERS

PCIe 5 DEMAND IS LASTING LONGER WHILE PCIe 6 ADDS ATTACH RATE AND ASP, CREATING AN OVERLAPPING RETIMER CYCLE (READ-THROUGH 4)

AFFECTED COMPANIES AND IMPACT: Credo Technology Group (CRDO: US) — positive, medium magnitude from stronger industry demand, but partially offset by Astera share pressure. Parade Technologies (4966: Taiwan) — positive, medium magnitude from extended retimer demand, with competitive risk. Broadcom (AVGO: US) and Marvell Technology (MRVL: US) — positive, low-to-medium magnitude for PCIe, CXL and high-speed SerDes portfolios.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Aries delivered record quarterly revenue in Q2. Management stated that PCIe 5 demand had persisted longer than expected because inference deployments are creating a new wave of system demand. PCIe 6 products represented more than 50% of company revenue, up from approximately 33% in Q1. Management said PCIe 6 systems have higher attach rates, higher data rates, additional cable applications and new platform opportunities. Astera is already investing in PCIe 7 to meet future customer timelines. Management characterized retimers as “a socket that somewhat keeps on giving.”

TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: The traditional risk in a semiconductor protocol transition is that the prior generation declines before the next generation reaches sufficient volume. Astera’s commentary indicates that PCIe 5 and PCIe 6 are overlapping rather than producing an immediate replacement cycle. Inference deployments continue to consume established PCIe 5 platforms, while new training and inference architectures are moving to PCIe 6. Higher speeds shorten the viable electrical reach, increase signal-integrity difficulty and create additional cable and board-level use cases, raising both retimer attach rates and ASPs.

This supports a longer and more durable revenue cycle for PCIe signal-conditioning suppliers. The positive market-demand implication is offset by a company-specific competitive warning for Credo and Parade: Astera described Aries as the established “gold standard” across major XPU and CPU platforms and reported record revenue during the transition. The market can expand while Astera simultaneously takes a disproportionate share of the incremental opportunity.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Expectations for a rapid PCIe 5 decline may prove too conservative for retimer suppliers exposed to inference-oriented server platforms. Peer guidance indicating stable legacy-generation demand alongside PCIe 6 ramps would corroborate the Astera signal.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: Successive transitions to PCIe 6 and PCIe 7 should increase rather than merely replace signal-conditioning content because link budgets become more difficult and system topologies more complex at each generation.

800G AEC AND 200G-PER-LANE COPPER ADOPTION IS ACCELERATING, BUT ASTERA’S ENTRY INCREASES SHARE RISK FOR CREDO (READ-THROUGH 5)

AFFECTED COMPANIES AND IMPACT: Amphenol (APH: US) — positive, medium-to-high magnitude. TE Connectivity (TEL: Switzerland) — positive, medium magnitude. Credo Technology Group (CRDO: US) — negative, medium magnitude on competitive share, despite a positive underlying market-demand signal. Marvell Technology (MRVL: US) — mixed, low-to-medium magnitude as the addressable market expands but competition increases in high-speed electrical connectivity.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Astera shipped pre-production quantities of 100G-per-lane Taurus Smart Cable Modules for 800G active electrical cables in Q2 and expects 800G deployments to contribute to second-half growth through higher attach rates and higher ASPs relative to 400G. The company also introduced 3.2T Smart Retimers and Smart Redrivers supporting 200G per lane for next-generation Ethernet and UALink. Management expects the new portfolio to double the Taurus market opportunity to more than $4 billion by 2030. Smart Swap allows customers to select a lower-power redriver or a longer-reach retimer without redesigning the board.

TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: The transition from 400G to 800G and eventually 1.6T and 3.2T increases the electrical content in cables, connectors and host boards. Higher lane rates reduce passive copper reach and increase the requirement for active electronics, redrivers, retimers and more sophisticated cable assemblies. Smart Swap lowers customer qualification and redesign costs by preserving a common board footprint, which can accelerate adoption and allow system configurations to change late in the development cycle.

This is a constructive demand signal for Amphenol and TE Connectivity because more high-speed links and more complex cable assemblies increase connector and interconnect content. No direct Astera supplier relationship was disclosed, so the read-through is industry demand rather than confirmed revenue exposure.

The company-specific implication for Credo is less favorable. Credo has been a primary public-market beneficiary of the AEC transition, while Astera is now shipping competing Smart Cable Modules and introducing a broader 200G-per-lane retimer and redriver portfolio. Astera’s established hyperscaler relationships, COSMOS software and ability to bundle Taurus with Scorpio and Aries increase the risk that it captures sockets that might otherwise have accrued to Credo.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Expansion of 800G AEC deployments in H2 2026 is positive for the broader electrical interconnect chain. Astera’s initial production volumes and Q3 contribution create an immediate competitive benchmark for Credo.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: The migration to 200G-per-lane systems should increase electrical content per link, but competitive differentiation will increasingly depend on power, reach, software, interoperability and the ability to bundle multiple connectivity products rather than on standalone AEC performance alone.

OPTICAL INTERCONNECTS

NPO BEGINS TO MATTER IN 2027, WHILE CPO IS A 2028-PLUS VALUE-CHAIN SHIFT RATHER THAN AN IMMEDIATE COPPER REPLACEMENT (READ-THROUGH 6)

AFFECTED COMPANIES AND IMPACT: Coherent (COHR: US) — positive, medium magnitude from rising optical component demand. Lumentum Holdings (LITE: US) — positive, medium magnitude from lasers and optical components. Marvell Technology (MRVL: US) — negative, medium magnitude from increased competition in optical DSPs and integrated optical engines, partially offset by overall market growth. Zhongji Innolight (300308: China) and Eoptolink Technology (300502: China) — positive near-term optical-volume read-through but negative, medium magnitude over the longer term if NPO and CPO reduce standalone pluggable-module value capture.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Management explicitly stated, “We do fully expect copper and optical to coexist.” Current-generation and next-generation scale-up connections inside the rack are expected to remain predominantly copper. Optical adoption is expected to increase as clusters expand from 1 or 2 racks to 4, 5 or more racks. Astera’s first optical phase is a high-density fiber-attach solution supporting approximately 50-meter reach, with an initial Tier-1 AI platform opportunity expected to enter volume production in 2027. Near-packaged-optics chipsets are also targeted for 2027. Fully integrated Scorpio switches with co-packaged optical engines are positioned for 2028 and beyond.

TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: The immediate optical opportunity is additive to copper rather than substitutive. Copper remains economically attractive within the rack, while optical links become necessary for longer-distance rack-to-rack scale-up connections. This supports simultaneous growth in electrical connectivity and optical components as cluster domains expand.

The value-chain implication changes as the architecture progresses from conventional pluggable modules to NPO and ultimately CPO. Astera is assembling internal capabilities across analog and mixed-signal design, DSP, electrical ICs, silicon photonics, connectors and optical packaging, with the objective of supplying complete optical engines and eventually a fully optically enabled Scorpio switch. If successful, more system value will be controlled by the switch and optical-engine provider, reducing the relative value available to standalone module assemblers.

Coherent and Lumentum receive a positive demand read-through because expanding multi-rack fabrics require more optical components, lasers and photonics. No supplier relationship was disclosed, so direct revenue attribution is unsupported. Marvell faces a more direct strategic challenge because Astera is moving into integrated DSP, electrical and photonic engine functionality that overlaps with Marvell’s optical interconnect position.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Astera’s roadmap does not support an imminent collapse in copper or pluggable optics demand. The relevant near-term optical catalysts are 2027 NPO qualification and production announcements.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: CPO from 2028 onward could shift profit pools from standalone optical modules toward switch-integrated silicon, photonics and packaging. Total optical demand can increase while the economics of traditional module vendors become less favorable.
