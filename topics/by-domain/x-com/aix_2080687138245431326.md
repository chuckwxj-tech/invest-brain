## 原文

$TSM beat and raised, hiked capex mid-year, committed another $100 billion to Arizona. The tape sold it. Our post-earnings interviews with four former TSMC operations and technology leaders surfaced three things worth knowing.

Packaging, not demand, is the binding constraint on AI revenue conversion. Management says they're working hard to close the gap. Three of four sources described it as structural. Each AI server package is a different design. Yields sit well below logic yields.

One source said the industry has no proven solution for the thermo-mechanical effects that compound with every added component. A veteran R&D manager who spent over a decade in TSMC's advanced packaging process: "Recently that new technology, the yield rate is not as high as advanced logic chips. And all of them is kind of like brand-new technology here. So that's the reason why the bottleneck is still on advanced packaging."

The capex signal itself, sources supported. All four described committed multi-year orders, prepayments, and active inventory checks behind the raise, consistent with the conservative planning process they knew inside. But one called the sheer scale new territory.

A former operations director who spent several years overseeing quarterly planning and capacity vetting at TSMC: "TSMC, in general, they are very conservative and careful in building new fabs… especially spending to build four more fabs in the US and also build 13 more fabs in Taiwan. And that is a very aggressive plan that I have not seen in the past."

On Arizona: in January 2025, a former senior executive who supported TSMC's overseas expansion told us the Arizona fab was "a terrible idea" and "uneconomical." Eighteen months later, the source closest to the ramp described a different picture. The early pain was real but converted into learning. A former operations manager who worked the Arizona construction and ramp-up: "From the experience of the first U.S. fab, the yield came out in recent quarters, and the yield is higher than expected. And the yield is probably comparable to a Taiwan fab. I mean, the advanced-node wafers."

The question forward: whether packaging caps how fast AI demand converts to TSMC revenue. Sources said the tell is CoWoS generation migration and whether the glass substrate pilot hits the roughly one-year maturity timeline management guided to.

Source views are those of former employees interviewed by Heron. Not investment advice.
