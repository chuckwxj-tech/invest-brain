ICYMI: CITI’S FMS 2026 READ IS A MIXED BAG — NAND SUPPLY STAYS TIGHT, HBF/KV CACHE MOVE CENTER STAGE, MICRON TARGET GETS CUT

TIL: Citi’s Future of Memory and Storage 2026 meetings pointed to a memory and storage market that is still tight, but getting more nuanced.

SanDisk says its FY2027 bit supply is already fully allocated and sees a roughly $50 billion calendar-2027 TAM. Seagate says nearline HDD capacity is fully allocated through 2027 and into 2028, with customers already discussing volumes beyond 2029. At the same time, Citi cut Micron estimates and lowered its target to $1,150 as DRAM and NAND pricing momentum is expected to cool from 2H27.

The technology discussion is shifting too. KV cache is driving more demand for lower-cost memory and storage tiers, especially where HBM capacity and pricing become constraints. HBF, stacked NAND, CXL memory pooling and high-capacity SSDs were major themes at FMS. Seagate also says KV cache can benefit HDD in workloads where latency is less critical and cost efficiency matters more.

China remains the other big variable. YMTC is adding NAND capacity aggressively and is seen as the larger oversupply risk, while CXMT is expanding DRAM but still faces bigger technology and wafer-efficiency gaps. Citi’s current view is that memory pricing can still stay high even as growth slows, with Micron gross margins potentially remaining around the mid-70% range in 2H27 despite assumed 5–10% price declines.
