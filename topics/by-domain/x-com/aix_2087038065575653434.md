## 原文

https://t.co/aXKa54CXli
Jensen Huang posted this today, which is essentially NVIDIA trying to transform compute from depreciating equipment to reusable infra with residual value.

A GPU has two very different values -
- liquidation value as hardware
- economic value as revenue-producing compute asset
Traditional equipment financing focuses primarily on former, while NVIDIA wants lenders to underwrite the latter. What it means is that for a company with $1B AI factory, traditional lender would ask "if this customer defaults, what can I recover by selling the equipment?". NVIDIA wants the lender to instead ask "If this customer defaults, how quickly can I put this compute in front of another customer and continue generating cash?". This is the fundamental change in underwriting philosophy.

Also, NVIDIA says it may provide 25% residual-value support, so conceptually saying that "You don't need to completely trust the residual value of a Blackwell/B-series GPU. I'll put some NVIDIA balance-sheet credibility behind the residual." This is significant since NVIDIA has information (such as roadmap, support duration, etc.) which the lender doesn't.

So, what does this mean for neoclouds like CoreWeave and Lambda? At least, CoreWeave has been doing this already with their infra + customer contracts + financing. NVIDIA is trying trying to create infra + customer contracts + institutional financing. This is could be simultaneously good and bad for CoreWeave - lower cost and easier access to financing, but then more competition due to lower barrier of entry.

On the other hand, from hyperscalars like Meta this is good news! They don't need to own the entire asset and with unlocked capital, can grow even faster. 

However, there are two risks to NVIDIA
- Suppose each GPU generation produces enormous performance gains every 12-18 months, then an older GPU may still work but it's $/token economics could become terrible.
- While the rack components (GPU, network, software) may be replaceable or upgradable, ecosystem around it might not be. Think of power, cooling, fiber, land costs, etc. Which means most valuable asset might not be GPU but irreplaceable/immovable infrastructure.

Finally, from AI infrastructure point of view, with institutional financing utilization will become much critical financial metric. A 70% GPU utilization will no longer be just an engineering KPI but affects EBITDA. Same for idle time, network utilization, NVLInk failure, and other such metrics. There could even be an AI infrastructure rating based on hardware health + utilization + workload mix + throughput + power efficiency + customer concentration + cluster reliability.
