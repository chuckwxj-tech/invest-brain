$AAOI KEY READ-THROUGHS FROM APPLIED OPTOELECTRONICS Q2 2026 EARNINGS CALL

Applied Optoelectronics’ Q2 2026 call provides strong evidence that AI data-center optical demand has moved beyond a conventional cyclical recovery and into a supply-constrained infrastructure expansion. Forecast demand for 800G and 1.6T transceivers exceeds AOI’s capacity through mid-2027, customer demand is estimated to be 20%-40% above available supply, Q3 800G revenue is expected to increase nearly 5x sequentially, and combined Q4 800G and 1.6T revenue is directionally expected to approach $330 million. The most important broader-market implications extend beyond optical-module demand. The call validates the 102.4T switching transition, indicates that DSPs and TIAs have become critical constraints in the 1.6T supply chain, suggests that high-power laser availability will slow the displacement of pluggable optics by co-packaged optics, and establishes U.S. manufacturing location as an increasingly important hyperscaler procurement criterion. The cable segment also indicates that DOCSIS 4.0 has progressed from technical qualification into meaningful field deployment. The highest-conviction beneficiaries are trusted non-Chinese optical suppliers, optical DSP and analog-component vendors, 102.4T switch-silicon providers, high-power laser manufacturers, and distributed cable-access suppliers. The most exposed negative read-throughs are Chinese optical-module exporters, incumbent cable-amplifier vendors losing upgrade share, and hyperscalers whose AI-cluster deployment schedules may be constrained by optical and networking-component availability.

AI DATA CENTER OPTICS, SWITCHING, AND CLOUD INFRASTRUCTURE

U.S. OPTICAL-SUPPLY RESTRICTIONS WOULD ACCELERATE A STRUCTURAL SHARE SHIFT TO TRUSTED DOMESTIC VENDORS (READ-THROUGH 1)

AFFECTED COMPANIES AND DIRECTIONAL IMPACT: Applied Optoelectronics (AAOI: United States) — HIGH POSITIVE. Coherent Corp. (COHR: United States) — MODERATE-HIGH POSITIVE. Lumentum Holdings (LITE: United States) — MODERATE-HIGH POSITIVE. Zhongji Innolight (300308: China) — HIGH NEGATIVE. Eoptolink Technology (300502: China) — HIGH NEGATIVE. Accelink Technologies (002281: China) — MODERATE-HIGH NEGATIVE.

CALL SUPPORT: Management described AOI’s U.S. manufacturing presence as “probably the most important element of our appeal to our customers” and stated that any restriction increasing customer interest in U.S.-manufactured transceivers would be beneficial. Thompson Lin indicated that early customer feedback suggested customers could become “more aggressive” in assigning AOI additional share, particularly for U.S.-manufactured products. Management also emphasized that AOI expects to be the largest domestic manufacturer of AI-focused optical transceivers.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: A formal restriction on new Chinese optical-transceiver models would transform country of origin and supplier nationality from secondary procurement considerations into primary qualification criteria. Hyperscalers would need to shift incremental qualification resources, long-term forecasts, and purchase commitments toward trusted U.S. and non-Chinese suppliers. AOI has the clearest relative advantage because its strategy includes actual transceiver and laser manufacturing in Texas rather than merely U.S. corporate domicile with production concentrated elsewhere.

The immediate benefit would be more visible in customer allocation, contractual commitments, pricing power, and valuation than in 2026 shipment volume. AOI’s capacity is already substantially committed through Q2 2027, meaning additional demand cannot be converted immediately into revenue. Management explicitly stated that the most practical response could be to accelerate capacity additions beginning in Q3 2027. The near-term trading catalyst is therefore an increase in the value of AOI’s existing capacity and qualification status rather than an immediate step-up in shipped units.

Coherent and Lumentum would benefit from a broader customer preference for trusted optical suppliers, particularly in lasers, components, and modules. The magnitude is lower than for AOI because the ultimate benefit will depend on the final policy’s treatment of supplier domicile, manufacturing location, component origin, and overseas assembly. A restriction focused narrowly on Chinese corporate entities would be more favorable to Coherent and Lumentum than a rule requiring substantial U.S. manufacturing content.

The negative impact on Zhongji Innolight, Eoptolink, and Accelink would be structurally significant. Loss of access to incremental U.S. hyperscaler designs would reduce the addressable market available for their newest 800G and 1.6T products, weaken global manufacturing scale, and potentially force excess output into Chinese and non-U.S. markets. That redirection could create aggressive pricing outside the United States, producing a secondary negative effect for non-Chinese optical suppliers in Europe and Asia even as U.S. pricing improves.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Publication of specific restrictions, clarification of covered products, hyperscaler supplier reallocations, and additional U.S. manufacturing announcements. The highest near-term equity sensitivity resides in AAOI and the Chinese optical-module companies.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: Optical transceivers are moving toward the same trusted-supply architecture already visible in advanced semiconductors and telecommunications equipment. Qualification breadth, domestic manufacturing, secure firmware, traceable components, and long-term capacity commitments should command greater strategic value. This favors vertically integrated suppliers with U.S. production and disadvantages companies whose cost leadership depends on Chinese manufacturing scale.

THE 1.6T RAMP VALIDATES THE 102.4T SWITCHING CYCLE AND A SHARP INCREASE IN AI NETWORK CONTENT (READ-THROUGH 2)

AFFECTED COMPANIES AND DIRECTIONAL IMPACT: Broadcom (AVGO: United States) — HIGH POSITIVE. Arista Networks (ANET: United States) — MODERATE-HIGH POSITIVE. Cisco Systems (CSCO: United States) — MODERATE POSITIVE. NVIDIA (NVDA: United States) — MODERATE POSITIVE. Marvell Technology (MRVL: United States) — MODERATE-HIGH POSITIVE.

CALL SUPPORT: AOI expects qualification of its initial 1.6T product with a major hyperscale customer within approximately 2-3 weeks, followed by initial shipments late in Q3 and a substantial Q4 ramp. The customer has placed more than $200 million of 1.6T orders. Management directionally confirmed that combined 800G and 1.6T revenue could approach $330 million in Q4. Customer feedback indicates potential demand for more than 500,000 1.6T transceivers per month by the end of 2027. The analyst question explicitly connected the expected 1.6T demand inflection to the Tomahawk 6 deployment cycle.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: The magnitude and timing of AOI’s 1.6T orders provide a direct demand signal for the 102.4T switch generation. A 102.4T switch can support 64 native 1.6T ports or 128 800G ports, and a material 1.6T optical deployment therefore requires corresponding availability of 102.4T switch silicon, systems, line cards, network-interface cards, power delivery, and thermal infrastructure.

Broadcom is the clearest beneficiary because Tomahawk 6 is directly aligned with the 102.4T switching transition discussed on the call. The AOI order does not identify the customer’s switch architecture, but a broad hyperscaler move from 800G to 1.6T increases the addressable market for Tomahawk 6 and strengthens the case that 102.4T is entering production deployment rather than remaining a 2027 concept. The read-through extends beyond a single switch-ASIC generation because higher optical speeds also increase demand for Broadcom’s SerDes, PHY, optical DSP, driver, and CPO portfolio.

Arista benefits through higher-value AI Ethernet switching systems, particularly as hyperscalers and large AI clusters transition to 800G and 1.6T fabrics. The positive implication is primarily a revenue and backlog signal rather than an immediate gross-margin signal. Rapid product transitions, early-system configurations, and constrained optics can create unfavorable mix or delay system acceptance even while demand remains strong.

Cisco receives a positive read-through through its 102.4T Silicon One systems, 1.6T optics, and integrated networking portfolio. Cisco’s magnitude is lower because AI back-end switching is a smaller portion of its consolidated revenue and because the call provides no evidence regarding the specific system vendor involved in AOI’s orders.

NVIDIA benefits from the broader confirmation that AI-cluster network bandwidth is scaling rapidly alongside accelerator density. NVIDIA’s Spectrum-X and InfiniBand platforms require increasing quantities of 800G and ultimately 1.6T connectivity. The call supports the view that networking content per AI cluster continues to rise, although the specific AOI programs should not be attributed to NVIDIA systems without customer disclosure.

The broader implication is that AI infrastructure spending is becoming more network-intensive. GPU performance increases do not translate into cluster performance without proportional improvements in scale-out bandwidth. The move from 400G to 800G and 1.6T therefore increases the networking dollar content required per deployed accelerator and extends the AI capital cycle beyond compute silicon.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Completion of AOI’s 1.6T qualification, confirmation of initial Q3 shipments, evidence that Q4 1.6T revenue reaches approximately $70 million-$80 million, and production-volume commentary from 102.4T switch vendors.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: The dominant AI-network port speed is transitioning from 400G and 800G toward 1.6T. This increases switch-silicon content, optical content, and network-system value per cluster. The primary risk is not demand but the ability of the optical and semiconductor supply chain to deliver synchronized volume.

DSP AND TIA SCARCITY CREATES A FAVORABLE PRICING AND MIX ENVIRONMENT FOR OPTICAL SEMICONDUCTOR SUPPLIERS (READ-THROUGH 3)

AFFECTED COMPANIES AND DIRECTIONAL IMPACT: Marvell Technology (MRVL: United States) — HIGH POSITIVE. Broadcom (AVGO: United States) — HIGH POSITIVE. MACOM Technology Solutions (MTSI: United States) — MODERATE-HIGH POSITIVE. Applied Optoelectronics (AAOI: United States) — HIGH NEGATIVE TO NEAR-TERM EXECUTION, POSITIVE TO UNDERLYING DEMAND. Coherent Corp. (COHR: United States) — MODERATE NEAR-TERM EXECUTION RISK. Lumentum Holdings (LITE: United States) — MODERATE NEAR-TERM EXECUTION RISK.

CALL SUPPORT: Management identified DSPs and TIAs as the most important incremental constraints affecting the 800G and 1.6T ramp. Thompson Lin stated that “the big change” was the availability of DSPs and TIAs and described the entire 1.6T supply chain as very tight. AOI is holding supply discussions with critical vendors weekly or twice weekly. Management indicated that Q4 1.6T revenue is constrained by materials rather than installed transceiver capacity and that it can currently commit to approximately $70 million-$80 million of Q4 1.6T revenue despite customers seeking substantially more.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: The call provides a direct positive demand signal for suppliers of PAM4 optical DSPs, TIAs, laser drivers, and related high-speed analog components. The relevant vendors were not identified, so the company-level mapping represents category exposure rather than confirmation that any named company supplies AOI.

Marvell is the highest-purity public beneficiary because its portfolio spans 800G and 1.6T PAM4 DSPs, TIAs, and drivers. A 1.6T module generally contains materially higher-value signal-processing content than a 400G module, and the transition therefore benefits Marvell through both volume and content per unit. Tight availability also strengthens supplier negotiating leverage, reduces near-term pricing pressure, and improves the likelihood that customers make non-cancellable commitments or provide longer-range forecasts.

Broadcom benefits from a similarly broad position across optical DSPs, integrated TIAs and drivers, SerDes, and switch ASICs. The company captures value on both sides of the optical link: the module electronics and the switch silicon driving the port. Supply tightness can support favorable mix and pricing, although Broadcom’s consolidated earnings impact is diluted by the scale of its software and broader semiconductor operations.

MACOM receives a positive read-through through its TIA, driver, photodiode, and high-speed analog portfolio. The magnitude may be substantial relative to the size of MACOM’s optical business, particularly if 1.6T module suppliers increasingly dual-source analog components to reduce dependence on a small number of integrated DSP vendors. MACOM can also benefit from linear pluggable optics, which remove the module DSP but still require high-performance TIAs and drivers.

The inverse read-through is that optical-module vendors can possess customer orders, production floor space, and assembly equipment without being able to recognize revenue. AOI expects Q4 assembly capacity to be sufficient, but component constraints limit 1.6T shipment commitments. This creates a mismatch between capital spending and revenue conversion, increases the risk of expedited freight and technical-support costs, and delays gross-margin expansion.

Management’s revised expectation for approximately 32%-33% Q4 gross margin, rather than the previously discussed mid-30% range, appears partly related to supplier technical fees and the economics of the early 1.6T ramp. Component suppliers are therefore capturing part of the value created by scarcity before module manufacturers have reached mature yields and scale.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Optical semiconductor backlog, lead-time commentary, 1.6T product revenue, supply-allocation announcements, and evidence that AOI or other module vendors receive sufficient Q4 components. Positive revisions at DSP and TIA suppliers could be accompanied by negative shipment revisions at module vendors if supply remains constrained.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: Optical semiconductors are becoming strategic AI infrastructure components rather than commoditized communications chips. The principal long-term offset is the adoption of linear pluggable optics and CPO, which could reduce DSP content in selected links. That risk is more significant for stand-alone DSP exposure than for suppliers with TIAs, drivers, SerDes, switch silicon, and coherent products.

HIGH-POWER LASER CONSTRAINTS SHOULD EXTEND THE PLUGGABLE-OPTICS CYCLE AND DELAY RAPID CPO SUBSTITUTION (READ-THROUGH 4)

AFFECTED COMPANIES AND DIRECTIONAL IMPACT: Lumentum Holdings (LITE: United States) — HIGH POSITIVE. Coherent Corp. (COHR: United States) — HIGH POSITIVE. Marvell Technology (MRVL: United States) — MODERATE POSITIVE. Fabrinet (FN: United States) — MODERATE POSITIVE. Applied Optoelectronics (AAOI: United States) — HIGH LONG-TERM POSITIVE, LIMITED NEAR-TERM BENEFIT. NVIDIA (NVDA: United States) — MODEST NEAR-TERM CPO TIMING RISK. Broadcom (AVGO: United States) — MODEST NEAR-TERM CPO TIMING RISK, OFFSET BY INTERNAL OPTICAL CAPABILITIES.

CALL SUPPORT: AOI stated that CPO external lasers require approximately 300 mW of power, compared with approximately 70 mW for 800G transceivers and approximately 100 mW for 1.6T transceivers. Management indicated that the laser die can be approximately 6x larger and that manufacturing yields are materially lower. The industry may need 8x-10x current laser capacity to support the expected CPO market. New high-volume laser capacity requires approximately 21-24 months from equipment ordering to production. AOI stated that customers “love our laser” but that the company “just can’t make enough of them to be involved in their current first-generation deployments” because internal transceiver demand has priority.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: The most important non-consensus implication is that CPO adoption is likely to be constrained by external laser-source availability even when switch ASICs, silicon photonics engines, and system architectures are technically ready. This weakens the thesis that CPO will rapidly displace pluggable 800G and 1.6T transceivers over the next several quarters.

CPO removes or shortens high-speed electrical paths by integrating optical engines close to the switch ASIC, but it does not eliminate the requirement for reliable laser power. External laser-source modules must provide high power, narrow linewidth, wavelength stability, and high reliability while operating within strict thermal limits. The larger die size, lower expected yields, and long equipment lead times make laser capacity structurally more difficult to scale than conventional transceiver assembly.

Lumentum and Coherent are the clearest beneficiaries because established high-power laser capability should command scarcity value. The market opportunity is not limited to unit growth. High specification requirements, qualification barriers, and limited supply support premium gross margins and reduce the probability of rapid commoditization. AOI estimates gross margins of approximately 55%-65% for lasers and more than 50% for external laser-source modules, indicating that the high-power laser layer could capture a disproportionate share of CPO economics.

The call is also positive for the existing pluggable-optics ecosystem. If CPO laser availability remains constrained, hyperscalers will continue deploying pluggable 800G and 1.6T modules for a longer period. This supports optical DSP demand at Marvell, module-assembly demand at Fabrinet, and transceiver demand across Coherent, Lumentum, AOI, and other module suppliers. The effect is particularly important for 1.6T, where the market has been concerned that CPO could shorten the pluggable product cycle before suppliers earn adequate returns on current capacity investments.

AOI is a long-term beneficiary but not a meaningful near-term CPO volume beneficiary. Internal lasers must first support the company’s rapidly expanding transceiver business. AOI expects limited ELSFP production during 2026, a larger ramp through 2027, and eventual capacity approaching approximately 400,000 units per month in 2028. The high-margin CPO opportunity should therefore be treated primarily as 2027-2029 optionality.

For NVIDIA and Broadcom, the implication is a potential pacing risk rather than a negative demand signal. Their CPO switch roadmaps may remain intact, but large deployments depend on synchronized laser availability. Broadcom’s own optical capabilities mitigate the risk. NVIDIA’s ability to secure high-power external lasers and qualify multiple sources will be important to the timing of its photonic switch deployments.

Management believes Chinese suppliers remain at least 2-3 years away from high-volume, high-quality 300 mW DWDM laser production. This assessment should be treated as management’s competitive view rather than an independently verified industry forecast. If accurate, it would extend the scarcity period for qualified Western high-power lasers and reduce near-term pricing pressure.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: High-power laser design wins, external laser-source qualification announcements, CPO deployment timing, and evidence that first-generation CPO systems remain supply constrained. Continued strength in pluggable 800G and 1.6T forecasts would support the extended-cycle thesis.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: The industry value pool is moving upstream from module assembly toward laser design, epitaxy, wafer fabrication, and external laser-source integration. CPO may ultimately reduce demand for conventional pluggable modules, but the transition is likely to be slower and more supply constrained than switch-roadmap announcements alone suggest.

OPTICAL SHORTAGES CAN BECOME A CRITICAL-PATH CONSTRAINT ON HYPERSCALER AI-CLUSTER DEPLOYMENT (READ-THROUGH 5)

AFFECTED COMPANIES AND DIRECTIONAL IMPACT: https://t.co/SpqvHNUxpK (AMZN: United States) — MODEST NEAR-TERM NEGATIVE TO DEPLOYMENT EFFICIENCY. Microsoft (MSFT: United States) — MODEST NEAR-TERM NEGATIVE. Alphabet (GOOGL: United States) — MODEST NEAR-TERM NEGATIVE. Meta Platforms (META: United States) — MODEST NEAR-TERM NEGATIVE. Arista Networks (ANET: United States) — POSITIVE TO DEMAND AND BACKLOG, NEGATIVE TO REVENUE TIMING RISK. Broadcom (AVGO: United States) — POSITIVE TO DEMAND, WITH SUPPLY-SYNCHRONIZATION RISK. Marvell Technology (MRVL: United States) — POSITIVE TO DEMAND, WITH SUPPLY-SYNCHRONIZATION RISK.

CALL SUPPORT: Management stated that near-term revenue is “bounded almost entirely by our production capacity and key component availability.” Forecast demand for 800G and 1.6T exceeds AOI’s production capacity through mid-2027. Customer demand is approximately 20%-40% above AOI’s supply, and customers are repeatedly requesting accelerated deliveries. Separately, AOI expects a $20 million-$25 million Q3 reduction in 100G revenue because a customer cannot obtain sufficient 100G switches, which management believes is related to memory availability.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: Optical transceivers represent a relatively small portion of total AI-cluster capital cost, but they are indispensable to system operation. A cluster with available GPUs, servers, and switch chassis cannot reach production if the required optical links are unavailable or have not completed qualification. This creates an asymmetric operating risk: a shortage in a comparatively low-cost optical component can delay utilization of much more expensive compute assets.

The call suggests that the AI infrastructure bottleneck is broadening from accelerators and HBM into network switches, optical DSPs, TIAs, lasers, and transceiver assembly. The $20 million-$25 million 100G shortfall provides a practical example of this propagation. AOI has optical-module demand, but the customer cannot deploy the modules because the associated switches are unavailable. The underlying optical order is deferred rather than cancelled, but revenue recognition and customer network expansion are delayed.

For hyperscalers, the near-term implications include higher optical procurement costs, greater use of prepayments and strategic agreements, more qualification expense, and potentially lower returns on compute capital if network completion lags server delivery. The effect on consolidated earnings is modest relative to the scale of Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, and Meta, but the operational effect can be material for specific AI clusters and regional capacity launches.

The call does not identify the customer associated with the 1.6T order or the customer affected by the 100G switch shortage. The company references should therefore be interpreted as hyperscaler-sector exposure rather than attribution of a particular AOI program.

A restriction on Chinese optical transceivers could intensify this risk if implemented before trusted alternative capacity is available. Such a policy would strengthen the long-term position of U.S. suppliers but could tighten near-term module availability, raise pricing, and delay deployments. The result would be positive for supplier economics but potentially negative for hyperscaler capital efficiency.

For Arista, Broadcom, and Marvell, supply constraints create a mixed quarterly effect. End demand and backlog improve, but revenue recognition can become volatile if switch systems, optics, NICs, and cables are not available simultaneously. A delay in any 1 component can move an entire system deployment between quarters.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Q3 and Q4 hyperscaler capex commentary, AI-network deployment timing, optical lead times, switch availability, memory-supply commentary, and the sequencing of GPU, switch, and transceiver deliveries.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: Hyperscalers are likely to treat high-speed optics as strategic capacity rather than standard procurement. This should produce longer-term purchase agreements, strategic investments, supplier warrants, capacity prepayments, dual-source qualification, and direct involvement in manufacturing roadmaps. These actions improve supplier revenue visibility but can deepen customer concentration and reduce module vendors’ pricing autonomy once sufficient supply is established.
