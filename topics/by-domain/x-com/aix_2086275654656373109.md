Lots of talk about memory today. Some thoughts (not that you guys asked lol):

1) Who cares if memory margins are decelerating and potentially topping in the next two quarters? Mfers, we're ~80% lol... even if we go down to 70% or 60%... STILL AMAZING. Memory companies are trading at 4-5x forward P/E and we're just beginning to scratch the surface of DIGITAL SUPER INTELLIGENCE. You can always go buy $LULU or $ELF (@steve2bacon) if you think those are more interesting.

2) Elon, on this week's SPCX earnings call, said that memory demand is growing at 200% while supply is growing at 20%. Jassy, on last week's AMZN earnings call, said that they will not have enough capacity to meet 2026 demand, same for 2027, and they already have demand for 2028. Every hyperscaler raised capex and I suspect they'll continue to raise capex because at the end of the day, owning compute is the moat. Even with new DRAM supply coming online in 2028, all new supply will likely get absorbed and accelerating demand keeps us in a supply constrained state for the foreseeable future. And we aren't even talking about compute at the edge or robotics yet.

3) This week, AMD announced they were acquiring Taalas and, in July, they announced a partnership with Cerebras. In December, Nvidia bought Groq. GPU makers are incorporating specialized inference silicon into their stack to boost tokens per second, but the goal isn't eliminating HBM. These chips take over lighter inference loads from HBM, but because they trade capacity for extreme bandwidth, they can't and won't support frontier models or large context windows. That being said, agents are quickly growing in capability, models are getting huuuuge, and KV cache requirements are growing with them, despite efficiency gains. Physics only allows so much HBM on-package. So either chips get bigger (which they most likely will) or memory moves off-package into CXL pools. Honestly probably both. Either way, there will continue to be strong demand for DRAM and memory makers stay in the loop.

It’s a classic denominator growth situation. DRAM’s percentage in the overall memory hierarchy will reduce, but mainly because the pie is going to grow so much, not because the numerator is lower. If supply and demand reach parity, then memory makers have less leverage to charge higher margins, at which point memory companies sell memory at lower margins than they're at today. At which point, cheaper compute leads to more use cases.

Until capex decelerates, I'm bullish. $MU, $SKHY, $DRAM

Anyways, do your own research and this is not financial advice.

Good luck out there!
