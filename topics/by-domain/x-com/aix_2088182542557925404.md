## 原文

📈 NVIDIA’s Feynman platform could be a much bigger packaging story than the market realizes.

According to DigiTimes, NVIDIA’s post-next-generation Feynman platform is expected to move directly to TSMC A16, while increasing the use of SoIC 3D stacking and potentially integrating:

A16 + SoIC + custom HBM + CPO

That combination matters.

If the roadmap holds, TSMC’s monthly SoIC capacity could expand toward ~50,000 wafers by the end of 2027.

But the more interesting part for me is capacity allocation.

Part of the AP7 P2 line, originally planned for SoIC, may reportedly be redirected toward CPO and CoWoS.

That suggests CPO commercialization may be moving faster than previously expected.

And this is where the supply-chain read-through gets interesting.

Even with TSMC aggressively expanding advanced packaging capacity, demand may still exceed what one supplier can handle.

That creates room for ASE / SPIL to take a larger role in NVIDIA-related packaging orders.

My takeaway:

Feynman may not just be another node transition.

It could accelerate a broader shift toward:

3D stacking → custom HBM → CPO → multi-supplier advanced packaging

If that happens, the bottleneck moves deeper into packaging capacity, optical integration and high-end memory.

That is the part of the AI supply chain I’m watching closely.
