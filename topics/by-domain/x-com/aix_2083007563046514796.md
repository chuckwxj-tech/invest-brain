## 原文

TSMC named its new packaging program "EMIB-like" - after the rival technology it is copying. The world's dominant foundry just admitted the AI chip race has moved from lithography to packaging.

The Information reports TSMC is building an embedded-bridge architecture with Taiwanese substrate maker Kinsus - a direct answer to Intel's EMIB, which reportedly hit 98% yields in mid-July. Markets read it instantly: TSMC up ~7%, Intel up ~13% in one session.

Here is the physics the policy debate keeps skipping. A single lithography exposure is capped at the reticle limit - roughly 858 mm2. Frontier AI chips have hit that wall, so every new accelerator is a multi-die assembly, and the format that stitches dies together decides performance, cost, and who can scale. CoWoS - the packaging behind every Nvidia flagship - tops out near 3.3x reticle today and is sold out through 2027, with 52-78 week lead times. Intel's EMIB already handles 8x reticle and targets 12x by 2028. Google booked 3M+ TPUs on Intel packaging for 2028. Nvidia is evaluating EMIB for its four-die Feynman GPU. TSMC is not extending CoWoS - it is copying the rival architecture. The clearest signal yet that packaging, not process nodes, is the binding constraint on AI compute.

Now the part export-control hawks are missing. Intel's next packaging partner is Chinese: last week it signed an MoU with Lens Technology for TGV glass-substrate packaging for AI chips, with a dedicated line due by year-end. China has no CoWoS-class packaging at scale. But glass substrates are a green field, and Chinese firms like Lens and BOE are already moving in. The US fights over chips while the layer Chinese manufacturing can actually enter is the one everyone is now scrambling for.

Expect the next decoupling fight to be over packaging. This time China is not starting a decade behind.
