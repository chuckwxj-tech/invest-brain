## 原文

$TSM is not merely a bet on AI.

It is a bet that the world will never be finished demanding more computing power.

That is the real reason I own it.

AI may be the largest demand engine TSMC has ever seen.

But AI did not create its moat.

It revealed how valuable that moat had already become.

Long before ChatGPT, TSMC was manufacturing advanced chips for smartphones, cloud infrastructure, gaming, vehicles and industrial systems.

Each generation strengthened its manufacturing expertise and earned more customer trust.

When Apple launches a new iPhone or Nvidia launches a new accelerator, customers do not simply need chip capacity.

They need millions of highly complex chips delivered on time, at strong yields and with consistent quality.

They are buying confidence.

In 2025, TSMC manufactured 12,682 products for 534 customers.

Its advantage is not one machine or one factory.

It is decades of technology leadership, manufacturing excellence and customer trust working together.

That is difficult to recreate.

People ask what happens if AI demand slows.

I think the better question is:

Will the world stop demanding more computing?

I do not think it will.

The next engine could be robotics, autonomous systems, industrial automation, edge computing, medical technology or something that barely exists today.

TSMC does not need to predict every winner.

It can manufacture for many of them.

There is also a quieter demand engine.

Replacement.

The chips inside today’s phones, servers, factories and vehicles will not remain sufficient forever.

Systems will need more performance, better energy efficiency and greater specialization.

Every successful generation of chips creates part of the demand for the next one.

Progress is also no longer only about smaller transistors.

Advanced packaging allows companies to connect several specialized chips into one more capable system.

That makes technologies such as CoWoS and SoIC increasingly important.

TSMC is becoming more than a manufacturer of individual chips.

It is helping enable the entire computing system.

That demand is now becoming physical.

TSMC plans to invest $165 billion in the United States across six fabs, two advanced-packaging facilities and an R&D center.

It is building closer to major customers that want more capacity and greater geographic resilience.

The trade-off is real.

Overseas fabs cost more, margins will face pressure during the ramp and the investment only creates value if customers fill the capacity.

But TSMC is not spending $165 billion hoping demand appears afterward.

It is preparing for demand customers are already signalling.

The latest quarter was exceptional.

Revenue grew 36%.

Operating income grew 65%.

Gross margin reached 67.7%.

Advanced nodes represented 77% of wafer revenue.

Net income grew 77.4%, although part of that increase came from gains on its VIS investment.

But one quarter is not my thesis.

Semiconductors remain cyclical.

Geopolitical risk matters.

Technology leadership must be earned again with every generation.

My thesis is simpler.

New industries will require chips.

Existing industries will upgrade.

Older systems will be replaced.

The applications will change.

The demand for more computing probably will not.

I do not need to predict every company that wins.

I want to own one of the manufacturers many of those winners may depend on.

Factories can be financed.

Machines can be purchased.

But decades of accumulated execution and customer trust cannot be recreated overnight.
