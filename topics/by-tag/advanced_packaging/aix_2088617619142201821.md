Most analysts just refused to acknowledge the best chip designer, AMD whether it is CPUs, GPUs, APUs, FPGAs, DPUs, Networking, chipsets, embedded solutions and other Semi-custom SoCs. AMD’s chiplet technology (modular multi-die design using smaller “chiplets” connected by Infinity Fabric) gives clear advantages in cost, yield, scalability, flexibility, and time to market compared with traditional large monolithic dies. 

1. Much Higher Manufacturing Yields and Lower Cost

This leads to superior TCO and more units. Smaller dies have dramatically better yields. Defects are randomly distributed on a wafer; a defect kills only one small chiplet instead of an entire large processor.
AMD gets far less wasted silicon for TSMC(TSMC also gets higher margin, and this is why I stated AMD will be 3rd largest customer in 2026 and #2 in 2027, and I was right), lower cost per core, and the ability to bin defective chiplets into lower core count products instead of scrapping them. AMD has publicly noted that chiplet designs can cost roughly half (or less) of an equivalent monolithic part for high core count chips, and the cost of adding cores scales roughly linearly rather than exponentially.

This economic advatange was decisive in AMD’s server (EPYC) comeback and remains strong for 6 generations even as Intel and others adopt multi-die designs.

2. Superior Scalability and Product Flexibility

AMD builds almost its entire CPU stack from the same reusable building blocks:
8-core Ryzen → 1 CCD + IOD
16-core Ryzen → 2 CCDs + IOD
High-core EPYC → up to 12–16 CCDs around one (or more) IOD(s

The same CCD silicon is shared between desktop Ryzen and server EPYC, dramatically cutting design and manufacturing costs => this again wins the Superior TCO for all customers and allow AMD has the best pricing power. Competitors traditionally needed largely separate monolithic designs (or more complex tile mixes) for different segments.

3. Heterogeneous Process Nodes

Compute logic benefits most from the latest process node (higher density, better performance/watt). I/O, analog, and SerDes do not scale as well and are cheaper/more mature on older nodes.  AMD routinely pairs leading-edge CCDs (TSMC 5 nm / 4 nm / 3 nm / 2 nm) with an IOD on a more mature node (6 nm or 3 nm). This optimizes both performance and cost something pure monolithic designs cannot do.

4. Chiplet allows Faster Innovation and Design Reuse

Developing and validating one small CCD + one IOD, then composing products from them, is faster and lower-risk than designing and tape outing an entirely new large monolithic die for every SKU or generation. Parallel development of different chiplets further accelerates cycles.

5. Overcoming Physical Limits and Enabling Extreme Scale

Reticle limits (800–858 mm²) cap single die size. Chiplets + advanced packaging (2.5D, 3D hybrid bonding, CoWoS, fan-out, etc.) let AMD build packages far larger than any monolithic chip; critical for high-core EPYC and especially for AI GPUs (Instinct MI300/MI400/MI455/Future series with multiple compute, I/O, and cache chiplets + HBM stacks).  Infinity Fabric provides the coherent, high-bandwidth interconnect that makes the multi-die package appear as a unified processor to software.
