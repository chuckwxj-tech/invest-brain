# There's no longer any need to get buried in hyperscalers' CapEx. The July 24–25, 2026 San Francisco AI Summit MOUs signal the strategic direction of Sovereign Data Centers (DCs)...

## 用户备注

来源: ai-x-discovery；机器初筛，仍需外部核查。

## 原文

There's no longer any need to get buried in hyperscalers' CapEx.

The July 24–25, 2026 San Francisco AI Summit MOUs signal the strategic direction of Sovereign Data Centers (DCs), establishing a distinct infrastructure layer separate from global hyperscalers.

The core significance of these landmark agreements is the buildout of sovereign AI infrastructure designed to secure two strategic capabilities:

Sovereign frontier-model inference, ensuring compliance with data residency and national AI governance requirements.

Sovereign Physical AI model weights, enabling countries and enterprises to internalize critical industrial and manufacturing know-how.

AI Infrastructure: Three-Tier Architecture

The emerging AI infrastructure stack can be segmented by workload characteristics and latency requirements:

1. Global Hyperscale Data Centers (U.S.)

Frontier model pre-training

Bandwidth-intensive workloads

HBM-centric architecture

2. Sovereign Data Centers (Domestic)

~90% Frontier model inference serving

~10% Physical AI training and simulation

Capacity-driven architecture emphasizing KV cache expansion, custom HBM, and inference ASICs

3. Factory Edge & Onboard Robotics

Physical AI inference

Ultra-low-latency (microsecond-level) control

LPDDR-based memory architecture

SK hynix & Naver – NVIDIA: High-Capacity HBM and KV Cache Expansion

The SK hynix (US$750B) + Naver (US$10B) – NVIDIA partnership represents a cornerstone of the Sovereign DC layer.

Large-scale deployment of next-generation HBM3E (12-high) and HBM4 (16-high) for NVIDIA's Rubin-generation GPUs.

Since inference accounts for roughly 90% of Sovereign DC workloads, KV cache capacity becomes the primary performance bottleneck.

SK hynix is likely addressing this through custom HBM integrating logic into the base die, significantly expanding effective KV cache capacity.

Naver can leverage this infrastructure to deploy sovereign AI services across enterprise (B2B) and government (B2G) markets.

Samsung – Broadcom: Inference ASICs and a Turnkey Manufacturing Ecosystem

The Samsung – Broadcom (US$200B) collaboration focuses on lowering the total cost of ownership (TCO) for Sovereign DCs.

Running inference entirely on premium GPUs is economically inefficient.

Broadcom provides custom inference ASICs optimized for KV cache efficiency, while Samsung manufactures them using its 2nm (SF2) process and I-Cube/X-Cube 2.5D/3D advanced packaging technologies.

This also strengthens the broader semiconductor ecosystem, driving demand for:

FC-BGA substrates

Hybrid bonding equipment

CXL controller IP

Acryl & Megazone Cloud: Data Center Resource Optimization

The Acryl – Megazone Cloud partnership addresses operational efficiency within Sovereign DCs.

These facilities will simultaneously support frontier-model inference and Physical AI simulation workloads.

Through GPUBASE, GPU partitioning and intelligent scheduling improve utilization of expensive GPU and HBM resources, maximizing infrastructure efficiency while minimizing idle capacity.

Key Takeaway

Physical AI inference is expected to migrate toward the edge, relying on LPDDR-based memory for ultra-low-latency execution.

Meanwhile, Sovereign Data Centers will become the primary platform for approximately 90% frontier-model inference and 10% Physical AI training and simulation.

The July 2026 MOUs therefore represent more than isolated partnerships—they collectively establish the complete Sovereign AI infrastructure stack by enabling:

1. Custom HBM for scalable KV cache capacity

2. Inference ASICs with advanced packaging to reduce TCO

3. GPU scheduling and resource optimization for higher utilization

Together, these components define the emerging architecture of sovereign AI infrastructure, creating a differentiated layer that complements—rather than competes directly with—global hyperscale data centers.
