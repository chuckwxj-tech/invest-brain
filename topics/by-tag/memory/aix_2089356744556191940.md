## 原文

Most people still judge AI infrastructure by who sells the GPUs.
That’s like judging a data center by the servers and ignoring the wires that connect them.

Astera Labs $ALAB is the company building the connectivity layer that makes large-scale AI actually work.

What they do;
Astera designs high-speed semiconductor solutions that move data between accelerators, memory, and networking inside AI racks. Their Intelligent Connectivity Platform includes:
• Aries — PCIe/CXL retimers and smart cable modules (the original franchise)
• Scorpio — Smart fabric switches for scale-up and scale-out AI networking
• Taurus — Ethernet signal conditioning and modules
• Leo — CXL memory controllers
• COSMOS — Software for configuration, telemetry, and fleet management
As AI clusters get denser and move to PCIe 6 and higher lane counts, signal integrity and fabric switching become critical bottlenecks. Astera sits right in the middle of that problem.
The numbers (as of Q2 2026)
• Revenue: $392.4M (+104% YoY, +27% QoQ)
• PCIe 6 products already >50% of revenue
• Gross margins ~73–74%
• Q3 guidance: $540–560M (≈40% sequential growth)
• Scorpio fabric switches expected to become the largest product family in Q3 — one quarter ahead of prior expectations
Trailing twelve-month revenue is already over $1.2B, and the company has turned solidly profitable with strong operating leverage.

Why it matters
Every additional GPU in a rack needs more connectivity silicon. Astera’s content per accelerator is rising as systems scale, and Scorpio moves them from selling components to supplying the fabric that stitches entire racks together.
They are one of the cleaner pure-plays on the plumbing of AI infrastructure — largely independent of which accelerator (Nvidia, AMD, custom ASICs) ultimately wins.
While the market obsesses over the next GPU generation, Astera is quietly compounding inside the racks that actually run the models.
