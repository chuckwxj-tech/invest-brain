## 原文

I’m becoming more patient on $ALAB’s Leo.

The frustrating part is that the use case is staring you in the face. Memory is scarce and expensive, inference workloads need more of it, and CXL lets customers put cheaper or reused memory behind the bus, either expanding a single host or pooling memory across hosts behind a switch.

On paper, the story completes itself.

The need is ready. The stack isn’t. It will probably take longer than anyone wants before that demand shows up meaningfully in revenue.

Astera guided to some Leo revenue in 2H26 and volume shipments to two U.S. hyperscalers in 2027. The forcing functions have finally arrived: memory costs, supply constraints, and KV-cache growth. The near-term case does not require optical memory to work.

But patience does not mean ignoring the product gap.

Leo today is still a two-channel CXL 2.0 endpoint. Marvell can connect more memory behind each controller, already has the switch layer needed for rack-level pooling, and expects to sample its newer CXL 3.x switch this quarter.

There are two kinds of “not ready” here, and they matter differently.

Software maturity, remote-memory latency, and platform qualification are category risks. They delay CXL for everyone. When the market eventually ramps, Astera still has the opportunity to win its share.

That is a timing problem.

Endpoint economics, CXL generation, and capacity per controller are company-specific risks. If Leo falls behind there, the ramp still happens. It just happens with somebody else’s controller in the socket.

That is not a delay. It is a transfer.

You can wait out the first kind. You cannot wait out the second.

What I’m watching from here: a Leo successor with better capacity, bandwidth, and economics per usable terabyte, later-generation CXL support, and evidence that both disclosed hyperscalers reach genuine production volume.

I’m willing to be patient with CXL’s timing. But hearing Astera explicitly say it wants to go after the optical engine makes the longer-term setup a lot more interesting 👀
