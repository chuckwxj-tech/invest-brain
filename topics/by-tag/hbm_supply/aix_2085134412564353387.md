$KLIC KEY READ-THROUGHS FROM KULICKE & SOFFA Q2 2026 EARNINGS CALL

Kulicke & Soffa’s Q2 2026 call provides a strongly positive signal for the global semiconductor back-end equipment cycle, with the most important conclusion being that AI infrastructure demand is stimulating both leading-edge advanced packaging and high-volume traditional packaging. Revenue increased 21.5% sequentially, general semiconductor revenue increased 19.4%, memory revenue increased 93%, automotive and industrial revenue increased 63%, and management guided the June quarter to approximately 28% sequential growth followed by another 5% to 10% increase in the September quarter. The breadth and acceleration of demand are constructive for assembly, test, inspection, packaging substrates, and semiconductor content suppliers. The highest-value non-consensus signal is that wire bonding and other traditional packaging technologies are not being displaced by AI; instead, AI data centers are creating incremental demand for networking, communications, power-management, and storage devices that continue to use conventional high-volume packaging. The principal negative implications are rising competitive intensity in thermo-compression bonding, delayed broad adoption of hybrid bonding, geographic concentration in China and Northeast Asia, persistent softness in Southeast Asia, and evidence that revenue upside may not translate proportionately into gross-margin and EPS upside because of customer mix, variable compensation, manufacturing expansion, and elevated advanced-packaging R&D.

SEMICONDUCTOR ASSEMBLY, TEST AND INSPECTION

AI INFRASTRUCTURE IS EXPANDING, RATHER THAN CANNIBALIZING, THE TRADITIONAL PACKAGING MARKET (READ-THROUGH 1)

Affected companies: ASMPT Limited (0522: Hong Kong), ASE Technology Holding Co., Ltd. (ASX: Taiwan), Amkor Technology, Inc. (AMKR: US), Cohu, Inc. (COHU: US).

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, high magnitude for ASMPT, ASE, and Amkor; positive, medium-to-high magnitude for Cohu.

Supporting commentary and data: Management stated that “data center growth also requires new capacity for high-volume traditional packaging solutions which support networking, communication, power management and storage requirements.” General semiconductor revenue increased 19.4% sequentially to $148.9 million, while memory revenue increased 93% sequentially to $31.3 million. Management also indicated that utilization had continued to rise over the preceding year and that the need for incremental capacity was increasing.

The critical implication is that the semiconductor back-end cycle is broader than the commonly emphasized CoWoS, HBM, chiplet, and hybrid-bonding investment themes. AI accelerators require a large supporting ecosystem of network switches, optical and electrical connectivity devices, power-management ICs, storage controllers, flash-memory components, voltage regulators, mixed-signal devices, and other semiconductors. Many of these products continue to use wire bonding, leadframes, mature substrates, and high-volume conventional assembly flows. Incremental AI infrastructure spending therefore increases demand for both leading-edge and legacy back-end equipment.

ASMPT is the most direct external beneficiary because its wire-bonding and broader assembly-equipment portfolio participates in many of the same high-volume applications. The call reduces the probability that advanced packaging causes an abrupt secular decline in wire-bonder demand. Instead, traditional assembly equipment can grow alongside advanced packaging as AI increases total semiconductor content and package volumes.

ASE and Amkor should benefit through higher package volumes, improved factory utilization, and incremental capacity requirements across networking, communications, power, and storage devices. The economic impact will depend on pricing discipline and customer mix, but utilization-led revenue growth should improve fixed-cost absorption within existing assembly facilities.

Cohu should benefit through higher demand for handlers, contactors, inspection equipment, and recurring interface products associated with the increased number of packaged devices. The read-through is particularly constructive because traditional package volumes are substantially larger than leading-edge package volumes, creating a broader installed-base and consumables opportunity.

Near-term trading catalyst: Positive order and estimate revisions for back-end assembly suppliers as customers add capacity through the remainder of calendar 2026. The June-quarter revenue guide and management’s expectation for further September-quarter growth indicate that the order cycle remained in acceleration rather than merely stabilization.

Longer-duration fundamental shift: AI infrastructure should create a dual-track packaging cycle in which advanced packaging captures the highest-value compute applications while traditional packaging benefits from the much larger surrounding component ecosystem. This materially improves the duration of the conventional assembly-equipment market relative to a thesis based solely on smartphone, PC, and consumer-electronics replacement cycles.

RISING ASSEMBLY UTILIZATION SHOULD DRIVE A FOLLOW-ON TEST AND INSPECTION CAPEX CYCLE (READ-THROUGH 2)

Affected companies: Advantest Corporation (6857: Japan), Teradyne, Inc. (TER: US), Cohu, Inc. (COHU: US), Camtek Ltd. (CAMT: Israel), Onto Innovation Inc. (ONTO: US).

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, medium-to-high magnitude for Advantest, Cohu, and Camtek; positive, medium magnitude for Teradyne and Onto Innovation.

Supporting commentary and data: China utilization was approximately 92%, with strong utilization also reported in Korea, Japan, and Taiwan. Management stated that customer sentiment remained strong, utilization across the largest served market was above average, and the need for incremental capacity continued to grow. K&S was aggressively ramping production in both core and advanced markets, while June-quarter revenue was expected to increase 28% sequentially to $310 million.

Assembly-capacity additions generally require corresponding investment in final test, system-level test, handlers, sockets, contactors, optical inspection, package metrology, and process-control equipment. Packaging equipment can be installed somewhat earlier in the production-capacity sequence, making K&S’s accelerating demand a potential leading indicator for adjacent test and inspection orders.

Advantest should benefit from increased test demand in memory, advanced logic, high-speed networking, and AI-related devices. The memory rebound is especially relevant because rising memory output and more complex stacked-memory products require substantial test content. Teradyne should also benefit from higher logic, mixed-signal, networking, and automotive semiconductor test requirements, although the magnitude will depend on the mix between high-end compute and more mature devices.

Cohu has a particularly direct transmission mechanism because its handlers, contactors, and inspection systems are deployed downstream of assembly. Higher utilization drives both capital-equipment demand and recurring consumables revenue. An increase in package volumes can therefore affect Cohu through initial system sales, installed-base utilization, replacement contactors, and service activity.

Camtek and Onto Innovation should benefit from increasing inspection intensity in advanced packaging. Thermo-compression bonding, heterogeneous integration, panel-level packaging, stacked memory, and high-density interconnects require tighter process control than conventional packages. Defect detection becomes more economically valuable as package content and the cost of known-good dies increase.

Near-term trading catalyst: Orders for handlers, test equipment, inspection tools, and interface products should strengthen over the next 2 to 4 quarters if K&S’s customer-utilization and capacity-addition signals persist.

Longer-duration fundamental shift: Advanced packaging increases inspection and test intensity per package, while AI-related traditional packaging increases total unit volumes. The combination can support a structurally larger back-end test and inspection market even if front-end wafer-fabrication spending becomes more volatile.

THERMO-COMPRESSION GROWTH SUPPORTS ADVANCED SUBSTRATES AND PACKAGING MATERIALS (READ-THROUGH 3)

Affected companies: Ibiden Co., Ltd. (4062: Japan), Unimicron Technology Corporation (3037: Taiwan), Kinsus Interconnect Technology Corporation (3189: Taiwan), Nan Ya Printed Circuit Board Corporation (8046: Taiwan), Ajinomoto Co., Inc. (2802: Japan).

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, medium-to-high magnitude for high-end substrate suppliers; positive, medium magnitude for Ajinomoto.

Supporting commentary and data: Management expected the thermo-compression bonding business to grow at least 70% during fiscal 2026 and generate more than $100 million of revenue. Demand was expanding across IDMs, foundries, and OSATs, with additional engagement from fabless semiconductor customers. Management described large logic applications and heterogeneous packaging as the principal drivers of the current TCB ramp.

Thermo-compression bonding is generally associated with increasingly complex multi-die packages, higher I/O density, tighter pitch, and more demanding thermal and electrical performance. These architectures tend to require high-end package substrates, advanced build-up films, underfill materials, molding compounds, thermal-interface materials, and increasingly precise package-level process control.

Ibiden, Unimicron, Kinsus, and Nan Ya PCB should benefit from higher demand for advanced substrates supporting heterogeneous logic packages and other high-performance applications. The direct transmission mechanism is not the bonder purchase itself; it is the production volume that the bonder enables. As customers move from engineering qualification into high-volume manufacturing, substrate demand scales with package output and may become a larger recurring revenue stream than the initial equipment installation.

Ajinomoto should benefit from greater use of advanced build-up substrates incorporating Ajinomoto Build-up Film and related high-performance dielectric materials. The earnings impact is likely less immediate than for equipment companies because materials demand follows package production rather than tool installation, but it should be more recurring once programs reach volume production.

Near-term trading catalyst: Substrate order visibility should improve as foundries, IDMs, and OSATs place and qualify additional TCB capacity. The strongest near-term read-through is for suppliers already positioned in high-end compute and networking substrates.

Longer-duration fundamental shift: Increasing package complexity should raise substrate and material value per semiconductor device. This creates an attractive recurring-content model that is less dependent on the timing of individual equipment orders and more dependent on the sustained production volumes of advanced packages.

KLIC’S TCB CAPACITY EXPANSION RAISES COMPETITIVE PRESSURE ON INCUMBENT BONDER VENDORS (READ-THROUGH 4)

Affected companies: BE Semiconductor Industries N.V. (BESI: Netherlands), ASMPT Limited (0522: Hong Kong), Hanmi Semiconductor Co., Ltd. (042700: South Korea). Secondary beneficiaries include ASE Technology Holding Co., Ltd. (ASX: Taiwan), Amkor Technology, Inc. (AMKR: US), and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co., Ltd. (TSM: Taiwan).

Directional impact and magnitude: Negative, medium-to-high magnitude for incumbent TCB equipment suppliers over the longer term; positive, low-to-medium magnitude for foundry and OSAT customers through increased vendor competition and supply diversification.

Supporting commentary and data: K&S expected TCB revenue to exceed $100 million in fiscal 2026 and grow at least 70%. The company planned to expand Advanced Solutions production capacity to support approximately $400 million of annual revenue. Management stated that growth was occurring across “OSAT, IDM as well as the foundry” and explicitly said: “We’ll both take market share and the market will grow, and we’ll enter markets that we’re currently not in.”

The $400 million capacity target is approximately 4 times the company’s stated fiscal 2026 TCB revenue objective. The investment does not demonstrate that $400 million of demand has been secured, but it does show that K&S intends to compete at materially greater scale. The threat is therefore not limited to incremental market growth; management is explicitly targeting share gains in logic and future entry into memory and HBM.

Besi faces a meaningful competitive read-through because its valuation and growth narrative are highly sensitive to advanced-packaging equipment adoption, including hybrid and thermo-compression-related applications. A credible additional vendor with differentiated fluxless processing, flexible material handling, and demonstrated foundry and IDM placements can pressure market-share assumptions, pricing, and customer concentration.

ASMPT faces similar competitive risk in TCB and advanced placement, although the broader market expansion remains positive for its total addressable market. The net impact is therefore mixed: industry revenue opportunity increases, but K&S’s capacity build and market-share ambitions raise the probability of more aggressive tool competition.

Hanmi Semiconductor faces limited immediate risk because K&S’s HBM offering remained in qualification, but the longer-term competitive threat increases if K&S successfully transfers its logic TCB platform into HBM production. K&S’s emphasis on fluxless processing and flexible handling suggests that it intends to compete across multiple package architectures rather than remain confined to a narrow logic niche.

Foundries and OSATs should benefit from a more competitive supplier market. A larger number of production-capable TCB vendors can reduce single-source risk, improve negotiating leverage, accelerate capacity additions, and provide greater flexibility across package designs. Fabless customers may also influence equipment selection through their foundry and OSAT supply chains, creating an additional pathway for vendor displacement.

Near-term trading catalyst: Increased K&S bookings, customer qualifications, and repeat orders would be negative relative catalysts for incumbent bonder vendors. The initial signal is competitive-intensity risk rather than proven share loss.

Longer-duration fundamental shift: TCB may evolve from a concentrated equipment market into a broader multi-vendor market. This would expand industry capacity but could reduce the scarcity value, pricing power, and customer lock-in of incumbent equipment suppliers.

HYBRID BONDING ADOPTION APPEARS LATER THAN THE MOST AGGRESSIVE MARKET EXPECTATIONS (READ-THROUGH 5)

Affected companies: BE Semiconductor Industries N.V. (BESI: Netherlands), ASMPT Limited (0522: Hong Kong), Hanmi Semiconductor Co., Ltd. (042700: South Korea).

Directional impact and magnitude: Negative, medium magnitude for Besi’s near-term hybrid-bonding revenue expectations; mixed-to-positive, medium magnitude for existing TCB suppliers because the commercial life of TCB is extended.

Supporting commentary and data: Management stated that hybrid bonding “may be still a few years away from gaining broad market adoption” and described TCB as “the production solution for today’s most complex heterogeneous applications.” K&S was accelerating hybrid-bonding R&D, but broad commercialization was not presented as an immediate fiscal 2026 or fiscal 2027 volume driver.

The call supports a slower technology-transition path than the most bullish hybrid-bonding scenarios. Hybrid bonding continues to offer compelling long-term density, power, and performance advantages, but production adoption appears constrained by yield, surface preparation, alignment, contamination control, throughput, and integration complexity. Customers are therefore likely to continue deploying TCB for a broader range of heterogeneous applications while hybrid processes mature.

Besi carries the greatest negative timing read-through because hybrid bonding is a prominent component of its long-term strategic narrative. The call does not invalidate the long-term opportunity, but it indicates that broad revenue conversion may remain dependent on a small number of leading-edge programs for longer than aggressive forecasts assume.

ASMPT and Hanmi should benefit from a longer TCB investment window. Customers that cannot yet achieve acceptable hybrid-bonding yield or throughput can continue scaling TCB-based packaging. This increases the useful commercial duration of existing platforms and allows suppliers to generate revenue while developing next-generation hybrid solutions.

The competitive interpretation requires caution because K&S has an incentive to emphasize the durability of TCB while developing its own hybrid platform. Nevertheless, the company is actively engaged with IDMs, foundries, OSATs, and fabless companies, making its view an important industry data point.

Near-term trading catalyst: Hybrid-bonding order expectations may need to remain concentrated in specific customer programs rather than being extrapolated into broad industry adoption. TCB-related estimate revisions should have greater near-term visibility.

Longer-duration fundamental shift: Hybrid bonding remains a likely destination for the most advanced applications, but the transition should be modeled as gradual and application-specific. TCB and hybrid bonding are likely to coexist for an extended period rather than producing an abrupt technology replacement cycle.

MEMORY AND REGIONAL BACK-END CAPACITY

HBM EQUIPMENT REMAINS QUALIFICATION-GATED, PROTECTING INCUMBENT SUPPLIERS IN THE NEAR TERM (READ-THROUGH 6)

Affected companies: Hanmi Semiconductor Co., Ltd. (042700: South Korea), ASMPT Limited (0522: Hong Kong), BE Semiconductor Industries N.V. (BESI: Netherlands).

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, medium magnitude for already-qualified HBM equipment suppliers in the near term; negative, medium magnitude over the longer term if K&S completes qualification and becomes a scaled alternative.

Supporting commentary and data: K&S delivered its first HBM system in December, but the tool remained under customer qualification at the time of the May call. Management said current Fluxless TCB adoption was being driven primarily by general semiconductor and logic, not HBM. Management also said Vertical Wire would contribute only a small amount of revenue in fiscal 2026 and was more of a fiscal 2027-and-beyond opportunity.

The read-through is that HBM packaging equipment remains a qualification-intensive market with meaningful barriers to rapid supplier entry. Tool capability alone is insufficient. Customers must validate throughput, placement accuracy, thermal control, warpage management, yield, process repeatability, service support, and integration with upstream and downstream processes before moving equipment into volume production.

This qualification burden protects incumbent suppliers during 2026 because customers are unlikely to switch large production volumes rapidly. Hanmi and other suppliers with established production credentials should retain an advantage until challengers demonstrate successful high-volume performance.

The longer-term implication is less favorable for incumbents. K&S is allocating additional resources to HBM, has a TCB platform already proven in logic applications, and is expanding manufacturing capacity substantially. Successful HBM qualification would broaden the customer’s supplier base and could introduce greater price and share competition.

Vertical Wire does not represent an immediate threat to conventional stacked-memory equipment. Management expected only limited fiscal 2026 revenue, indicating that the technology remains a medium-duration option rather than a near-term substitute for established HBM assembly processes.

Near-term trading catalyst: Customer qualification announcements, production-tool orders, or evidence of repeat HBM placements will determine whether K&S becomes a material competitor. Until those events occur, the incumbency advantage remains intact.

Longer-duration fundamental shift: The HBM equipment market should gradually become more competitive as additional vendors complete qualification. The economic moat will shift from basic equipment availability toward demonstrated yield, throughput, process integration, and global service execution.

THE BACK-END UPTURN IS GEOGRAPHICALLY CONCENTRATED IN CHINA AND NORTHEAST ASIA (READ-THROUGH 7)

Affected companies: JCET Group Co., Ltd. (600584: China), Tongfu Microelectronics Co., Ltd. (002156: China), Tianshui Huatian Technology Co., Ltd. (002185: China), ASE Technology Holding Co., Ltd. (ASX: Taiwan), Powertech Technology Inc. (6239: Taiwan), Inari Amertron Berhad (0166: Malaysia), Unisem (M) Berhad (5005: Malaysia), Malaysian Pacific Industries Berhad (3867: Malaysia), Amkor Technology, Inc. (AMKR: US).

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, high magnitude for Chinese OSATs; positive, medium magnitude for Taiwan-based packaging companies; negative, medium magnitude for Southeast Asian OSATs on a relative basis. Mixed for Amkor because of its diversified geographic exposure.

Supporting commentary and data: China utilization was approximately 92%, while Korea, Japan, and Taiwan were also strong. Southeast Asia remained “a bit soft,” despite some improvement. Management further stated that “a lot of the Chinese memory OSATs are expanding significantly,” which was driving K&S’s China Ball Bonding business.

The recovery is therefore not geographically uniform. China and Northeast Asia appear to be leading capacity additions, while Southeast Asian back-end activity remains below comparable levels. This is an important cross-portfolio distinction because a generic global OSAT recovery thesis may overstate near-term earnings improvement for companies with heavier exposure to Malaysia, the Philippines, and other Southeast Asian assembly locations.

JCET, Tongfu, and Huatian should benefit from higher utilization and local memory-packaging capacity additions. Increased activity can improve fixed-cost absorption, support revenue growth, and justify additional equipment purchases. The strongest near-term benefit is likely in conventional memory, mature-node logic, and high-volume packaging rather than the most advanced HBM applications.

ASE and Powertech should benefit from strong Taiwan utilization and broader memory and logic activity. Taiwan’s position across foundry, substrate, memory packaging, and advanced assembly creates multiple transmission channels from higher semiconductor utilization into OSAT revenue.

Inari, Unisem, and Malaysian Pacific Industries receive a less constructive signal. Southeast Asia was improving but remained soft, indicating that the regional recovery may lag China, Korea, Japan, and Taiwan. This could delay utilization recovery, pricing improvement, and capital spending for Southeast Asia-focused back-end companies.

Amkor’s read-through is mixed. The company should benefit from global advanced-packaging and automotive demand, but its meaningful Southeast Asian manufacturing footprint creates exposure to the region identified as relatively weak. Its customer and technology mix may offset the geographic softness, but the call does not support a uniformly positive conclusion for all of its factories.

Chinese memory OSAT expansion also introduces a longer-term competitive risk for non-China commodity-memory packaging providers. Additional Chinese capacity can increase pricing pressure, reinforce local sourcing, and reduce the amount of China-related business available to Taiwan, U.S., and Southeast Asian OSATs.

Near-term trading catalyst: Revenue and utilization upside should be concentrated in Chinese and Northeast Asian packaging names, while Southeast Asian companies may require more selective positioning.

Longer-duration fundamental shift: China’s continued investment in domestic packaging capacity can drive local OSAT share gains and increase competitive intensity in commodity and mature-node packaging. This is positive for Chinese equipment demand but potentially negative for global OSAT pricing and market share.
