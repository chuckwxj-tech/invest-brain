1. What is SOXX, basically

An ETF is like an index-token. You buy one “coin” (fund share), and under the hood it holds 30 different companies. You don’t have to build the basket yourself; the fund does it for you and automatically rebalances.
SOXX ETF facts:
Holds 30 chip-making companies (US + a couple foreign).
Fund fee 0.34% per year, like a vault’s management fee. Not much.
Fund size ~$47B, essentially its TVL.
Current price ~$582 per “coin”. Over the past year it ranged from $237 to $655.
Once per quarter the fund rebalances the basket (like an index-vault) so no single holding gets too big relative to the overall index.
Why does it exist at all?
Chips are the “oil” of the AI era. Want exposure to the entire AI bull without picking individual winners? Take the basket and you’re in the theme as a whole.
Traded in crypto: Arcus Exchange – SOXX

2. What’s under the hood and why this is about AI
Think of the entire “AI factory” as a pipeline. SOXX holds companies at almost every stage of making AI:
Brains (logic): NVIDIA, AMD — the GPUs that train neural nets. ~8% each.
Memory: Micron — memory chips (HBM), without which GPUs can’t store data. ~8%.
“Networking” inside data centers: Broadcom, Marvell, Astera Labs — connect thousands of chips together to produce more powerful compute.
Equipment for fabs: Applied Materials, KLA, Lam Research, ASML — sell machines to those who make chips. These are “picks and shovels for gold miners”. ~21% of the fund.
Foundries: TSMC — actually prints chips for everyone. ~4.4%.

Important nuance on weighting:
No single company exceeds ~8%. For comparison, in the competitor SMH, NVIDIA alone is ~20% of the fund. SOXX intentionally diversifies the bet. In crypto terms: SOXX is a more diversified index, SMH is an index heavily skewed toward one blue-chip.

5. Expensive now or cheap?

Here we introduce the main metric: P/E (Price-to-Earnings).
P/E in simple terms: how many years of earnings you’re paying for the right to own the companies. P/E = 20 means you’re paying as if for 20 years of profit. The higher, the “more expensive” and the more growth is already priced in. In crypto, the closest analogue is price (or FDV) relative to real protocol revenue: the higher, the more hype in the price.
Current P/E ≈ 69. This is very expensive by historical standards (a year ago it was ~30–40). So most of $SOXX’s price growth is that “the market is willing to pay more for the same earnings”. Classic multiple expansion on hype.
Bulls’ argument (fair):
“Earnings are exploding! Micron revenue +345% YoY. If you calculate on forward earnings, P/E isn’t 69, it’s ~30 and that’s normal.”
Counter-argument (also strong):
This is a cyclical business at the peak. Memory (Micron et al.) is like mining in crypto. In a bull run margins are insane, then competitors ramp capacity → prices crash → profits evaporate. So “cheap forward P/E” is misleading because it’s calculated on peak earnings that may not hold. That’s the trap of cyclical instruments in a fund.
6. Where are we in the cycle
Chips live in boom-bust cycles, just like crypto lives through halvings and altseasons. The question isn’t “is this a good sector”, but “are we at the start of a bull or already at the highs?”
Signs we’re closer to the highs than the start:
Memory is leading, and memory usually outperforms at the end of the cycle (late signal).
Everything is rallying, even laggards: Intel +278%, Micron +304%, SanDisk +856% in six months (yes, those flash drives from the 2000s). When “even memecoins are pumping”, it’s obviously a late stage.
Price has detached from the average more than at any time since 2000 (dot-com bubble).

Record money flowing into the fund (see below).
Conclusion: the AI fundamental story is real and multi-year, but positioning and price are at late-cycle extremes. So the sector itself isn’t bad right now; the entry timing is.
7. Two camps right now
🐂 Bulls: “AI build-out is just accelerating, data centers are contracted 4–5 years ahead, profits are record-breaking. Any dip is a chance to deploy.”
🐻 Michael Burry (yes, that guy): On 30 June he opened a short against SOXX (plus against NVIDIA, Micron, AMAT, Tesla). His thesis is that the sector has detached from reality like in 2000 and is due for a ~30% correction.
What his short is: he bought put options, i.e. “insurance against a drop” / opened a short on perps with limited risk. If the sector falls, he profits; if not, he only risks the option premium. Caveat: Burry has been right before, but often early (opened shorts that kept rallying for months). So this is a “watch” signal.
8. The most interesting part: record FOMO into $SOXX
After SOXX fell −16% over two weeks (partly due to noise around Burry’s short), this happened:
$5.4B flowed into the fund in one day, 4x the previous record and the highest since 2001.
Plus another $1.2B into the 3x leveraged fund.
Over the year, total inflows + $13.3B.
So people aggressively bought the dip. Bulls will say “smart money is entering on the drop”. But there’s another reading, familiar from crypto: record FOMO right at the top is often a sign of overheating, not cheapness. When everyone deploys big at once, the fuel for further growth is, at least partially, burned.
