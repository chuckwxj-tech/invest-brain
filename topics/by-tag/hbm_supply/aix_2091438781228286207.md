# The $NVDA $7 billion maneuver to license Poolside's "Model Factory" and absorb its engineering talent is ultimately designed to drive enterprise demand for local, on-premise com...

## 原文

The $NVDA $7 billion maneuver to license Poolside's "Model Factory" and absorb its engineering talent is ultimately designed to drive enterprise demand for local, on-premise compute. By providing organizations with powerful, free Nemotron models,  Nvidia incentivizes the construction of localized data centers. As these localized clusters are built out to run open-weight AI securely, the entire physical hardware stack stands to benefit.

Here is a breakdown of the supply chain sectors and public companies positioned to gain from this widespread hardware deployment:

1. High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) and CXL Solutions
$MU $PENG $MXL $SKHY $MRVL
Running frontier-level open-weight models locally requires immense memory capacity to prevent inference bottlenecks.

The Gain: As enterprises deploy Nemotron, they require servers packed with the latest HBM. Memory manufacturers like SK Hynix and Micron Technology $MU  are direct beneficiaries. Furthermore, companies developing Compute Express Link CXL hardware will see increased adoption as data centers look to efficiently pool memory across multiple server nodes to handle heavy AI workloads.

2. Optical Interconnects and Silicon Photonics

To scale the training and synthetic data generation required by these advanced models, ultra-fast data transfer between GPU clusters is mandatory. Traditional copper cabling quickly becomes a severe supply chain bottleneck at these transfer speeds.

The Gain: Companies supplying optical transceivers, continuous-wave lasers, and co-packaged optics hardware are critical. Public entities such as Coherent $COHR, Lumentum $LITE, and contract manufacturers like Fabrinet $FN $FLEX $SANM and $CLS $ANET stand to gain significantly as the demand for electro-optic infrastructure scales alongside local GPU cluster deployments. 

3. Thermal Management and Liquid Cooling

The intense computational load of running specialized Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures on massive proprietary datasets pushes traditional air cooling past its physical limits.

The Gain: Providers of direct-to-chip liquid cooling racks and broader thermal infrastructure are essential for these deployments. Companies like Vertiv $VRT $ETN $GEV and Super Micro Computer $SMCI $DELL $HPE with its current value at $22.34—are positioned to capitalize on the massive server upgrade cycle required to manage this unprecedented heat dissipation.  

4. On-Site Power Generation and Microgrids

Widespread enterprise adoption of local AI hardware places enormous strain on existing municipal electrical grids. Corporate data centers cannot host continuous inference workflows without robust, reliable power.

The Gain: Public companies providing independent power infrastructure will see sustained demand. This includes providers of solid oxide hydrogen fuel cells, such as Bloom Energy $BE, as well as companies engineering natural gas turbines and microreactors to create resilient, on-site microgrids for these localized compute hubs.

Broader / Secondary BeneficiariesCloud and hosting providers $MSFT $GOOGL $AMZN #ORCL, and neo-cloud players: They still sell GPU instances even when customers run open models. Managed inference and fine-tuning services for Nemotron can grow.
Enterprise software platforms that already integrate or fine-tune Nemotron (examples from NVIDIA announcements include $NOW $PLTR $CDNS $CRWD,Siemens, and various coding/agent tools): Better base models improve their agentic features and reduce their own inference costs.
Memory and storage adjacent plays beyond pure HBM (e.g., other high-performance DRAM or SSD suppliers) if overall AI system shipments rise.

In short, the biggest near-term public-company beneficiaries sit in the physical supply chain that enables more NVIDIA GPUs and the data-center infrastructure that hosts them. The Poolside-enabled push on Nemotron is one more catalyst that can accelerate that demand, particularly for efficient, customizable open models suited to enterprise and agentic workloads.Important CaveatsGains are indirect and not guaranteed. The deal helps NVIDIA improve Nemotron; the magnitude of extra demand depends on how competitive the resulting models become versus DeepSeek, Kimi, Llama successors, and closed frontier models.

Open models can also increase competition for non-NVIDIA accelerators over time, though NVIDIA’s software optimizations (CUDA, TensorRT-LLM, NeMo) currently create a strong lock-in effect.
Capacity constraints (TSMC CoWoS, HBM supply, power availability) remain the real bottlenecks; companies that can expand capacity or relieve those constraints capture more of the upside.
Valuation and timing matter: many of these stocks already price in significant AI growth, so incremental benefit from one model family may be modest relative to the broader AI infrastructure supercycle.
