Updated comparison with NVIDIA H100 and AMD MI300X performance metrics

Here is the refined packaging and performance comparison, now including key peak theoretical metrics for the NVIDIA H100 (Hopper) and AMD Instinct MI300X.

Packaging Recap + Performance Metrics
| Aspect / Metric                  | NVIDIA H100 (SXM)                          | AMD Instinct MI300X                          | Notes |
|----------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|-------|
| Packaging                   | CoWoS-S (2.5D silicon interposer)             | SoIC (3D hybrid bonding) + CoWoS-S (3.5D hybrid) | MI300X is significantly more complex |
| Process                     | TSMC 4N (monolithic GH100 die)                | TSMC N5 (XCDs) + N6 (IODs)                      | — |
| HBM Memory                  | 80 GB HBM3                                    | 192 GB HBM3                                     | MI300X has 2.4× capacity |
| Memory Bandwidth           | 3.35 TB/s                                     | 5.3 TB/s                                        | MI300X ~1.6× higher |
| TDP                         | 700 W                                         | 750 W                                           | Similar power envelope |
| FP16 / BF16 Tensor (dense)  | ~989 TFLOPS                                   | 1,307 TFLOPS                                    | MI300X higher peak |
| FP8 (dense)                 | ~1,979 TFLOPS                                 | 2,615 TFLOPS                                    | MI300X higher peak |
| FP8 with sparsity           | 3,958 TFLOPS                                 | 5,230 TFLOPS                                   | Both support structured sparsity |
| FP64                        | ~34 TFLOPS (vector)                           | 81.7 TFLOPS (vector) / 163.4 matrix             | MI300X stronger in double precision |
| FP32                        | ~67 TFLOPS                                    | 163.4 TFLOPS                                    | MI300X higher |

Sources for peak figures are official AMD datasheets and consistent NVIDIA Hopper specifications (SXM5 variant). Actual sustained performance in real workloads depends heavily on software stack (CUDA vs ROCm), model characteristics, utilization, and system-level factors. Independent benchmarks often show NVIDIA maintaining advantages in optimized training and inference efficiency despite the raw peak differences in some precisions.

Key Takeaways with Metrics Context
Memory advantage for MI300X**: The combination of 192 GB HBM3 and 5.3 TB/s bandwidth (enabled by the large CoWoS-S interposer + 8 HBM stacks) is a major strength for large models and memory-bound inference. A single MI300X can often hold models that require multiple H100s.
Compute peaks**: MI300X shows higher theoretical TFLOPS in FP16/BF16, FP8, and especially FP64/FP32. H100 remains highly competitive (and frequently faster in practice) due to mature software, tensor core utilization, and ecosystem optimizations.
Packaging impact**:  
  H100’s relatively simpler CoWoS-S design with a monolithic die prioritizes high-volume manufacturability and strong HBM bandwidth.  
  MI300X’s SoIC 3D hybrid bonding of multiple chiplets + CoWoS-S enables higher aggregate compute density, larger memory capacity, and process-node mixing, at the cost of greater packaging complexity.
Relation to Foveros**: The vertical hybrid-bonding portion of MI300X (SoIC) is conceptually closest to Intel’s Foveros Direct, while both H100 and MI300X rely on TSMC CoWoS for the critical logic-to-HBM 2.5D integration.

Summary:  
NVIDIA H100 exemplifies efficient high-volume CoWoS-S packaging paired with strong software-optimized performance (80 GB / 3.35 TB/s / ~989 FP16 TFLOPS).  
AMD MI300X showcases aggressive 3.5D packaging (SoIC + CoWoS-S) that delivers superior memory capacity/bandwidth (192 GB / 5.3 TB/s) and higher peak TFLOPS in several formats, at higher packaging complexity.  

Both highlight how advanced 2.5D (CoWoS) and 3D (SoIC / Foveros-style) technologies are combined to push AI accelerator capabilities. Real-world choice often hinges on workload type, software ecosystem, total cost of ownership, and system integration rather than peak specs alone.
