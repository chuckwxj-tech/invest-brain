💾 Memory Market Daily: 
Profit Up 557%—Still Not Good Enough | July 29, 2026
（📍Data From https://t.co/rtKhA53pFF Daily Report）

The memory supercycle is producing record profits, surging prices and aggressive capacity expansion.
Unfortunately, investors priced in something even better than record-breaking.

Today’s Key Developments

🔥 SK Hynix Rejects the AI Slowdown Narrative
• SK Hynix said AI-related memory demand should remain resilient beyond 2027, pushing back against concerns that hyperscalers are preparing to reduce investment.
• The company is pursuing roughly ten long-term supply agreements—typically lasting five years—to improve demand visibility and reduce pricing volatility.
• Customers apparently want guaranteed supply for years. The market still panicked over one quarterly miss.

📊 Operating Profit Surges 557%—and Disappoints
• Second-quarter revenue climbed 257% YoY to KRW 79.3 trillion, below the market forecast of approximately KRW 84 trillion.
• Operating profit jumped 557% to a record KRW 60.5 trillion, but missed the KRW 64 trillion consensus estimate.
• Net profit rose more than thirteenfold to KRW 93.9 trillion, partly supported by gains from the Kioxia stake sale.

A 557% profit increase used to be called extraordinary. In the AI market, it is now called “three and a half trillion won short.” Reuters
🚀 HBM4 Production to Accelerate
• SK Hynix plans to expand HBM4 production during the second half of 2026.
• Shipment delays limited the company’s ability to capture stronger pricing during the latest quarter.
• The production ramp will be critical as next-generation AI accelerators demand greater bandwidth and energy efficiency.
🧩 Customized DRAM Gains Momentum
• Interest in integrating logic and memory through advanced 3D IC packaging continues to increase.
• Closer integration could reduce data-transfer bottlenecks while improving performance and power efficiency.
• The trend is pushing DRAM away from standardized commodity products and toward customized, customer-specific architectures.
Memory used to be the interchangeable part of the computer. AI is turning it into a bespoke luxury component.

💽 AI Storage Demand Lifts Seagate
• Seagate reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $3.6 billion, up 49% from a year earlier and above expectations.
• Adjusted earnings reached $5.71 per share, versus $2.59 a year earlier and the $5.10 consensus estimate.
• The company expects fiscal first-quarter 2027 revenue of approximately $4.1 billion and adjusted earnings of $7.30 per share.
AI may live in the cloud, but the cloud still needs an industrial quantity of hard drives. Seagate
📅 Customers Extend Plans Beyond 2029
• Seagate said customers have not materially reduced capital-expenditure plans.
• Several major customers are extending storage-capacity planning beyond 2029 as AI generates increasingly large data volumes.
• Longer planning horizons provide stronger visibility for nearline HDD demand—but also raise the stakes if AI monetization eventually disappoints.
📱 Smartphone Memory Prices Surge
• Industry estimates indicate that some smartphone memory prices increased by more than 80% quarter-over-quarter during Q2.
• AI infrastructure demand has redirected production toward HBM and server memory, restricting supply for smartphones and other consumer electronics.
• Global smartphone shipments consequently fell to their lowest second-quarter level in 13 years, according to Counterpoint Research. Counterpoint Research

Smartphones are not becoming more expensive because they suddenly became better. Their memory simply lost a bidding war against AI data centers.

⚙️ ScaleFlux Launches Gen6 and CXL Controllers
• ScaleFlux introduced the FC6116 PCIe Gen6 NVMe SSD controller, targeting AI, hyperscale and enterprise-storage workloads.
• The controller supports throughput of up to 28 GB/s and seven million IOPS.
• Its new MC600 CXL 3.2 Type 3 controller supports as much as 2TB of DDR5 memory, with DDR4 compatibility providing a more flexible migration path.
• Customer sampling is scheduled to begin in Q4 2026.
📌 Key Takeaways
• AI memory demand remains strong, but market expectations have become dangerously demanding.
• HBM4 execution will determine whether SK Hynix can maintain its technology and pricing advantage.
• DRAM is becoming increasingly customized through logic-memory integration.
• AI storage demand benefits both high-performance flash and mass-capacity HDDs.
• Consumer-electronics manufacturers are absorbing the cost of capacity being redirected toward AI infrastructure.
• PCIe Gen6 and CXL are becoming essential components of next-generation AI systems.
👀 What Investors Should Watch

• SK Hynix’s HBM4 production yield and shipment schedule.
• The terms of long-term HBM supply agreements.
• Hyperscaler capital-expenditure guidance through 2027–2029.
• Seagate’s nearline HDD pricing and shipment volumes.
• Smartphone demand destruction caused by rising memory costs.
• Adoption of PCIe Gen6 SSDs and CXL memory expansion.
The memory market is booming. The problem is that stocks are no longer priced for a boom—they are priced for perfection, delivered early and with upgraded guidance.
