$KEYS KEY READ-THROUGHS FROM KEYSIGHT TECHNOLOGIES Q3 FY2026 EARNINGS CALL

Keysight’s Q3 FY2026 earnings call provides unusually strong cross-market evidence because the company participates early in customer design cycles, during product qualification, and increasingly through production deployment. The most important conclusion is that AI infrastructure demand has progressed beyond experimental R&D into simultaneous R&D and volume-manufacturing activity across optical networking, switching, silicon photonics, advanced semiconductors, high-bandwidth memory, printed circuit boards, connectors, and passive components. The call also confirms that defense modernization, resilient navigation, autonomous platforms, and software-defined vehicle architectures remain durable investment cycles. The highest-conviction negative implications are that the AI supply chain is economically more concentrated around a small number of hyperscalers than supplier customer lists imply, elevated hyperscaler capital intensity remains a near-term free-cash-flow headwind, specialized component constraints are limiting revenue conversion, and Keysight’s expanding platform breadth creates relative share pressure for narrower test-and-measurement competitors. Orders increased 56%, core orders increased 52%, Commercial Communications revenue increased 56% to more than $1B, Wireline orders more than doubled, and management stated that demand is “far exceeding our ability to supply.” These data collectively support continued upward estimate pressure across selected AI infrastructure and semiconductor-capital-equipment beneficiaries, while increasing the importance of supply execution, customer concentration, and valuation discipline.

AI DATA CENTER NETWORKING, OPTICAL CONNECTIVITY AND ELECTRONIC COMPONENTS

AI OPTICAL DEMAND HAS MOVED FROM DESIGN ACTIVITY INTO VOLUME PRODUCTION (READ-THROUGH 1)

Affected companies and directional impact: Coherent Corp. (COHR: United States) — positive, high magnitude; Lumentum Holdings (LITE: United States) — positive, high magnitude; Fabrinet (FN: United States) — positive, high magnitude; Celestica Inc. (CLS: Canada) — positive, moderate-to-high magnitude; Arista Networks (ANET: United States) — positive, moderate-to-high magnitude; Flex Ltd. (FLEX: United States) — positive, moderate magnitude; Jabil Inc. (JBL: United States) — positive, moderate magnitude.

Supporting call evidence: Wireline orders more than doubled year over year and reached a record. Commercial Communications generated its 1st $1B quarter, with revenue increasing 56%. Wireline revenue exceeded Wireless revenue for the 1st time and was also larger year to date. Management stated that customers are “ramping production of 1.6 terabit as we speak right now” and that demand is “very, very strong.” The Wireline revenue mix has moved from approximately 80% R&D and 20% manufacturing historically to approximately 2/3 R&D and 1/3 manufacturing over the most recent quarters. Management also stated that customers are already engaging on 3.2T technology while 800G remains in deployment and 1.6T is scaling.

The most important implication is that the optical cycle is no longer dependent solely on pre-production laboratory purchases. R&D demand remains strong, while manufacturing-related demand is becoming a larger incremental contributor. This simultaneous growth in R&D and production is materially more constructive than a simple transition from one phase to another because it indicates that new architectures are entering volume production while subsequent generations are already being designed.

Coherent and Lumentum should benefit through increasing demand for high-speed lasers, optical components, transceiver subassemblies, and silicon-photonics-related products. Fabrinet is among the clearest manufacturing read-throughs because increasing 800G and 1.6T production volumes directly increase outsourced optical manufacturing, assembly, testing, and packaging requirements. Celestica benefits through AI switching, networking systems, and higher-complexity manufacturing programs. Arista benefits because rising transceiver production and switch-validation activity indicate that AI clusters are progressing from planned capacity into deployed networking infrastructure.

The strongest near-term trading implication is positive estimate risk for companies with existing 1.6T production exposure rather than companies whose investment cases depend primarily on future 3.2T products. Keysight’s commentary supports an active volume ramp now, not merely a future roadmap. Optical-component, switch, and manufacturing suppliers should therefore continue to report stronger AI-related mix, backlog, and customer engagement over the next several quarters, subject to supply availability.

The longer-duration implication is that optical transitions are compressing. 800G, 1.6T, and 3.2T are overlapping rather than following widely separated adoption curves. This should reduce the historical air pockets between technology generations and increase the duration of engineering and production spending. The beneficiaries will be companies capable of supporting multiple generations simultaneously, maintaining acceptable yields at higher speeds, and scaling manufacturing without sacrificing quality.

The geographic read-through is also constructive for Southeast Asian electronics manufacturing. Keysight stated that its Southeast Asia business more than doubled and was the fastest-growing region for both the quarter and the year. The commentary supports continued expansion of AI and electronics manufacturing capacity in Thailand, Malaysia, Vietnam, Singapore, and adjacent markets. Fabrinet has particularly direct exposure to this manufacturing geography, while Flex and Jabil should benefit from broader customer efforts to diversify production capacity and establish additional regional manufacturing flexibility.

HETEROGENEOUS AI SYSTEMS ARE CREATING A STRUCTURAL INCREASE IN CONNECTIVITY-SILICON CONTENT (READ-THROUGH 2)

Affected companies and directional impact: Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: United States) — positive, high magnitude; Marvell Technology Inc. (MRVL: United States) — positive, high magnitude; Credo Technology Group Holding Ltd. (CRDO: United States) — positive, high magnitude; Astera Labs Inc. (ALAB: United States) — positive, high magnitude; NVIDIA Corp. (NVDA: United States) — positive, moderate-to-high magnitude.

Supporting call evidence: Management stated that AI architectures increasingly include “GPUs, CPUs, DPUs, NICs” and a growing number of protocols across every layer of the stack. A compute or switch tray has moved “from tens to hundreds of high-speed pinouts.” Customers are shifting from monolithic chips to chiplet architectures, requiring interoperability testing and system-level stress emulation. Management also stated that higher data rates and lower latency are reducing design margins and that AI networks must operate with extremely low packet loss.

This is a direct positive read-through for Ethernet switch silicon, optical DSPs, SerDes, retimers, active electrical cables, PCIe and CXL switches, DPUs, NICs, and signal-conditioning products. The number of compute devices per rack is increasing, but the more important economic driver is that each compute device requires a greater number of higher-speed connections. Connectivity content can therefore grow faster than accelerator units or rack counts.

Broadcom benefits through high-speed Ethernet switch silicon, custom AI silicon connectivity, SerDes intellectual property, and optical-networking components. Marvell benefits through optical DSPs, data-center interconnect silicon, switching products, and custom silicon. Credo is particularly exposed to the increased use of high-speed SerDes and active electrical cables as rack density and connection counts rise. Astera Labs benefits through PCIe and CXL connectivity, retimers, and switches required to connect increasingly heterogeneous compute and memory systems. NVIDIA benefits through the combined growth of accelerators, Spectrum-X networking, DPUs, and integrated AI-cluster architecture.

The near-term trading implication is that AI infrastructure revenue should increasingly broaden from accelerator silicon into the connectivity layer. Strong GPU demand alone is insufficient to build functioning clusters; switches, NICs, retimers, DSPs, cables, and network software must scale concurrently. Keysight’s record demand for bit-level, protocol-level, and workload-level testing indicates that customers are actively developing and deploying these systems rather than merely reserving future capacity.

The longer-duration implication is that heterogeneous system architecture increases connectivity content structurally. The move toward chiplets, multiple processor types, disaggregated memory, customized accelerators, and specialized networking devices increases the number of interfaces that must be connected and validated. This architecture favors companies with differentiated high-speed analog, SerDes, protocol, and packaging capabilities. The strongest beneficiaries are not necessarily the companies shipping the largest number of chips, but those capturing more content at every connection point.

The negative relative implication is for networking and semiconductor vendors concentrated in lower-speed, legacy enterprise, campus, or traditional telecom applications. Keysight’s demand strength is centered on lossless, high-speed AI networking and heterogeneous compute, not on a broad recovery in all networking categories. Market share and valuation should continue to shift toward companies with direct exposure to high-speed data-center connectivity.

HIGH-SPEED INTERCONNECT, PCB AND PASSIVE-COMPONENT CONTENT IS BEING PREMIUMIZED (READ-THROUGH 3)

Affected companies and directional impact: Amphenol Corp. (APH: United States) — positive, high magnitude; TTM Technologies Inc. (TTMI: United States) — positive, moderate-to-high magnitude; Unimicron Technology Corp. (3037: Taiwan) — positive, moderate-to-high magnitude; Murata Manufacturing Co., Ltd. (6981: Japan) — positive, moderate magnitude; TDK Corp. (6762: Japan) — positive, moderate magnitude.

Supporting call evidence: Keysight stated that AI-related innovation is increasing demand for high-performance multilayer printed circuit boards and capacitors. Management cited “higher frequencies, tighter tolerances, and greater GPU and CPU density” as drivers of increasing production complexity and quality requirements. The company’s precision-measurement solutions are being adopted to qualify these components in production. Management also highlighted the increase from tens to hundreds of high-speed pinouts in current compute and switching systems.

The read-through is positive for premium connectors, high-speed cables, advanced printed circuit boards, low-loss materials, capacitors, and related components. AI infrastructure does not merely require more components; it requires components capable of meeting tighter electrical, thermal, and mechanical specifications. This distinction favors suppliers with strong engineering capabilities, manufacturing yields, customer qualification histories, and the capacity to invest ahead of technology transitions.

Amphenol is the clearest public beneficiary because higher pin counts, faster data rates, and denser racks increase connector, cable, and high-speed interconnect content. TTM Technologies and Unimicron should benefit from increasing layer counts, more complex routing, tighter tolerances, and premium materials in AI servers, accelerators, and networking equipment. Murata and TDK should benefit through higher capacitor and passive-component content, particularly where power integrity, signal quality, and miniaturization requirements are increasing.

The near-term trading catalyst is continued mix improvement toward high-performance AI products. Premium products typically carry higher average selling prices and better margins than standard industrial or consumer components. The call supports the view that qualification and production activity are rising now, which should translate into improving revenue mix for suppliers already qualified on leading AI platforms.

The longer-duration shift is an increase in supplier qualification barriers. At lower speeds and densities, component substitution is relatively easier. At higher frequencies, a small deviation in a connector, PCB trace, capacitor, or backplane can impair system performance or manufacturing yield. Customers are therefore more likely to consolidate spending with proven suppliers, creating share gains and pricing durability for market leaders.

The corresponding negative read-through applies to lower-end or underinvested component suppliers. Rising test requirements, lower tolerances for defects, and greater capital intensity may reduce their ability to compete for premium AI programs. The result should be an increasing separation between high-end component companies and commodity suppliers, even if aggregate industry volumes remain strong.

HYPERSCALER SPENDING IS STRONG, BUT SUPPLIER DIVERSIFICATION IS LESS REAL THAN REPORTED CUSTOMER MIX SUGGESTS (READ-THROUGH 4)

Affected companies and directional impact: Arista Networks (ANET: United States), Broadcom (AVGO: United States), Marvell Technology (MRVL: United States), Credo Technology Group (CRDO: United States), Coherent Corp. (COHR: United States), Lumentum Holdings (LITE: United States), Fabrinet (FN: United States), and Celestica (CLS: Canada) — positive, high magnitude near term but exposed to high-magnitude downside if hyperscaler capital spending decelerates; Microsoft Corp. (MSFT: United States), https://t.co/SpqvHNUxpK Inc. (AMZN: United States), Alphabet Inc. (GOOGL: United States), Meta Platforms Inc. (META: United States), and Oracle Corp. (ORCL: United States) — negative, moderate magnitude for near-term free cash flow and depreciation, with positive longer-duration strategic implications.

Supporting call evidence: Keysight stated that hyperscalers account for only approximately 10% of direct revenue but have “a lot of downstream effect and influence across the ecosystem.” Management emphasized the “tremendous capital that they’re deploying,” their development of custom silicon, and their influence over the broader supply chain. Keysight’s engagements with hyperscalers are deepening, and the company stated that customers are planning further ahead.

The positive near-term read-through is that hyperscaler AI capital expenditure remains strong and is propagating through silicon designers, optical suppliers, switch vendors, contract manufacturers, foundries, and test-equipment companies. This supports continued estimate revisions across AI infrastructure suppliers and reduces the probability of an immediate broad capex pause.

The more important risk implication is that direct customer concentration metrics materially understate economic concentration. A supplier may report hundreds or thousands of customers, but the end-demand funding can still originate from 4 or 5 hyperscalers. An optical component may be purchased by a transceiver manufacturer, assembled by a contract manufacturer, integrated into a switch, and ultimately deployed by a hyperscaler. Each stage appears to have a different customer, but the economic exposure remains linked to the same hyperscaler capital budget.

This creates high downside correlation across Arista, Broadcom, Marvell, Credo, Coherent, Lumentum, Fabrinet, Celestica, and adjacent suppliers if hyperscaler capex growth slows. Supplier diversification should therefore be evaluated on an end-demand basis rather than by direct customer count. The call’s comment that the largest customers are growing at high-double-digit rates is positive today but reinforces the importance of monitoring hyperscaler capex revisions.

For hyperscaler equities, the read-through is moderately negative for near-term free cash flow, capital efficiency, and depreciation. The scale of capital deployment required to support AI racks, optical networking, custom silicon, and data-center infrastructure will continue to consume cash before the full revenue opportunity is realized. Higher depreciation will also flow through operating margins over subsequent periods.

The longer-duration effect on hyperscalers is potentially positive because infrastructure capacity, custom silicon, and network control can create competitive advantages in cloud and AI services. The central portfolio issue is timing. Infrastructure suppliers monetize the buildout earlier, while hyperscalers bear the capital burden before AI utilization and monetization fully mature. Near-term earnings sensitivity therefore favors selected suppliers, while hyperscaler performance will depend increasingly on evidence that AI revenue and customer demand are scaling fast enough to earn an acceptable return on capital.

SPECIALIZED COMPONENT CONSTRAINTS WILL CAP REVENUE CONVERSION DESPITE STRONG ORDERS (READ-THROUGH 5)

Affected companies and directional impact: Keysight Technologies Inc. (KEYS: United States) — negative, moderate magnitude near term; Arista Networks (ANET: United States) — negative, moderate magnitude near term; Ciena Corp. (CIEN: United States) — negative, moderate magnitude near term; Coherent Corp. (COHR: United States) — negative, moderate magnitude near term; Lumentum Holdings (LITE: United States) — negative, moderate magnitude near term; VIAVI Solutions Inc. (VIAV: United States) — negative, moderate magnitude near term.

Supporting call evidence: Management stated that demand is “far exceeding our ability to supply” and later clarified that “Demand is not the limiter.” The supply environment is less flexible than it was approximately 1 year ago. Constraints have shifted from internal new-product ramp capacity toward incoming parts that are being sought by “numerous players” from a similar set of component suppliers. Management expects supply to remain “a governor of our ability to convert demand into revenue for the next several quarters.” Keysight is extending planning beyond 18 months, qualifying 2nd sources, redesigning selected products, and considering longer-term supply agreements.

The negative read-through is not an end-demand warning. It is a revenue-timing and execution risk. Strong orders and expanding backlog will not automatically produce proportional near-term revenue if specialized components remain unavailable. Hardware companies may therefore report strong bookings and book-to-bill while providing more conservative revenue guidance than order momentum would otherwise imply.

The transmission mechanism operates through delayed shipments, elevated lead times, increased working capital, product redesign expense, expedited freight, supplier prepayments, and potential gross-margin pressure. The need to qualify 2nd sources can require engineering resources and customer requalification. Longer delivery times can also give customers incentives to qualify alternative suppliers.

The near-term trading catalyst is shipment execution rather than demand creation. Investors should place greater weight on scheduled backlog, component availability, lead-time commentary, and revenue guidance than on headline order growth alone. Companies that demonstrate faster backlog conversion should receive a relative premium, while companies that continue to accumulate backlog without improving shipments may face estimate risk.

The longer-duration implication is potentially positive if supply constraints are resolved. Existing backlog can become deferred revenue, allowing companies to sustain growth even if order growth later normalizes. However, this upside should not be assumed until component availability improves. The call supports a strong demand environment but also establishes that the pace of earnings realization is constrained by operational factors outside the customer’s willingness to spend.

SEMICONDUCTOR CAPITAL EQUIPMENT, MEMORY AND PRODUCTION TEST

ADVANCED-NODE, HBM AND SILICON-PHOTONICS CAPITAL SPENDING REMAINS BROAD AND ACTIVE (READ-THROUGH 6)

Affected companies and directional impact: ASML Holding N.V. (ASML: Netherlands) — positive, high magnitude; Applied Materials Inc. (AMAT: United States) — positive, high magnitude; Lam Research Corp. (LRCX: United States) — positive, high magnitude; KLA Corp. (KLAC: United States) — positive, high magnitude; Advantest Corp. (6857: Japan) — positive, high magnitude; Teradyne Inc. (TER: United States) — positive, moderate-to-high magnitude; FormFactor Inc. (FORM: United States) — positive, moderate-to-high magnitude; Onto Innovation Inc. (ONTO: United States) — positive, moderate-to-high magnitude; Micron Technology Inc. (MU: United States), SK hynix Inc. (000660: South Korea), and Samsung Electronics Co., Ltd. (005930: South Korea) — positive, moderate magnitude near term for volume and demand validation, with a longer-duration supply-risk offset.

Supporting call evidence: Keysight’s semiconductor business delivered another record quarter. Management cited “ongoing capacity expansion for advanced nodes, high bandwidth memory, and silicon photonics.” Semiconductor R&D demand remained healthy, and management stated that forecasts for wafer-fabrication equipment and capital spending “continue to expand.” Commercial production of silicon photonics is accelerating across leading foundries and integrated device manufacturers.

The cleanest positive read-through is for semiconductor-capital-equipment, metrology, inspection, and test suppliers. Keysight’s demand is generated when semiconductor customers design, qualify, install, and ramp new production capacity. Record demand for Keysight’s semiconductor solutions therefore corroborates active capital deployment rather than simply management intentions or long-range spending plans.

ASML benefits because advanced-node capacity expansion requires additional leading-edge lithography. Applied Materials and Lam Research benefit through increasing process-step intensity in advanced logic, memory, HBM, and advanced packaging. KLA and Onto Innovation benefit because shrinking design rules, complex packaging, silicon photonics, and HBM stacking require more inspection, process control, and metrology.

Advantest, Teradyne, FormFactor, and Onto Innovation should benefit from rising test intensity across accelerators, HBM, chiplets, advanced packaging, and optical devices. Management’s observation that customers can no longer guarantee performance solely through design is particularly important for production-test suppliers. More complex devices require longer test times, more test insertions, higher-performance probe cards, thermal management, and more sophisticated system-level testing.

The near-term trading implication is continued positive order and backlog risk for semiconductor-equipment and test companies with exposure to advanced logic, HBM, packaging, and silicon photonics. The call does not support a narrow semiconductor recovery confined to 1 product category. It indicates simultaneous investment across multiple AI-enabling technologies.

The longer-duration implication is that test and metrology intensity should increase faster than wafer volume. Chiplets, stacked memory, optical interfaces, and advanced packaging introduce more potential failure points and more expensive downstream consequences if defects escape early testing. Customers should therefore increase spending on inspection and testing to protect yield and avoid scrapping high-value packaged devices.

The read-through is less uniformly positive for HBM manufacturers than for equipment suppliers. Capacity expansion validates strong current demand and supports near-term shipment growth for Micron, SK hynix, and Samsung. However, additional capacity also accelerates the future supply response. If AI demand growth moderates after current capacity is installed, HBM pricing and utilization could normalize. The highest-conviction positive exposure is therefore the equipment and test layer, which monetizes the capital buildout regardless of which memory producer ultimately gains share.
