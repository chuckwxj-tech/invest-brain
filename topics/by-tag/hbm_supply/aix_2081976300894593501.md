What a interesting article by @damnang2 

Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron all came under pressure following CXMT’s listing.

he’s latest piece argues that CXMT’s pricing advantage and manufacturing efficiency remain far less threatening than the headlines suggest, while meaningful new supply funded by the IPO is still years away.

I also do not think that CXMT itself is the largest risk facing the three major memory players.

I do belive the more important question sits one layer deeper:

Will AI accelerators continue to consume HBM in the same way they do today??

The more expensive HBM becomes, the stronger the incentive to design around it.

You know that 
1. $QCOM  is trying to shorten the distance between memory and compute by stacking LPDDR above the accelerator, HBC. 
2. $NVDA is reducing the number of bits that must be read for each weight in Rubin.
3. CXL and external memory tiers are attacking the capacity side of the bottleneck from another direction.

Whether CXMT eventually pressures conventional DRAM pricing matters. Over the longer term, however, I think the more important issue is how these architectural changes reshape the role of HBM—and the earnings mix of Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron.

My latest piece examines these three routes around the memory wall, and where optical connectivity moves when the architecture changes.
