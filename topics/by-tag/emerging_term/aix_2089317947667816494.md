## 原文

$COHR makes more sense as an optical stack than as a transceiver vendor.

An ai network needs several photonic layers.

Lasers create the light. modulators encode data onto it. transceivers move that data between racks. optical circuit switches redirect the light without converting it back to electrical.

Coherent sits across all four. inp lasers, emls, vcsels, silicon photonics, 800g shipping and 1.6t ramping, cpo, and ocs.

The ocs part is the one people underrate.

Traditional switching converts optical to electrical, processes packets, converts back. an ocs just changes the physical light path. fewer conversions, less power, lower latency on long lived gpu links.

And it is not a slide. ocs revenue grew sequentially in fq4 and management doubled the addressable opportunity to $4b+ since ofc. coherent runs liquid crystal, lumentum runs mems.

✅ Nvidia put $2b of equity in plus a multibillion dollar purchase commitment and capacity rights. that is a supply chain lock, not a press release.

✅ Datacenter and comms revenue hit $5.28b in fy26, up 40%. 79% of the company in q4.

✅ Vertical integration from substrate to system is hard to replicate on a two year timeline.

❌ Nvidia signed the exact same $2b deal with lumentum on the same day. dual sourced on purpose. this is validation of the category, not of $COHR specifically.

❌ Broadcom controls switch silicon and system architecture. owning the photons does not mean owning the network design.

❌ Two customers were each over 10% of fy26 revenue. same as fy25. those customers control qualification, volume and price.

❌ Indium phosphide output is the ceiling, not demand. supply constrained across basically every datacenter line.

That is the trade.

The wider the stack gets, the more $COHR is worth inside a cluster. but the biggest buyer is also funding the closest competitor, and two customers still hold the pen on pricing.
