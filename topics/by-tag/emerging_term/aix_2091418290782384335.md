## 原文

@TheValueist The W→Mo transition in advanced NAND is real, but the investment implication is more nuanced than “more Mo = miners win.”

As wordlines shrink, tungsten’s nanoscale resistance and WF6-related fluorine problems become harder to manage. Barrierless Mo improves conductivity, fill and reliability; Lam reports >50% resistance improvement, and Micron has already confirmed Mo in mass production.

But the commodity math is much smaller. Even assuming full NAND penetration, only ~14–100 tonnes of Mo would remain in finished wafers versus ~305,000 tonnes of annual global demand.

So the scarcer asset may not be molybdenum itself, but the qualified semiconductor process around it: precursor purity and delivery, deposition, etch/clean, CMP and years of customer qualification.

That suggests the W→Mo transition could create more direct value for semiconductor equipment and electronic-material suppliers than for miners alone.
