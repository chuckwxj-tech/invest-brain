# $AAOI designs and manufactures the lasers, optical components and high-speed transceivers that move data through hyperscale data centers. Its current growth cycle is being drive...

## 原文

$AAOI designs and manufactures the lasers, optical components and high-speed transceivers that move data through hyperscale data centers.

Its current growth cycle is being driven by the transition from 400G connectivity to 800G and eventually 1.6-terabit optics. These are not optional upgrades: as AI models grow and GPU clusters become larger, every generation of compute requires faster connections between servers, switches and data-center halls.

$AAOI generated second-quarter 2026 revenue of $191.9 million, an increase of 86% year over year and its fifth consecutive quarterly revenue record. Data-center revenue reached $107.7 million, up 140%, while 800G product volumes more than doubled sequentially. Management expects third-quarter revenue of between $255 million and $290 million and forecasts that customer demand for 800G and 1.6T products will continue to exceed production capacity through approximately mid-2027.

This is important because $AAOI is no longer selling investors a distant technology roadmap. It has received more than $124 million of 800G orders from one major hyperscale customer since mid-March and a separate volume order worth more than $200 million for 1.6T transceivers from a long-standing hyperscale customer. The company has not identified those customers publicly, so it would be speculative to attach specific names to either order. What matters is that 1.6T has progressed from development and qualification into a real volume order, with shipments expected to begin during the second half of 2026.

The named customer relationships are equally compelling. $MSFT represented 28.8% of AAOI’s 2025 revenue and has entered into multi-year agreements covering laser manufacturing, product design and assembly services. Oracle was also a significant data-center customer in 2024. These relationships suggest that $AAOI is not simply selling interchangeable components through distributors; it is becoming integrated into the product development and supply chains of major cloud operators.

$AMZN provides the most interesting strategic alignment. In March 2025, $AAOI issued an Amazon affiliate warrants to purchase as many as 7.95 million shares at approximately $23.70 per share. About 1.32 million warrants vested immediately; the remainder can vest based on purchases made by Amazon and its affiliates, up to $4 billion over ten years.

@AppliedOpto competitive advantage may be its vertical integration. Unlike suppliers that purchase most of their optical components from third parties, $AAOI develops lasers, light engines, components and complete transceiver modules. The company says its U.S. laser fabrication is entirely in-house, supported by approximately 600 engineers across the United States and Asia. Its internally developed automation platform is intended to reduce labor requirements, improve manufacturing yields and allow similar production systems to be deployed in different geographic locations.

That capability matters because hyperscalers increasingly want alternative supply chains outside China. $AAOI is positioning itself as one of the few high-volume American manufacturers of advanced optical transceivers. The company’s expansion in Texas should eventually provide capacity for as many as 700,000 units of 800G and 1.6T transceivers per month in the Houston area, while laser-fabrication capacity is expected to expand by approximately 350% by the end of 2027. Current capacity is considerably lower, management described it as approaching 200,000 units per month in August, but expects to reach approximately 650,000 monthly units by the end of 2026.

The company also has a second growth engine that receives less attention. $AAOI supplies DOCSIS 4.0 equipment used to upgrade cable broadband networks. Through its exclusive distribution relationship with Digicomm, $AAOI is the primary vendor supporting Mediacom’s upgrade of approximately one million homes by the end of 2026. This business represented 43% of first-half revenue and can partially diversify $AAOI away from the AI data-center cycle.

If hyperscaler demand continues to exceed supply, Amazon-linked purchases expand, 1.6T becomes a meaningful revenue contributor and AAOI’s automated U.S. manufacturing delivers the expected economics, the company could emerge as one of the most strategically important optical suppliers in the American AI ecosystem.

Its roadmap extending into 3.2T transceivers, external laser sources and co-packaged optics also gives the company a path beyond the current 800G cycle.
