## 原文

$MU $SKHY Most will fail to see how bullish this quote actually is.

“The era of competing on price per I/O rather than price per GB is coming. Performance and power efficiency will be the core competitiveness of next-generation memory.” -Jeremy W. Micron SVP

Price per GB is how you sell a commodity.
Price per I/O is how you sell infrastructure.

The shift is actually reframing the entire memory trade for the next decade.

You're getting paid for bandwidth, latency under load, power envelope, and thermal behavior inside a specific system. Think HBM stacks feeding a GPU, LPDDR next to an AI accelerator, CXL modules extending memory across a fabric.

This is the Memory Wall in dollar terms. Multi million dollar GPU and TPU clusters sit idle waiting for data. Faster, more efficient memory directly lifts the utilization and ROI of that silicon, which is why hyperscalers are willing to pay upfront premiums for next-gen parts. Memory becomes a per query cost line, not a BOM line.

So what gets negotiated is the spec sheet (GB/s per watt, effective bandwidth, tail latency), not cents per bit. 

Increasing I/O speeds means enterprise customers get more compute performance out of their existing silicon infrastructure, making next-gen memory a high-ROI purchasing decision.

Better Total Cost of Ownership per token, not lower cost per bit.
