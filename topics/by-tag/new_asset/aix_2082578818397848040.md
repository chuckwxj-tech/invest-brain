$APH KEY READ-THROUGHS FROM AMPHENOL Q2 2026 EARNINGS CALL

Amphenol’s Q2 2026 call provides a materially positive cross-check on global AI infrastructure investment, data-center connectivity, electrical power distribution, semiconductor-factory investment, commercial aerospace production, defense electronics, and selected industrial-automation markets. The most important conclusion is not simply that AI demand remains strong, but that spending is becoming more connectivity-intensive across high-speed copper, optical fiber, power delivery, sensing, and cooling. IT datacom revenue increased 63% organically and 22% sequentially, with “virtually all” of the sequential increase attributed to AI-related products, while Q3 IT datacom revenue is expected to rise another mid-teens percentage sequentially. CommScope’s IT datacom business approximately doubled year over year, and its integration into Amphenol is producing substantially more revenue and earnings accretion than initially expected. The principal negative read-through is a sharp divergence between data-center connectivity and traditional communications infrastructure: communications-network revenue declined 6% organically and is expected to fall by a mid-teens percentage sequentially in Q3. The call therefore supports a pronounced long data-center connectivity, optics, power and cooling thesis, while remaining negative for carrier-exposed telecom equipment and selected legacy network infrastructure suppliers. Outside TMT, the call also provides constructive confirmation of semiconductor-factory investment, industrial electrification, aircraft-production improvement, and sustained defense modernization.

AI ACCELERATOR AND NETWORKING SHIPMENTS REMAIN IN ACCELERATION (READ-THROUGH 1)

Affected companies: NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA: US); Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: US); Arista Networks, Inc. (ANET: US); Celestica Inc. (CLS: Canada).

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive. High-magnitude near-term trading read-through at the AI infrastructure sector level and medium-to-high magnitude for each individual company.

Call evidence: IT datacom represented 43% of Amphenol’s Q2 revenue and increased 89% reported, 63% organically, and 22% sequentially. Management stated that “virtually all of this growth was driven by sales of AI-related products.” Q3 IT datacom revenue is expected to increase by a mid-teens percentage sequentially. Total company orders reached $10.732B, increased 94% year over year and 14% sequentially, and produced a 1.23:1 book-to-bill ratio. Organic orders increased 63%.

Transmission mechanism: Amphenol’s high-speed data, optical and power products are incorporated into accelerator trays, switches, servers, racks, backplanes, cable assemblies and data-center power architectures. Orders for these products are placed relatively close to actual platform production and data-center deployment schedules. The strength therefore represents a more advanced supply-chain signal than early semiconductor design wins or cloud-capital-expenditure announcements.

Based on the disclosed revenue mix, Q2 IT datacom revenue was approximately $3.8B. A mid-teens Q3 increase would add approximately $550M-$600M of revenue. Amphenol’s total Q3 revenue midpoint is approximately $590M above Q2, meaning that IT datacom is likely to contribute essentially all consolidated sequential growth before offsets and growth contributions from other markets. This indicates that the AI infrastructure buildout is not merely sustaining an elevated run rate; it is still accelerating in absolute dollars.

NVIDIA benefits through continued accelerator-system deployment. Broadcom benefits through switching silicon, networking platforms and custom AI accelerators. Arista benefits through increased deployment of high-bandwidth Ethernet fabrics. Celestica benefits through manufacturing, integration and hardware-platform exposure across AI compute and networking systems. The signal is particularly supportive of system-level revenue and unit growth because Amphenol reported both strong shipments and a book-to-bill ratio substantially above 1.0.

Near-term trading catalyst: The call supports upward revisions to Q3 and H2 AI server, accelerator and networking shipment assumptions. The absence of broad component bottlenecks also reduces the risk that strong orders cannot be converted into revenue.

Long-duration fundamental shift: AI systems are requiring structurally greater interconnect, networking and power content per deployed unit. The resulting revenue opportunity can grow faster than accelerator-unit shipments if bandwidth, rack density and system scale continue to rise.

The read-through is strongest for the AI infrastructure basket rather than for any specific vendor’s market share. Amphenol explicitly serves GPU, ASIC and other XPU architectures and did not identify individual customers. The call confirms aggregate system demand but does not prove that NVIDIA, Broadcom, Arista or Celestica is gaining share within that demand.

CUSTOM AI SILICON, INFERENCE AND HIGH-SPEED CONNECTIVITY ARE BROADENING THE AI BENEFICIARY SET (READ-THROUGH 2)

Affected companies: Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: US); Marvell Technology, Inc. (MRVL: US); Credo Technology Group Holding Ltd. (CRDO: US); Astera Labs, Inc. (ALAB: US).

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive. Medium-to-high near-term impact and high long-duration strategic impact.

Call evidence: Management stated that Amphenol works with customers developing “GPU architectures,” “ASIC architectures,” and “XPU architectures.” Management also stated that “inference seems to grow at an even faster pace” and emphasized that training and inference both require an unprecedented intensity of interconnect products. On the copper-versus-fiber debate, management stated: “They need more of everything. More high-speed copper, more fiber optic solutions, and more power solutions.”

Transmission mechanism: The call supports a broadening of AI capital expenditure beyond frontier-model training clusters built primarily around merchant GPUs. Custom ASIC deployments and inference clusters require large-scale east-west networking, PCIe and CXL connectivity, retimers, active electrical cables, optical DSPs, Ethernet switching and other high-speed connectivity products. Inference growth is particularly important because inference infrastructure can be deployed across more cloud regions, enterprises and workload locations than the most concentrated training clusters.

Broadcom and Marvell benefit from custom AI silicon, switching, DSP and connectivity exposure. Credo benefits from active electrical cables, SerDes and optical connectivity products. Astera Labs benefits from PCIe, CXL and fabric-connectivity requirements as accelerator systems become larger and more heterogeneous.

The call also weakens the thesis that optical adoption necessarily displaces electrical connectivity. Amphenol’s message was that customers require both technologies in greater quantities. Copper remains economically and technically attractive over shorter distances and within racks, while optical connectivity becomes increasingly important across racks, clusters and longer-distance links. Higher system complexity can therefore increase both electrical and optical content simultaneously.

Near-term trading catalyst: Strong Q3 IT datacom guidance and Amphenol’s 1.23:1 book-to-bill ratio support continued order strength for connectivity semiconductors and active electrical products.

Long-duration fundamental shift: AI infrastructure is likely to evolve into a hybrid architecture involving merchant GPUs, custom accelerators, electrical links, optical links and increasingly sophisticated fabric management. This reduces dependence on a single accelerator architecture and expands the addressable market for connectivity suppliers.

The implication is not negative for NVIDIA. Aggregate training demand remains strong. The more differentiated conclusion is that AI infrastructure growth is broad enough to support multiple silicon and connectivity architectures, reducing the validity of a narrow single-vendor AI investment framework.

DATA-CENTER OPTICS IS BOOMING, BUT THE CALL DOES NOT SIGNAL A TELECOM OPTICS RECOVERY (READ-THROUGH 3)

Affected companies: Fabrinet (FN: US) — positive; Corning Incorporated (GLW: US) — positive; Coherent Corp. (COHR: US) — mixed-positive; Lumentum Holdings Inc. (LITE: US) — mixed-positive; Ciena Corporation (CIEN: US) — negative; Nokia Oyj (NOKIA: Finland) — negative; Telefonaktiebolaget LM Ericsson (ERIC B: Sweden) — negative.

Directional impact and magnitude: High-magnitude positive for data-center optical volumes; high-magnitude negative near-term for carrier-exposed communications infrastructure. The effect on diversified optical-component suppliers is positive but mix dependent.

Call evidence: CommScope’s IT datacom business “essentially doubled” year over year. Management stated that all of CommScope’s IT datacom products are “optics, advanced complex optical interconnect solutions.” IT datacom is expected to represent slightly less than 50% of CommScope’s 2026 revenue, compared with approximately 33% in 2025. In contrast, Amphenol’s communications-network revenue declined 6% organically because of weaker demand from network operators and wireless-equipment manufacturers. Q3 communications-network revenue is expected to decline by a mid-teens percentage sequentially.

Transmission mechanism: Optical demand is bifurcating sharply by end market. Hyperscale data centers are increasing fiber density because larger clusters, faster switch speeds, longer distances and thermal constraints make optical links increasingly necessary. Traditional telecommunications operators, by contrast, continue to constrain spending on wireless equipment, carrier networks and selected access infrastructure.

Fabrinet is positively exposed through optical manufacturing and advanced packaging volumes. Corning benefits from greater fiber consumption, although the call is more positive for volume than for scarcity-driven pricing. Management explicitly stated that CommScope had secured the fiber required to support its growth and that Amphenol saw no meaningful bottleneck. The absence of a shortage is positive for shipment conversion but limits the case for unusually strong raw-fiber pricing.

Coherent and Lumentum receive a positive data-center optical read-through, but the magnitude depends on product and end-market mix. Their data-center laser, transceiver and optical-component operations should benefit from rising optical content. Carrier and telecom exposure remains a countervailing negative.

Ciena, Nokia and Ericsson receive a more directly negative near-term signal. The 6% organic decline in communications networks and expected mid-teens sequential Q3 contraction indicate that carrier capital expenditure remains weak. Management’s reference to moderation from both network operators and wireless-equipment manufacturers is particularly negative for radio-access-network and carrier-infrastructure suppliers.

Near-term trading catalyst: Positive estimate revisions are supported for data-center optical suppliers, while telecom-equipment estimates remain exposed to further reductions. A relative-value framework favoring data-center optical exposure over carrier-network exposure is more strongly supported than a broad long-optics thesis.

Long-duration fundamental shift: Data traffic growth remains favorable for both data centers and communications networks over time, but capital-expenditure timing has decoupled. Data-center optical intensity is rising immediately, while carrier infrastructure remains in a delayed and more cyclical investment phase.

AI POWER DELIVERY, HIGHER VOLTAGES AND LIQUID COOLING ARE BECOMING A PARALLEL CAPITAL-EXPENDITURE CYCLE (READ-THROUGH 4)

Affected companies: Vertiv Holdings Co (VRT: US); Eaton Corporation plc (ETN: US); nVent Electric plc (NVT: US); Modine Manufacturing Company (MOD: US).

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive. High near-term and long-duration impact.

Call evidence: Management described increasing use of higher voltages, liquid cooling, embedded sensors and “smarter power connectors.” Amphenol’s power portfolio extends from the point at which electricity enters the data center through switchgear and rack-level distribution to board-level and chip-adjacent power connections. Management characterized AI as the “conversion of electrons into tokens” and emphasized that reducing electrical loss creates material value for customers.

Transmission mechanism: Higher accelerator density increases power draw per rack, raises cooling requirements and creates demand for more sophisticated electrical distribution. More AI compute therefore requires not only additional chips and networking equipment but also switchgear, uninterruptible power systems, busway, power-distribution units, electrical enclosures, liquid-cooling loops, heat exchangers, pumps and monitoring systems.

Vertiv benefits through power systems, thermal-management infrastructure and liquid-cooling exposure. Eaton benefits through switchgear, power distribution and electrical infrastructure. nVent benefits through enclosures, electrical connections, liquid-cooling infrastructure and thermal-management components. Modine benefits through data-center cooling equipment and heat-transfer systems.

The significance of the call is that these requirements were discussed as current engineering and deployment priorities rather than distant technical possibilities. Amphenol is already producing components designed for higher-voltage systems, liquid cooling and sensor-enabled power connections. This indicates that the infrastructure transition is moving through qualification and production rather than remaining solely at the conceptual stage.

Near-term trading catalyst: Strong AI server and networking deployment should translate into higher order volumes for power and cooling equipment. Amphenol’s statement that no broad bottlenecks are currently preventing shipment conversion reduces execution risk for the broader data-center infrastructure chain.

Long-duration fundamental shift: Power availability and cooling efficiency are becoming binding determinants of AI deployment. Capital expenditure is therefore shifting from a compute-centric model toward a broader physical-infrastructure model in which electrical and thermal equipment receives a larger portion of total project spending.

The principal caveat is timing. Large electrical and cooling projects can be delayed by permitting, utility interconnection, site construction and customer acceptance. Amphenol’s component demand confirms project activity but does not guarantee a smooth quarterly revenue cadence for every infrastructure vendor.

TRADITIONAL ENTERPRISE AND CLOUD IT DEMAND IS IMPROVING ALONGSIDE AI (READ-THROUGH 5)

Affected companies: Dell Technologies Inc. (DELL: US); Hewlett Packard Enterprise Company (HPE: US); Cisco Systems, Inc. (CSCO: US).

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive. Medium-magnitude near-term read-through.

Call evidence: Management attributed IT datacom strength to AI applications “together with robust growth in our base IT datacom business.” The Q3 outlook assumes that “enterprise and cloud customers expand their demand for traditional IT datacom equipment.”

Transmission mechanism: Amphenol’s base IT datacom products are incorporated into traditional servers, storage systems, switches, routers and enterprise computing platforms. Growth outside explicitly AI-related products indicates that conventional infrastructure budgets are improving rather than being completely crowded out by AI capital expenditure.

Dell and Hewlett Packard Enterprise benefit through server, storage and integrated-system demand. Cisco benefits through enterprise and cloud networking, although its service-provider exposure creates an offsetting headwind from the weak communications-network outlook.

The signal is more important than the absolute size of the non-AI contribution. Market expectations have increasingly assumed that AI absorbs a disproportionate share of enterprise and cloud budgets. Amphenol’s call indicates that traditional infrastructure demand is also expanding, potentially because AI workloads require upgrades to surrounding networks, storage and general-purpose computing environments.

Near-term trading catalyst: Improved base IT datacom demand supports a more constructive H2 outlook for enterprise hardware and networking vendors, particularly companies with subdued expectations or easy comparisons.

Long-duration fundamental shift: AI investment may create a complementary refresh cycle rather than a permanent substitution away from traditional enterprise hardware. Networks, storage, security and general-purpose servers must often be upgraded to support AI deployment and data movement.

The read-through is less powerful than the AI signal because Amphenol did not quantify base IT datacom growth separately. It nevertheless provides direct evidence that the quarter’s strength was not exclusively attributable to accelerators.

COMMSCOPE’S RAPID IMPROVEMENT RAISES THE COMPETITIVE BAR FOR CONNECTOR AND CABLING PEERS (READ-THROUGH 6)

Affected companies: TE Connectivity plc (TEL: US); Belden Inc. (BDC: US).

Directional impact and magnitude: Negative on a relative competitive basis. Medium near-term and medium-to-high long-duration impact.

Call evidence: Amphenol increased expected 2026 CommScope revenue from $4.1B to $4.6B and doubled expected adjusted EPS accretion from $0.15 to $0.30. CommScope generated slightly more than $1.2B of Q2 revenue and operated above a 20% margin, including acquired-backlog amortization. Management attributed the profitability improvement to manufacturing execution, vendor management, operating leverage and SG&A control rather than pricing.

Transmission mechanism: CommScope gives Amphenol scaled optical-interconnect and building-connectivity capabilities that complement its existing high-speed copper, power and sensor businesses. Amphenol can now approach data-center customers with a broader architecture-agnostic product suite and use longstanding relationships with chip designers, system manufacturers, hyperscalers and data-center builders to expand CommScope’s market access.

TE Connectivity faces greater competitive pressure in high-speed connectors, data-center power, automotive connectivity and industrial interconnect. Belden faces more direct pressure in enterprise cabling, data-center connectivity and building-network infrastructure. The acquisition also gives Amphenol access to CommScope’s global building-connectivity distribution network, increasing its ability to cross-sell legacy Amphenol products into construction and industrial channels.

The read-through is relative rather than an absolute earnings negative. TE Connectivity and Belden should still benefit from many of the same AI, industrial and electrification demand drivers. The negative implication is that Amphenol’s execution, breadth and acquisition integration may allow it to capture a disproportionate share of incremental market growth.

Near-term trading catalyst: Peer results will face a higher comparison standard. Investors are likely to scrutinize whether TE Connectivity and Belden can demonstrate comparable organic growth, data-center exposure and operating leverage.

Long-duration fundamental shift: Amphenol is evolving from a connector supplier into a more comprehensive provider of copper, optical, power and sensing infrastructure. This can reduce customer fragmentation and make product bundling, co-development and global manufacturing scale more important competitive advantages.

Amphenol’s acquisition of Wilder Technologies reinforces this dynamic. Wilder was a key supplier of high-speed test and measurement solutions to Amphenol. Internalizing that capability can shorten product-development cycles and deepen engineering engagement in next-generation high-speed systems.

SEMICONDUCTOR-FAB, TEST AND INSTRUMENTATION INVESTMENT REMAINS STRONG (READ-THROUGH 7)

Affected companies: KLA Corporation (KLAC: US); Applied Materials, Inc. (AMAT: US); Lam Research Corporation (LRCX: US); Keysight Technologies, Inc. (KEYS: US); Teradyne, Inc. (TER: US).

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive. Medium-to-high near-term impact and medium long-duration impact.

Call evidence: Industrial revenue increased 18% organically and 13% sequentially. Management specifically identified “instrumentation growing very strongly” and “factory automation growing very strongly,” adding that this was “not surprising given factories and the build out of semiconductors.” Amphenol also acquired Wilder Technologies, a supplier of high-performance test and measurement solutions for high-speed interconnect applications.

Transmission mechanism: Semiconductor-factory construction and equipment production require substantial volumes of precision connectors, sensors, cable assemblies and harsh-environment interconnect products. Instrumentation demand also rises as new process technologies and faster electronic interfaces increase testing, calibration and signal-integrity requirements.

KLA, Applied Materials and Lam Research benefit from continued wafer-fabrication and semiconductor-equipment investment. Keysight benefits from higher-speed electrical and optical test requirements. Teradyne benefits from semiconductor and system-level test demand, although the precise relationship depends on the composition of fab, device and system spending.

The Wilder acquisition provides a separate strategic confirmation. High-speed interface development is becoming sufficiently complex and important that Amphenol chose to acquire an existing test-and-measurement supplier. This indicates rising value in signal-integrity verification as data rates increase.

Near-term trading catalyst: The industrial commentary supports continued equipment and instrumentation demand through H2 rather than an immediate peak following prior semiconductor-capacity announcements.

Long-duration fundamental shift: AI, advanced packaging, high-speed networking and regional semiconductor localization are increasing both manufacturing capacity and test intensity. The number of qualification and measurement steps can rise even when semiconductor unit growth is less dramatic.

The signal should not be interpreted as proof that every wafer-fabrication-equipment category is accelerating equally. Amphenol’s exposure spans factories, equipment, instrumentation and automation. The read-through is strongest for aggregate semiconductor capital intensity and test complexity.
