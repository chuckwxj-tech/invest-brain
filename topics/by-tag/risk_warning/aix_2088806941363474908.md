@__Con_ B. The "Memory Wall" is a Permanent Problem
*  AI models are getting larger, but compute (FLOPS) is growing faster than memory bandwidth. This "memory wall" means that **memory bandwidth is the primary bottleneck** for AI performance.
*  This isn’t a temporary trend; it’s a fundamental physics/engineering constraint. As long as AI models grow, the need for more, faster, and more specialized memory will be **constant and growing**.
**HBM (High Bandwidth Memory)** is no longer a commodity. It’s a specialized, high-margin product with a limited number of qualified suppliers.
*  **Customization:**
**HBM is hard to make.** It requires advanced packaging (like TSV - Through-Silicon Vias) and has a much lower yield rate than standard DRAM. This means supply can’t just be ramped up quickly in response to a price spike. The bottleneck is **manufacturing capacity and expertise**, not just capital expenditure.
**The Commodity Segment (Standard DRAM/NAND):** Still cyclical, still driven by consumer electronics and general IT demand.
2. **The Strategic Segment (HBM, CXL, Custom AI Memory):** Becoming more like **semiconductor logic** (e.g., CPUs/GPUs) than traditional memory. It’s characterized by:
*  High barriers to entry.
*  Long-term contracts.
*  Customization.
*  Steady, growing demand driven by inference.
