@bubbleboi @drsessasarasota maybe a tactical a/b test from NVIDIA because they've been eyeing CXL as a hedge - along with NAND  - for HBM bottleneck; current set up is CXL 2.0 

NVIDIA's 88-core Vera CPU with native CXL 3.1 over PCIe Gen 6 and 1.8 TB/s NVLink-C2C was designed to bridge proprietary NVLink to open rack-scale pooling, offloading Agentic AI KV caches from costly HBM to cheaper DDR5 to prevent Rubin GPU bottlenecks and enable dynamic memory composition via fabric switches and controllers from Astera Labs and Marvell, targeting $200 billion server CPU market. 

BUT everyone's suspiciously delayed standardization for "reasons"

Samsung has pushed its 1TB, 72GB/s CXL 3.1 module mass production to Q1 2027 plus, with SK Hynix and Micron also pausing CXL 3.1 to stay on CXL 2.0, because CXL 3.1 requires PCIe 6.0 hosts that are absent as Intel Diamond Rapids slips to Q2/Q3 2027 and AMD Venice sampling arrives only post-September 2026. Vera will therefore launch early into a vacuum, forcing NVIDIA to rely on CMX and CXL 2.0 interim while hyperscaler-wide pooling slips to 2027 to 2028.

HBM4: 2 TB/s, 2.8 OC per stack, 64GB max, 32 channels, ∼10ns; CXL 3.1: 128GB/s per x16, 72GB/s 1TB device, fabric-shared to 16 hosts, 80-214ns plus 50-120ns over DDR5.
