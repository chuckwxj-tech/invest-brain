INVESTMENT CONCLUSION

The comment contains 3 distinct claims. The 3D NAND materials claim is validated. The molybdenum supply-structure claim is broadly validated but materially simplified. The conclusion that FCX and SCCO will be rerated once the connection becomes appreciated is not validated by the available evidence and should be treated as a conditional upside scenario rather than a base-case investment thesis.

Advanced 3D NAND manufacturers have begun replacing tungsten with molybdenum in wordline gate electrodes because tungsten becomes increasingly resistive and difficult to integrate as wordline dimensions shrink and NAND stacks become taller. Micron has publicly confirmed that molybdenum is being used in mass production in its latest-generation NAND. Lam Research has reported early adoption at leading high-volume 3D NAND manufacturers with fabs in Korea and Singapore, while Kioxia and Western Digital have demonstrated significant performance, reliability, and density benefits through joint development work. The technically correct formulation is that the industry is entering a node-selective transition toward molybdenum, not that tungsten has already been displaced throughout the installed 3D NAND base. (Lam Research Newsroom)

The statement that molybdenum is mostly produced as a byproduct of copper is directionally correct. Published estimates indicate that approximately 55-60 percent of molybdenum is produced as a byproduct or co-product of porphyry copper mining. However, primary molybdenum mines, Chinese production, secondary recovery, conversion capacity, and inventories remain important. China alone represented approximately 45 percent of global mine production in 2025. The byproduct structure makes a significant portion of supply relatively insensitive to molybdenum prices in the short term, but it does not make global supply permanently fixed. (ScienceDirect)

The rerating assertion conflates a possible earnings uplift with a change in valuation multiple. Higher molybdenum prices would raise FCX and SCCO cash generation, but that does not automatically produce a multiple expansion. A durable rerating would require semiconductor demand to become large enough to alter the global molybdenum balance, persist through several semiconductor and commodity cycles, remain difficult to supply, and generate economic rents that accrue to miners rather than primarily to semiconductor-precursor, delivery-system, deposition-equipment, etch, clean, and process-integration suppliers. Current evidence supports incremental molybdenum optionality for FCX and SCCO, not a semiconductor-driven transformation of either equity.

TECHNICAL VALIDATION OF THE TUNGSTEN-TO-MOLYBDENUM TRANSITION

The reference to “word line control gates” is substantively understandable but technically imprecise. In replacement-gate 3D NAND, the horizontal wordlines form gate electrodes around the vertical memory channel. “Wordline gate electrodes” is therefore the more precise terminology. During fabrication, sacrificial material is removed from the multilayer stack and replaced with conductive gate metal through extremely long, narrow lateral openings. As the stack height rises and wordline pitch contracts, the deposited metal must fill increasingly severe high-aspect-ratio structures without voids, seams, contamination, or unacceptable resistance.

Tungsten has historically been used because the WF6-based deposition process is mature, conformal, and well understood. Its scaling limitations become increasingly material at advanced dimensions. The bulk resistivity difference between tungsten and molybdenum is relatively modest, but the device-level difference is considerably larger because nanoscale tungsten resistivity rises sharply as line dimensions contract. Grain-boundary scattering, surface scattering, nucleation layers, and barrier or adhesion layers reduce the effective conductive cross-section of tungsten. Molybdenum can be integrated without the conventional barrier or adhesion layer, allowing more of the available wordline cross-section to conduct current. Lam Research reports resistance improvement exceeding 50 percent relative to conventional tungsten in most instances, reflecting the combined effect of lower nanoscale resistivity, barrierless integration, and improved feature fill rather than merely the difference between the bulk resistivities of the 2 metals. (Lam Research Newsroom)

Tungsten also presents a chemistry and reliability problem. WF6 introduces fluorine into the process. As wordline dimensions shrink, voids and slits in the tungsten fill can retain fluorine residues. Those residues can attack the cell dielectric and inter-wordline dielectric, creating leakage paths and reliability failures. Kioxia’s fluorine-free molybdenum process instead uses MoO2Cl2. Kioxia reported that optimized molybdenum embeddability reduced the leakage failure rate to 1/100 of the tungsten result. At a 17 percent reduction in wordline pitch, molybdenum improved wordline resistance-capacitance performance by 18 percent. Kioxia also reported that equivalent resistance-capacitance performance could permit wordline-pitch contraction exceeding 7.3 percent. At a 3.7 percent reduction in memory-hole pitch, molybdenum’s leakage failure rate was 1/25 of tungsten’s, supporting an estimated bit-density increase exceeding 16.3 percent within the same silicon volume. These results were generated through joint Kioxia and Western Digital development and presented at the 2024 VLSI Symposium, making them strong technical evidence but not confirmation of full commercial deployment across Kioxia’s production portfolio. (KIOXIA Corporation)

The transition is not process-trivial. Molybdenum does not eliminate fill-related risk; it changes the relevant defects. Kioxia found that residual oxygen can accumulate in molybdenum voids when embeddability is inadequate. That oxygen can diffuse into adjacent dielectrics and create leakage paths. Commercial implementation therefore requires control over precursor purity, sublimation stability, nucleation, film conformality, oxygen and chlorine contamination, lateral fill, chamber conditions, thermal budget, work function, threshold-voltage distribution, wet etch, recess uniformity, chemical-mechanical planarization, post-deposition cleaning, and long-term reliability. Molybdenum’s physical advantages are genuine, but acceptable yield depends on an integrated process solution rather than a direct metal-for-metal substitution. (KIOXIA Corporation)

Molybdenum is also not the only alternative. Imec has investigated barrierless molybdenum and ruthenium and demonstrated improved resistivity and memory characteristics at wordline pitches as low as 40nm. Optimized tungsten processes may remain economically preferable in less advanced nodes, while ruthenium or other conductive materials could capture portions of future architectures. The relevant competitive question is therefore not whether molybdenum is better than legacy tungsten under some conditions; that has been demonstrated. The relevant question is the proportion of NAND layers, wafers, nodes, and manufacturers for which molybdenum produces sufficient performance and density gains to offset precursor, tool, integration, qualification, and yield costs. (imec)

COMMERCIAL ADOPTION STATUS

Commercial adoption has progressed beyond laboratory development. Micron’s public statement that Lam’s ALTUS Halo enabled molybdenum to enter mass production in its latest-generation NAND is direct high-volume manufacturing confirmation. Lam also reports early adoption at leading high-volume 3D NAND fabs in Korea and Singapore, advanced-logic adoption, and continuing development with DRAM customers. Air Liquide stated in 2025 that its South Korean molybdenum-precursor plant was supplying 2 early adopters. These disclosures collectively confirm that a real semiconductor materials transition has started. (Lam Research Newsroom)

The disclosures do not establish universal adoption. No public primary-source evidence establishes that every major NAND manufacturer has moved all advanced production to molybdenum, that molybdenum is present in every wordline of every qualifying product, or that older tungsten-based capacity will be rapidly converted. Semiconductor materials transitions normally occur node by node and product by product. Mature NAND capacity can continue using tungsten for years because the performance benefit of molybdenum may not justify requalification costs where pitch, layer count, and resistance remain manageable. The installed base therefore should not be modeled as undergoing an immediate wholesale conversion.

The strongest upside extension is adoption beyond 3D NAND. Lam has identified advanced logic adoption and DRAM development, while precursor suppliers are offering MoO2Cl2 for logic, DRAM, and 3D NAND. Potential logic applications include selected contacts, vias, local interconnects, and future backside-power or gate-all-around structures where barrierless integration and low nanoscale resistance are valuable. DRAM could eventually represent a significant additional materials opportunity. However, logic and DRAM processes require their own electrical, thermal, reliability, and integration qualifications. NAND adoption cannot be mechanically extrapolated into an immediate industry-wide transition across all semiconductor devices. (Lam Research Newsroom)

MOLYBDENUM SUPPLY STRUCTURE

Global molybdenum mine production was 672.6 million lb in 2025, equivalent to approximately 305,000t. Reported usage was 671.8 million lb, leaving production and usage approximately balanced on an annual flow basis. China produced 302.1 million lb, South America produced 175.6 million lb, and North America produced 133.7 million lb. Q1 2026 production was 168.4 million lb and reported usage was 180.7 million lb, producing an apparent quarterly gap of 12.3 million lb. Quarterly production-use differences cannot be interpreted as precise deficits because inventories, shipment timing, intermediate products, trade flows, and reporting conventions can bridge the difference, but the data indicate that the market entered 2026 without a large visible production surplus. (Imoa)

The copper-byproduct characteristic does increase the potential price sensitivity of incremental demand. A copper mine is generally optimized around copper grades, recoveries, concentrate quality, mine sequencing, throughput, and copper economics. A higher molybdenum price does not immediately create additional molybdenum-bearing ore or change the mine plan. Recovery rates can sometimes be improved and stockpiles can be processed differently, but additional byproduct supply generally requires suitable ore, concentrator availability, flotation capacity, roasting or conversion capacity, and sufficient copper production. SCCO’s own Q2 2026 results illustrate this dynamic: molybdenum prices increased 43 percent year over year while molybdenum production declined 11 percent because ore grades fell. (SCC)

The supply inelasticity argument should nevertheless not be taken too far. Approximately 40-45 percent of global output is not copper byproduct production under the published estimates. FCX operates 2 primary molybdenum mines, and China has substantial primary and co-product production. Sustained elevated prices can support primary-mine restarts, expansions, recovery investments, inventory releases, substitution, and demand thrifting. FCX’s primary molybdenum mine unit cash cost is expected to approximate $17.91/lb in 2026, versus a Q2 realized price of $28.75/lb, demonstrating that current prices provide meaningful operating margin and potential supply incentives. (SEC)

The end-use mix is also critical. Approximately 20 percent of new molybdenum is used in molybdenum-grade stainless steel. Engineering steels, tool steels, high-speed steels, cast iron, and superalloys represent another 60 percent. Only the remaining 20 percent is allocated to upgraded products including lubricants, chemicals, and molybdenum metal. Semiconductor applications represent a subset of that final category. The global molybdenum price is therefore still predominantly determined by steel, energy, industrial, aerospace, defense, chemical-processing, and broader macroeconomic demand rather than semiconductor demand. (Imoa)

A small new end market can nevertheless influence price disproportionately when inventories are low and short-term supply is inelastic. Commodity prices are established by the marginal unit, not by the average end-use share. The semiconductor transition should therefore not be dismissed merely because semiconductor tonnage is small. The appropriate conclusion is narrower: semiconductor demand can be an incremental tightening factor and potentially a narrative catalyst, but evidence is insufficient to identify it as the dominant driver of the molybdenum market or of the 2025-2026 price increase.

ORDER-OF-MAGNITUDE 3D NAND DEMAND TEST

A physical-volume test indicates that the amount of molybdenum permanently embedded in NAND wafers is likely to be extremely small relative to the global molybdenum market. SEMI estimated 2025 global 3D NAND installed capacity at 3.7 million 200mm-equivalent wafers per month. Adjusting for wafer area converts this to approximately 1.64 million 300mm wafers per month, or approximately 19.7 million 300mm wafers annually at 100 percent utilization. (SEMI)

Under an illustrative advanced-NAND geometry of 200-400 wordline layers, an effective molybdenum thickness of 10-25nm per layer, 50-70 percent effective array and metal coverage, and molybdenum density of approximately 10.28g/cm³, embedded molybdenum would approximate 0.7-5.1g per 300mm wafer. Applying that range to 19.7 million annual wafers produces approximately 14-100t of molybdenum permanently embedded in NAND devices. Against approximately 305,000t of annual global molybdenum usage, the result represents only approximately 0.005-0.033 percent of the market.

This calculation is an analytical sensitivity rather than a reported industry forecast. Exact wordline dimensions, array coverage, layer count, film thickness, deposition selectivity, utilization, yield, and the proportion of layers using molybdenum are proprietary. The calculation is intentionally favorable to the demand thesis because it assumes 100 percent utilization and 100 percent molybdenum penetration across the entire published 3D NAND capacity base. Actual near-term penetration is substantially lower because mature nodes remain tungsten-based and only an early group of advanced products has entered molybdenum production.

Precursor demand is higher than the molybdenum retained in finished wafers. Losses arise during precursor synthesis and purification, canister loading, sublimation, gas delivery, chamber deposition, purge cycles, chamber-wall deposition, cleaning, abatement, qualification, and rejection of material that fails semiconductor purity specifications. Using a 5x feed-to-retained-film factor would generate approximately 72-502t of elemental molybdenum demand, equivalent to approximately 0.02-0.16 percent of global usage. A deliberately conservative 20x factor would generate approximately 287-2,007t, equivalent to approximately 0.09-0.66 percent of global usage. Even the 20x upper case assumes complete industry penetration and full utilization.

For NAND alone to add 1 percent to global molybdenum demand under the embedded-film range, end-to-end retention would need to be only approximately 0.47-3.30 percent, implying feed requirements approximately 30-213x the molybdenum retained in finished devices. Such poor aggregate utilization is possible in an immature specialty process but should not be used as a base case without fab-level precursor-consumption evidence. Semiconductor manufacturers and suppliers have strong economic, uptime, contamination, environmental, and abatement incentives to improve material utilization as the process matures.

Even the approximate 2,007t upper sensitivity would contain only about $133 million of underlying molybdenum at $30/lb before purification and specialty processing. The semiconductor precursor can have a much higher selling value than the contained commodity metal, but that incremental value is generated by purity, chemistry, packaging, delivery, process control, qualification, and intellectual property. The calculation illustrates why the semiconductor opportunity can be important for specialized materials suppliers while remaining modest for the aggregate economics of global molybdenum miners.

AI-related storage growth does not translate directly into proportional molybdenum consumption. Molybdenum demand is driven by molybdenum penetration multiplied by wafer starts and material consumed per wafer. NAND bit demand is driven by exabytes. Higher layer counts, tighter memory-hole pitch, improved cell architecture, and higher bits per cell increase the number of bits produced per wafer. Rising bit output can therefore be accommodated partly through higher bit density rather than proportionally higher wafer starts. More wordline layers increase material per wafer, but they also increase bits per wafer. The net molybdenum intensity per exabyte is consequently ambiguous and must be modeled through wafer-start and architecture assumptions rather than through NAND bit-demand growth alone.

VALUE-CHAIN CAPTURE

The most immediate bottleneck is not mine production but semiconductor-grade chemistry and delivery. MoO2Cl2 is a solid precursor requiring ultra-high purity, very low moisture, stable temperature, reliable sublimation, controlled flow, corrosion-resistant packaging, and uninterrupted delivery into ALD or CVD equipment. Air Liquide’s Subleem platform combines high-purity solid precursors with proprietary gas-phase distribution systems. Air Liquide has described solid metal-chloride delivery as a historical limitation and has developed high-flow sublimation systems capable of serving multiple deposition tools. Entegris and EMD Electronics also market high-purity MoO2Cl2 and specialized solid-source delivery systems for logic, DRAM, and 3D NAND. (Air Liquide Electronics)

The transition creates additional process-equipment and consumables opportunities. Lam’s ALTUS Halo is specifically designed for high-volume molybdenum ALD. Molybdenum also requires dedicated etch and clean chemistries because conventional tungsten processes do not transfer directly. Selectivity, top-to-bottom uniformity, residue control, corrosion, chamber coating, filtration, CMP, and post-CMP cleaning must be reoptimized. The monetizable value pool therefore spans deposition equipment, precursor production, purification, precursor delivery, canisters, cabinets, filtration, coatings, etch chemistry, cleaning chemistry, CMP consumables, process control, and abatement. (Lam Research Newsroom)

These economics suggest that the most direct semiconductor beneficiaries are likely to be process-equipment and electronic-material suppliers rather than mine owners. Raw molybdenum is fungible after appropriate conversion, whereas a qualified semiconductor precursor and delivery system is highly specialized and can require several years of customer collaboration, defect qualification, reliability testing, and process-of-record integration. The resulting premium is more likely to accrue to the companies controlling precursor chemistry, purification, delivery technology, process recipes, installed tools, and customer qualifications.

FCX has a stronger strategic position in this value chain than a conventional copper producer because its Climax and Henderson mines produce high-purity, chemical-grade molybdenum concentrate, and most of its molybdenum concentrate is processed through company conversion facilities. FCX produces value-added molybdenum chemical products and therefore has a plausible path to supply precursor manufacturers with higher-quality feedstock. However, no reviewed public disclosure identifies FCX as a qualified producer of semiconductor-grade MoO2Cl2 or as a direct recipient of semiconductor-precursor pricing. The absence of disclosure does not rule out indirect sales through chemical processors, but the semiconductor premium should not be credited to FCX without evidence of product qualification, contractual pricing, or incremental conversion margins. (SEC)
