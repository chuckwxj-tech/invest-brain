EVERYTHING YOU NEED TO KNOW ABOUT LUMENTUM $LITE

Lumentum may look like another optical components company, but the easiest way to understand it is this: AI chips are only valuable if they can move enormous amounts of data quickly and efficiently. $LITE supplies many of the lasers, optical components, transceivers and switching systems that make this possible.

The business has undergone a stunning transformation. Revenue was $1.65 billion in FY2025. At the midpoint of current guidance, FY2026 revenue should approach $3.0 billion, representing roughly 82% growth. Non-GAAP operating margin was only 9.7% in FY2025, reached 32.2% in the latest quarter and is guided to reach 35% to 36% in Q4.

NVIDIA has invested $2 billion, demand exceeds available laser capacity and several new product ramps have barely begun. However, the market is no longer ignoring the opportunity. At roughly $890 per share, $LITE is already an $85 billion company.

That creates the central question for investors: Is Lumentum still early in a multi-year AI optics supercycle, or is the stock already pricing in most of that future success?

WHAT DOES LUMENTUM ACTUALLY DO?

Lumentum produces optical and photonic technologies used in AI data centers, cloud networks, telecom infrastructure, submarine networks, advanced manufacturing and sensing applications. In simple terms, Lumentum helps convert electrical data into light, move that light through fiber and direct it toward the correct destination.

Its portfolio can be separated into two categories. Components include semiconductor laser chips, laser assemblies, pump lasers, wavelength management products and coherent networking components. Systems include complete transceivers, optical circuit switches and industrial laser systems.

Components represented 66% of Q3 revenue, while Systems represented the remaining 34%. Components generally carry stronger margins, but Lumentum is increasingly moving upward in the value chain by selling complete modules and optical systems instead of only individual chips.

Simply put: $NVDA provides many of the brains inside an AI factory, while $LITE helps those brains communicate.

WHY AI DATA CENTERS NEED MORE OPTICS

As AI clusters grow, the amount of data moving between GPUs, switches, racks and separate data center buildings increases dramatically. Electrical copper connections remain effective over short distances, but bandwidth, reach and power consumption become increasingly problematic at higher speeds.

That is where optics enters the picture. Fiber can move more data over longer distances while consuming less power per bit. This matters because power, cooling and physical space are becoming major limitations for hyperscalers.

There are three important networking layers:

• Scale-up connects accelerators within the same compute domain.

• Scale-out connects racks and compute domains across a data center.

• Scale-across connects separate buildings, campuses or geographically distributed data centers.

Lumentum participates across all three layers through transceivers, high-speed lasers, co-packaged optics, coherent components and optical circuit switches. This breadth is one of the most important parts of the thesis because the company is not entirely dependent on one optical architecture winning.

THE CURRENT FOUNDATION: EMLs, CW LASERS AND SCALE-ACROSS

Lumentum’s present cash engine is its indium phosphide laser portfolio.

An EML combines a laser and a modulator on the same chip, allowing electrical data to be converted into a high-speed optical signal. EMLs are heavily used inside 800G and 1.6T pluggable transceivers. A 1.6T module can use eight 200G optical lanes, making the transition from 100G to 200G lanes an important content and pricing upgrade.

Q3 set new company records for both 100G and 200G EML shipments. Total laser chip shipments doubled year over year, while revenue from 200G EMLs more than doubled sequentially. Management expects EML unit capacity in the December 2026 quarter to be more than 50% above December 2025.

Even that may not be enough. Management estimates that EML demand currently exceeds supply by more than 30%, customers are requesting significantly more volume and parts of the laser portfolio remain effectively sold out. Lumentum’s OFC presentation showed more than two years of backlog for EML and CW laser chips.

CW lasers provide continuous light that can be modulated separately by a silicon photonics chip. These are important for silicon photonics transceivers and future co-packaged optical systems. This means a transition from EML-based modules toward silicon photonics would not automatically remove Lumentum from the supply chain. It would shift demand toward another part of Lumentum’s laser portfolio.

The company also sells pump lasers, narrow-linewidth laser assemblies and wavelength-selective switches used to connect separate data centers. Narrow-linewidth laser shipments increased more than 120% year over year in Q3, while pump laser shipments grew 80%. These products receive less attention than transceivers and CPO, but they were significant contributors to the recent margin expansion.

GROWTH ENGINE 1: CLOUD TRANSCEIVERS

The 2023 acquisition of Cloud Light transformed Lumentum from primarily a component supplier into a much stronger complete transceiver competitor. Lumentum paid approximately $705 million for the business, gaining advanced cloud optical modules and an expanded manufacturing footprint in Thailand.

That acquisition is now producing substantial growth. Systems revenue reached $275.1 million in Q3, increasing 24% sequentially and 121% year over year. Cloud transceiver shipments increased more than 40% sequentially and accounted for most of that growth.

Lumentum is now ramping 1.6T transceivers, and management believes the company has entered the leading group of suppliers. The transition matters for two reasons. First, AI clusters require substantially more bandwidth. Second, management says the margin structure of 1.6T is better than 800G.

Lumentum is also beginning to place internally manufactured CW lasers inside its own modules. Around 20% of the modules included in Q4 guidance were expected to use internal lasers. Increasing this percentage should lower external component dependency and allow Lumentum to capture more value from every transceiver it sells.

The weakness is that transceiver margins still trail some competitors. Management has openly acknowledged this. Better yields, lower scrap rates, increasing 1.6T volume and additional internal laser content must continue improving profitability.

GROWTH ENGINE 2: OPTICAL CIRCUIT SWITCHES

An Optical Circuit Switch, or OCS, uses tiny MEMS mirrors to create direct optical paths between different parts of an AI cluster. Instead of repeatedly converting data from light into electricity and back into light, OCS can keep large data flows inside the optical domain.

Lumentum says its 300x300 MEMS-based OCS offers latency measured in tens of nanoseconds and consumes less than 10% of the power required by comparable packet-switching systems. Its design is also wavelength and data-rate agnostic, meaning the underlying optical engine can remain useful as networking speeds increase.

This business is only beginning to affect reported results. OCS contributed more than $25 million of Q3 revenue, but Lumentum has already secured a multi-year, multi-billion-dollar purchase agreement. The company previously disclosed more than $400 million of backlog expected to be fulfilled during the second half of 2026 and is targeting an OCS revenue run rate above $1 billion during 2027.

The opportunity is enormous, but so is the execution risk. Management described OCS as its biggest operational “tightrope” because production must increase from almost zero to significant volume in a very short period. New customer requests and different port configurations are also placing pressure on the product roadmap.

This is an important distinction: OCS demand appears proven, but Lumentum still needs to prove that it can manufacture these complex systems at the required scale, quality and cost.

GROWTH ENGINES 3 AND 4: CO-PACKAGED OPTICS

Co-Packaged Optics, or CPO, moves optical connectivity much closer to the networking silicon. This shortens power-hungry electrical connections and can significantly reduce the energy required to move data.

Lumentum divides this opportunity into scale-out and scale-up CPO.

Scale-out CPO is the nearer-term opportunity. Lumentum is ramping ultra-high-power lasers and expects meaningful revenue to begin in the December 2026 quarter. The company has already received a multi-hundred-million-dollar order scheduled for delivery during the first half of 2027.

Scale-up CPO could become considerably larger. Management expects initial scale-up shipments in late 2027 and estimates the first phase could represent three to four times the opportunity of scale-out CPO. This would bring optics deeper into the AI compute domain as copper reaches its bandwidth and power limitations.

Lumentum initially supplies the ultra-high-power laser chips, but it is developing vertically integrated External Laser Source modules. An ELS combines multiple components into a complete, serviceable light-source module. Management believes this could approximately double Lumentum’s content compared with supplying the laser chip alone.

To support future demand, Lumentum acquired a compound semiconductor facility in Greensboro, North Carolina, from Qorvo. The facility is being converted from gallium arsenide to indium phosphide production. Management says it could eventually support around $5 billion of annual revenue capacity, with meaningful production expected to begin in early 2028.

This new capacity is not included in the company’s current revenue projections. That creates major long-term upside if CPO adoption follows management’s expectations, but investors should remember that the factory still needs to be converted, qualified and ramped successfully.

WHY NVIDIA INVESTED $2 BILLION

The NVIDIA agreement is more than a simple investment.

NVIDIA made a $2 billion strategic investment through approximately 2.9 million convertible preferred shares priced at $695.31 each. The shares can convert one-for-one into common stock. The agreement is nonexclusive, but it includes a multi-billion-dollar purchase commitment and future access rights to advanced laser capacity.

For NVIDIA, this helps secure one of the most important potential bottlenecks in next-generation AI networking. For Lumentum, it provides capital to expand manufacturing, accelerate research and build additional US-based capacity.

The deal validates the strategic importance of Lumentum’s technology, but investors should not treat NVIDIA’s $695 investment price as a permanent floor. It was part of a broader commercial and capacity agreement, not a normal open-market stock purchase.

It also creates dilution. That is acceptable if the capital allows Lumentum to capture several billion dollars of additional high-margin revenue, but the resulting value must ultimately be measured on a per-share basis.

THE FINANCIAL INFLECTION

The speed of Lumentum’s financial transformation is difficult to ignore:

• FY2025 revenue: $1.645 billion

• FY2025 non-GAAP gross margin: 34.7%

• FY2025 non-GAAP operating margin: 9.7%

• FY2025 non-GAAP EPS: $2.06

In Q3 FY2026, revenue reached $808.4 million, increasing 21.5% sequentially and 90.1% year over year. Non-GAAP gross margin expanded to 47.9%, non-GAAP operating margin reached 32.2% and non-GAAP EPS increased to $2.37 from $0.57 one year earlier.

For Q4, management guided:

• Revenue of $960 million to $1.01 billion

• Non-GAAP operating margin of 35% to 36%

• Non-GAAP EPS of $2.85 to $3.05

At the $985 million midpoint, full-year revenue would reach approximately $2.99 billion, representing roughly 82% growth from FY2025. This is not only a revenue story. Higher factory utilization, selective price increases, richer product mix and relatively stable operating expenses are creating enormous operating leverage.

The key question is how much of the recent margin expansion is structural. A favorable shortage environment and pricing power are helping today, but vertical integration, 1.6T adoption, higher-value laser products, OCS and improved manufacturing efficiency could allow margins to remain elevated even as supply eventually catches up.

BALANCE SHEET, CAPEX AND DILUTION

Lumentum ended Q3 with $3.17 billion of cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments, compared with approximately $3.28 billion of debt. The NVIDIA investment therefore strengthened liquidity substantially, but Lumentum is not sitting on $3 billion of completely excess cash. Much of the capital is required for manufacturing expansion, debt management and working capital.

During the first nine months of FY2026, operating cash flow reached $388.4 million while capital expenditures totaled $284.5 million. Q3 alone included approximately $125 million of CapEx. Inventory increased to $632.8 million as the company prepared for higher AI and cloud demand.

Investors also need to watch the share count. Non-GAAP diluted shares increased from 72 million in Q4 FY2025 to 95.2 million in Q3 FY2026, while Q4 guidance assumes approximately 102 million shares. That represents roughly 42% year-over-year growth in the diluted share count, driven by convertible debt, debt exchanges, equity compensation and NVIDIA’s preferred investment.

The business is growing much faster than the dilution, but this still matters. Revenue and operating income can explode while per-share value grows more slowly.

WHAT IS LUMENTUM’S MOAT?

The first part of the moat is indium phosphide manufacturing. Producing reliable lasers at high volume requires specialized material science, wafer fabrication, packaging knowledge and years of customer qualification. Lumentum is expanding from what it describes as the industry’s largest existing InP manufacturing baseline.

The second part is reliability. Technologies originally developed for demanding submarine and long-haul networks can now be applied inside AI data centers. Customers building billion-dollar AI clusters care about performance, but they also care deeply about component failure rates.

The third part is portfolio breadth. Lumentum can provide EMLs, CW lasers, ultra-high-power lasers, transceivers, coherent components, ELS modules and optical circuit switches. This allows the company to benefit from several optical architectures instead of betting entirely on one design.

The fourth part is customer integration. Long qualification cycles and multi-year purchase agreements make it difficult to replace a supplier once its products are designed into a platform.

The moat is real, but not permanent. $COHR remains a formidable competitor and also received a $2 billion NVIDIA investment. Chinese suppliers including Innolight and Eoptolink remain highly competitive in transceivers, while alternative technologies such as silicon photonics and VCSELs will continue evolving.

Lumentum is better protected from architecture changes than an EML-only supplier because it also sells CW and ultra-high-power lasers. It is not immune if its current high-margin product mix changes faster than expected.

THE $2 BILLION PER QUARTER TARGET

Management has outlined a target model exceeding $2 billion of quarterly revenue at approximately 40% non-GAAP operating margin. At that level, Lumentum would generate more than $8 billion of annualized revenue, with new growth drivers representing around 60% of the mix.

The company’s target model assumes:

• $5 billion annualized revenue: 45% to 48% gross margin and 33% to 37% operating margin

• $8 billion annualized revenue: 49% to 52% gross margin and 38% to 42% operating margin

Cloud transceivers, OCS, scale-out CPO, EMLs and DCI products are expected to drive the company toward $2 billion per quarter. Scale-up CPO, additional OCS use cases and the Greensboro fab could provide growth beyond that level.

This is a target model, not formal quarterly guidance. The difference matters. Investors should evaluate each product ramp instead of treating $8 billion of annual revenue as guaranteed.

THE POTENTIAL FCC CATALYST

The latest policy catalyst is the reported US proposal to restrict new Chinese optical transceivers from American data centers. Such a rule could shift demand toward non-Chinese suppliers including Lumentum, Coherent and Applied Optoelectronics.

My read is that this would be positive for $LITE, but not as simple as “Chinese competitors disappear, therefore Lumentum gets everything.” Lumentum also supplies laser components into a global module ecosystem that includes Chinese manufacturers, and non-Chinese suppliers do not currently have enough spare capacity to replace all Chinese volume immediately.

A restriction could improve pricing power and accelerate domestic capacity investment, but a poorly managed transition could also limit the total number of transceivers available to AI customers. It remains a proposed policy, not something I would place inside the core valuation yet.

VALUATION: THE COMPANY IS EXCEPTIONAL, BUT THE STOCK KNOWS

At Friday’s closing price of $890.17, Lumentum carried a market capitalization of approximately $85.6 billion.

Current FactSet consensus estimates call for adjusted EPS of approximately:

• FY2026: $8.23

• FY2027: $18.58

• FY2028: $29.53

That places $LITE at roughly 108 times FY2026 earnings, 48 times FY2027 earnings and 30 times FY2028 earnings.

The FY2027 multiple may appear more reasonable, but it requires adjusted EPS to more than double next year. Even if Lumentum reaches its $8 billion annualized revenue target, the current market capitalization would still represent approximately 10.7 times that future revenue.

This does not automatically make $LITE overvalued. A company growing this quickly, with potential 40% operating margins and exposure to a genuine infrastructure bottleneck, deserves a major premium. It does mean that investors are already paying for a meaningful portion of the OCS and CPO roadmap before those products have reached full-scale production.

The market is no longer asking whether Lumentum can grow. It is asking whether Lumentum can grow even faster than the extremely bullish expectations already embedded in the stock.

THE BULL CASE

The strongest bull argument is that current earnings still exclude most of the future OCS and CPO contribution. Revenue nearly doubled and operating margin reached 32.2% while OCS generated only modest revenue and scale-up CPO remained in its infancy.

Lumentum’s industry framework expects the optical AI market to expand from approximately $18 billion in 2025 to more than $90 billion by 2030. The company has multi-year backlogs, pricing power, a strategic NVIDIA relationship, expanding US manufacturing and exposure across scale-up, scale-out and scale-across networking.

If OCS exceeds a $1 billion run rate, CPO orders convert as expected, 1.6T transceiver margins improve and the Greensboro fab comes online successfully, current earnings estimates could continue moving materially higher.

In that scenario, $LITE is not merely an optical component company. It becomes one of the central infrastructure platforms behind AI networking.

THE BEAR CASE

The bear case begins with valuation. A company can produce excellent results and still generate poor stock returns if expectations were even higher.

Manufacturing execution is the next major risk. Management admits that OCS is operating on a tightrope, laser demand exceeds supply and several critical ramps are happening simultaneously. Delays, yield problems or component shortages could push revenue into later periods.

Customer concentration is also significant. Two unnamed customers represented 26% and 12% of Q3 revenue, respectively. Losing a major program, seeing a customer reduce orders or allowing one customer too much negotiating leverage could materially affect results.

There is also the risk that current shortages lead customers to double-order. If Lumentum, Coherent and other suppliers all add capacity at the same time demand normalizes, pricing and margins could eventually decline.

Finally, CPO timing remains uncertain. Copper may remain competitive longer than expected, hyperscalers may use different architectures and scale-up optical adoption could be delayed. The business could still grow strongly while failing to reach the aggressive timeline currently reflected in the valuation.

WHAT MATTERS IN TOMORROW’S EARNINGS

Lumentum reports fiscal Q4 and full-year 2026 results on Tuesday, August 11, after the market closes. Wall Street expects approximately $990 million of revenue and $2.98 of adjusted EPS, almost exactly at the midpoint of management’s guidance.

Options markets are pricing a move of approximately 13%, so volatility should be expected. However, the Q4 numbers themselves are only part of the story. These are the most important things I will be watching:

Does Q4 merely land inside guidance, or exceed the $1.01 billion high end?

How strong is the Q1 FY2027 revenue and margin outlook?

Is the $400 million-plus second-half OCS ramp still on schedule?

Have additional OCS customers or configurations been secured?

Is meaningful CPO revenue still expected in the December quarter?

Is the multi-hundred-million-dollar CPO order still on track for the first half of 2027?

Is EML demand still more than 30% above available supply?

Are 1.6T yields, margins and internal CW laser integration improving?

How quickly are CapEx, inventory and the diluted share count increasing?

Can management provide a clearer timeline toward the $2 billion quarterly revenue target?

A normal beat may not be enough after the stock’s massive run. Investors will want evidence that FY2027 estimates still need to move materially higher.

MY FINAL VIEW ON $LITE

$LITE is one of the rare AI infrastructure stories where the narrative has already turned into extraordinary financial results. Revenue is nearly doubling, operating margins are expanding rapidly and this is happening before the largest OCS and CPO opportunities have fully entered the numbers.

The long-term bull case is absolutely real. Lumentum owns scarce optical technology, operates inside major AI networking bottlenecks and has received one of the strongest possible validations through NVIDIA’s investment and purchase commitment.

But at an $85 billion valuation, the market is no longer asleep. Investors are already paying for substantial success across products that still need to be manufactured and shipped at unprecedented scale.

I am bullish on Lumentum’s position in the AI optics cycle, but much more price-sensitive on the stock after this run. The easy rerating has already happened. From here, the next leg higher must be earned through flawless execution, continued estimate revisions and visible progress toward the $2 billion quarterly revenue model.

Great company. Incredible opportunity. Very demanding valuation.

That combination is exactly what makes tomorrow’s report so important.
