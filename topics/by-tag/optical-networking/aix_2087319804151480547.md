$LITE KEY READ-THROUGHS FROM LUMENTUM HOLDINGS Q4 FY2026 EARNINGS CALL

Lumentum’s Q4 FY2026 earnings call provides unusually strong confirmation that AI-networking demand is broadening from a conventional pluggable-optics upgrade cycle into a multi-layer infrastructure build encompassing data-center interconnect, coherent transport, 1.6T scale-out networking, optical circuit switching, high-power laser sources, near-packaged optics, co-packaged optics, advanced semiconductor packaging, and higher-density PCB manufacturing. The most important market-level conclusion is that optical and networking content per unit of deployed AI compute is increasing rather than merely following accelerator-unit growth. Distributed data-center construction, higher SerDes speeds, custom AI clusters, and the movement of optical links into domains previously served by copper are simultaneously increasing the number, speed, complexity, and value of optical connections. The highest-conviction near-term beneficiaries are coherent optical-system suppliers, 1.6T switching and connectivity silicon vendors, incumbent InP component suppliers, substrate suppliers, and optical manufacturing providers. The principal negative read-throughs are higher capital intensity for hyperscalers, relative share pressure on late-to-market 1.6T module vendors, and a longer-duration risk to copper interconnect and potentially some electrical-switching content as NPO, CPO, and in-tray OCS architectures enter production from 2028.

DATA CENTER NETWORKING AND OPTICAL TRANSPORT

AI DATA-CENTER INTERCONNECT IS ENTERING A NON-LINEAR CAPACITY CYCLE (READ-THROUGH 1)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: Ciena Corporation (CIEN: United States); Cisco Systems (CSCO: United States); Nokia Corporation (NOK: Finland).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Positive and high for Ciena; positive and moderate for Cisco and Nokia. A secondary negative is potential gross-margin pressure from scarce optical-component inputs and supplier pricing.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Stronger order intake, backlog, and revenue guidance for coherent transport, data-center interconnect, optical amplification, and related network systems over the next several quarters.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: AI infrastructure is creating a structurally larger inter-data-center transport market because compute clusters are increasingly distributed across multiple sites rather than contained within a single campus.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Lumentum reported that narrow-linewidth laser assemblies increased more than 130% year over year and grew sequentially for the 10th consecutive quarter. Pump-laser shipments increased more than 80% year over year, remained effectively sold out, and are expected to increase approximately 4-fold over the next several quarters. Management stated that, for 1 major hyperscaler, “the network capacity connecting just 2 AI data center sites is double the total global backbone capacity they built over the entirety of the last decade.” Lumentum also disclosed multiple predominantly 3-year agreements with network-equipment manufacturers, with many arrangements structured on a take-or-pay basis and containing pricing mechanisms.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: Pump lasers and narrow-linewidth laser assemblies are critical enabling components for optical amplification and coherent transmission. Lumentum’s inability to meet current demand, despite rapid capacity expansion, indicates that equipment-level demand for coherent transport is materially stronger than reported component shipments. The 4-fold planned pump-laser increase is unlikely to be undertaken solely against speculative forecasts because management indicated that network-equipment manufacturers are underwriting portions of the capacity expansion through long-term contractual commitments.

Ciena is the highest-conviction public beneficiary because optical transport and data-center interconnect constitute a much larger percentage of its revenue and earnings base than they do for Cisco or Nokia. Greater inter-site bandwidth should increase demand for coherent line systems, transponders, pluggable coherent optics, optical amplifiers, ROADMs, network-management software, and associated services. The call suggests that the AI-driven transport cycle is moving beyond incremental upgrades to existing backbone capacity and toward construction of dedicated AI interconnect networks whose bandwidth can exceed the scale of prior general-purpose backbone deployments.

Cisco should benefit through its Acacia coherent-optics portfolio and broader routing and optical systems, while Nokia should benefit through its optical-networking portfolio. The impact is diluted at both companies by their broader revenue bases. The call does not identify the specific network-equipment manufacturers that signed the long-term agreements, so the company allocation is an industry read-through rather than confirmation of a specific customer order.

The principal negative is input-cost pressure. Lumentum stated that pricing levers are embedded in the long-term pump-laser agreements and that customers have helped offset the required capital expenditures. This implies that equipment vendors have accepted higher component economics and contractual commitments to secure supply. Gross-margin pressure could therefore accompany stronger revenue, particularly for vendors unable to pass higher optical-component costs through to hyperscale customers. The net read-through remains decisively positive because the implied volume and duration of demand materially outweigh the component-cost headwind.

DISTRIBUTED AI CLUSTERS IMPLY A SECOND WAVE OF FIBER AND CABLE DEMAND (READ-THROUGH 2)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: Corning Incorporated (GLW: United States); Prysmian S.p.A. (PRY: Italy).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Positive and moderate-to-high for Corning; positive and moderate for Prysmian.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Accelerating orders for optical fiber, ribbon cable, high-density data-center cabling, and metro or inter-data-center network deployments as hyperscalers connect new AI sites.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: Regulatory, power, and permitting limitations are encouraging more geographically distributed AI infrastructure, increasing fiber kilometers and optical connectivity per unit of compute capacity.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Lumentum attributed pump-laser demand partly to political and regulatory constraints that favor smaller, more modular data-center builds. Management stated that these distributed facilities require full-rate connectivity between sites and highlighted the example in which the bandwidth connecting only 2 AI data-center locations was twice the hyperscaler’s entire global backbone capacity built over the prior decade.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: A centralized data-center campus can move a substantial amount of traffic over short internal links. A distributed architecture requires that equivalent workloads traverse campus, metro, regional, or long-haul fiber networks. The number of required optical links therefore increases not only with accelerator count, but also with the number of physical locations over which the compute fabric is distributed. Each additional site can require new fiber routes, higher fiber counts, denser cable systems, additional patching and connectivity, and greater route redundancy.

Corning is the clearest beneficiary because its optical-communications business is directly exposed to fiber, cable, and connectivity products used in carrier and data-center networks. The read-through is stronger than a normal increase in data-center traffic because Lumentum’s example implies that individual AI interconnect projects can approach or exceed the bandwidth scale of national or global telecom backbones. Prysmian should benefit from greater demand for fiber-optic cable and network construction, although the effect is less concentrated within the company.

The principal offset is that improvements in coherent spectral efficiency and the availability of existing dark fiber can absorb part of the bandwidth increase without a proportional increase in new fiber kilometers. However, the magnitude of the network-capacity requirement described by Lumentum, combined with the move toward multiple modular sites, makes exclusive reliance on existing routes improbable. The call therefore supports a multi-year fiber-volume and high-density connectivity cycle rather than a temporary inventory replenishment.

THE 1.6T TRANSITION IS ACCELERATING THE SWITCH-SILICON AND NETWORK-SYSTEM UPGRADE CYCLE (READ-THROUGH 3)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: Arista Networks (ANET: United States); Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: United States); Marvell Technology (MRVL: United States); Cisco Systems (CSCO: United States).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Positive and high for Arista and Broadcom; positive and moderate-to-high for Marvell; positive and moderate for Cisco. The longer-duration impact becomes more mixed as optical circuit switching expands.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Faster adoption of 1.6T transceivers through calendar 2027, pulling forward demand for 51.2T and 102.4T switching, 200G-per-lane SerDes, optical DSPs, drivers, switch systems, and associated software.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: AI networks are moving to higher port speeds faster than traditional cloud networks, but scaled OCS adoption may eventually reduce the amount of electrical switching and network overprovisioning required per cluster.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Lumentum reported record 800G shipments, began 1.6T production shipments, and expects “the 1.6T transceiver uptake to intensify, starting in fiscal Q1, and sustain through calendar 2027.” The ramp is anchored by a Tier 1 hyperscaler deploying custom AI clusters. Systems revenue increased 123% year over year, and supply-chain tightness capped shipments below total demand. Separately, OCS shipments doubled sequentially, Q1 guidance includes the first quarter with OCS revenue meaningfully above $100 million, and calendar 2H2026 OCS revenue remains targeted at more than $400 million.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: The transition from 800G to 1.6T requires 200G-per-lane electrical and optical signaling, higher-capacity switch silicon, more advanced signal integrity, and greater DSP and SerDes content. Lumentum’s ramp is therefore a direct demand indicator for the adjacent switching and connectivity semiconductor stack. The fact that 1.6T demand is being driven by custom AI clusters rather than only general-purpose cloud traffic increases the probability that the upgrade occurs at an accelerated cadence and at very high deployment volumes.

Arista should benefit as customers deploy new high-radix, high-bandwidth switches and replace lower-speed network fabrics. Broadcom is positioned to benefit through switch ASICs, SerDes, custom AI silicon, and other connectivity products. Marvell should benefit through optical DSPs, high-speed connectivity silicon, and custom cloud ASIC programs. Cisco benefits through data-center switching and its Acacia optical portfolio, although its broader corporate mix reduces the impact.

The supply-chain comment is also important. Lumentum stated that shipments were capped below total market demand by shortages of certain externally sourced components. This indicates that the observed 1.6T revenue trajectory is supply limited rather than demand limited. A relaxation of those bottlenecks can produce incremental upside across switch systems, DSPs, and optical modules without requiring another acceleration in end demand.

The longer-duration caveat is OCS. Lumentum is already moving OCS from a niche category to quarterly revenue above $100 million and is developing specialized in-tray products for 2028. Optical circuit switches can dynamically reconfigure high-bandwidth paths and reduce the need to overprovision every connection through multiple electrical packet-switching layers. The high-conviction conclusion is not that OCS will replace Ethernet switching, but that OCS may reduce incremental electrical-switch-port intensity in selected AI architectures. This creates a modest terminal-value risk for Arista, Broadcom, and Marvell even while their near-term 1.6T exposure remains strongly positive.

OPTICAL COMPONENTS, MODULES, AND MANUFACTURING

INP LASER SCARCITY VALIDATES A STRUCTURALLY STRONGER COMPONENT CYCLE FOR COHERENT (READ-THROUGH 4)

AFFECTED COMPANY: Coherent Corp. (COHR: United States).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Positive and moderate-to-high for Coherent’s optical-component operations; partially offset by competitive risk in complete transceiver modules.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Higher utilization, stronger pricing, and increased shipment volumes for EMLs, CW lasers, pump lasers, and other differentiated InP products.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: InP lasers are becoming a strategic bottleneck across multiple AI-networking architectures, creating sustained scarcity economics for qualified incumbent suppliers.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Lumentum stated that EML demand remains materially above supply and that the supply-demand imbalance has not improved. Ultra-high-power laser demand has accelerated sufficiently that Lumentum is now “very much further behind” customer demand despite executing its capacity plan. CW lasers are commanding a meaningful price premium because tighter performance distributions improve downstream transceiver yields. Management also stated that customers in a constrained environment will use whichever qualified laser source they can obtain, making architecture selection partly a function of component availability rather than only technical preference.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: The call indicates that scarcity extends across several distinct InP product categories rather than being confined to a single 800G or 1.6T design. EMLs remain constrained, CW-laser economics have improved, pump lasers are sold out, and ultra-high-power lasers have experienced an additional demand acceleration. This breadth supports a stronger and more durable component cycle for Coherent than would be implied by a single transceiver-speed transition.

Coherent is one of the few scaled public suppliers with broad semiconductor-laser, optical-component, and transceiver capabilities. Lumentum’s inability to satisfy demand creates an opportunity for Coherent to absorb incremental customer allocations, improve factory utilization, and capture favorable pricing. The statement that customers will use whichever laser source is available is particularly constructive because it implies that supply scarcity can accelerate qualification and adoption of secondary sources.

The read-through is not uniformly positive across Coherent. Lumentum claims technology and performance differentiation in EMLs, CW lasers, and high-power laser chips, and it is adding capacity aggressively. Coherent must maintain competitive yield, reliability, and power efficiency to capture the available demand. In addition, Lumentum’s early lead in complete 1.6T transceivers creates a separate module-level competitive risk. The net effect remains positive because component scarcity is broad and industry-wide, while the transceiver-share risk appears more customer and product specific.

LUMENTUM’S EARLY 1.6T LEAD IS A RELATIVE NEGATIVE FOR COMPETING MODULE VENDORS (READ-THROUGH 5)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: Coherent Corp. (COHR: United States); Applied Optoelectronics (AAOI: United States); Zhongji Innolight Co. (300308: China); Eoptolink Technology Inc. (300502: China).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Negative and moderate on a relative basis over the next several quarters; absolute industry demand remains positive and limits the downside.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Customer qualification announcements, 1.6T shipment ramps, relative revenue growth, and transceiver gross-margin progression through calendar 2027.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: Early qualification and vertically integrated laser supply can create durable share advantages during the initial phase of high-speed optical transitions, although customer multi-sourcing should reduce concentration over time.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Lumentum stated that it appears to be “first to market in many instances, ahead of larger competitors,” and expects the resulting market-share advantage to be maintained through the cycle. Transceiver profitability is improving through higher yields, better capacity utilization, internally sourced lasers, and the higher ASP of 1.6T products. Management characterized its signal-integrity capabilities as a material differentiator.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: High-volume hyperscale optical qualifications are time consuming because customers must validate signal integrity, thermals, reliability, firmware, interoperability, manufacturing consistency, and field performance. A supplier that qualifies early can secure initial production allocations before competitors complete equivalent validation. Those early allocations are particularly valuable because pricing and margins tend to be strongest during the beginning of a speed transition, when supply is limited and customer urgency is high.

Lumentum’s vertical integration in EML and CW lasers creates an additional advantage. Competing module vendors dependent on externally sourced lasers can face shipment constraints even when their module designs are qualified. Lumentum can allocate internally produced laser capacity to its own transceiver programs, improve yield through tightly controlled component specifications, and capture economics at both the component and module levels.

The read-through is most negative for peers whose earnings expectations assume meaningful initial share at the same Tier 1 customer or customers driving Lumentum’s ramp. The exact competitors and customers were not disclosed, so the conclusion is relative rather than customer specific. Coherent, Applied Optoelectronics, Innolight, and Eoptolink should still benefit from the broad 1.6T market expansion and from customer requirements for second sourcing. The negative implication is that industry growth may not translate into proportional share for every supplier during the highest-margin portion of the cycle.

AXT IS THE MOST DIRECT MATERIALS BENEFICIARY OF THE INP CAPACITY BOTTLENECK (READ-THROUGH 6)

AFFECTED COMPANY: AXT Inc. (AXTI: United States).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Positive and high.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Incremental substrate purchase commitments, expanded customer forecasts, stronger pricing, and the possibility of additional supply arrangements within the next 1 or 2 quarters.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: InP substrates are becoming a strategic limiting input for AI optical infrastructure, increasing the value of qualified substrate capacity and long-term supply agreements.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Lumentum disclosed that the surge in ultra-high-power laser demand drove the company to secure additional substrate support from AXT. Management stated that current supply from a Japanese partner and AXT is sufficient for the moment, but added that, if the demand vector continues, “that may not be true a quarter or 2 from now either.” The substrate requirement spans EMLs, CW lasers, ultra-high-power lasers, and other InP products.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: InP wafer demand rises with the number of laser devices produced, but the growth is amplified when product mix shifts toward additional laser categories and customers. Lumentum is expanding EMLs, CW lasers, pump-related products, mid-power NPO lasers, and ultra-high-power CPO or external-light-source products simultaneously. The resulting substrate requirement is therefore broader and potentially more durable than demand tied to a single module generation.

AXT benefits directly through higher substrate volumes, improved capacity utilization, stronger customer visibility, and potentially better pricing or contractual economics. The read-through is especially material because Lumentum indicated that the AXT arrangement was not simply routine diversification; it was a response to a demand surge that exceeded the prior supply plan. Management’s suggestion that further substrate support may be required within 1 or 2 quarters indicates that the current arrangement may represent the beginning rather than the end of the procurement increase.

The principal risks are production execution, quality qualification, and customer concentration. Substrate supply must meet tight defect, uniformity, and reliability requirements. Any inability to scale qualified output would limit revenue conversion despite strong demand. Nevertheless, the call materially strengthens the case that InP substrate supply is an industry choke point and that AXT has become strategically relevant to one of the largest expanding buyers.

THE INP FAB BUILDOUT IS A MULTI-YEAR POSITIVE FOR MOCVD EQUIPMENT SUPPLIERS (READ-THROUGH 7)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: Veeco Instruments (VECO: United States); AIXTRON SE (AIXA: Germany).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Positive and moderate. The sector read-through is high conviction, while the allocation between individual equipment suppliers is not disclosed.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Reactor orders, backlog additions, installation revenue, and service demand associated with Lumentum’s Japanese wafer-fab expansion and Greensboro conversion.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: AI optical demand is forcing a global expansion of compound-semiconductor capacity, creating a larger installed base of InP epitaxy and process equipment through 2028 and beyond.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Lumentum is expanding capacity across 2 InP wafer fabs in Japan and qualifying CW and EML processes on new tools. The Greensboro facility is being converted from gallium arsenide to indium phosphide, and management specifically identified reactors as long-lead items. Initial Greensboro revenue remains expected in early 2028, followed by a broader ramp through calendar 2028 and into 2029.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: InP laser expansion requires epitaxial growth tools, wafer-processing equipment, metrology, testing, and recurring service. MOCVD reactors are among the most capital-intensive and technically critical elements of the capacity build. Lumentum’s simultaneous investment in Japan and North Carolina indicates that the current cycle is not a minor debottlenecking exercise; it is a multi-region expansion intended to support several years of optical demand.

Veeco and AIXTRON are the most direct public equipment read-throughs because both participate in compound-semiconductor epitaxy and MOCVD equipment. The exact vendor selected by Lumentum was not identified, preventing a definitive company-specific order attribution. However, Lumentum is only 1 participant in a broader industry increase in InP capacity. Coherent, Chinese suppliers, and other optical-component manufacturers are also incentivized to add capacity as demand and pricing rise, increasing the probability of broader tool demand.

The geographic distribution is also significant. Capacity is being added in Japan and the United States rather than concentrated in a single location. This increases total equipment requirements because geographically separated fabs require distinct tool sets, qualification flows, spare parts, and service infrastructure. The installed-base opportunity should therefore extend beyond initial reactor sales into recurring service and replacement demand.
