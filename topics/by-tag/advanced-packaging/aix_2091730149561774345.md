## 原文

Yesterday, @ishmohit1 Sunday Session on the @soicfinance   YouTube channel covered Temsons Instruments and its unique business model.

Here are 10 key takeaways I noted from the session 👇

1. 🔥 Niche business with a niche moat

Tempsens operates in a specialised space covering temperature sensing, industrial heating and specialised cables.

The interesting part is the technical expertise, certifications and qualification requirements needed to compete.

2. 🔥 Backward integration is a big advantage

The company has several manufacturing stages in-house, including alloy processing, wire drawing and final assembly.

This gives better control over quality, cost and supply chain.

3. 📈 Strong financial growth

Revenue increased from ₹138 Cr to ₹445 Cr.

PAT grew from ₹18 Cr to ₹71 Cr, while operating margins remained around 19–22%.

4. 🔥 Recurring demand hidden inside a capital goods business

Temperature sensors may need replacement every 12–24 months.

So despite being an industrial business, part of the revenue has a recurring or consumable nature.

5. 🚀 Make in India can be a major growth trigger

Semiconductors, EV batteries, defence, green hydrogen, nuclear and advanced manufacturing can create significant demand for specialised temperature solutions.

6. 🔥 Capacity utilisation can drive the next phase of growth

Specialised cable capacity is already running at high utilisation, while newer units are being ramped up.

This can support growth without requiring massive fresh capex immediately.

7. ⚠️ Working capital is a major watch-out

Inventory days increased from around 125 to 170 days.

CFO/PAT conversion also declined from roughly 90% to ~60%.

This is something investors should track closely after listing.

8. ⚠️ Forensic red flags should not be ignored

The analysis highlighted the Marathon Heater merger, goodwill creation, promoter-linked transactions and loss-making subsidiaries.

The amounts may be relatively small, but governance still needs monitoring.

9. 🔥 Good business does not automatically mean good investment

The IPO valuation was discussed at around 35x PE.

The business can grow strongly, but investors still need to ask how much of that future growth is already priced in.

10. 🔥 The biggest lesson from Ishmohit

Don't start with:

“Should I buy this stock?”

Start with:

“What does the business do?
Why is it difficult to compete with?
How can it grow?
What can go wrong?
And what price am I paying?”

🔥 A unique business is worth studying.
A unique business at the right valuation is worth owning.
