BREAKING $AMD $TSM 👀👀👀 
TSMC's advanced packaging technology makes another leap forward! He Jun: CoWoS aims for 14 times the mask size by 2029.

The demand for AI computing power is driving the rapid upgrade of advanced packaging. Ho Chun, Vice President of Advanced Packaging Technology and Services at TSMC (2330), stated on the 11th that TSMC's CoWoS has entered high-volume production with 5.5 times the mask size, and the yield rate of many AI customer products exceeds 98%, with some even reaching 99%. As the company maintains its pace of launching a new generation of packaging technology every year, it is expected to further advance to 14 times the mask size by 2029. In terms of capacity, TSMC's CoWoS capacity has almost doubled every year for the past three years, and it currently has 10 advanced packaging plants. The contribution of advanced packaging to the company's operations and profits continues to increase.

In his keynote speech at the OCP APAC Summit, He Jun pointed out that AI's near-endless demand for computing power is driving the rapid growth of CoWoS, SoIC, and 3DIC technologies. AI applications are also gradually expanding from data center training to smartphones, PCs, AR/VR, robots, and automobiles, bringing more diverse requirements for performance, power consumption, size, and reliability. Semiconductor component and system manufacturers must engage in closer collaborative development.

He stated that future advanced AI packaging will be centered around advanced logic chips, combining HBM, Chiplet, SoIC hybrid bonding, active bridging chips, integrated voltage regulators, capacitors, and silicon photonics. The role of the interposer will no longer be limited to providing inter-chip connections, but will gradually integrate computing, power supply, and optical interconnect functions.

He Jun pointed out that electronic signals are suitable for computation, but photonics have a greater advantage in energy efficiency when it comes to high-speed data transmission. Therefore, silicon photonics will become an important direction for the next stage of AI packaging integration. As different functional chips and optoelectronic components are integrated into the same package, advanced packaging also creates more space for system manufacturers to innovate their architectures.

In addition to CoWoS, TSMC is also continuing to advance true 3D stacking of SoIC. Ho Chun said that SoIC hybrid bonding can provide more than 50 times the interconnect density and about 5 times the energy efficiency. Since last year, TSMC's 6-micron pitch hybrid bonding technology has entered high-volume production, and it is expected to further introduce about 4.5-micron pitch technology by 2029, and move towards advanced chip stacking such as A14 to A14.

He further explained that the benefits of Chiplet not only break through the limitations of single-chip photomask size, but also separate analog and computational blocks, reducing the cost of simultaneously migrating all functions to the most advanced process. Through die selection, matching, and algorithms, Chiplet can also reduce performance fluctuations caused by process variations, improve performance consistency across the packaging layer, and increase die utilization.

However, the continuous increase in package size also brings new challenges to process and system reliability. He Jun pointed out that different logic chips, HBMs, interposers, and substrates have different thermal and mechanical properties. The higher the integration density, the more obvious the differences in thermal expansion, stress, and warpage between materials become. AI data centers use a large number of parallel computing components. In order to keep the overall failure rate within an acceptable range, the quality of individual packaged components must even exceed automotive-grade standards.

He Jun emphasized that component-level certification is no longer sufficient to support the next stage of AI systems, and the industry must accelerate the adoption of System Technology Co-optimization (STCO). In the past, there have been cases where chips performed normally during the packaging and testing stage, but after being installed on different PCBs and systems, they experienced chip edge cracking due to differences in board-level structure, passive component configuration, and mechanical stress. This shows that the verification scope must be extended from chips and packaging to PCBs, SMT, heat dissipation, and the overall system.
