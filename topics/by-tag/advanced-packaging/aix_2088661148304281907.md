## 原文

I posted $KLIC publicly on 7/24, so I’m going to keep posting this one publicly.

$KLIC had a solid Q3 on 8/5, but it’s still sitting down here relatively slept on. Revenue came in at $330.4M vs. $312M expected, adj. EPS was $1.20 vs. $1.01 expected, and Q4 guidance came in well above expectations at $375M ± $20M revenue and $1.42 ± 10% adj. EPS.

What interests me is the positioning. Kulicke & Soffa sits in the backend of the semiconductor supply chain, providing the equipment used for wire bonding, advanced packaging, die attach and other critical assembly processes.

Think $FORM, $UCTT, $ICHR, $ASYS, $ACMR. These are just tastes of the different ideas you can expect in subs.

Sharing entries and exits is great, but finding opportunities that aren’t the same crowded $NBIS $ARM $MSFT $NVDA $MU ideas matters too.

BE ORIGINAL ON HERE GANG.

$KLIC has dominant positioning in traditional wire bonding while aggressively expanding into advanced packaging, particularly fluxless thermocompression bonding. 

That gives it exposure to both the cyclical semiconductor recovery AND the structural AI/data center packaging buildout.

Financially, the recovery is moving fast. Nine-month FY26 revenue is already around $773M, while Q3 revenue jumped 36.2% sequentially and 122.6% YoY. 

Gross margin reached 47.8%, operating income was $68.3M, non-GAAP net income was $64.2M and adjusted FCF was $41M. KLIC also ended the quarter with roughly $516.6M in cash and short-term investments.

Q4 guidance calls for another step up to $375M ± $20M revenue, ~48% gross margin and $1.42 ± 10% non-GAAP EPS. That puts the business on a very different trajectory than the trough investors were valuing it against.

The demand side is what really keeps me interested. Management said purchase orders are already extending into Q2 FY27, which is unusual for KLIC, while utilization is >95% in China and around 90% globally across general semiconductor and memory.

Traditional wire-bonding capacity has been increased roughly 4x over the last couple quarters, while Advanced Solutions/TCB is targeting >$100M in FY26 and roughly $150–200M in FY27.

KLIC’s ball-bonding business still provides the cash-generating foundation, but TCB, hybrid bonding, HBM, chiplets and heterogeneous integration create a much larger opportunity as AI accelerators and data-center infrastructure become increasingly packaging-intensive.

KLIC is estimated to hold >60% share in traditional ball bonding, giving it a huge installed base and recurring aftermarket opportunity through tools, spares, upgrades and services.

This is the niche I think the market is still underappreciating: KLIC isn’t making the GPU, but it provides critical equipment that helps turn those increasingly complex chips into finished products.

Technically, I’m watching the ~$96 rising 100-MA closely. We’re holding that area with improving volume, rising MACD and BB %B.

The next level I want to see reclaimed is ~$108, the daily 50-MA.

Hold the 100-MA and reclaim $108 with confirmation, and this setup gets much more interesting.

$KLIC is exactly the kind of overlooked semiconductor infrastructure name I’m talking about.
