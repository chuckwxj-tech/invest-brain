## 原文

The custom AI silicon market is undergoing a seismic shift: CoWoS-S packaging bottlenecks are forcing TPU cuts at Broadcom ($AVGO ), while AMD ($AMD ) breaks into Google’s ($GOOGL ) TPU v10 roadmap using 3D SoIC chiplets.

Here is what this hardware shakeup means for the custom ASIC landscape, and why Indium Phosphide (InP) optical infrastructure is the ultimate bottleneck winner:

1/ Broadcom's Packaging Wall ($AVGO )
CoWoS-S constraints are forcing $AVGO  to revise down 2H26 TPU v7x Ironwood output by ~500k units (down to 2.7M) and lower 2027 targets. Broadcom is reallocating scarce wafer capacity to Meta’s MTIA 400 Iris (55k wafers) and OpenAI’s Jalapeno. Pure compute scale is hitting a physical packaging wall.

2/ AMD’s Strategic Breakout ($AMD)
Google ($GOOGL) collaborating with AMD on TPU v10 breaks Broadcom’s historical ASIC monopoly. AMD wins on technical stack: its 3D packaging (SoIC) and x86 CPU IP allow direct integration of on-package CPU cores onto TPUs, a game changer for complex reinforcement learning (RL) pre-processing.

3/ The InP Optical Inflection
As compute silicon fragments into heterogeneous, multi-chip architectures, copper reaches its physical limit. Offloading workloads across 3D-stacked ASIC clusters requires massive bandwidth scale-out, driving demand for Indium Phosphide (InP) lasers (EMLs/CW) and high-speed transceivers.

4/ Raw Materials Control the Margin
Dense, multi-vendor custom silicon means no single chipmaker locks in interconnect protocols. The bottleneck shifts upstream: refined minor metal inputs (like high-purity InP and Germanium substrates) dictate feasibility. Upstream material control and epiwafer supply will capture the structural value as AI clusters scale.
