## 原文

Everyone keeps calling $COHR and $LITE the same AI-optics trade. That’s lazy.

The latest numbers are already in. The real question is what comes next.

$COHR is breadth. Q4 revenue hit $2.05B; Datacenter & Communications reached $1.62B (+59% YoY, 79% of sales). It owns more of the stack—from InP and lasers to transceivers, CPO and OCS—so it can win even if the architecture changes.

$LITE is torque. Q4 revenue hit $1.01B (+109% YoY), non-GAAP gross margin reached 50.4%, and Q1 guidance implies ~24% sequential growth at the midpoint. 1.6T has begun shipping; OCS, CPO lasers and ELS/NPO are the optionality.

The bigger industry shift: pluggables fund today’s boom, but the next profit pool is moving upstream into light sources and sideways into optical switching. Faster modules alone won’t solve rack-scale bandwidth, power and reconfiguration.

Breadth = COHR.
Torque = LITE.

I’m bullish on both—but not blindly.

COHR’s FY26 inventory hit $2.58B while operating cash flow was only $79.5M against $1.10B of PP&E additions. That capacity ramp needs to become cash, not stranded equipment.

LITE has sharper upside, but also concentrated customers, real dilution and more value riding on future OCS/CPO execution.

My forward read: if AI optics keeps broadening, COHR is the safer architecture bet. If 1.6T + OCS + external lasers ramp on time, LITE can outperform.

I’d own both—with the smaller position in LITE. 👀
