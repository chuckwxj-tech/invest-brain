## 原文

Two things the glass substrate delays are actually telling us.

1. $Samsung's slip is worse than the headline.

The Elec / DigiTimes: GlaSSEM's *line start* moved 2H27 -> 2028. Mass production is now "after 2028."

The real tell isn't guidance, it's POs — equipment orders pushed at least 3x
(Dec -> Mar -> Jun), and as of Aug 12 suppliers still have no order schedule.

A line that hasn't ordered tools in 2026 doesn't mass produce in 2028.

2. These are probably not two delays. They're one.

SEMCO has been sampling glass core to Broadcom since last year — its largest
potential customer. Absolics' non-embedding samples went to a US comms chipmaker.

Same gatekeeper. Same reliability bar.

One customer's qual standard is setting the industry clock. Single point of
failure — and single point of unlock.

What actually failed sits downstream of the tools.

Nobody is failing at drilling glass. LIDE is qualified. E&R shipped to a US
customer that completed validation. Philoptics supplies both Koreans.

The bottleneck is package-level reliability — CTE mismatch and warpage on >500mm
panels, metallization of sub-10um vias.

So $LPK / Philoptics / E&R volume is gated by their customers' qual, not their
own readiness. Step function, not a ramp.

LPKF is honest about it: FY26 guide EUR 105-120m excludes high-volume AP orders.
H1 revenue -38% YoY. That makes it a pure timeline option — which means timeline
news *is* the fundamental.

The anchor never moved.

$TSM CoPoS: 310x310mm, pilot 2027, volume 2H28. TrendForce still sees glass core
at commercial scale post-2030.

Korea sliding into 2027-28 isn't divergence from the roadmap. It's convergence
to it.

On Corning — wrong vehicle, both directions.

$GLW booked $2.07B of optical comms sales in Q2 alone (+32% YoY, $438M segment
NI). The entire glass-core substrate market is ~$200M for all of 2026.

Corning sells low-CTE panels to whoever wins (~25% of panel supply), sells
carriers that ramp whether the core ends up glass or organic, and sells glass
bridge / TGV into CPO.

A glass delay defers option value. It doesn't touch the P&L.

Who gets paid on delay: the ABF incumbents. Ibiden put ¥500B into AI substrate
capacity in February. And the binding constraint into 2027 is T-glass cloth, not
glass core.

And the clock China is running: TrendForce counted 18 Chinese firms on Aug 7.
BOE — CNY 993M pilot line, says it has delivered 20-layer large-format glass core
prototypes to leading AI chip vendors.

What changes my mind, in order:

— GlaSSEM actually issues equipment POs (watch Philoptics / Chemtronics
disclosures, not press releases)
— SKC Q3 call confirms the Taiwan package-level eval passed
— LPKF folds high-volume AP orders into guidance
— Broadcom puts glass on a public packaging roadmap

Until then, "imminent" is a direction, not a date.
