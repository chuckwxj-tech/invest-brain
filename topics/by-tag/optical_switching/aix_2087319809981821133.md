OUTSOURCED OPTICAL MANUFACTURING SHOULD BENEFIT FROM THE OCS AND 1.6T VOLUME INFLECTION (READ-THROUGH 8)

AFFECTED COMPANY: Fabrinet (FN: Thailand).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Positive and moderate. The relationship is an industry inference because Lumentum did not identify its contract-manufacturing partners.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Additional contract-manufacturing capacity for OCS, increased 1.6T transceiver volumes, and higher optical assembly and test requirements through calendar 2027.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: Optical systems are becoming more integrated and manufacturing intensive, increasing the strategic value of high-yield, high-complexity optical assembly and test capabilities.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Lumentum doubled OCS shipments sequentially and expects Q1 OCS revenue to be meaningfully above $100 million. Management has begun adding OCS capacity with contract manufacturers while continuing to expand internal factories. The 1.6T transceiver ramp is expected to intensify immediately and persist through calendar 2027. Lumentum has also received its first order for a complete external-light-source module, which carries substantially more assembly content and a meaningfully higher ASP than the underlying laser chips.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: Rapid optical-product ramps often require outsourced assembly because internal capacity cannot be expanded quickly enough without creating excess fixed costs or execution risk. OCS systems require optical, mechanical, electronic, and software integration, while 1.6T transceivers require precision alignment, high-speed electrical performance, thermal management, calibration, and extensive testing. External-light-source modules add another layer of packaging and system-level assembly.

Fabrinet is the clearest public read-through because its manufacturing model is heavily exposed to complex optical products and high-mix, high-reliability assembly. Incremental OCS outsourcing and sustained 1.6T growth should increase factory utilization, revenue, and potentially mix. The opportunity becomes larger if external-light-source modules and NPO-related products transition from initial orders to volume production.

The main qualification is that Lumentum did not name the contract manufacturer. The read-through therefore depends on Fabrinet or comparable suppliers winning the actual programs. The profitability of the outsourced volume also may not equal the profitability of the end product because Lumentum retains the highest-value laser and system intellectual property. The expected impact is therefore stronger on Fabrinet’s revenue and utilization than on its gross-margin percentage.

HYPERSCALE COMPUTE, ADVANCED PACKAGING, AND INTERCONNECT ARCHITECTURE

AI NETWORKING IS BECOMING A LARGER AND MORE PERSISTENT HYPERSCALER FREE-CASH-FLOW BURDEN (READ-THROUGH 9)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: Microsoft Corporation (MSFT: United States); Alphabet Inc. (GOOGL: United States); https://t.co/SpqvHNV5fi Inc. (AMZN: United States); Meta Platforms (META: United States); Oracle Corporation (ORCL: United States).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Negative and moderate for near-term free cash flow and capital intensity; positive and moderate-to-high for long-term AI infrastructure capability and revenue capacity.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Upward revisions to data-center and networking capital-expenditure expectations, higher supplier prepayments or contractual commitments, and potentially slower free-cash-flow conversion.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: Networking is becoming a co-equal constraint with compute and power, requiring hyperscalers to sustain elevated investment in optical transport, switching, OCS, and scale-up interconnect for longer than a normal server-replacement cycle.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: The bandwidth connecting only 2 AI data-center sites for 1 hyperscaler was described as twice the total global backbone capacity built by that customer over the prior decade. The 1.6T ramp is being driven by custom AI clusters. Network-equipment customers are helping offset Lumentum’s capacity expenditures and entering multi-year, frequently take-or-pay arrangements. OCS demand is expected to remain exceptionally strong in 2027, while NPO and CPO scale-up deployment is expected to begin around 2028.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: Hyperscaler AI capital spending is commonly analyzed through accelerator, server, power, and construction expenditures. Lumentum’s call indicates that optical networking is becoming an additional major capital layer. Distributed AI sites require extensive inter-data-center transport. Higher accelerator densities require faster scale-out links. Multi-rack scale-up architectures require new optical-engine and external-light-source content. OCS adds another dedicated switching layer.

The need for customers to support supplier capacity and accept take-or-pay structures indicates that hyperscalers or their equipment vendors are committing capital before the final systems are deployed. This improves supply assurance but reduces capital flexibility and can create cash outflows ahead of revenue realization. It also indicates that networking investment is unlikely to fall quickly even if accelerator procurement growth moderates, because the existing installed compute base still requires additional connectivity.

The call does not identify the hyperscaler customers, so no specific company should be treated as the disclosed counterparty. The read-through applies to the group. Companies executing the most aggressive custom AI-cluster and multi-site buildouts face the greatest near-term free-cash-flow drag. The long-term benefit is that superior networking improves accelerator utilization, enables larger training and inference clusters, and increases the amount of usable compute that can be monetized. The equity implication is therefore negative for near-term cash-flow estimates but positive for the durability and competitive defensibility of AI infrastructure.

OPTICAL SCALE-UP VALIDATES NEXT-GENERATION ACCELERATOR ROADMAPS, BUT THE MATERIAL REVENUE EVENT IS 2028 RATHER THAN 2026 (READ-THROUGH 10)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA: United States); Advanced Micro Devices (AMD: United States); Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: United States); Marvell Technology (MRVL: United States); Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. (TSM: Taiwan); GlobalFoundries Inc. (GFS: United States); ASE Technology Holding Co. (ASX: Taiwan).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Positive and moderate-to-high over the longer term; neutral for near-term accelerator earnings and negative for expectations of material 2026 CPO or NPO revenue.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Engineering milestones, customer qualifications, initial external-light-source shipments in calendar 2H2027, and disclosures regarding next-generation silicon incorporating optical scale-up interfaces.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: Optical scale-up should enable larger multi-rack accelerator domains while increasing semiconductor, photonics, interposer, and advanced-packaging content per AI system.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Lumentum stated that NPO is not being retrofitted onto currently shipping XPUs. Adoption is associated with new GPUs, XPUs, TPUs, SerDes generations, racks, and multi-rack systems expected to reach the market around 2028. Each NPO optical engine was described as supporting approximately 6.4T of bandwidth, equivalent to 4 1.6T modules. CPO remains the expected end state and requires “foundry-level advanced packaging.” Lumentum expects high-volume laser shipments in calendar 2H2027 ahead of customer deployments in 2028 and has received its first external-light-source module order for delivery in calendar 2H2027.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: Current copper-based scale-up links become increasingly difficult to extend as lane speeds rise because power consumption, insertion loss, signal integrity, and reach deteriorate. Moving optical engines closer to the accelerator allows larger scale-up domains to span multiple racks without requiring prohibitively power-intensive electrical connections. This should improve cluster size, accelerator utilization, system bandwidth, and the economic value of next-generation AI platforms.

NVIDIA and AMD benefit because optical scale-up can support larger connected accelerator systems and increase system-level content. Broadcom and Marvell benefit through custom AI ASICs, SerDes, DSP, switching, and optical-connectivity silicon. TSMC and GlobalFoundries benefit from silicon-photonics and advanced-packaging requirements, while ASE benefits from higher packaging, assembly, and test complexity.

The timing clarification is equally important. Lumentum explicitly rejected the notion that NPO or CPO could be added to existing XPU generations. The material deployment event is tied to new silicon and system architectures arriving around 2028. Expectations for large CPO or NPO revenue contributions in calendar 2026 or early 2027 should therefore be reduced. Initial orders and engineering revenue may appear earlier, but they should not be confused with high-volume production.

The architecture is also likely to be heterogeneous. Optical interfaces may become more standardized, while physical implementation remains proprietary to each customer’s rack and system design. This favors suppliers with broad custom-silicon, packaging, and design capabilities and makes the opportunity less likely to become a fully commoditized standard module market in its early years.

OPTICS MOVING INTO THE COPPER DOMAIN CREATES A 2028 TERMINAL-VALUE RISK FOR ACTIVE ELECTRICAL CABLES (READ-THROUGH 11)

AFFECTED COMPANY: Credo Technology Group Holding Ltd. (CRDO: United States).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Negative and moderate for active electrical cable exposure over the longer term; limited near-term impact and partially offset by optical DSP and SerDes opportunities.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Minimal. Lumentum stated that NPO and CPO will not be retrofitted onto current accelerator generations, limiting immediate substitution risk.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: Optical engines placed adjacent to XPUs can eliminate or shorten high-speed copper links across racks, reducing the addressable market for some active electrical cable and retimer applications from 2028.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Lumentum described NPO and CPO as architectures that allow “optics to begin to penetrate the copper domain.” NPO places optical engines on the board next to the accelerator, while CPO places optics directly on the substrate or interposer. Management indicated that broad optical scale-up adoption is associated with new 2028 processors and rack architectures rather than existing products.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: Credo’s active electrical cables address short-reach, high-speed connectivity where passive copper becomes insufficient but optics remain comparatively expensive or power intensive. As SerDes speeds increase, the technically viable distance for electrical links contracts. NPO and CPO reduce the electrical distance between the accelerator and the optical conversion point, allowing optical links to replace copper across longer intra-rack and inter-rack reaches.

The near-term impact should remain limited because current accelerator platforms are not being redesigned retroactively. Existing active electrical cable deployments and current-generation rack architectures should continue through their normal product cycles. The material risk begins when next-generation systems are designed from inception around optical scale-up.

The company-level impact is moderated by Credo’s exposure to optical DSPs, SerDes intellectual property, and other high-speed connectivity products that can participate in the optical transition. The call is therefore not a uniformly negative read-through for Credo. It is specifically negative for terminal-value assumptions that extrapolate active electrical cable growth indefinitely across future multi-rack AI scale-up networks. The appropriate implication is a lower long-term growth ceiling for copper cable content, partially offset by greater optical and SerDes content.

ADVANCED PCB AND ELECTRONICS MANUFACTURING

AI ACCELERATOR AND 1.6T BOARD COMPLEXITY IS SUPPORTING A HIGHER-VALUE PCB MIX (READ-THROUGH 12)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: TTM Technologies (TTMI: United States); Unimicron Technology Corp. (3037: Taiwan); Compeq Manufacturing Co. (2313: Taiwan); Gold Circuit Electronics Ltd. (2368: Taiwan).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Positive and moderate.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Higher demand for advanced AI accelerator boards and 1.6T optical-module PCBs, improved high-end capacity utilization, and favorable mix toward higher-layer-count and higher-density products.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: Rising accelerator power, I/O density, and optical bandwidth require more microvias, tighter geometries, higher layer counts, and more demanding manufacturing tolerances, increasing PCB value per AI system.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Lumentum stated that its industrial-laser business is seeing increased adoption of ultra-fast lasers for high-density PCB via drilling used in advanced AI XPU boards and 1.6T optical modules.

ANALYSIS AND TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: Laser via drilling is required when PCB feature sizes and interconnect densities exceed the practical limits of conventional mechanical drilling. Greater accelerator I/O, memory bandwidth, power delivery, and optical connectivity increase the number of layers, blind and buried vias, microvias, and high-speed signal structures required on each board. The direct result is greater manufacturing complexity, longer process flows, higher capital intensity, and a larger revenue opportunity per unit.

TTM, Unimicron, Compeq, and Gold Circuit are positioned to benefit through higher demand for advanced, high-layer-count, and high-density-interconnect boards. The economic benefit should appear through both volume and mix. Suppliers capable of maintaining yields at tighter specifications can gain share and command higher pricing, while less advanced producers may be unable to participate.

The reference to both AI XPU boards and 1.6T optical modules indicates that the PCB-content increase is occurring across multiple layers of the AI system rather than only on the accelerator motherboard. Faster optical modules require more complex electrical interfaces, power delivery, thermal management, and high-frequency signal integrity. The call therefore supports a multi-year high-end PCB cycle tied to AI-network complexity, not merely to accelerator-unit shipments.
