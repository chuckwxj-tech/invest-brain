Wafers for tomorrow's invisible components

This week, PLANOPTIK AG published its 2026 half-year financial report – a micro-cap still unknown to many, but operating at a fascinating intersection.

🔬 𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗣𝗟𝗔𝗡𝗢𝗣𝗧𝗜𝗞 𝗱𝗼𝗲𝘀
The company develops, manufactures, and markets wafers made of glass, glass-silicon combinations, and quartz for microsystems technology – as well as the microstructured elements produced from them. Application fields include health care (micro-dosing systems, micro-inhalers, lab-on-chips), automotive, aerospace, and consumer electronics. The wafers are thinned, polished to sub-nanometer surface smoothness, and fitted with microstructures.
🌐 𝗚𝗹𝗮𝘀𝘀 𝗮𝘀 𝗮 𝗺𝗮𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗶𝗮𝗹
What's interesting is the context in which PLANOPTIK operates. Glass is currently experiencing a genuine surge of attention in the semiconductor industry – as a carrier material for advanced packaging in AI chips. Organic substrates are hitting physical limits with ever larger, hotter AI accelerators: warping, thermal instability, limited wiring density. Glass substrates are seen as a solution – offering better flatness, lower dielectric loss, and CTE values that can be matched to silicon. Intel, Samsung, AMD, TSMC, Corning, AT&S, and SCHOTT are investing heavily; first pilot production runs are underway in 2026, with volume production expected from 2027/2028, at growth rates of over 60% CAGR in some cases through the 2030s. This isn't PLANOPTIK's immediate business area – glass interposers for AI chips are a different application than microfluidic wafers for medical technology – but it shows: material expertise in "glass as a semiconductor carrier material" is becoming more relevant industry-wide.

📊 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗻𝘂𝗺𝗯𝗲𝗿𝘀
Group revenue rose 5.7% to €6.24 million in the first half of 2026. EBITDA improved from €900k to €980k, EBIT from €185k to €228k. Growth was driven primarily by key existing customers having completed their inventory drawdown and increased their order volumes.
🏗️ 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗹 𝘀𝘁𝗼𝗿𝘆
PLANOPTIK is preparing wafer-based series production of components with several new customers. From 2027 onward, a significant increase in group revenue is expected to follow. Accordingly, the company is investing heavily: workforce expansion, two new cleanrooms in Germany and Hungary. For 2026, revenue growth of over €1 million as well as a marked improvement in EBT are expected.

𝙈𝙮 𝘾𝙤𝙣𝙘𝙡𝙪𝙨𝙞𝙤𝙣:
PLANOPTIK has been listed on the Regulated Market since September 2025, but with trading volumes in the stock having risen significantly since then. The current numbers are unspectacular, and the company is deliberately investing in capacity and customer relationships with effect from 2027. Anyone who finds the structural rise in importance of glass as a material in microelectronics and medical technology compelling should at least keep this stock on their radar.
