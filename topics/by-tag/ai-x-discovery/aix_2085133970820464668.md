$VIAV KEY READ-THROUGHS FROM VIAVI SOLUTIONS Q4 FY2026 EARNINGS CALL

Viavi Solutions’ Q4 FY2026 call provided one of the clearest bottom-up confirmations to date that AI-networking investment is broadening from semiconductor development and hyperscaler architecture design into optical-component manufacturing, production test, network deployment, and field monitoring. The most important signal was not Viavi’s 69.2% year-over-year NSE revenue growth, which included acquired Spirent revenue, but management’s disclosure that the data-center business excluding Spirent more than doubled year over year and that lab and production products continued to generate double-digit sequential growth in absolute revenue dollars. Data center now represents approximately 50% of NSE revenue, or approximately 40% of total company revenue, and production was identified as the strongest source of incremental growth. The call therefore supports a constructive read-through for optical-component manufacturers, high-speed networking vendors, connector and fiber suppliers, and test-equipment providers exposed to 800G, 1.6T, co-packaged optics, and optical circuit switching. It also indicates that the current cycle has greater duration than a single-speed transition because 400G, 800G, and 1.6T are expected to coexist for several years. The principal negative implication is that the same production-capacity expansion supporting near-term equipment demand could eventually create optical-component oversupply, price pressure, and a more severe digestion phase. A second negative signal is the continued absence of a meaningful wireless recovery, reinforcing a bifurcated communications-equipment market in which AI data-center optical spending remains strong while conventional service-provider wireless capital expenditure remains subdued.

AI OPTICAL COMPONENTS AND MODULE MANUFACTURING

AI OPTICAL PRODUCTION CAPACITY IS STILL EXPANDING RAPIDLY, SUPPORTING OPTICAL TRANSCEIVER AND COMPONENT SUPPLIERS (READ-THROUGH 1)

Affected companies: Coherent Corp. (COHR: US), Lumentum Holdings Inc. (LITE: US), Fabrinet (FN: Cayman Islands), Applied Optoelectronics Inc. (AAOI: US), Innolight and Eoptolink supply-chain participants, Amphenol Corp. (APH: US), and Marvell Technology Inc. (MRVL: US)

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, high magnitude for the optical-component and module supply chain; positive near-term trading catalyst and positive medium-duration fundamental read-through.

Supporting commentary and data: Viavi stated that its data-center business excluding Spirent “more than doubled” year over year. Management identified production as the largest source of incremental growth and said that “what’s growing really, really fast is the production piece of it.” The production opportunity spans “making fiber optic modules,” “making fiber-optic cables,” and emerging CPO test applications. Management also stated that lab and production revenue was generating “double-digit revenue growth in absolute dollars quarter-over-quarter.”

Transmission mechanism: Production-test equipment is generally purchased when optical-module manufacturers add capacity, qualify new module architectures, increase test parallelism, or replace existing test stations for higher speeds. Viavi’s acceleration therefore indicates that optical vendors are not merely conducting laboratory development for future products; manufacturing capacity is being installed or upgraded now. This supports continued unit growth for lasers, photonic integrated circuits, optical engines, DSPs, drivers, transimpedance amplifiers, connectors, and assembled modules.

Coherent and Lumentum benefit through increased demand for lasers, modulators, photonic components, and optical subsystems. Fabrinet benefits because higher optical-module production volumes increase outsourced manufacturing and advanced-packaging requirements. Applied Optoelectronics benefits where hyperscaler and data-center customers increase purchases of high-speed transceivers. Marvell benefits through electro-optical DSP content and custom connectivity silicon. Amphenol benefits through high-speed interconnect and fiber connectivity content.

The non-consensus aspect is that Viavi’s strongest growth is occurring in production rather than only in R&D laboratories. Production test is a stronger near-term indicator of actual component shipments and capacity deployment than prototype announcements or technology demonstrations. It suggests that optical demand is advancing beyond design activity into monetizable manufacturing volume.

The principal limitation is that production-test demand can temporarily lead end-unit shipments. Manufacturers may install capacity in anticipation of demand, creating a lag between equipment purchases and utilization. Nevertheless, the breadth and sequential acceleration described by Viavi support a high-conviction positive read-through for the next several quarters.

1.6T IS RAMPING, BUT 800G IS NOT APPROACHING AN ABRUPT CLIFF (READ-THROUGH 2)

Affected companies: Coherent Corp. (COHR: US), Lumentum Holdings Inc. (LITE: US), Fabrinet (FN: Cayman Islands), Applied Optoelectronics Inc. (AAOI: US), Marvell Technology Inc. (MRVL: US), Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: US), Credo Technology Group Holding Ltd. (CRDO: Cayman Islands), and Astera Labs Inc. (ALAB: US)

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, medium-to-high magnitude; positive near-term catalyst for 1.6T exposure and positive longer-duration support for 800G-linked revenue.

Supporting commentary and data: Viavi stated that “800 is still the biggest driver, but 1.6 is ramping very quickly.” Management expects 1.6T to approach parity with 800G during 2027, after which 1.6T should become larger. However, management also emphasized that “it’s going to be multiple nodes in parallel running” and that 800G will remain a meaningful driver because of the large installed base. Even 400G is expected to persist for an extended period.

Transmission mechanism: A multi-node environment is favorable for the optical supply chain because it reduces the risk that 1.6T cannibalization produces an immediate collapse in 800G revenue. New AI clusters and leading-edge fabrics increasingly adopt 1.6T, while existing data centers continue deploying, upgrading, maintaining, and expanding 800G and 400G infrastructure. Suppliers can therefore monetize multiple generations simultaneously.

Broadcom and Marvell benefit from the switch-silicon, DSP, and connectivity transition toward 1.6T. Credo and Astera Labs benefit from increasing bandwidth density, signal-integrity challenges, retimers, active electrical cables, and connectivity complexity surrounding high-speed fabrics. Optical vendors benefit from a broader product portfolio rather than a single replacement cycle.

The key investment implication is that the market should avoid treating 1.6T as a simple substitution cycle. Viavi explicitly distinguished technology transition from capacity expansion. The greatest industry growth will occur where new capacity, higher port counts, additional test insertions, and broader customer adoption supplement the speed transition. Companies with exposure to both 800G and 1.6T are therefore better positioned than suppliers dependent on a rapid 1.6T-only ramp.

The negative counterpoint is that some 1.6T demand will substitute for 800G. Suppliers with narrow product portfolios or poor 1.6T qualification could experience share loss even as the overall market grows. The favorable industry read-through does not imply uniform performance across vendors.

OPTICAL MODULE CAPACITY EXPANSION RAISES A LONGER-DURATION OVERSUPPLY RISK (READ-THROUGH 3)

Affected companies: Coherent Corp. (COHR: US), Lumentum Holdings Inc. (LITE: US), Applied Optoelectronics Inc. (AAOI: US), Fabrinet (FN: Cayman Islands), and lower-cost Asian optical-module manufacturers

Directional impact and magnitude: Negative, medium magnitude over a longer duration; limited near-term trading relevance but material 2027-2028 fundamental risk if capacity additions outpace port demand.

Supporting commentary and data: Viavi described extremely rapid production-test growth across optical modules, cables, and emerging architectures. Management linked its opportunity directly to manufacturing capacity: “For production, you’re looking really at the capacity being in place or capacity being replaced because that’s what’s ultimately driving your dollars.”

Transmission mechanism: Viavi benefits when customers add optical manufacturing capacity regardless of whether that capacity ultimately operates at full utilization. For component and module vendors, however, broad-based capacity expansion can eventually produce excess supply, lower utilization, shorter lead times, and aggressive price competition. The same indicator that is positive for test-equipment vendors can become a warning signal for manufacturers if installed capacity grows faster than hyperscaler optical-port demand.

Coherent, Lumentum, and Applied Optoelectronics face potential gross-margin pressure if module and component supply expands ahead of demand. Fabrinet is partly insulated because it can gain outsourced manufacturing share even in a competitive market, but excessive customer inventory or utilization declines would still affect production volumes. Lower-cost Asian suppliers may intensify pricing pressure as 800G manufacturing matures and 1.6T qualification broadens.

This is not an immediate bearish signal. Viavi’s current commentary indicates strong demand and active production expansion, not a downturn. The read-through becomes important because optical cycles have historically transitioned from constrained supply to overcapacity relatively quickly. The most relevant leading indicators will be equipment utilization, module lead times, cancellation rates, average selling prices, inventory days, and the divergence between production capacity and hyperscaler deployment volumes.

CO-PACKAGED OPTICS, OPTICAL SWITCHING, AND ADVANCED PACKAGING

CO-PACKAGED OPTICS IS ADVANCING INTO EARLY COMMERCIAL DEPLOYMENT, SUPPORTING SWITCH SILICON, PHOTONICS, AND ADVANCED PACKAGING (READ-THROUGH 4)

Affected companies: Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: US), Marvell Technology Inc. (MRVL: US), Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. Ltd. (TSM: Taiwan), ASE Technology Holding Co. Ltd. (ASX: Taiwan), Amkor Technology Inc. (AMKR: US), Coherent Corp. (COHR: US), Lumentum Holdings Inc. (LITE: US), and Teradyne Inc. (TER: US)

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, high strategic magnitude but moderate near-term earnings magnitude; positive longer-duration fundamental shift with an emerging near-term catalyst.

Supporting commentary and data: Viavi rejected the view that CPO progress is materially slowing, stating that “CPO and all that thing is moving forward.” Management added that the program is “progressing and I have POs to show for that.” Initial CPO revenue was already beginning in the current quarter, with acceleration expected in the December quarter. Management emphasized that yield-management challenges require testing of optical engines, substrates, and integrated assemblies.

Transmission mechanism: CPO places optical engines adjacent to or within the switch package, reducing electrical reach and power consumption but increasing packaging and test complexity. Commercial deployment requires high-performance switch silicon, photonic engines, advanced substrates, precise assembly, thermal management, known-good-die processes, and optical validation at multiple manufacturing stages.

Broadcom is the most direct beneficiary because it is a leading supplier of high-performance Ethernet switch silicon and has been actively developing integrated optical solutions. Marvell benefits through custom silicon, DSP, optical interconnect, and CPO-related platforms. TSMC, ASE, and Amkor benefit from advanced packaging, assembly, and test requirements if CPO moves into volume manufacturing. Coherent and Lumentum can benefit from optical engines, lasers, photonic components, and coupling technologies. Teradyne may benefit where optical integration creates incremental semiconductor-test complexity, although Viavi’s commentary specifically emphasized optical-plane test rather than conventional electrical automatic test equipment.

The most important non-consensus implication is that CPO’s manufacturing difficulty may expand the test and packaging opportunity rather than merely delay adoption. Management stated that yield challenges lead to “more tests” of optical engines, substrates, and assemblies. Each additional test insertion increases capital intensity and creates opportunities for specialized equipment suppliers.

The near-term earnings effect should remain modest because Viavi did not quantify revenue, customer count, or production volume. The strategic significance is much larger. Purchase orders and initial revenue indicate that CPO is progressing beyond conceptual development, but the industry remains in an early commercialization phase.

CPO COMPLEXITY IS POSITIVE FOR TEST CONTENT BUT NEGATIVE FOR EARLY YIELDS, COSTS, AND DEPLOYMENT TIMING (READ-THROUGH 5)

Affected companies: Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: US), Marvell Technology Inc. (MRVL: US), Coherent Corp. (COHR: US), Lumentum Holdings Inc. (LITE: US), Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. Ltd. (TSM: Taiwan), ASE Technology Holding Co. Ltd. (ASX: Taiwan), and Amkor Technology Inc. (AMKR: US)

Directional impact and magnitude: Mixed, with a medium near-term negative for manufacturing economics and a high longer-term positive if the architecture reaches scale.

Supporting commentary and data: Management acknowledged that CPO involves “complexity and yield and all these difficulties” and “comes at a much higher cost.” The rationale for adoption is lower power and higher performance, potentially allowing an older semiconductor node to deliver system performance comparable to a more advanced node in certain applications.

Transmission mechanism: CPO can improve system-level power efficiency and bandwidth density, but early implementations require more expensive packaging, tighter alignment tolerances, complex thermal design, additional test steps, and more costly rework or scrap. These factors can delay customer qualification and suppress early gross margins for optical-engine, packaging, and system suppliers.

Broadcom and Marvell may incur elevated development and qualification costs before CPO reaches high-volume adoption. Optical-component suppliers face stringent yield and reliability requirements. TSMC, ASE, and Amkor may capture more packaging value but must also invest in process capability and test infrastructure. Customers may initially restrict CPO to the highest-performance applications where power savings and bandwidth advantages justify the cost premium.

The positive long-duration transmission mechanism is powerful. If CPO delivers meaningful power and performance benefits, adoption becomes increasingly compelling as AI clusters confront electrical I/O limits and power constraints. The architecture could increase semiconductor, photonics, packaging, and test content per system. The negative near-term implication is that revenue ramps may be less linear and more qualification-dependent than optimistic industry forecasts imply.

OPTICAL CIRCUIT SWITCHING IS BROADENING BEYOND A SINGLE HYPERSCALER, SUPPORTING AN EMERGING NETWORK ARCHITECTURE (READ-THROUGH 6)

Affected companies: Alphabet Inc. (GOOGL: US), Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: US), Arista Networks Inc. (ANET: US), Cisco Systems Inc. (CSCO: US), Coherent Corp. (COHR: US), Lumentum Holdings Inc. (LITE: US), and optical-switching specialists

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, medium magnitude; modest near-term trading catalyst and potentially important longer-duration architecture shift.

Supporting commentary and data: Viavi stated that some OCS revenue is already included in results, while “the majority of OCS” incremental revenue is expected over the next several quarters. Management said Viavi has historically sold equipment to “a big OCS vendor” and a hyperscaler that develops its own systems, but demand is now becoming broader as additional companies introduce OCS and evaluate optical switching in their core networks.

Transmission mechanism: OCS allows optical paths to be reconfigured with lower power and potentially lower latency than architectures requiring repeated electrical switching. AI workloads with large, predictable communication patterns can benefit from dynamically configured optical fabrics, particularly where electrical switching power becomes a constraint.

Alphabet has historically been associated with internally developed optical switching architectures, making Viavi’s reference to a hyperscaler building its own systems directionally relevant. Broader industry adoption would support optical-component suppliers and specialized switching vendors. Arista and Cisco face a more nuanced impact. OCS can create additional orchestration, control, monitoring, and integration opportunities, but it could also alter the mix of conventional packet-switching equipment within certain portions of the AI network.

Broadcom remains positioned to benefit from control silicon, switch integration, and broader networking content, although purely optical switching can reduce the number of electrical switching stages in specific architectures. Coherent and Lumentum benefit through optical components, switching elements, lasers, and associated photonics.

The high-conviction conclusion is not that OCS will displace Ethernet switching broadly. The stronger conclusion is that adoption is moving beyond a single proprietary deployment and is becoming an incremental network layer. The impact should initially be additive and concentrated in the largest AI clusters rather than disruptive to the entire switching market.

ETHERNET, SWITCH SILICON, AND AI NETWORKING

ULTRA ETHERNET VALIDATION DEMAND CONFIRMS CONTINUED INVESTMENT IN ETHERNET-BASED AI FABRICS (READ-THROUGH 7)

Affected companies: Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: US), Arista Networks Inc. (ANET: US), Cisco Systems Inc. (CSCO: US), Marvell Technology Inc. (MRVL: US), Credo Technology Group Holding Ltd. (CRDO: Cayman Islands), and Advanced Micro Devices Inc. (AMD: US)

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, medium-to-high magnitude; positive near-term catalyst and positive longer-duration competitive signal for Ethernet AI networking.

Supporting commentary and data: Viavi launched what management described as the industry’s first validation solution for Ultra Ethernet transport, “purpose built to support large-scale AI and high-performance computing workloads.” The product emerged from the combined Viavi and Spirent roadmap, with integration described as ahead of schedule.

Transmission mechanism: Investment in specialized Ultra Ethernet test platforms indicates that customers, semiconductor vendors, and system suppliers are actively developing Ethernet-based scale-up and scale-out networks for AI workloads. Validation equipment is required when standards, congestion-control mechanisms, packet behavior, latency, reliability, and interoperability are sufficiently complex to justify dedicated testing.

Broadcom benefits as the leading merchant Ethernet switch-silicon provider. Arista benefits from Ethernet switching systems and AI-cluster networking. Cisco benefits through Silicon One, Ethernet systems, optics, and networking software. Marvell and Credo benefit from high-speed connectivity, DSPs, PHYs, and signal-conditioning content. AMD benefits strategically because Ultra Ethernet provides a broader open networking ecosystem around AI accelerators, potentially reducing dependence on vertically integrated proprietary fabrics.

The competitive read-through is directionally negative for proprietary-networking incumbency at the margin, although it does not imply near-term displacement. Ethernet is attracting sufficient development activity and test investment to remain a credible architecture for large AI clusters. This supports a multi-vendor ecosystem and increases the probability that hyperscalers maintain or expand merchant-silicon and open-networking strategies.

Viavi’s evidence is particularly valuable because test-tool development generally follows direct customer engagement and identified validation requirements. The product launch therefore suggests real engineering activity rather than solely standards-body interest.

AI CAPEX IS TRANSLATING INTO NETWORKING AND OPTICAL EQUIPMENT, NOT ONLY COMPUTE SILICON (READ-THROUGH 8)

Affected companies: Arista Networks Inc. (ANET: US), Broadcom Inc. (AVGO: US), Marvell Technology Inc. (MRVL: US), Coherent Corp. (COHR: US), Lumentum Holdings Inc. (LITE: US), Amphenol Corp. (APH: US), Corning Inc. (GLW: US), and Fabrinet (FN: Cayman Islands)

Directional impact and magnitude: Positive, high magnitude; positive near-term and longer-duration cross-sector read-through.

Supporting commentary and data: Viavi described demand from a data-center ecosystem including “high-performance semis, optical modules, NEMs and hyperscalers” and cited strength across lab, production, field, fiber monitoring, data-center buildout, maintenance, and monitoring. Management stated that data-center ecosystem demand “remains very strong” and expects “continued robust growth in this segment for the next several quarters.”

Transmission mechanism: Viavi sits downstream from architecture decisions but upstream from full-scale deployment. Demand for its products indicates that AI capital expenditure is being converted into physical networking infrastructure: switches, optical modules, cables, fiber monitoring, manufacturing capacity, and field installation. This supports companies whose earnings depend on network bandwidth and optical-port growth rather than solely GPU shipments.

Arista benefits through AI back-end and front-end switching. Broadcom and Marvell benefit through switch silicon, connectivity, and optical DSPs. Coherent, Lumentum, and Fabrinet benefit from optical components and module manufacturing. Amphenol benefits through high-speed interconnect and fiber assemblies. Corning benefits where new data-center campuses and higher fiber density increase cabling requirements.

The call also clarifies the timing relationship between headline hyperscaler capital expenditure and supplier revenue. Management cautioned that some capital expenditure initially goes toward construction and trenching rather than equipment. The networking and optical revenue opportunity emerges as campuses are equipped and production capacity is installed. This implies that the downstream optical and networking cycle can lag initial data-center construction announcements and potentially remain strong after the earliest construction spending is recognized.

The read-through supports a broader AI-infrastructure basket rather than an exclusively compute-silicon trade. It also indicates that the next phase of AI capital spending is increasingly constrained by connectivity, power, and optical bandwidth, raising the relative value of networking and photonics.
