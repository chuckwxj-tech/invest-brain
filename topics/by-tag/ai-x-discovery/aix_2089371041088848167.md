## 原文

Applied Materials’ latest quarterly revenue reached $9.12 billion, up 25% year over year. Lam Research reported $6.72 billion in its latest quarter, up 15% sequentially, while KLA’s latest quarterly revenue came in at $3.66 billion.

That made me rethink one question: if this AI CapEx race keeps going, who gets paid no matter which chip ultimately wins?

Semiconductor equipment is one of those layers.

$NVDA wants more GPUs, so fabs need to expand capacity. $MU, Samsung, and SK Hynix want more HBM and DRAM, which means more wafers, more etch, more deposition, and more packaging capacity. As advanced nodes keep getting more complex, inspection and yield control also become more important.

Micron has even raised its long-term U.S. 

manufacturing and technology investment plan to more than $250 billion, with the goal of eventually producing around 40% of its DRAM in the U.S.

I can’t say with certainty whether memory prices will be higher or lower in the future. But as long as memory makers want to add capacity, cut costs, or move to more advanced processes, equipment orders have to happen first.

That’s also why I’m starting to see an interesting difference between semiconductor equipment and optical networking at this stage.

I’m still bullish on optical networking.

$LITE ’s latest quarterly revenue has already reached $1.01 billion, up 109% year over year, and demand for 1.6T, OCS, and CPO lasers is starting to show up quickly in the numbers. The fundamentals are very strong.
