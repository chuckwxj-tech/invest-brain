# My key takeaways from the $LITE (Lumentum Holdings) Q4 2026 earnings call. 1. The high-power laser shortage got worse this quarter, not better. Citi's Papa Silla asked for an up...

## 原文

My key takeaways from the $LITE (Lumentum Holdings) Q4 2026 earnings call.

1. The high-power laser shortage got worse this quarter, not better. Citi's Papa Silla asked for an update on the supply-demand gap CEO Michael Hurlston flagged as 30%+ last quarter. On EMLs (electro-absorption modulated lasers, the laser type used in most optical transceivers) the gap is roughly unchanged, still shipping behind demand. On high-power lasers, the ones feeding next-gen CPO and NPO builds, Hurlston said "we are very much further behind" than three months ago, even after signing a new substrate supply deal with AXT this quarter to catch up. That's proof AI demand is real. It's also the single biggest execution risk into the 2027-2028 scale-up ramp.

2. Management is raising long-term targets. JPMorgan's Joseph Cardoso asked how to think about the next set of financial targets given the company is running ahead of plan on revenue, gross margin and operating margin. Hurlston said new targets come at the next OFC conference (the big optical networking industry event). CFO Wajid Ali said the guide already reflects "adding nearly $250 million incrementally" versus what was modeled at the last OFC, driven by NPO, CW lasers and OCS upside management hadn't previously baked in.

3. NPO and CPO are a 2027-2028 story, not a 2026 one. Barclays' Thomas O'Malley asked whether NPO (near-packaged optics, the optical engine sitting right next to the chip) can be retrofitted onto GPUs and XPUs shipping today. Hurlston said no, current-gen silicon doesn't have fast enough SERDES speed (the chip-to-chip data rate) to use it. New silicon built for NPO and CPO (co-packaged optics, optics placed directly on the chip package) starts arriving mid to late 2027 and into 2028. The lead CPO customer's scale-up shipments begin second half of calendar 2027 for 2028 deployments.

4. OCS is tracking to plan, not beating it, despite the headline triple-digit quarter. Citi's Silla asked if optical circuit switch revenue is running ahead of the $400 million-plus second-half guide from OFC. Hurlston pushed back directly: "I would not say tracking ahead," citing early supply chain issues now resolved. He still expects to overtake one large customer's internal OCS supply and become the top vendor by early 2027. Management correcting an analyst's framing instead of taking the compliment is a decent tell they aren't overpromising this line.

5. Chinese indium phosphide competitors get waved off, but the evidence is thin either way. Raymond James' Simon Leopold asked about new Chinese indium phosphide (the semiconductor material these lasers are built on) fab entrants. Hurlston said Lumentum has seen no impact on its numbers, pointing to a price premium on CW lasers (continuous wave, used with silicon photonics) from tighter manufacturing spec and better yield for customers. He added some of these Chinese suppliers aren't shipping real volume yet, so their claims have no track record behind them. Fair point, but one worth rechecking in a couple quarters rather than taking as settled.

6. The EML versus CW laser margin gap is closing fast. Susquehanna's Christopher Rolland and Mizuho's Vijay Rakesh both pressed on the mix shift in 200G-per-lane transceivers. EMLs are 25% of that mix today; management expects 50%+ by mid-2027. Shrinking the CW laser die size "closed the margin gap quite considerably" versus EMLs, though EMLs still carry the better margin. GBU president Wupen Yuen added that in a supply-constrained market customers take whichever laser has open capacity, so the split is partly about who can ship, not just preference.

7. Pump lasers are the most underappreciated part of this story, by management's own admission. Stifel's Ruben Roy asked about the Rose Orchard pump laser ramp. Hurlston called it "an area of incredible strength for us, which really I think is underappreciated," citing 70-80% market share and mostly three-year, take-or-pay-style agreements with network equipment makers that help fund the capex, with pricing levers built in. This is the business quietly funding the CPO and NPO bets everyone keeps asking about.

Bottom line: Lumentum is telling a growing-ahead-of-plan story, tightening laser margins, raising targets at the next OFC, and the numbers back it up so far. But the same high-power laser shortage that proves AI demand is also what could cap how fast the company actually captures the 2027-2028 CPO and NPO ramp it keeps describing.
