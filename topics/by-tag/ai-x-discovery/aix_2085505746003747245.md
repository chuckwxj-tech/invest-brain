$ONTO KEY READ-THROUGHS FROM ONTO INNOVATION Q2 2026 EARNINGS CALL

Onto Innovation’s Q2 2026 earnings call provides a materially positive read-through for the semiconductor capital-equipment, HBM, advanced-packaging, leading-edge foundry, OSAT, panel-level packaging, and silicon-photonics ecosystems. The strongest signals are the increase in Onto’s 2026 advanced-packaging growth outlook to approximately 80% from 50%, 50% sequential growth in advanced-node revenue, 30% sequential growth in Dragonfly-dominated inspection, a record backlog above $1.1 billion, more than $200 million of Dragonfly orders from a single OSAT, and more than $50 million of silicon-photonics orders. These data indicate that AI infrastructure spending is driving both capacity additions and a structural increase in process-control intensity across HBM, 2.5D logic, heterogeneous integration, sub-2nm transistor production, and next-generation optical interconnect. The principal negative read-throughs are company-specific share pressure on competing inspection and metrology vendors, increasingly aggressive customer order placement to secure production slots, elevated capital intensity at OSATs and memory manufacturers, and growing cross-portfolio dependence on a common AI-capex cycle. Near-term industry estimate risk remains skewed positively through H1 2027, while the probability of order digestion, shipment deferrals, and utilization pressure rises beyond that period.

The source is an initial-draft transcript containing several obvious transcription errors. References to “OSET,” “Ragaku,” “dyes,” and “2017” have been interpreted as OSAT, Rigaku, dies, and 2027 where the intended meaning is unambiguous. SOURCE BASIS: Onto Innovation Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript dated 08/06/26.

ADVANCED-PACKAGING PROCESS-CONTROL SPENDING IS ACCELERATING FASTER THAN THE BROADER PACKAGING MARKET (READ-THROUGH 1)

CALL SUPPORT: Onto increased its 2026 advanced-packaging growth outlook to approximately 80% from 50% only 3 months earlier. Dragonfly-dominated inspection revenue increased 30% sequentially, with strength across both HBM and 2.5D logic. Management described “unprecedented demand across an expanding set of customers,” indicated that existing customers are deploying Dragonfly across a broader range of applications, and disclosed more than $200 million of Dragonfly orders from a single OSAT, with most systems scheduled for delivery in 2027. Management also identified strong growth in 2D inspection, 3D bump metrology, and subsurface inspection.

AFFECTED COMPANIES AND DIRECTIONAL IMPACT: Camtek Ltd. (CAMT: Israel) — positive, high magnitude at the end-market level. KLA Corporation (KLAC: United States) — positive, moderate-to-high magnitude. BE Semiconductor Industries N.V. (BESI: Netherlands) — positive, high magnitude. Applied Materials, Inc. (AMAT: United States) — positive, moderate-to-high magnitude. ASMPT Limited (0522: Hong Kong) — positive, moderate magnitude. Kulicke and Soffa Industries, Inc. (KLIC: United States) — positive, moderate magnitude.

The critical read-through is that advanced-packaging equipment content is increasing faster than package volumes. HBM and heterogeneous logic packages require inspection and metrology at multiple manufacturing steps rather than only at final assembly. Onto cited demand for conventional 2D defect inspection, 3D bump-height and dimensional metrology, and subsurface inspection of defects concealed beneath additional layers. As packages incorporate more dies, interposers, redistribution layers, micro-bumps, hybrid-bonding interfaces, and optical components, the number of potential defect-generation points rises nonlinearly. Yield loss on a completed package containing multiple high-value dies is economically severe, increasing customers’ willingness to insert process control earlier and more frequently.

Camtek is the most direct positive demand read-through because its growth is highly exposed to inspection and metrology for advanced packaging, HBM, chiplets, and high-density interconnect. Onto’s 80% growth outlook, 30% sequential inspection increase, and multiyear OSAT orders materially strengthen the probability that Camtek’s addressable market is expanding well above broad semiconductor unit growth. Near-term estimates for Camtek should benefit from sustained HBM and 2.5D capacity additions, although company-specific share risk is addressed separately below.

BESI is a high-magnitude positive read-through because advanced inspection orders generally accompany, or precede, purchases of bonding and placement equipment. Growth in HBM, SOIC, chiplets, and other heterogeneous packages increases demand for high-precision die placement and hybrid-bonding architectures. Onto’s comments that G5 is opening opportunities in SOIC and that customers remain capacity constrained support a multiyear advanced-packaging equipment cycle rather than a short-lived replacement cycle.

Applied Materials, ASMPT, and Kulicke and Soffa benefit through adjacent deposition, plating, materials engineering, bonding, assembly, and packaging steps. Onto’s evidence does not establish individual share gains for these companies, but it does indicate that customers are building complete packaging lines rather than purchasing isolated inspection tools. The process flow requires coordinated investment across multiple equipment categories.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: The immediate implication is upward risk to 2026 and H1 2027 advanced-packaging equipment estimates, particularly for companies whose order books are directly exposed to HBM, CoWoS-class packaging, hybrid bonding, and high-resolution inspection. Order announcements, backlog increases, and stronger-than-expected bookings from Camtek, BESI, ASMPT, and related suppliers would be consistent with Onto’s demand signal.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: Advanced packaging is becoming a process-control-intensive manufacturing discipline rather than a comparatively low-intensity back-end assembly activity. Inspection, metrology, bonding, lithography, and software content should grow faster than package volumes as package value, layer count, die count, and defect sensitivity increase. This supports structurally higher equipment spending per unit of AI compute shipped.

ONTO’S SHARE GAINS CREATE A NEGATIVE RELATIVE READ-THROUGH FOR DIRECT INSPECTION AND METROLOGY COMPETITORS (READ-THROUGH 2)

CALL SUPPORT: Management explicitly stated that Onto is adding new customers, finding new applications within existing customers, and gaining share. The company said customers are adopting Dragonfly “in new areas at a much stronger pace than even we expected three months ago.” Management described recent logic integrated-metrology wins as share gains, noting that “a year ago, we wouldn’t have had hardly any, maybe zero logic for integrated metrology.” Atlas G6 is gaining adoption in both logic and DRAM because its smaller measurement spot and higher precision allow customers to perform optical critical-dimension measurements in areas that previously required test structures. Dragonfly G5 front-end evaluations are also being compared directly with incumbent tools.

AFFECTED COMPANIES AND DIRECTIONAL IMPACT: Camtek Ltd. (CAMT: Israel) — negative relative read-through, moderate-to-high magnitude. Nova Ltd. (NVMI: Israel) — negative relative read-through, moderate magnitude. KLA Corporation (KLAC: United States) — negative relative read-through, low-to-moderate magnitude.

Camtek remains positively exposed to the underlying advanced-packaging market, but Onto’s call is a negative competitive signal. Dragonfly competes for inspection and metrology spending in several of Camtek’s highest-growth applications, including 2D inspection, 3D bump metrology, HBM, heterogeneous packaging, and advanced interconnect. Onto is not merely benefiting from customer capacity growth; it is broadening the number of applications addressed per customer and adding customers. That distinction matters because Camtek’s valuation and earnings trajectory depend on both market growth and maintenance of its own share in high-growth inspection categories.

The negative signal does not imply that Camtek’s absolute revenue must decline. The market may be expanding rapidly enough for both companies to grow strongly. The implication is that Onto’s above-market growth reduces confidence that all advanced-packaging inspection vendors can simultaneously sustain uninterrupted share gains. Incremental customer wins, broader tool deployment, and a higher-resolution G5 platform increase the probability of competitive pricing, elevated application-development spending, or lower-than-expected share capture for Camtek.

Nova faces a separate but related risk. Onto’s Atlas, Iris films, and integrated-metrology platforms increasingly overlap with Nova’s optical metrology exposure across logic and memory. Onto reported new logic integrated-metrology applications, DRAM adoption of Atlas G6, record films and integrated-metrology revenue, and an ability to measure smaller device-relevant areas. These developments indicate that Onto is expanding its share of the same front-end process-control budgets targeted by Nova.

KLA’s competitive exposure is less material in percentage terms because KLA is substantially larger and more diversified. However, Onto’s front-end G5 evaluations and comparisons against incumbent solutions are a negative signal at the margin. Successful qualification would allow Onto to enter additional front-end inspection applications, while the combination of optical metrology, inspection, software, and potential x-ray capability could strengthen its position in bundled customer engagements.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Camtek and Nova’s next order, backlog, and customer-adoption disclosures should be evaluated against Onto’s claim of adding customers and applications. Strong category growth can support both companies, but any moderation in inspection order growth, lower incremental margins, or more cautious share commentary would reinforce the competitive read-through.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: Onto is attempting to create a common process-control platform spanning 2D inspection, 3D metrology, subsurface inspection, films metrology, integrated metrology, optical critical-dimension measurement, and potentially x-ray. A broader platform can increase switching costs, improve customer data integration, and enable cross-selling. The long-term risk to competitors is not one isolated product win, but the creation of a multi-application installed base that captures a greater percentage of customer process-control spending.

HBM AND ADVANCED DRAM CAPACITY EXPANSION REMAIN STRONG THROUGH AT LEAST H1 2027 (READ-THROUGH 3)

CALL SUPPORT: Onto’s advanced-node business increased 50% sequentially to approximately $120 million. Memory represented approximately 60% of advanced-node revenue and increased approximately 60% sequentially. Management expects to ship multiple Atlas G6 systems to a major DRAM customer in H2 2026 for next-generation memory production. HBM was identified as one of the strongest sources of advanced-packaging demand, and management stated that it is “not seeing any signs of overcapacity.” Customers were described as constrained and under pressure from their own customers to release additional capacity.

AFFECTED COMPANIES AND DIRECTIONAL IMPACT: SK hynix Inc. (000660: South Korea) — positive, high magnitude. Micron Technology, Inc. (MU: United States) — positive, high magnitude. Samsung Electronics Co., Ltd. (005930: South Korea) — positive, moderate-to-high magnitude. Lam Research Corporation (LRCX: United States) — positive, high magnitude. Applied Materials, Inc. (AMAT: United States) — positive, high magnitude. KLA Corporation (KLAC: United States) — positive, moderate-to-high magnitude. ASML Holding N.V. (ASML: Netherlands) — positive, moderate magnitude.

The most important memory read-through is that HBM demand is simultaneously driving front-end DRAM investment and back-end packaging investment. HBM requires advanced DRAM dies, more silicon content per accelerator, complex stacking, tight bump and bonding tolerances, and exceptionally high final-package yield. The resulting capital demand extends across wafer-fabrication equipment, metrology, inspection, wafer thinning, bonding, packaging, and test.

SK hynix and Micron receive the strongest positive read-through because both are highly leveraged to incremental HBM output, and the reported equipment commitments indicate that customers continue to expand capacity rather than prepare for an immediate plateau. Onto’s statement that customers are being pressured to release more capacity supports continued volume growth and favorable utilization. The call does not identify which memory manufacturer placed specific orders, so no company-specific order allocation can be inferred.

Samsung receives a positive industry-demand read-through, although the magnitude is more dependent on its own HBM qualification, product execution, and customer share. Strong sector capacity growth provides Samsung with an opportunity to participate, but equipment purchases alone do not resolve company-specific competitive positioning.

Lam Research and Applied Materials are direct beneficiaries of DRAM capacity additions and technology transitions. Higher HBM demand increases the required volume of advanced DRAM wafers, while next-generation DRAM introduces additional etch, deposition, materials engineering, and patterning complexity. KLA benefits from tighter yield requirements and higher process-control intensity. ASML benefits from advanced DRAM lithography and continued node migration, although its HBM exposure is more indirect than that of deposition, etch, and process-control suppliers.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Memory WFE estimates for 2026 and H1 2027 retain upward risk. Additional HBM capacity announcements, stronger memory-equipment bookings, and higher capital-expenditure guidance from SK hynix, Micron, or Samsung would confirm the signal. Lam Research and Applied Materials have the clearest near-term earnings sensitivity because DRAM capacity expansions rapidly translate into etch and deposition tool demand.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: HBM increases semiconductor capital intensity because AI accelerators consume substantially more advanced memory content than conventional servers and because stacked-memory yields require more stringent process control. This supports equipment spending growth above underlying server-unit growth. The longer-term offset is that aggressive capacity additions can eventually reduce memory scarcity, pressure HBM pricing, and produce an order digestion cycle. Onto’s call argues against an imminent capacity surplus, but it does not eliminate the possibility of oversupply after the current buildout.

THE 2.5D LOGIC AND OSAT CAPACITY CYCLE REMAINS SUPPLY-CONSTRAINED, SUPPORTING TSMC, ASE, AND AMKOR (READ-THROUGH 4)

CALL SUPPORT: Onto reported strong growth across both HBM and 2.5D logic, with the 2 categories contributing at broadly similar rates during H1 2026. Management attributed the increase partly to OSATs “picking up more volume” and disclosed more than $200 million of Dragonfly orders from a single OSAT. The majority of those systems will be delivered in 2027. Advanced-packaging visibility has improved because demand is now being driven more by AI hyperscalers than by difficult-to-predict consumer-electronics cycles.

AFFECTED COMPANIES AND DIRECTIONAL IMPACT: Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Limited (TSM: Taiwan) — positive, high magnitude. ASE Technology Holding Co., Ltd. (ASX: Taiwan) — positive, moderate-to-high magnitude. Amkor Technology, Inc. (AMKR: United States) — positive, moderate-to-high magnitude. Unimicron Technology Corp. (3037: Taiwan) — positive, moderate magnitude. Ibiden Co., Ltd. (4062: Japan) — positive, moderate magnitude.

TSMC receives a high-magnitude positive read-through because the reported 2.5D logic strength is consistent with continued expansion of CoWoS-class capacity and other advanced heterogeneous-integration platforms. Onto’s process-control orders indicate that customers are not simply discussing future capacity; they are procuring equipment required to qualify and operate additional production lines. TSMC was also specifically referenced in the discussion of CoPoS panel-level packaging, providing a separate indication that the company is preparing for larger-format packaging.

ASE and Amkor receive positive read-throughs because AI accelerator and HBM packaging demand is increasingly extending beyond captive foundry packaging and into global OSAT capacity. A single OSAT committing more than $200 million to one equipment family is a significant indicator of multiyear customer demand and planned line expansion. The transcript does not identify the OSAT, so the order cannot be assigned to ASE, Amkor, or another provider. The company-specific impact is therefore less certain than the sector-level signal.

The transmission mechanism to OSAT earnings is higher advanced-packaging volume, improved utilization of high-value packaging assets, greater revenue per package, and potentially stronger customer commitments. The negative offset is capital intensity. Large equipment orders increase depreciation and working-capital requirements before the associated revenue is fully realized. OSAT free cash flow may therefore lag revenue growth during the buildout phase. Returns will depend on sustained utilization, customer pricing, yield execution, and the duration of AI package demand.

Unimicron and Ibiden benefit through higher demand for advanced substrates used in large, high-density packages. More dies, larger package footprints, and increasing interconnect density raise substrate complexity and value. The read-through is positive but less direct because Onto’s call did not disclose substrate orders or specific customer allocations.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: OSAT capital-expenditure guidance, advanced-packaging utilization, and customer prepayments or long-term agreements are the most relevant confirmation points. Higher capex is initially positive for demand visibility but should be evaluated against free-cash-flow dilution and future depreciation.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: The transition from consumer-driven packaging demand toward AI-led multiyear capacity planning should improve revenue visibility and reduce seasonality for leading OSATs. Advanced packaging is also capturing a larger portion of semiconductor value creation, potentially improving the strategic position of packaging providers relative to traditional commoditized assembly and test.

SUB-2NM AND GATE-ALL-AROUND TRANSITIONS ARE INCREASING PROCESS-CONTROL CONTENT PER FAB (READ-THROUGH 5)

CALL SUPPORT: Advanced-node revenue increased 50% sequentially, with logic revenue increasing more than 40%. Management increased the full-year advanced-node growth outlook to more than 35% and expects the business to outgrow WFE. Atlas G6 is being used for transistor metrology at “several nodes below 2 nanometer,” and its smaller spot size allows measurements in device-relevant areas where customers previously relied on test structures or scribe-line measurements. Onto also described new logic applications for integrated metrology and record revenue expectations for films metrology.

AFFECTED COMPANIES AND DIRECTIONAL IMPACT: Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Limited (TSM: Taiwan) — positive, high magnitude. ASML Holding N.V. (ASML: Netherlands) — positive, high magnitude. KLA Corporation (KLAC: United States) — positive, high magnitude at the industry level. Applied Materials, Inc. (AMAT: United States) — positive, moderate-to-high magnitude. Lam Research Corporation (LRCX: United States) — positive, moderate-to-high magnitude.

The central implication is that leading-edge WFE content can grow faster than wafer-start capacity. Gate-all-around and sub-2nm structures introduce tighter dimensional tolerances, more complex three-dimensional geometries, new materials, and greater sensitivity to process variation. Customers must measure more parameters, at more points in the process, with higher precision. Onto’s ability to expand optical critical-dimension measurements from test structures into smaller device-relevant areas is evidence that process-control insertion is broadening rather than merely replacing older tools.

TSMC is the clearest foundry beneficiary because higher process-control spending is generally associated with active technology ramps, customer demand, and efforts to accelerate yield learning. Additional metrology spending can initially raise capital intensity, but it reduces costly yield excursions and supports faster commercialization of advanced nodes.

ASML benefits because sub-2nm production requires continued investment in advanced lithography. Applied Materials and Lam Research benefit from the associated increases in deposition, etch, patterning, materials engineering, and structural complexity. KLA receives a strong category-level positive because advanced nodes require greater inspection and metrology intensity, although Onto’s share gains create a company-specific competitive offset.

NEAR-TERM TRADING CATALYST: Leading-edge foundry capex, 2nm equipment shipments, and process-control bookings should remain supported through current ramp cycles. Stronger-than-expected 2nm and gate-all-around utilization would provide additional upside to ASML, KLA, Applied Materials, and Lam Research.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: Process control is taking a larger percentage of the total WFE budget as transistor structures become smaller and more three-dimensional. This is structurally positive for inspection and metrology spending even if aggregate wafer-start growth moderates. The most attractive beneficiaries are companies capable of addressing new measurement applications rather than relying only on replacement demand.
