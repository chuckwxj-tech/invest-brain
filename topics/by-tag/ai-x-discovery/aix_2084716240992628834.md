The sub $5bn picks and shovels companies reporting tonight Aug 4th AMC

TONIGHT — AMC Aug 4

$MEC  ($0.54B, Coil 9) Mayville Engineering is North America's largest metal fabricator, and it's in the middle of a pivot that the street hasn't caught up to. The old business was commercial vehicles and powersports — cyclical, shrinking. The new business is data center electrical infrastructure: switchgear, busway, PDU enclosures, cooling assemblies. The Accu-Fab acquisition in Q3 2025 brought DC capabilities and customers, and since then DC revenue has been running +71% organically. They booked $50M in new DC awards in Q1 alone — a single-quarter record. Management guided DC to exceed 20% of 2026 revenue with a Q4 exit rate approaching 25%. The street is asking for +10.3% revenue growth and -$0.08 EPS, which is absurdly conservative when the DC engine is accelerating and the comp is depressed (-19% a year ago). Every power-systems peer is confirming the demand: ATKR +28%, ETN +7%, NVT +6%, RELL +21%. Launch costs compressed margins in Q1 (manufacturing margin 7% vs 11.3% a year ago), but management said those costs begin tapering in Q2. A short needs the entire DC fabrication theme to crack, and nothing in the ecosystem suggests that. At $540M market cap, this is a cyclical fabricator being repriced as an AI infrastructure supplier and the street is behind.

$ASYS  ($0.23B, Coil 8) — Amtech Systems makes thermal reflow ovens used in advanced semiconductor packaging — the 2.5D/3D stacking, chiplet integration, and HBM assembly that's at the heart of the AI chip build. After three quarters of revenue contraction (-27%, -18%, -22%), Q2 FY2026 snapped back +31.4%, and management explicitly guided Q3 acceleration. AI-related equipment now exceeds 30% of the Thermal Processing segment, parts and service surged +56%, and gross margins expanded 290 basis points. Backlog is $22.3M with book-to-bill above 1.0x. The street wants +37.8% revenue and $0.10 EPS — fair given the guided acceleration, and very beatable with the backlog providing visibility. Beat history is 5/7. The risk is concentration: two accounts make up 51% of backlog, so a delay from either one hits hard. KLAC's -10.8% drop shows packaging names aren't immune. But the setup is strong for a company in the sweet spot of the AI packaging wave at a $230M market cap.

$LMB  ($0.87B, Coil 8) — Limbach is an MEP contractor that's quietly built a national data center team and has the Jake Marshall fabrication facility with explicit excess capacity for multiple hyperscale packages simultaneously. Q1 bookings jumped to $209M with about 27% ($56M) from data centers — up from less than 5% of revenue and a single $10M award in prior quarters. Book-to-bill hit 1.5x. One hyperscale project alone is $30M+ and set to contribute immediately. The street wants +33.2% revenue and $0.93 EPS (flat YoY). That revenue bar looks aggressive until you look at the bookings conversion math. The short case rests on the Pioneer margin drag persisting and gross margins staying stuck at ~22%, but if Q2 prints 24.5%+ gross margin and EBITDA guidance goes above $94M, the margin recovery narrative is validated. EME's +17.5% post-earnings pop confirms the construction-for-DC demand environment is real.

$ATOM ($0.20B, Coil 7) — Atomera is a pure binary. Zero revenue ($11K last quarter), 0/7 beat history, burning $5-6M per quarter with $41M in cash. The entire thesis is that their MST semiconductor technology gets licensed by a Tier 1 logic or memory manufacturer for gate-all-around transistors. Two GAA developers are currently evaluating customer wafers. A signed royalty-bearing license would transform the stock overnight. Anything else — more "we're making progress" language, timeline slips, or a dilutive raise — and the short wins. The ecosystem is favorable (advanced packaging and leading-edge node investment is accelerating), but this company has been "almost there" for years. You're betting on a single binary event tonight.

$CTRI ($2.81B, Coil 7) — Centuri is a utility infrastructure contractor levered to two tailwinds: utility grid modernization and DC power delivery. Under new CEO Christian Brown, Q1 revenue jumped +27.2% with organic electric growth, the Connect Atlantic acquisition, and a 44% surge in US gas. They're targeting $300M in data center awards. But the street is asking for +52% revenue growth — a massive step-up that requires a sharp organic acceleration plus a full quarter of DC work plus a healthy gas season. Beat history is only 3/7. There's also a customer dispute hanging over 13% of revenue. The setup is interesting but the bar is demanding and the execution track record doesn't inspire confidence. If they hit $300M DC awards and raise guidance above $3.2B, shorts scramble. But the probability of clearing +52% is lower than the other names on this list.

$DBRG ($2.89B, Coil 7) — DigitalBridge is technically an incredible business — they leased 2.6 GW of DC capacity in a single quarter, capturing roughly a third of all US hyperscale leasing, with a sales funnel over 7 GW. Fee-earning equity under management hit $40.7B. But here's the thing: SoftBank is acquiring them at $16.00 cash per share, shareholders approved the merger in April, and there's no signaled regulatory or financing risk. The stock is a merger-arb play capped at the deal price. You could get a blowout quarter and the stock won't move because the deal price is fixed. Not a coiled spring — a locked box. Skip this one for earnings plays.

$FTEK ($0.05B, Coil 7) — Fuel Tech is a $50M company that makes SCR pollution control systems for gas turbines, and they've disclosed a pipeline of $75-100M across 8-10 data center projects at $1-3M per unit. They just won a ~$10M utility APC award on two GE Vernova turbines — the exact platform used in DC gas plants. The stock trades near its $0.98/share net cash; enterprise value is approximately zero. The market has priced the entire DC growth thesis at nothing. The street expects +2.8% revenue and -$0.02 EPS — treading water. What matters is one thing only: does management announce a signed DC contract tonight? If yes, the stock reprices violently from net cash to growth story. If no, it stays dead. GEV and other gas turbine peers have confirmed the demand is real, so the pipeline should be active. Pure asymmetry play.

$PRIM ($4.77B, Coil 7) — Primoris got destroyed twice: first the Q1 miss in May (stock -45%, six solar projects bid in 2024 with cost overruns that crushed the Energy segment), then in June the COO departed and additional overruns surfaced. The demand problem is zero — every E&C peer is thriving (EME +17.5%, CTRI growing, PWR strong). This is 100% a self-inflicted execution problem on a specific vintage of solar contracts. Management has stopped pursuing new work in those geographies and is pivoting aggressively into gas-generation and DC construction, with $800M in verbal pipeline for gas-gen awards. The street expects +5.6% revenue and -$0.32 EPS, which bakes in significant pain. Backlog is $11.6B. The coil thesis is that Q2 shows the renewables bleeding is contained, the pivot is gaining traction, and the backlog converts. The short's hope is more overruns proving the problem is systemic. At $4.77B market cap with $11.6B backlog, the valuation already reflects a damaged story — any "all clear" signal on margins would re-rate it.

$CC ($2.57B, Coil 6) — Chemours makes fluoropolymers for semiconductor and data center applications (Teflon PFA), plus TiO2 paint pigment and refrigerants. The APM segment (advanced performance materials) has order velocity at multi-year highs driven by semi and DC demand, but the Washington Works plant outage cost $25M in EBITDA in Q1. The issue is the bar: +20.9% revenue growth is demanded, but full-year guidance is only 3-5% growth. For Q2 to print +21% when Q1 was +1%, you'd need a massive one-quarter snapback that would blow the annual guide out of the water. Hard to see how that happens without management sandbagging. Meanwhile, chemical peers HUN (-19%) and OLN (-17%) both got destroyed on their reports, signaling the broader chemical cycle is cracking. The AI-adjacent APM story is real but it's a small piece of a larger chemicals business under pressure.

$TDC ($3.02B, Coil 5) — Teradata is trying to reinvent itself from a legacy on-prem data warehouse vendor into a hybrid AI platform. They've branded it "Teradata 3.0" with sovereign AI demand and a GPU-integrated hardware refresh expected late Q2/Q3. They ran 150+ AI POCs in 2025, more than doubling the prior year. But the CFO admitted the AI revenue "is not yet reflected" in the financials, and the sole hardware assembler (Flex) has capacity booked for years while NVIDIA GPUs are supply-constrained. The street expects -5.3% revenue and $0.55 EPS — an easy bar on paper, but the company hasn't earned trust. The real catalyst would be an ARR growth guidance raise from 2-4% to 5%+, which would validate the AI pivot. Without that, this is a legacy vendor with aspiration but no proof. AP had this on the watch list — the coil rating of 5 suggests it's not wound tight enough.

$CE ($4.81B, Coil 4) — Celanese is riding a temporary acetyl chain windfall from a Middle East supply disruption, which drove EBITDA +125% in Q4. The Engineered Materials segment has lifted margins above 20% through the "Growth and Fortify" program. But the street wants +14.9% revenue growth and $2.23 EPS (a 55% YoY jump), which requires a massive sequential swing from Q1's -2.2%. The ecosystem has already spoken: HUN and OLN both fell nearly 20% on their reports, clear evidence that chemical pricing momentum is breaking. If acetyl margins compressed in Q2 and EM price increases got rejected, this misses badly. Avoid.

$OGS ($4.92B, Coil 4) — ONE Gas is a regulated gas utility in Oklahoma, Kansas, and Texas with a pipeline of 6 large-load transport projects that could support up to 3 GW of generation for data centers. One deal is signed — a 20 MMcf/d transportation agreement that's "immediately accretive." The -56% revenue headline is noise (gas price pass-through); the real bar is $0.64 EPS (+21% YoY), which is demanding for a utility. The DC story is embryonic — one signed contract is a token, not a trend. A second large-load contract announcement would change the narrative, but without it, this is a boring utility with an aspirational pipeline. Not enough spring tension.

$TBI ($0.23B, Coil 4) — TrueBlue is a staffing company shifting from temp labor toward skilled workers for energy and DC construction. Energy revenue doubled in the surge quarter, and SG&A cuts are creating operating leverage even though gross margins compress on pass-through revenue. But the street is asking for +12.8% growth, a 520bp acceleration from Q1's +7.6%, which basically demands a repeat of the Q3 energy surge. Management itself warned about energy deceleration. The bar is hard for a company that hasn't proven it can sustain the energy ramp. A miss here confirms the deceleration narrative. The staffing model is also facing the question of whether the standalone strategy is viable. Not enough conviction.

$GSM ($0.61B, Coil 3) — Ferroglobe makes silicon metal and ferroalloys. They're moving record volumes — silicon-based alloy shipments hit a five-year high in Q1 — but printed a microscopic $3M adjusted EBITDA on 0.9% margins. Silicon metal went negative. The street wants +22% revenue growth, nearly doubling from +13.2%, and the company hasn't strung together consecutive quarters of accelerating growth. The promised margin recovery from trade protections and volume leverage is entirely unproven. Chemical peers (HUN, OLN) confirm the cycle is ugly. The short's thesis — record volume at zero margin continues — is the most likely outcome. Avoid.
