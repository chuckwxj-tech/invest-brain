Street Signal | Nomura: Korea’s AI supply chain faces persistent memory and MLCC bottlenecks

Nomura held an Asian technology roadshow in Seoul on Aug. 20-21, 2026, covering updated management guidance from Samsung Electronics, SK hynix, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, Samsung SDI, HD Hyundai Electric, LG Display, HPSP and Soulbrain.

Samsung Electronics said DRAM and NAND fulfillment rates are only 60%-70%. The company has signed long-term agreements with five major data-center customers, with another five in final negotiations. DRAM output growth is capped at about 20% and will remain constrained even after 2028.

SK hynix has made a major change to its capital-return policy: 50% of free cash flow has shifted from a ceiling to a floor. The company plans to conduct share repurchases and cancellations within the next three months and is discussing five-year LTAs with about 10 customers.

Samsung Electro-Mechanics has won a KRW 1.5 trillion, two-year silicon capacitor contract from a hyperscale customer. AI MLCC capacity growth of more than 20% annually still cannot meet demand, indicating that AI demand is substantially stronger than the market recognizes.

HD Hyundai Electric has signed a new LTA worth about KRW 1.1 trillion with a major technology company, with deliveries scheduled for 2027-28. Data-center orders are expected to rise from 1.8% of the company’s orders in 2025 to an estimated 16.0% in 2027.

The note says Samsung Electronics’ management characterized the HBM de-spec issue as a forced customer decision caused by insufficient chip supply, rather than evidence of a structural change in demand. SK hynix’s shift from a capital-return ceiling to a floor was significantly above expectations.

Samsung Electro-Mechanics’ assertion that more than 20% annual capacity growth still will not satisfy AI demand also points to demand well above market expectations.

Nomura’s underlying view is that AI-driven memory supply tightness is structural. Long fab-construction lead times and wafer trade-offs involving HBM limit DRAM output growth. LTAs are a key tool for addressing concerns about the memory cycle and improving revenue visibility.

The note concludes that Korea’s technology supply chain is in an AI-driven structural upcycle. Memory supply tightness, MLCC capacity bottlenecks and AI data-center infrastructure demand for power equipment are the three main themes.

Leading companies are using LTAs to secure long-term revenue visibility, while stronger capital returns could further increase shareholder value.

Nomura identifies potential beneficiaries including Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, supported by tight memory supply, LTA coverage and stronger capital returns; Samsung Electro-Mechanics, supported by AI MLCC pricing and its new silicon capacitor contract; Samsung SDI, where energy-storage-system growth is strong and which the note identifies as the

preferred battery exposure; HD Hyundai Electric, as an AI data-center infrastructure growth driver; and LG Display and Soulbrain, which are positioned in the semiconductor materials upcycle.

The note says disagreement over the durability of AI demand remains, and that the structural improvement in revenue visibility from LTAs has not yet been fully reflected in valuations.

Potential catalysts cited by Nomura are: SK hynix executing the planned repurchase and cancellation within the next three months; the Samsung SDI-Stellantis joint venture beginning LFP battery production in October 2026; the MLCC industry approaching full capacity in the second half of 2026 through 2027E; HD Hyundai Electric’s data-center order share

reaching an estimated 16% in 2027; Samsung Electro-Mechanics beginning first mass production of glass substrates in 2028E; and HPSP’s capital-expenditure decision in the fourth quarter of 2026 through the first quarter of 2027E.
