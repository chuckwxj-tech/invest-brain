# Nomura’s initiation on $CXMT is one of the most aggressive semiconductor forecasts I have seen, and the report should be separated into two very different conclusions. The strat...

## 原文

Nomura’s initiation on $CXMT is one of the most aggressive semiconductor forecasts I have seen, and the report should be separated into two very different conclusions. The strategic thesis is credible and important: CXMT is becoming a genuine fourth force in global DRAM, China will continue directing capital and customers toward domestic memory, AI is structurally increasing memory intensity, and the incumbent suppliers’ prioritization of HBM is creating room for CXMT to expand in conventional DRAM. The financial model, however, requires such an extraordinary combination of capacity execution, node migration, price increases, margin expansion, uninterrupted equipment access and valuation re-rating that the CNY116 target price should be treated as a blue-sky scenario rather than a conventional base case.

The report initiates CXMT at Buy with a CNY116 target price against an IPO price of CNY8.66, implying upside of 1,239.5%. Nomura expects revenue and net profit to compound by 63% and 74%, respectively, between 2026 and 2028, supported by capacity expansion, node migration and higher memory prices, while CXMT’s global DRAM market share rises from roughly 10% today to approximately 18% by the end of 2028. The target price applies 20 times earnings to projected 2028 EPS of CNY5.79, twice the approximately 10 times historical valuation assigned to Micron, on the argument that CXMT deserves both a Chinese-market premium and a growth premium. 

The core industrial thesis is stronger than it may initially appear. CXMT is no longer merely a policy experiment or laboratory-stage semiconductor company. It is already China’s largest DRAM producer and the fourth-largest globally, behind Samsung, $SKHY and $MU, with products spanning DDR4, DDR5, LPDDR4X, LPDDR5 and LPDDR5X. The company’s market share reportedly increased from roughly 4% in 2024 to approximately 8% in 2025, while capacity utilization improved to around 95%. It also has access to a large captive domestic customer ecosystem that includes Alibaba Cloud, ByteDance, Tencent, Lenovo, Xiaomi, Honor, OPPO and vivo. This matters because China does not need CXMT to become the world’s most technologically advanced memory producer immediately; it needs CXMT to become reliable enough to replace imported DRAM across an increasing share of Chinese smartphones, personal computers, servers and AI infrastructure. 

The most credible part of the bullish case is therefore not that CXMT will immediately defeat SK Hynix in HBM. It is that CXMT can absorb the conventional DRAM demand increasingly neglected by the global leaders as they allocate more engineering resources, clean-room space and advanced packaging capacity toward HBM. AI is tightening the entire memory complex, but the economic benefits are distributed differently. SK Hynix captures the highest-quality rents through HBM, while CXMT can benefit from a secondary shortage in DDR and LPDDR as customers search for additional supply. This is particularly powerful inside China, where policy support, procurement preference and national-security considerations can create demand even when CXMT’s products are slightly less competitive on cost or performance.

Nomura estimates that CXMT’s prices are approximately 0% to 20% below those of the leading overseas DRAM suppliers, while its mainstream manufacturing technology remains around five years behind and produces only a few hundred gross dies per wafer compared with more than one thousand for the global leaders. Yet Chinese customers may still accept those economics because procurement decisions are not based solely on the lowest cost per bit. Supply-chain resilience, sanction exposure and domestic qualification increasingly have strategic value. CXMT therefore does not need to win a purely commercial contest against Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron; it needs to become the preferred domestic alternative within a politically protected and very large market. 

The capacity plan is also industrially plausible, although highly ambitious. Nomura estimates that CXMT had approximately 280,000 wafer starts per month at the end of 2025, rising to 350,000 by the end of 2026 and then adding approximately 100,000 per month in each of 2027 and 2028, reaching 550,000 wafer starts per month. CXMT raised CNY57.9 billion through its IPO, potentially increasing to CNY66.6 billion following the greenshoe, while Nomura estimates that each additional 1,000 monthly wafer starts costs approximately US$100 million. The IPO proceeds could therefore finance close to 100,000 monthly wafer starts, with subsequent expansion funded through operating cash flow, additional financing or government-linked capital. 

CXMT is also developing HBM capabilities, including a potential 50,000-wafer-per-month packaging facility in Shanghai, has reportedly supplied HBM3 samples to major Chinese technology customers and is developing HBM3E. However, investors should be precise about what this means. Packaging capacity is not equivalent to DRAM wafer capacity, product sampling is not equivalent to commercial qualification, and producing functional HBM is not equivalent to achieving the yields, bandwidth, thermal performance and reliability demanded by Nvidia-class accelerators. Nomura itself acknowledges that HBM qualification could take time, that initial yields may be lower and that CXMT’s technology remains materially behind the international leaders. 

This is why the investment implications are more immediately negative for commodity DRAM than for frontier HBM. CXMT is likely to become disruptive first in LPDDR, DDR4 and DDR5, where China’s domestic market, policy support and tolerance for weaker initial economics provide a natural route to scale. Its HBM ambitions are strategically significant, but the company is not yet a like-for-like competitor to SK Hynix. Our preference within global memory therefore remains SK Hynix first, Micron second and Samsung third. SK Hynix retains the strongest HBM positioning, Micron has meaningful HBM exposure but greater vulnerability to conventional DRAM competition, while Samsung faces both execution challenges at the frontier and growing pressure from CXMT in China.

The largest problem with the report is not the strategic argument. It is the extraordinary financial operating leverage embedded in Nomura’s forecasts.

CXMT’s revenue is projected to rise from CNY61.8 billion in 2025 to CNY290.7 billion in 2026, CNY560.8 billion in 2027 and CNY773.3 billion in 2028. Net profit attributable to shareholders is forecast to increase from only CNY1.9 billion in 2025 to CNY130.3 billion in 2026, CNY277.2 billion in 2027 and CNY393.1 billion in 2028. Gross margin rises from 41.0% to 83.7% in one year and subsequently reaches 90.5%, while operating margin expands to 73.0% and net margin exceeds 50% by 2028. 

Those are software-like margins for a capital-intensive commodity semiconductor manufacturer whose technology is reportedly five years behind the leaders and whose products are sold at a discount.

The mechanics of the model are even more aggressive than the headline margins suggest. Between 2025 and 2026, revenue increases by approximately CNY229 billion, but cost of goods sold rises by only around CNY11 billion. This means approximately 95% of every incremental yuan of revenue is assumed to become gross profit. The same incremental gross-margin assumption persists through 2027 and 2028. Nomura is effectively assuming that CXMT can sell dramatically more bits at sharply higher prices while the incremental manufacturing cost remains extraordinarily low.

There is a cyclical logic behind this assumption. A memory fab has high fixed costs, so when selling prices rise while utilization remains high, enormous operating leverage can occur. However, sustaining incremental gross margins above 90% for several consecutive years would require an exceptional and persistent shortage without the normal market response of demand destruction, customer substitution, inventory adjustment or aggressive competitor capex. It would also require CXMT’s node migration and yield improvement to progress with remarkably little disruption despite the company expanding capacity, moving into more difficult DDR5 and HBM products and increasing domestic equipment usage simultaneously.

The report’s industry supply-and-demand model shows why the earnings forecasts become so extreme. Nomura assumes memory demand can compound at more than 60% between 2026 and 2030, while industry bit supply grows only 30% to 40%. The table on page 18 consequently produces theoretical inventory levels below zero and utilization rates exceeding 100%, alongside an increase in total DRAM revenue from approximately US$169 billion in 2025 to nearly US$2.1 trillion by 2030. These are not physically possible inventory or utilization outcomes; they are mathematical representations of unsatisfied demand. 

That reveals the central weakness of the model: it does not properly clear the market. If demand exceeds supply by twenty or thirty percentage points for several years, the resulting price increases will change behavior. Customers will optimize models more aggressively, deploy lower-precision inference, improve caching, shorten context windows, delay workloads, substitute NAND or CXL memory where possible and redesign systems around available supply. Memory manufacturers will simultaneously increase capex, redirect wafer capacity and accept lower returns to capture the opportunity. The market will not passively sustain negative inventory while prices and volumes rise almost without interruption.

Nomura does account for memory-efficiency technologies such as quantization, grouped-query attention, PagedAttention, prompt caching, multi-head latent attention and TurboQuant, and applies a roughly fourfold reduction to prospective memory usage. Nevertheless, it still assumes agentic AI task volumes rise fiftyfold by 2030 and total AI-related memory usage increases by more than seven times, producing a memory-demand CAGR exceeding 60%. 

The direction is credible. Agentic AI should consume more memory than simple chatbot inference because persistent reasoning, tool calls, repeated context integration and multi-step execution create larger KV caches and more frequent data movement across HBM, DDR and storage. The report is also correct that memory efficiency may trigger Jevons paradox, whereby lower memory consumption per task makes AI cheaper and expands total usage. However, a 60% compound growth assumption across the entire memory complex remains highly sensitive to user adoption, task duration, concurrency, pricing and architectural change. Multiplying several uncertain variables together can produce an impressive exponential forecast, but it also means modest errors in each assumption can reduce the final demand estimate dramatically.

The report therefore combines a very bullish demand curve with a constrained supply curve, strong memory pricing, full fab utilization, rapid node migration, significant capacity expansion and improving yields. Each assumption can be individually defended, but assuming all of them occur simultaneously creates a highly convex earnings model in which nearly everything must go right.

The valuation then adds another layer of optimism on top of the earnings optimism. At the CNY8.66 IPO price, CXMT trades at only 4.2 times Nomura’s 2026 EPS, 2.1 times 2027 EPS and 1.5 times 2028 EPS. This apparent cheapness is not primarily evidence that the shares are mispriced; it is the mechanical result of forecasting a seventyfold increase in profit within one year. Nomura then applies a 20 times multiple to the already heroic 2028 earnings forecast, producing the CNY116 target price. 

In other words, the target requires two major victories simultaneously. CXMT must deliver the earnings explosion, and the market must subsequently re-rate those peak-cycle earnings from 1.5 times to 20 times.

The target multiple is justified partly by comparing ACM Research’s Shanghai-listed shares with its US-listed parent and observing that Chinese semiconductor companies often trade at one to three times US valuation multiples. Nomura then applies a twofold premium to Micron’s historical average multiple of approximately 10 times. This may describe how the A-share market behaves, but it is not a fundamental valuation argument. A scarcity premium, policy premium and domestic-listing premium can certainly exist, particularly for a strategically important company with only 2.8% free float, but those premiums can reverse rapidly when supply increases, lockups expire or earnings expectations normalize. Nomura’s target therefore depends heavily on market structure rather than simply on cash-flow generation. 

At CNY116 and approximately 67.9 billion diluted shares, CXMT would carry an implied equity value approaching CNY7.9 trillion, or more than US$1 trillion at a broad exchange-rate approximation. That would value CXMT at a level comparable with, or potentially above, the world’s largest and most technologically advanced memory producers even though Nomura acknowledges that its process technology remains approximately five years behind, its gross dies per wafer are materially lower and its HBM products remain in qualification. The target can be reached mathematically, but the required valuation would imply that investors are paying not merely for China’s fourth-largest DRAM company, but for an eventual national champion with near-software margins and an extraordinarily protected growth runway.

The balance-sheet forecast provides another indication of how aggressive the model has become. Despite annual capital expenditure of approximately CNY72 billion, CXMT is expected to generate free cash flow of CNY147 billion in 2026, CNY333 billion in 2027 and CNY500 billion in 2028, accumulating more than CNY1 trillion of cash and approximately CNY933 billion of net cash by 2028. This causes forecast EV/EBITDA to become negative by 2027 at the IPO valuation. 

A company whose cash balance is forecast to exceed its current market capitalization within three years is either one of the cheapest large companies ever listed or the earnings assumptions are too high. The more probable answer is that some combination of memory pricing, margins, capacity timing, yields, capex and working capital will normalize well before the balance sheet reaches that outcome.

Geopolitics is the largest non-cyclical risk, and the report may understate how deeply it interacts with the earnings model. Nomura estimates that only around 40% of CXMT’s semiconductor equipment is currently sourced domestically. China has made significant progress in etching, deposition, cleaning and packaging equipment, but advanced photoresists and several other process-critical materials remain dependent on foreign suppliers. Nomura specifically notes that the absence of a domestic substitute for high-end Japanese photoresist could prevent CXMT from moving below 15nm if restrictions tighten. 

In the report’s own downside scenario, the loss or delay of the proposed Shanghai expansion removes 200,000 monthly wafer starts and reduces 2028 revenue by approximately 30% and net profit by around 33%. Applying the same reduction to projected 2028 EPS would lower earnings from CNY5.79 to approximately CNY3.88. Even retaining the generous 20 times multiple would reduce the target value to roughly CNY78. If geopolitical risk also caused the valuation multiple to fall toward Micron’s historical 10 times, fair value would fall closer to CNY39. That remains significantly above the IPO price, but it illustrates how sensitive the CNY116 target is to both operational execution and multiple selection.

The report also deserves scrutiny on customer and distribution quality. More than 85% of revenue is generated through distributors, while the top five customers represented approximately 68% of 2025 business revenue. This may be normal during the early scale-up of a strategically directed domestic supply chain, but it creates less visibility into genuine end demand, inventory placement and sustainable pricing than a broadly diversified direct-sales model. It also raises the possibility that some growth is being accelerated by channel inventory, policy-driven procurement or customers qualifying alternative supply ahead of sanctions rather than by recurring market demand alone.

None of these criticisms invalidate the strategic opportunity. CXMT may be one of the most important semiconductor companies China has ever built. It has achieved something that would have appeared improbable a decade ago: commercially viable general-purpose DRAM production at meaningful global scale, a growing domestic customer base, advancing process nodes and an increasingly complete local equipment and packaging ecosystem. Its IPO financing, state support and guaranteed domestic demand give it a runway that purely commercial entrants would never receive.

The strongest investment thesis is therefore that CXMT becomes a structurally larger participant in global DRAM, not that every line of Nomura’s model proves correct. Market share rising toward the mid-teens is plausible. Revenue can grow substantially through capacity expansion, node migration and domestic substitution. Profitability can improve sharply from the depressed historical base. China’s new domestic DUV capability further reduces the probability that foreign restrictions can stop CXMT entirely.

The weaker thesis is that CXMT simultaneously reaches 18% global share, maintains full utilization, achieves rapid node migration, enters HBM successfully, compounds revenue above 60%, generates gross margins above 90%, accumulates CNY1 trillion of cash and deserves twice Micron’s valuation multiple. That is not impossible, but it is not an appropriately conservative base case.

Our sharpest conclusion is that Nomura is probably right about the company and too optimistic about the numbers.

CXMT is a strategic Buy as an industrial asset because China will support it, customers will qualify it and its global market share is likely to rise. Whether the listed shares are a Buy depends entirely on the market price after the IPO begins trading freely. At CNY8.66, the risk-reward would be extraordinary even after applying large discounts to Nomura’s earnings forecasts. At CNY116, investors would already be paying for near-perfect execution, sustained memory scarcity and a permanent Chinese valuation premium.

The report should therefore not be read as evidence that CXMT is worth exactly CNY116. It should be read as evidence that the global memory oligopoly is beginning to change.

CXMT is unlikely to displace SK Hynix at the HBM frontier soon, but it can become a serious source of pressure in conventional DRAM, particularly against Micron and Samsung in China. Over time, this could make the memory industry more bifurcated: a technologically demanding HBM market in which SK Hynix and the global leaders retain strong economic rents, and a conventional DRAM market in which CXMT uses scale, state financing and protected domestic demand to compress the incumbents’ pricing power.

That structural shift is real.

The CNY116 target price is still a leap of faith.
