CUSTOM AI SILICON, HBM AND ACCELERATOR COMPETITION

DIRECT HYPERSCALER ARCHITECTURE CONTROL IS A STRUCTURAL TAILWIND FOR CUSTOM ASIC ENABLERS AND A RELATIVE SHARE RISK FOR NVIDIA (READ-THROUGH 6)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: Broadcom (AVGO: US), Marvell Technology (MRVL: US), Synopsys (SNPS: US), Cadence Design Systems (CDNS: US), Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. (2330: Taiwan), NVIDIA (NVDA: US).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Highly positive over the long term for custom ASIC providers, EDA vendors, interface-IP providers, and advanced foundries. Moderately negative for NVIDIA’s share of incremental hyperscaler accelerator spending, although not necessarily negative for NVIDIA’s absolute revenue.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Rambus disclosed an IP design win with a Tier-1 US hyperscaler for next-generation HBM in a future AI chip. Management stated that “hyperscalers are playing a growing role in defining their own architectures,” whether they build products internally or use ASIC and semiconductor companies to implement them. Rambus further stated that once a hyperscaler selects a memory-controller implementation, “that proliferates into their own ecosystem.”

NEAR-TERM TRADING IMPLICATION: The commentary supports continued strength in custom AI ASIC design pipelines at Broadcom and Marvell and supports EDA and interface-IP demand at Synopsys and Cadence. The initial revenue effect is likely to appear in design activity, NRE, licensing, and engineering engagements before significant production silicon revenue is recorded.

The signal is less immediately negative for NVIDIA because hyperscaler custom chips require extended development and qualification periods. NVIDIA’s current accelerator ecosystem, software position, and deployment scale remain substantial. The near-term implication is greater customer investment in alternatives rather than an abrupt displacement of merchant GPUs.

TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: Hyperscalers increasingly optimize silicon around their own models, software frameworks, network topologies, power budgets, and deployment economics. They can define the architecture and then engage Broadcom, Marvell, or other custom-silicon partners to implement the chip. Synopsys and Cadence benefit through design tools and interface IP. TSMC benefits through leading-edge wafer fabrication and advanced packaging.

The selected architecture can be used across multiple generations or implemented by multiple semiconductor partners. This creates a multiplier effect for third-party IP. Rambus can license the same critical memory-controller architecture to different companies participating in the hyperscaler’s ecosystem.

NVIDIA faces a relative share-of-wallet risk because each successful hyperscaler-developed accelerator reduces dependence on merchant GPUs for at least a portion of internal workloads. The risk is greatest for stable, high-volume inference workloads that can justify the upfront cost of custom silicon. NVIDIA can continue to generate substantial absolute growth if the AI infrastructure market expands rapidly, but the mix of incremental spending may become more diversified.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: The AI accelerator market is moving from a predominantly merchant-GPU model toward a mixed architecture that includes merchant GPUs, hyperscaler-designed accelerators, custom network silicon, domain-specific inference chips, and specialized memory subsystems. This broadens the semiconductor value chain participating in AI capex.

Broadcom and Marvell are positioned to capture design and implementation value. Synopsys and Cadence benefit from greater design complexity and a larger number of advanced SoCs. TSMC benefits because custom chips add incremental leading-edge tape-outs and advanced packaging demand. NVIDIA remains the leading merchant platform but faces more sophisticated customers that are actively reducing strategic dependence on a single supplier.

PRIMARY RISK: Custom silicon projects can be delayed, canceled, or limited to narrow internal workloads. The economics are most compelling for hyperscalers with sufficient volume and engineering resources. The read-through is a structural diversification of AI silicon, not a forecast of broad merchant-GPU displacement over the next several quarters.

NEXT-GENERATION HBM DEMAND IS BROADENING BEYOND MERCHANT GPU VENDORS (READ-THROUGH 7)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: SK hynix (000660: South Korea), Micron Technology (MU: US), Samsung Electronics (005930: South Korea), Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. (2330: Taiwan), Rambus (RMBS: US).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Highly positive for the long-term HBM and advanced-packaging demand outlook. Near-term physical shipment impact is limited because the referenced AI chip remains in development, while near-term licensing impact is positive for Rambus.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Rambus disclosed “an exciting design win with a Tier-1 US hyperscaler for next-generation HBM in future AI chips.” Management clarified that this is an IP design win in a future SoC and that Rambus’s silicon-IP customers often take 12, 18, or 24 months to move from licensing and development into product deployment.

NEAR-TERM TRADING IMPLICATION: The design win is an immediate positive indicator for HBM development activity but should not be interpreted as near-term HBM shipment revenue for the memory suppliers. Rambus can recognize license revenue during the chip-development phase, while physical HBM stacks and advanced packaging will be required closer to the customer chip’s production ramp.

TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: A custom AI accelerator using next-generation HBM adds another high-volume potential buyer to the HBM market. Each accelerator typically requires multiple HBM stacks and a sophisticated memory controller. Increasing bandwidth requirements can raise the number of stacks per package, the capacity per stack, the number of interconnects, and the complexity of packaging.

SK hynix, Micron, and Samsung benefit through a larger and more diversified HBM customer base. TSMC benefits through advanced logic manufacturing and packaging because HBM-based accelerators generally require close integration between the compute die and memory stacks. Rambus benefits through licensing of the memory-controller or interface IP embedded in the SoC.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: HBM demand is becoming less dependent on the product cycles of a limited number of merchant accelerator vendors. Hyperscaler-developed accelerators, networking processors, and specialized AI SoCs can become additional HBM demand sources. This improves the durability of the HBM market and reduces the risk that demand is concentrated entirely in NVIDIA and AMD accelerator programs.

The HBM market remains highly dependent on supplier qualification and execution. The call does not identify which DRAM manufacturer will supply the hyperscaler program. The benefit is therefore positive for sector demand but not sufficient to determine company-specific share. SK hynix, Micron, and Samsung will capture different portions depending on performance, yield, qualification, capacity, and commercial terms.

COMPETITIVE IMPLICATION: The custom ASIC trend broadens HBM demand but can increase customer bargaining power over time as more accelerator suppliers emerge. Memory vendors benefit from volume and mix, while hyperscalers gain additional options for balancing performance, supply availability, and cost.

PRIMARY RISK: A design win for controller IP does not guarantee that the associated accelerator reaches high-volume production. Chip development, software readiness, model economics, and packaging availability remain potential constraints.

AI CONNECTIVITY AND INTERFACE IP

PCIe 7.0 CONFIRMS ANOTHER HIGH-SPEED AI CONNECTIVITY UPGRADE CYCLE (READ-THROUGH 8)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: Astera Labs (ALAB: US), Broadcom (AVGO: US), Marvell Technology (MRVL: US), Synopsys (SNPS: US), Cadence Design Systems (CDNS: US), Rambus (RMBS: US).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Positive. The impact is moderate to high over the long term for high-speed connectivity silicon and IP. Near-term financial benefits should accrue first to IP vendors, with merchant hardware revenue following later.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Rambus introduced PCIe 7.0 Switch IP supporting 128 GT/s and stated that the technology is intended for next-generation AI scale-up and scale-out architectures where bandwidth and latency are critical. Management indicated that license revenue can begin “in the coming quarters,” while customer chips may take 12 to 24 months to reach the market.

NEAR-TERM TRADING IMPLICATION: Synopsys, Cadence, and Rambus can monetize advanced interface development before end systems ship. Increasing customer engagement around PCIe 7.0 indicates that advanced AI-chip designs are already moving toward the next connectivity generation. The read-through is therefore more immediate for IP bookings and license revenue than for physical switch, retimer, or accelerator shipments.

TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: Higher-speed PCIe links increase signal-integrity challenges, power requirements, latency sensitivity, and the need for advanced switching and retiming. As AI systems scale across more accelerators, CPUs, memory pools, network interfaces, and storage devices, the number of high-speed links increases. The architecture can require additional switch silicon, retimers, PHYs, controllers, and validation IP.

Astera Labs benefits through increased demand for PCIe connectivity, retimers, and fabric products. Broadcom and Marvell benefit through switching, connectivity, and custom silicon. Synopsys and Cadence benefit through interface IP, verification, and design tools. Rambus benefits through licensing of its PCIe 7.0 Switch IP.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: AI system performance is increasingly constrained by data movement rather than compute alone. Higher accelerator counts do not generate proportional performance if data cannot be moved quickly between processors, memory, networking, and storage. Connectivity content per AI system should therefore rise as scale-up and scale-out architectures become more complex.

PCIe 7.0 also demonstrates that interface development is occurring before the corresponding end markets reach broad deployment. This creates a leading indicator for future hardware cycles. IP-license activity during 2026 can precede merchant silicon and system revenue by multiple years.

PRIMARY RISK: Proprietary accelerator fabrics and Ethernet-based architectures may capture portions of AI connectivity that might otherwise use PCIe. PCIe should remain foundational for host and peripheral connectivity, but its precise role in large-scale accelerator interconnects will vary by architecture.

CXL IS STRATEGICALLY VALIDATED, BUT THE MERCHANT PRODUCT TAM REMAINS FRAGMENTED (READ-THROUGH 9)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: Astera Labs (ALAB: US), Broadcom (AVGO: US), Marvell Technology (MRVL: US), Synopsys (SNPS: US), Cadence Design Systems (CDNS: US), Rambus (RMBS: US).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Positive for CXL protocol adoption, interface IP, and custom silicon. Cautious to moderately negative relative to aggressive assumptions for rapid standardization of a broad merchant CXL product market.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Management described CXL as “a very important interconnect protocol” that can play a role in agentic AI by managing the memory hierarchy and moving data between colder and hotter memory tiers. However, Rambus stated that the product market remains fragmented, with many implementations appearing “ASIC-like or customer-specific.” Rambus therefore intends to participate through IP while prioritizing product investment in scalable standard products.

NEAR-TERM TRADING IMPLICATION: The call supports the strategic relevance of CXL but suggests that industry revenue may initially accrue through custom programs, design services, and IP licensing rather than a rapid, uniform ramp of merchant memory-expansion products. This is favorable for Broadcom, Marvell, Synopsys, Cadence, and Rambus.

For Astera Labs, the read-through is mixed. CXL protocol adoption remains positive for the company’s broader connectivity position. However, Rambus’s direct customer feedback suggests that the standalone merchant CXL device opportunity may develop more slowly and with greater customer customization than aggressive total-addressable-market assumptions imply.

TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: Hyperscalers can use CXL to pool memory, tier capacity, improve utilization, and manage the movement of data between memory classes. However, each hyperscaler may have different CPU architectures, memory requirements, software stacks, latency targets, and deployment models. These differences encourage custom controllers, switches, and ASIC implementations.

IP vendors benefit because their interface technology can be embedded across many custom chips. Custom ASIC providers benefit because they can integrate CXL into customer-specific silicon. Merchant product vendors benefit only when specifications, workloads, and deployment models converge sufficiently to support standardized high-volume products.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: CXL is likely to become an important enabling protocol without necessarily producing a single dominant merchant product category in the near term. This distinction matters for valuation. Protocol adoption can be strong while revenue remains distributed across IP, custom ASICs, switches, controllers, and integrated SoCs.

The most favorable business models may be those that monetize CXL across multiple implementation paths rather than depend on 1 standardized product. Broadcom and Marvell can participate through custom silicon and connectivity. Synopsys, Cadence, and Rambus can participate through licensable IP. Astera Labs can benefit from ecosystem growth but must demonstrate that its merchant products achieve broad deployment rather than remain limited to selective architectures.

PRIMARY RISK: Standardization could advance more quickly than Rambus currently expects, creating a larger merchant product opportunity. Conversely, proprietary memory architectures and software complexity could delay CXL deployment even within custom systems.

CHINA DRAM AND GEOGRAPHIC COMPETITION

CXMT’S RISE IS POSITIVE FOR MEMORY IP LICENSORS BUT A LONGER-TERM COMMODITY DRAM RISK (READ-THROUGH 10)

AFFECTED COMPANIES: Rambus (RMBS: US), Micron Technology (MU: US), SK hynix (000660: South Korea), Samsung Electronics (005930: South Korea).

DIRECTIONAL IMPACT AND MAGNITUDE: Positive but initially modest for Rambus. Negative and moderate over the longer term for incumbent DRAM suppliers’ commodity-market share and pricing. Near-term impact on the global DRAM balance is likely to be limited relative to the longer-duration competitive signal.

SUPPORTING CALL EVIDENCE: Management stated that CXMT is expected to become “a strong player in the market” and confirmed that CXMT has a license agreement with Rambus. Management added, “Every company that builds memory has to have a license agreement with us, and they’re one of them,” and characterized CXMT’s progress as positive for Rambus over the long run.

NEAR-TERM TRADING IMPLICATION: CXMT’s development is directionally positive for Rambus’s licensing franchise because increased Chinese DRAM production reinforces the necessity and geographic breadth of Rambus intellectual property. However, the contract economics may include fixed or negotiated elements, so CXMT production growth should not be assumed to translate linearly into quarterly Rambus royalties.

For Micron, SK hynix, and Samsung, the near-term earnings impact is likely to remain manageable. Leading-edge process technology, customer qualifications, product quality, and scale take time to establish. The immediate signal is competitive validation rather than an abrupt increase in globally available high-end DRAM supply.

TRANSMISSION MECHANISM: As CXMT expands, China can source a greater portion of domestic DRAM demand from a local supplier. This can reduce addressable share for incumbent suppliers in Chinese consumer, PC, server, and industrial applications. Additional supply can also pressure industry pricing during periods when demand is not sufficiently strong to absorb new capacity.

Rambus benefits because an additional memory producer requires access to foundational memory-interface intellectual property. The relationship expands Rambus’s licensing base and demonstrates that its IP remains relevant across geopolitical and regional supply chains.

LONGER-DURATION FUNDAMENTAL SHIFT: Chinese memory self-sufficiency is progressing from a strategic policy objective toward a commercial competitive factor. CXMT’s emergence could gradually weaken the supply discipline historically associated with a concentrated DRAM industry. The risk is greater in commodity DDR products than in HBM and the most advanced server memory, where technology, packaging, yields, and customer qualifications remain more difficult.

Micron is potentially more exposed to geopolitical and competitive developments involving Chinese memory supply because of its US domicile and historical sensitivity to China-related restrictions. Samsung and SK hynix face broader market-share and pricing risk but retain scale, technology leadership, and diversified product portfolios.

The HBM market provides an important buffer for incumbents. Even if CXMT becomes more competitive in commodity DDR, SK hynix, Micron, and Samsung can protect mix and profitability through HBM, advanced server DRAM, and other differentiated products. The call therefore supports a bifurcated memory thesis: rising China competition in conventional DRAM alongside strong structural demand for advanced HBM and high-performance server memory.

PRIMARY RISKS: CXMT’s ultimate scale, yield, technology level, and customer penetration remain uncertain. Geopolitical restrictions can either slow its progress or accelerate domestic Chinese purchasing and investment. The direction of competitive pressure is clearer than its timing or magnitude.

PORTFOLIO SYNTHESIS

The highest-conviction near-term positive read-throughs are stronger CPU-based server demand for AMD and Intel, higher DDR5 content for Micron, SK hynix, and Samsung, and continued high-speed interface demand for Rambus and the broader server-memory ecosystem. The combination of approximately 12% server-unit growth, approximately 20% Rambus product growth, rising channel counts, and no visible customer inventory buildup indicates that data center demand is both expanding and becoming more memory-intensive.

The most important near-term negative read-through is that MRDIMM should not be treated as a meaningful Q4 2026 revenue catalyst. The architecture remains strategically attractive, but broad commercial adoption is expected in 2027. Platform launch dates should not be equated with immediate component revenue. A second near-term constraint is the lack of pricing power in standard memory-interface products. Tight supply may support shipment visibility and competitive share for well-prepared vendors, but it is unlikely to produce substantial ASP-driven gross-margin expansion.

The strongest longer-duration positive read-through is the proliferation of hyperscaler-defined custom AI silicon. Broadcom, Marvell, Synopsys, Cadence, TSMC, HBM suppliers, and advanced connectivity vendors are positioned to capture a larger portion of AI-system value as hyperscalers design proprietary accelerators, networking chips, and memory subsystems. Rambus’s direct Tier-1 hyperscaler HBM engagement provides concrete evidence that this architecture shift is occurring before end chips are publicly deployed.

The corresponding longer-duration relative risk applies to NVIDIA’s share of incremental hyperscaler accelerator spending. The risk is not an immediate decline in overall NVIDIA demand; it is a gradual diversification of AI capex toward internal accelerators and custom ASICs for stable, high-volume workloads. The total AI market can remain highly expansionary while NVIDIA captures a smaller proportion of incremental units at selected hyperscalers.

Within connectivity, PCIe 7.0 is a clear structural positive, with IP revenue preceding merchant hardware by 12 to 24 months. CXL is more nuanced. The protocol is strategically relevant, but the product market remains fragmented and custom. This favors IP and ASIC business models and creates execution risk for merchant CXL forecasts that assume rapid standardization.

Within memory, the medium-term setup is bifurcated. HBM, high-performance DDR5, higher channel counts, and MRDIMM support premium content and structural bit growth. At the same time, CXMT’s emergence introduces a longer-duration competitive risk to commodity DRAM pricing and incumbent share. The most favorable positioning remains concentrated in companies with exposure to differentiated HBM, high-speed server memory, custom silicon, interface IP, and advanced connectivity rather than undifferentiated commodity memory alone.
