Wind Financial Morning Post: July 31, 2026

Market Brief

Central Political Bureau of the CPC convened a meeting, deciding to convene the Fifth Plenary Session of the 20th Central Committee.

The Political Bureau meeting laid out economic work for the second half of the year: planning new policy measures and strengthening countercyclical regulation.

Vice Premier He Lifeng of the State Council held a video call with U.S. Treasury Secretary Bessent and U.S. Trade Representative Greer.

Reports indicate the Japanese government intervened again in the foreign exchange market, with the yen rebounding over 3% against the U.S. dollar.

On Thursday, global stock markets diverged; U.S. storage chip stocks surged sharply, with the Nasdaq Composite rising 2.78%.

Microsoft’s stock surged over 15%, adding about $450 billion in market value, setting a new record for single-day market value gain in global stock markets.

Apple’s fourth fiscal quarter revenue guidance fell short of expectations, with its after-hours stock price dropping over 8%.

Samsung Electronics’ second-quarter operating profit increased 18-fold; it expects continued shortages in memory chip supply.

Top Stories

On July 30, the Political Bureau of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China convened a meeting, deciding to hold the Fifth Plenary Session of the 20th Central Committee of the Communist Party of China in Beijing in October this year. The main agenda includes the Political Bureau reporting its work to the Central Committee and studying key issues concerning the persistent advancement of comprehensive and strict party governance. The meeting analyzed the current economic situation and deployed economic work for the second half of the year. The meeting emphasized implementing a more proactive fiscal policy and a moderately loose monetary policy, fully leveraging the effectiveness of existing policies, promptly planning and introducing practical and effective new policy measures, strengthening countercyclical regulation, and expanding domestic demand and optimizing supply. Macroeconomic policies must be strengthened and made more efficient, accelerating fiscal spending and bond fund utilization, and vigorously promoting the “two major projects” and “two new initiatives.” Ensure the basic livelihoods of grassroots levels. Comprehensively apply and appropriately adjust monetary policy tools, and optimize policies for fiscal-financial synergy to stimulate domestic demand.

The Political Bureau meeting emphasized steadily advancing the planning and construction of the “six networks.” Deeply implement the “AI+” initiative, develop new forms of intelligent economy, and improve the artificial intelligence governance system. Actively promote breakthroughs in frontier technologies and the development of future industries, focusing on building emerging pillar industries and continuously promoting the transformation and upgrading of traditional industries. Continue comprehensive rectification of “inwardly competitive” competition. Solidly build safety barriers. Stabilize the real estate market, implement a comprehensive debt resolution plan, and steadily advance the reform and risk mitigation of local small and medium-sized financial institutions, reducing scale while improving quality. Deepen comprehensive reforms in capital market financing and investment, enhancing the resilience and confidence of the capital market.

He Lifeng, China’s lead on Sino-U.S. economic and trade affairs and Vice Premier of the State Council, held a video call with U.S. Treasury Secretary Bessent and U.S. Trade Representative Greer, the U.S. leads on economic and trade affairs. They had candid, in-depth, and constructive exchanges on maintaining stability in Sino-U.S. economic and trade relations in the next stage, expanding practical cooperation, and properly addressing each other’s concerns. China expressed serious concerns regarding recent U.S. trade restrictions on China.

On Thursday, Microsoft’s stock surged 15.51%, marking its largest single-day gain since October 2008, driving the company’s market value to increase by about $450 billion. This gain surpassed NVIDIA’s previous record—last year, after former U.S. President Trump announced a 90-day suspension of tariffs, NVIDIA’s market value rose by $440 billion in a single day.

Apple Inc.’s third fiscal quarter revenue reached $109.42 billion, up about 16% year-over-year, slightly above market expectations; earnings per share (EPS) were $2.02, up about 29%. Revenue from the Greater China region was $18.82 billion, and revenue from services was $30.74 billion, both below market expectations. Apple CEO Tim Cook stated that EPS and operating cash flow for the third fiscal quarter both reached record highs for the same period in the company’s history. Apple expects fourth fiscal quarter revenue to grow 9%–11%, below the market expectation of 12.1% growth; the company’s after-hours stock price dropped over 8%.

Samsung Electronics reported consolidated revenue of 171.5 trillion won in the second quarter, up 130% year-over-year; operating profit reached 89.5 trillion won, up 1,814%, both historic highs. The semiconductor division’s operating profit reached 89.2 trillion won. Looking ahead to the second half of the year, Samsung Electronics expects demand growth for server DRAM, enterprise SSDs, and HBM to accelerate further, and anticipates continued tight supply in the memory chip market.

China Macro & Policy

In response to the United States adding foreign power inverters and advanced robotics to its “covered list,” the Ministry of Commerce responded that this move essentially discriminates against and suppresses Chinese enterprises and products. China urged the U.S. to immediately revoke these measures and cease such erroneous practices. If the U.S. persists, China will resolutely counteract to safeguard its legitimate rights and interests.

Data released by the National Bureau of Statistics showed that in 2025, the added value of the “three new” economy—centered on new industries, new business forms, and new business models—reached 25.79 trillion yuan, up 6.2% year-over-year; it accounted for 18.39% of GDP, an increase of 0.38 percentage points from the previous year.

The China National Laboratory for Financial Development and Growth released the “Second Quarter 2026 Macro Leverage Ratio Report,” showing that the macro leverage ratio decreased by 1.1 percentage points to 308.2% in the second quarter, marking the first quarterly decline since 2022. The household sector’s leverage ratio fell by 1.3 percentage points, continuing deleveraging since the second quarter of 2024.

China Equities Markets

A-shares fluctuated lower; the SSE Index closed down 0.62% at 3,804.69, the Shenzhen Component Index fell 2.73%, the ChiNext Index dropped 3.97%, the Star 50 Index slid 5.38%, and the Wind All China Index fell 1.97%. Market turnover reached 2.36 trillion yuan. On the board, semiconductors and computing hardware supply chains continued to adjust, while the CPO theme plunged. Large consumer and large financial sectors rose against the trend, with liquor stocks leading gains; Industrial and Commercial Bank of China and China Construction Bank both hit all-time highs.

Hong Kong markets traded narrowly; the Hang Seng Index closed up 0.2% at 25,858.88, the Hang Seng Tech Index fell 1.25%, and the Hang Seng China Enterprises Index edged up 0.25%. Semiconductor supply chains and large model concepts plunged; Zhipu fell nearly 17%. Southbound funds net sold HK$8.571 billion; Alibaba and SMIC were heavily sold off by over HK$1.1 billion each, while Tencent Holdings received net buying of HK$1 billion.

Prominent investors and institutions, including Bi Dong, Ge Weidong, Ding Ying, Chairman of Kangmande, and Chongyang Investment, have recently issued frequent views on the current technology stock correction. Although their analyses of the short-term market volatility differ in emphasis, most agree that this adjustment is primarily a temporary correction driven by deleveraging and crowded trades, rather than a fundamental reversal in the AI industry’s underlying logic.

China’s first listed humanoid robotics company has arrived! Yutu Technology announced that it plans to issue 40.446434 million shares in its STAR Market IPO, accounting for 10% of total post-issue shares. The preliminary inquiry date is August 5, and the subscription date is August 10. Yutu Technology’s employee asset management plan intends to participate in the IPO strategic placement, with a scale not exceeding 10% of the offering. Yutu Technology also disclosed that it expects revenue for the first half of this year to range between 1.052 billion yuan and 1.128 billion yuan, with net profit between 258 million yuan and 306 million yuan.

China Industry News

The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology established the Technical Committee on Quantum Information Standardization. The committee primarily handles the formulation and revision of industry standards in areas including foundational common technologies for quantum information, quantum computing, quantum communication, and quantum precision measurement.

The National Energy Administration reported that in the first half of this year, China added 117 million kilowatts of new renewable energy capacity, accounting for 73.9% of total new capacity. Renewable energy generation reached nearly 2 trillion kilowatt-hours, accounting for 41.2% of total power generation, surpassing 40% for the first time. In the first half of the year, the proportion of coal-fired power generation in total generation fell below 50% for the first time.

At 24:00 on July 31, China’s retail oil price cap will undergo its next adjustment. According to calculations by Zuo Chuang Information, as of the close on July 29, China’s reference crude oil change rate stood at 15.38%, with an expected increase of 680 yuan per ton for gasoline and diesel, corresponding to a gasoline price increase of approximately 0.53 yuan per liter for grade 92.

In the first half of this year, China’s exports of optical communication equipment laser transceivers and integrated circuits used for AI computing infrastructure increased by 22.3% and 88.7% year-over-year, respectively; exports of laser ranging radar for intelligent driving services increased by 58.3%; and 3D printer exports increased by 109.3%.

According to data from the National Bureau of Statistics, in the first half of the year, China’s large-scale cultural enterprises achieved revenue of 7.2 trillion yuan, up 4.6% year-over-year. Among these, cultural new business forms represented by digital publishing and intelligent unmanned aerial vehicle manufacturing for entertainment achieved revenue of 3.52 trillion yuan, up 9.6%.

Fintech

OpenAI announced a price reduction for its two latest AI models, GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-5.6 Luna, just about three weeks after their public release. The price of Terra was cut by 20%, with the input token rate reduced to $2 per million tokens and the output token rate to $12 per million tokens; Luna’s price was cut by 80%, with the input token rate reduced to $0.2 per million tokens and the output token rate to $1.2 per million tokens.

Corporate News & Updates

Meta stated that it has committed nearly $700 billion in future spending through long-term and short-term agreements, covering artificial intelligence data centers, cloud computing, and other areas.

Sources familiar with the matter revealed that TSMC is developing an advanced chip packaging technology, targeting Intel’s existing EMIB solution. The development remains in an early stage, and the timeline for commercial mass production has yet to be determined.

Xiaomi Auto held a technology launch event, officially unveiling the “Xiaomi Kunlun Technology Architecture.” The Xiaomi Pengcheng N90 seven-seat extended-range SUV and Xiaomi Pengcheng N70 five-seat extended-range SUV, both equipped with this architecture, were unveiled and opened for pre-sale at prices of 299,900 yuan and 259,900 yuan respectively, with official release scheduled for September.

ByteDance launched an organizational restructuring for its AI business. The Feishu product team and the DouBao product team will be integrated into a new DouBao product team. The Feishu GTM team will also be merged with the Volcano Engine team to form a new ToB GTM organization, responsible overall for the marketing, sales, and customer service of ByteDance’s cloud services.

Tencent’s WorkBuddy released version 5.3.5, jointly with Tencent Docs launching the “human-AI co-writing” capability, enabling users to directly collaborate and co-edit in real time within documents such as Word, Excel, PPT, and Markdown.

Toyota’s global sales in the first half of the year reached 5.3905 million vehicles, down 2.8% year-over-year, marking its first year-over-year decline in a year. Sales in China declined 17.1% to 694,700 vehicles in the first half.

Porsche delivered 122,300 new vehicles globally in the first half of the year, down 16.5% year-over-year, the lowest level for the same period since 2020. Deliveries in China fell sharply by 31.93% to 14,500 units.

Global Macro & Policy

The U.S. Central Command issued a statement that U.S. forces conducted a large-scale strike against Iran on the evening of the 29th Eastern Time, in response to Iran’s attempted attack on U.S. forces in the Middle East the previous day. On July 30, Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps issued a statement that, in response to the U.S. strike, Iran attacked U.S. bases in Kuwait and Jordan.

As the Iran conflict and the Russia-Ukraine war continue to deplete U.S. weapons stockpiles, the U.S. Department of Defense has pledged nearly $100 billion to defense contractors to accelerate production of “Patriot” and “THAAD” interceptors. Additionally, the Pentagon ordered 14 nuclear submarines, with a contract value of $76.6 billion.

The Bank of England maintained its benchmark interest rate unchanged at 3.75%, in line with market expectations. However, internal divisions among monetary policymakers widened further, with the number of members advocating a 25-basis-point rate hike increasing to three. The Bank of England stated that it expects inflation to rise later this year and is prepared to take necessary action at any time to prevent persistent high inflation.

The U.S. second-quarter GDP preliminary annualized quarterly growth rate was 1.5%, below the first quarter’s 2.1% and market expectations. Consumer spending and investment remained strong, but a decline in net exports dragged down the overall figure. The U.S. June core PCE price index rose 0.1% month-over-month and 3.3% year-over-year, easing from May. The overall PCE price index fell 0.1% month-over-month in June, marking its first negative reading since the outbreak of the pandemic in 2020.

The eurozone’s second-quarter GDP grew 0.4% quarter-over-quarter and 1% year-over-year, both exceeding market expectations. Germany, the eurozone’s largest economy, recorded a 0.2% quarter-over-quarter GDP growth, surpassing expectations. Meanwhile, France and Italy both recorded 0.2% quarter-over-quarter GDP growth, and Spain recorded 0.7%.

Germany’s July CPI rose 2.8% year-over-year, higher than June’s 2.4%, marking the highest level in nearly three months. On the same day, Spain’s July inflation rate rose to 3.8%, above market expectations. Economists expect the eurozone’s overall inflation rate in July to rise from 2.7% to 2.9%, further strengthening market expectations of a rate hike in September.

The European Union launched a plan to build AI super-factories, inviting companies to apply for the construction of up to seven publicly funded AI data centers. The EU and its member states will provide a total of 10 billion euros in public funding, with the potential to leverage at least 20 billion euros in private investment.

Japanese Prime Minister Kishi Hayashi announced that, as a measure to combat rising prices, the government plans to reduce the consumption tax rate on food and beverages from the current 8% to 1% starting April 2027, for a two-year period. This will be the first tax rate reduction since Japan introduced the consumption tax in 1989.

Saudi Arabia’s real GDP contracted 4.8% year-over-year in the second quarter this year, dragged down by a significant negative growth in the oil sector. Data showed that Saudi Arabia’s oil sector shrank 24.7% in the second quarter, while the non-oil sector grew 0.9%.

Global Equity Markets

All three major U.S. stock indices closed higher; the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 1.19% to 52,208.06, the S&P 500 rose 1.66% to 7,437.63, and the Nasdaq Composite rose 2.78% to 25,122.18. Microsoft rose over 15%, posting its largest single-day gain since October 2008. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index surged over 8%, ending a five-day losing streak. Roundhill Storage ETF jumped nearly 16%, SanDisk rose about 26%, Micron Technology rose over 18%, and SK Hynix rose over 17%. The Wind US Magnificent Seven Index rose 2.28%, Amazon rose nearly 4%, and Tesla rose over 3%. The Nasdaq Golden Dragon China Index rose 1.05%, China Telecom rose over 16%, and TAL Education Group rose over 13%.

Europe’s three major stock indices closed mixed; Germany’s DAX rose 0.6% to 25,612.03, France’s CAC 40 rose 0.92% to 8,485.64, and the UK’s FTSE 100 fell 0.1% to 10,897.27. Strong corporate earnings lifted French and German markets, but escalating Middle East tensions weighed slightly on the UK market.

Major Asia-Pacific markets closed with mixed results. The KOSPI fell 1.23% to 5,593.56, with heavyweight SK Hynix down nearly 6%. Regulatory filings showed that SK Group Chairman Choi Tae-won purchased 3,620 shares of SK Hynix. The Nikkei 225 rose 0.71% to 61,867.43; AI and semiconductor sectors rebounded, with Advantest rising 10.85%. India’s SENSEX 30 rose 0.35% to 77,928.15.

Amazon’s second-quarter net sales were $200.6 billion, exceeding the expected $197.01 billion; operating profit was $27.46 billion, surpassing analyst expectations of $23.61 billion; Amazon’s free cash flow outflow over the past twelve months decreased to $7.6 billion. Amazon expects third-quarter net sales of $197 billion to $202 billion, below the analyst expectation of $203.93 billion.

BMW Group’s operating profit for the first half of the year was 3.64 billion euros, down 37.4% year-over-year; net profit was 2.87 billion euros, down 28.5% year-over-year; revenue was 62.27 billion euros, down 8%. Vehicle deliveries in the first half fell to 1.157 million units.

LG Electronics’ second-quarter sales increased 14.9% year-over-year to 23.83 trillion won, and operating profit rose 147% year-over-year to 1.58 trillion won, both reaching historic highs for the same period. Net profit rose 28.1% year-over-year to 781.3 billion won.

Bonds

China’s interbank bond market rallied strongly; major government bond yields declined significantly; the 50-year Treasury bond “26 Super Long Special Treasury Bond 03” yield fell 3.25 basis points to 2.32%. Treasury futures closed higher across the board; the 30-year main contract rose 0.59%, extending its eight-month high. The People’s Bank of China conducted 270.5 billion yuan in 7-day reverse repurchase operations and 600 billion yuan in overnight reverse repurchase operations, net injecting 66.5 billion yuan.

The Ministry of Finance will tender an additional 15 billion yuan in RMB government bonds in Hong Kong on August 5, including 5 billion yuan in 2-year, 4 billion yuan in 3-year, 4 billion yuan in 5-year, 1 billion yuan in 15-year, and 1 billion yuan in 30-year bonds.

U.S. Treasury yields mostly declined; the 2-year yield fell 3.09 basis points to 4.240%, the 3-year yield fell 3.13 basis points to 4.292%, the 5-year yield fell 1.93 basis points to 4.384%, the 10-year yield fell 0.4 basis point to 4.669%, and the 30-year yield rose 1.91 basis points to 5.216%.

Commodities

U.S. crude oil futures fell 0.59% to $83.96 per barrel; Brent crude futures fell 1.23% to $87.01 per barrel. Prospects for new negotiations between the U.S. and Iran eased supply risk concerns, pushing oil prices lower.

International precious metals futures generally closed higher, with COMEX gold futures rising 1.61% to $4,162.80 per ounce, and COMEX silver futures climbing 2.02% to $59.27 per ounce. 

Most London base metals rose; LME tin rose 2.46% to $55,205.0 per ton, LME copper rose 1.60% to $13,798.5 per ton, LME zinc rose 1.12% to $3,609.5 per ton, LME nickel rose 1.04% to $17,315.0 per ton, LME aluminum rose 0.41% to $3,193.0 per ton, and LME lead fell 0.32% to $1,893.5 per ton.

The World Gold Council’s latest report showed that global gold demand in the second quarter was flat year-over-year at 1,269 tonnes; first-half cumulative demand reached 2,522 tonnes, up 2% year-over-year, with total demand value reaching $380 billion, a record high. Global central banks net purchased 289 tonnes of gold in the second quarter, up 62% year-over-year, the highest quarterly purchase in nearly four years. Gold ETFs recorded a net outflow of 45 tonnes in the second quarter; jewelry demand fell to 278 tonnes, the lowest single-quarter level since the pandemic.

The Russian government announced that to stabilize the domestic fuel market, it will extend the ban on exports of gasoline, diesel, marine fuel, and light diesel by six months until January 31, 2027. Starting September 1, fuel producers will be exempt from the ban on direct exports of diesel, marine fuel, and light diesel.
